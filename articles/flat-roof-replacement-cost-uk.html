Two Flat Roof Quotes, 500 Pounds Apart. Divide Both By The Years And The Cheap One Loses

A flat roof is the one job where the cheapest quote is almost always the most expensive decision, and the reason is not workmanship. It is lifespan. Here is what felt, EPDM and fibreglass actually cost once you spread each one over the years it is expected to last.

Ruhul Amin Published 14 September 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The short version

  • Felt is cheapest to buy and dearest to own. Around 40 to 80 pounds per square metre fitted, with an expected life of roughly 10 to 20 years. Take the middle of both and it works out near 80 pounds a year on a 20 square metre roof.
  • EPDM rubber costs more and lasts far longer. Around 60 to 95 pounds per square metre fitted, expected life 20 to 30 years. Same roof, around 68 pounds a year.
  • The payback: the extra 500 pounds you pay for EPDM is repaid in full at year 15, the point at which the felt roof would need doing again. Everything after that is free roof.
  • GRP fibreglass is the odd one out. Dearest per square metre and no longer lived than EPDM, so it only makes sense where the detailing genuinely needs it.
  • Scaffolding and access are quoted separately and are charged again on every replacement. Two felt roofs means paying for access twice.
  • There is nothing to buy on Amazon here and we have not linked any. This is a trade job and a patch kit on a failed roof is money set on fire.

1. Why the two quotes are not comparable

A roofer hands you two numbers for the same 20 square metre garage roof. Felt at 1,200 pounds. Rubber at 1,700 pounds. Most people take the first one, and most people are wrong, but not for the reason the salesman gives.

The two quotes are not for the same thing. They are quotes for different lengths of time. One buys you roughly fifteen years of dry garage. The other buys you roughly twenty five. Comparing them as though they were the same purchase is like comparing a one year car lease with a four year one because both say "car".

The fix is a single division. Take the fitted price, divide it by the expected life in years, and compare the results. That one sum reorders almost every flat roof quote in Britain, and it is the same method we used on pitched roof repair against replacement.

2. Felt, EPDM and GRP: what you are actually buying

Torch on felt. Layers of bitumen felt bonded with heat. Cheap, quick, every roofer can fit it, and it is what is already on most British garages and extensions. Trade guidance across UK roofing suppliers puts fitted cost at roughly 40 to 80 pounds per square metre and expected life at 10 to 20 years. The wide life range is real: a three layer built up system laid properly is at the top of it, a two layer cheap job is at the bottom.

EPDM rubber. A single sheet of synthetic rubber, usually one piece with no seams on a domestic roof. Fitted cost sits around 60 to 95 pounds per square metre, with some London quotes running to 120. Expected life is 20 to 30 years, and manufacturers commonly warrant it for 20. No seams is the whole point, because seams are where felt roofs fail.

GRP fibreglass. Resin and matting laid wet and cured in place. Roughly 90 to 120 pounds per square metre fitted, expected life 20 to 25 years. It gives a hard walkable surface and neat moulded edges, which matters on a balcony or a roof terrace. On a garage that nobody walks on, you are paying for a property you will never use.

Felt is the cheapest roof to buy and the most expensive roof to own. Those two facts are not in tension. They are the same fact seen from different ends of fifteen years.

4. Worked example: the Chowdhury household in Luton

A detached garage with an 18 square metre flat roof, felt, laid some time in the early 2010s and now blistering along one edge. Two quotes, both from local firms with real addresses.

Felt, three layer built up: 18 x 60 pounds = 1,080 pounds, plus 280 pounds access. Total 1,360 pounds. Over 15 years that is 90.67 pounds a year.

EPDM, single sheet: 18 x 85 pounds = 1,530 pounds, plus the same 280 pounds access. Total 1,810 pounds. Over 25 years that is 72.40 pounds a year.

The premium is 450 pounds. The felt roof would need replacing again at year 15, at which point the Chowdhurys would spend another 1,360 pounds. The EPDM roof is still working. Payback on the 450 pound premium: year 15.

Two honest caveats on that sum. It assumes they still own the house in 2041, and it assumes nobody puts a ladder through the rubber. If the plan is to sell within five years, the felt roof is the rational choice and the cost per year arithmetic does not apply to you, because you will not be there to pay the second bill.

The second caveat is the one roofers actually care about. Both lifespans depend entirely on the deck underneath and on the falls, meaning the slope that carries water to the outlet. A perfect EPDM sheet over rotten chipboard with standing water on it will not see twenty five years. If a quote does not mention the deck, get another quote.

5. When repairing beats replacing

A flat roof is worth repairing rather than replacing in exactly two situations. The first is a single clean mechanical failure, a split at one upstand or a lifted flashing, on a covering that is otherwise sound and under about eight years old. The second is when you are selling inside a year and the roof is watertight.

Everything else is a false economy, and the reason is that flat roof failures are rarely local. Water tracks under the covering and rots the deck a long way from where it shows inside. By the time there is a stain on the garage ceiling the wet area is usually several times larger than the stain. Paying 250 pounds for a patch on a fifteen year old felt roof buys you a winter, not a repair.

Guttering is worth checking at the same time and costs almost nothing to sort while the access is already up, which we cover in replacing versus cleaning guttering. If the flat roof in question is over a conservatory rather than a garage, the arithmetic is different again and is set out in the conservatory roof replacement payback guide.

Frequently asked

Do I need planning permission to replace a flat roof? Not for a like for like replacement at the same height. If you are changing the height, the shape, or converting the roof into a balcony or terrace, that is a different question and you should check with your local planning authority before ordering anything.

Is a 20 year EPDM warranty the same as a 20 year life? No, and this is the most common misreading in the trade. A material warranty covers the rubber sheet failing on its own. It does not usually cover installation faults, the deck, the falls, or anything the installer did. Ask specifically whether the warranty is a manufacturer material warranty or an insurance backed installation guarantee, because they are worth very different amounts.

Can I fit EPDM myself? Physically yes, and the materials are sold to the public. The seams and upstands are where DIY installations fail, and a failed flat roof damages the structure below it rather than just leaking. Given that the whole argument above rests on getting 25 years rather than 6, this is not the job to learn on.

Why did my roofer quote per roof rather than per square metre? Because small roofs carry fixed costs that do not scale, mainly access, waste disposal and a minimum day rate. On anything under about 12 square metres the per square metre figures above will look too low. Ask for the quote broken into materials, labour, access and disposal, and compare those four lines between firms rather than comparing totals.

The honest verdict

If you are staying in the house for more than about eight years, fit EPDM. The extra 450 to 500 pounds buys roughly ten extra years and repays itself the day the felt roof would have needed doing again.

If you are selling inside five years, fit felt, and do not let anyone talk you into paying for a lifespan you will not own.

Whichever you pick, spend the money on the deck and the falls rather than on the brand of covering. Every flat roof that fails early in Britain fails for the same two reasons, and neither of them is the top layer.
