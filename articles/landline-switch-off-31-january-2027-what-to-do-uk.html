The Old Phone Network Is Switched Off On 31 January 2027. 1.5 Million Lines Have Not Moved. Here Is What Each Kind Of Household Needs.

On 31 July 2026 Openreach said there were six months left and no extension. The analogue network that has carried landline calls for decades, the PSTN, is retired on 31 January 2027, and around 1.5 million lines were still on it. This is not a BT-only story: every provider that uses the Openreach copper network is on the same clock. What you need depends on which of three households you are.

The short version

  • The PSTN is retired on 31 January 2027. Openreach's 31 July 2026 statement: about 1.5 million lines still on it, including around 350,000 business premises, and "no extension to the deadline".
  • Your phone number does not change and most handsets keep working. The phone plugs into your broadband router instead of the wall socket. Extension sockets around the house usually stop working.

1. What is actually being switched off

The Public Switched Telephone Network is the analogue system that turns your voice into a signal on a copper pair and switches it through exchanges. Openreach, which runs the network most UK providers sell over, is withdrawing it on 31 January 2027. Calls after that run as data over your broadband connection. The trade name varies by provider, BT calls it Digital Voice, others say digital landline or VoIP, but the mechanism is the same: the handset plugs into the router, or into a small adapter, rather than the socket on the wall.

The original stop date was December 2025. It was pushed back thirteen months in 2024 after telecare alarms stopped working following migrations. Openreach said on 31 July 2026 that the deadline stands, and it has raised the wholesale rental price of the old line product by 40% in July 2026 and a further 40% in October to push providers to move customers.

Ofcom does not run the switch-off, but it sets the rules providers must follow. The two that matter to a household are that providers must give customers who depend on their landline at least one hour of power back-up to reach 999 in a power cut, free of charge, and that they must identify and protect vulnerable customers through the move. Ofcom has said it will take enforcement action against providers that do not.

2. Household one: broadband, a phone, nothing special

This is most homes, and very little happens. Your provider writes with a date, and on that date your handset is plugged into the phone port on the back of the router. Your number stays the same. Cordless phones with a base station work as before. Call charges are set by your tariff, not the technology.

Two things do change. Extension sockets go dead, because the wall wiring is no longer connected to anything. A bedroom phone on an extension needs either a cordless handset paired to a base at the router, or an adapter from the provider to reuse the old wiring. And in a power cut the router is off, so the landline is off. If you have a charged mobile in the house, providers treat that as your back-up and may not send a battery unit unless you ask.

The switch should be free. Providers have been moving customers for years without charge. If you are quoted a fee, ask exactly what it is for; if the answer is a new router, ask whether the existing one has a phone port.

3. Household two: a pendant alarm, a lift, or a medical device

This is where the risk actually sits and why the deadline moved. Telecare alarms, the pendant or wrist button that dials a monitoring centre, were designed for the analogue line. Some work over digital, some do not, and a device that dials but cannot be understood by the centre is worse than one that fails openly.

The commitments here are written down. Under the charter agreed in December 2023 with the government, BT, Sky, Virgin Media O2, TalkTalk and others committed not to migrate a telecare user until a compatible solution is in place, and to provide battery back-up that goes beyond Ofcom's one hour minimum. Openreach and the other network builders signed a second charter in March 2024. The government's own guidance page, "UK transition from analogue to digital landlines", carries the list.

The commitment only works if your provider knows. Providers cannot see what is plugged into your line. The action is one phone call or one form: tell your provider that the line carries a telecare alarm, and tell the alarm provider, usually your council or a housing association, that your landline is due to switch. Ask the alarm provider directly whether the unit is digital-ready and, if not, what they will replace it with and when. The same applies to a lift emergency phone in a block, a burglar alarm that dials out, and some home health monitors.

The same call is worth making if someone in the house should be registered as vulnerable with the provider: age, disability, a health condition. That registration triggers the free battery unit and the extra checks.

4. Household three: a landline and no broadband

About the clearest misunderstanding in this whole story is that you must buy broadband to keep a phone. You do not. The line still needs a data connection to carry the call, but providers sell a voice-only digital service that uses the connection for calls and nothing else, and the price is set as a phone line, not a broadband package. BT's version has been available for several years, and other providers have their own. Ask for a digital voice-only or landline-only service by name, and check the current monthly price before agreeing, because it is not always the same as the old line rental.

If the household has no mobile signal either, say so. Ofcom's guidance treats a customer with no other way to reach 999 as one who needs the battery back-up unit, and it must be free.

A household that only keeps the line for a telecare alarm has two options: a voice-only digital line, or an alarm unit with a mobile SIM that needs no landline. Councils increasingly supply the second kind.

5. A worked example in Luton

Doris in Luton is 83, has a pendant alarm through the council's careline service, a corded phone in the hall and an extension in the bedroom, and no broadband. Her provider has written to say the line will switch in November.

What she needs, in order: first, she or her son calls the provider and says two things, that the line carries a council telecare alarm and that she has no mobile. That should stop any migration until the alarm is sorted, and it flags her for the free battery unit. Second, they call the careline number on the alarm box and ask whether the unit is digital-compatible. If it is not, the council replaces it, and Doris should ask for a date in writing. Third, they ask the provider for a voice-only digital service and the monthly price. Fourth, the bedroom extension: a cordless handset paired to the base by the router replaces it, which is the one item she may need to buy.

Total cost to Doris if the provider and council do what they have committed to: a cordless phone, and possibly a change in monthly line rental. Nothing else should be charged.

6. The honest verdict

If you have broadband and a phone and nothing plugged into the line, do nothing until your provider writes, then plug the phone into the router. That is the whole job for most homes.

If there is a telecare alarm, a lift phone or a dial-out device on the line, make the call this month, not when the letter arrives. The commitments protect you only once the provider knows.

If you have no broadband, ask for a voice-only digital line, and do not let anyone sell you a broadband package to keep a phone. The switch itself should cost nothing.

Frequently asked

Will my phone number change? No. Numbers move across with the line. If a provider suggests otherwise, ask why.

Do I have to buy broadband? No. Providers offer a digital voice-only service for calls alone. Ask for it by that name and check the current price.

What happens in a power cut? The router loses power, so the landline stops. Ofcom requires providers to give customers who depend on the landline at least one hour of access to 999, free of charge, usually a battery unit. If you have a mobile, that may be treated as your back-up unless you say you need more.

Is the deadline going to move again? Openreach said on 31 July 2026 that there is no extension. It moved once before, from December 2025, after telecare failures. Plan on 31 January 2027.
