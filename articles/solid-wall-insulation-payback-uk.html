Solid Wall Insulation Costs £12,000 To £15,000 And Saves £140 To £500 A Year. Nobody Should Buy It To Save Money. Here Is Why Some People Should Still Buy It.

Around a quarter of UK homes were built before 1920 with solid walls and no cavity to fill, and they lose about a third of their heat through those walls. The fix is real, the saving is real, and the arithmetic is the worst on this site: 24 years to pay back in the best case and more than a hundred in the worst. This is the article that says do not do it for the bills, and then explains the three situations in which the bills were never the point.

1. The figures: £12,000, £15,000, and £140 to £500 a year

The Energy Saving Trust's solid wall insulation page, updated 18 June 2026 and read again on 5 September 2026, gives two typical installation costs for a three-bedroom semi-detached house in Great Britain: internal wall insulation around £12,000 and external wall insulation around £15,000. Those prices assume a company does the whole house in one go and, for internal insulation, fully redecorates afterwards. They are the honest full price, not the price of a room.

The savings come from an embedded table at July 2026 fuel prices for a gas-heated home: £500 a year for a detached house, £280 for a semi, £150 for a mid-terrace, £220 for a detached bungalow and £140 for a mid-floor flat. Those are the same order of magnitude as cavity wall insulation, because the heat lost through a wall does not care whether the wall has a cavity. The cost is five to seven times higher.

Put the two together and the payback runs from 24 years, a detached house insulated internally, to 107 years, a flat insulated externally. Even the best case is longer than the 25-year guarantee. The Energy Saving Trust, to its credit, does not claim a payback anywhere on the page. It lists the benefits as reduced heat loss, lower bills, less condensation and fewer draughts, and leaves the reader to notice that the second of those will never cover the first.

The saving is for a gas-heated home. All-electric homes save roughly three times as much per unit of heat kept, because electricity costs 26.32p a kWh against 7.97p for gas under the October 2026 cap, so a solid-walled house on storage heaters or panel heaters is the one case where the payback drops towards 10 to 15 years. Those houses exist, mostly off the gas grid, and their owners should do the sum with their own bills.

3. Why the cavity comparison is the one that matters

The right column of the table is the most important thing on this page. A house built after about 1920 usually has cavity walls, and filling them costs £950 to £3,900 for the same saving. A house built before 1920 usually has solid walls and costs £12,000 to £15,000 for the same saving. The heat loss is identical; the wall construction sets the price.

So check which you have. Bricks all laid lengthways in an even pattern is a cavity wall; an alternating pattern with brick ends showing is solid. A wall 260 to 350 mm thick at a door reveal is probably a cavity; under 260 mm, or stone at any thickness, is probably solid. A cavity under 50 mm counts as solid.

If it turns out to be a cavity, read the cavity wall article and close this one. If it is solid, the sum above is yours, and the question becomes whether one of the three situations below applies.

4. The three situations where the bill sum is not the point

A grant pays most of it. Solid wall insulation is exactly the kind of high-cost, high-carbon-saving measure that ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme exist to fund for eligible households, and the Warm Homes Plan promises fully funded insulation for low-income homes and low or zero-interest loans for others. If the £12,000 becomes £2,000 or an interest-free loan, the payback is eight years or the loan repayment is smaller than the saving plus the comfort. The sum flips. Check eligibility before you assume you are not.

The work is happening anyway. The Energy Saving Trust's costs assume the insulation is the whole job. If the outside of the house is being re-rendered, the roof replaced or scaffolding is up for new windows, the marginal cost of adding external insulation is a fraction of £15,000. If a room is being stripped back to brick for a renovation, the marginal cost of insulating that wall internally is a few hundred pounds in board and plaster. Insulate the wall you have already paid to expose. Do not open a wall to insulate it.

The house is cold, damp or unlettable. A solid-walled house at EPC F or G cannot legally be let in England and Wales below the minimum standard without an exemption, and a house with condensation running down the inside of the front wall every winter is not a house the saving figure describes. Solid wall insulation raises the inside surface temperature of the wall, which is what stops condensation forming on it, and it is one of the few measures that moves an EPC by whole bands. For a landlord facing an exemption or a seller facing a survey, the £12,000 is a cost of keeping the asset, not an investment in the bills.

Outside those three, spend the money elsewhere: draught proofing, a loft top-up and heating controls together save more than £280 a year for under £1,000 in most solid-walled houses.

5. Damp, guarantees and the Find and Fix audit

Solid walls in older houses manage moisture by breathing: brick, stone, lime and timber absorb and release it. Insulation changes that. Internal insulation makes the wall colder on the outside so moisture can build up inside it; external insulation can trap moisture if the materials are wrong or the wall was damp to begin with. The Energy Saving Trust is explicit that the installer must use vapour-permeable materials, fix every existing damp problem first, repair pointing, roofs and gutters before starting, and leave airbricks, wall vents and trickle vents alone. If a quote does not mention any of that, get another.

The reason to be careful is on the same page. In October 2025 the National Audit Office reported on the quality of wall insulation fitted under government schemes, and the Energy Saving Trust now says that 98 per cent of homes fitted with external wall insulation under ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme have problems that need fixing. The government's response is TrustMark's free Find and Fix audit: affected homes were sent a letter from Ofgem in early 2026 with a Unique Measure Reference Number, TrustMark inspects, and any problems are fixed at no cost. If you had external wall insulation under either scheme and have not heard, contact TrustMark; if someone offers to audit your home and cannot quote the reference number, it is a scam.

For a private job, the protection is the guarantee: any TrustMark solid wall installer will offer 25 years, insurance-backed or through SWIGA. Get it in writing, get three quotes, and get the ventilation and moisture plan in the quote. Listed buildings and conservation areas need permission for external work.

6. Worked example: Derek and Joan in Bath

Three-bed Edwardian mid-terrace, solid brick walls rendered at the back, gas central heating, EPC E, front bedroom wall damp every February

Derek and Joan have had two quotes for internal wall insulation on the three external walls: £11,500 and £13,200, both with 25-year guarantees, both requiring them to clear and redecorate two bedrooms and the front room. The Energy Saving Trust saving for a mid-terrace is £150 a year.

The bill sum. £11,500 divided by £150 is 77 years. On bills alone, no.

The cavity check. The front wall shows headers every fourth course, the reveal measures 230 mm: solid. No cheap option.

The grant check. Their income is above the ECO4 and GBIS thresholds. The Warm Homes Plan loan is not yet open in their area. No help.

The work-anyway check. They are planning to strip and replaster the front bedroom next spring because of the damp. Their builder prices insulated plasterboard on that one wall, with a breathable lime plaster finish and a vapour control layer, at £1,400 on top of the replastering they were doing anyway. That wall is roughly a fifth of the external wall area, so the saving it produces is perhaps £30 a year: a 47 year payback still, but the damp stops, the room becomes usable in winter, and the redecoration was already paid for.

What they do. The one wall, with the renovation, for £1,400. Not the whole house for £11,500. They also spend £90 on draught strip, a chimney balloon and a loft hatch seal, which on Energy Saving Trust figures saves more than the wall does.

7. The honest verdict

Do not buy solid wall insulation to save money on bills. At £12,000 to £15,000 for £140 to £500 a year it pays back in 24 years at the very best and over a century at worst, and the Energy Saving Trust does not pretend otherwise. First confirm the wall is actually solid, because a cavity costs a tenth as much to insulate for the same saving.

Buy it in three cases: a grant or zero-interest loan removes most of the cost; the wall is being opened or the outside re-rendered anyway, so the marginal cost is small; or the house is damp, cold or below the EPC minimum and the insulation is the fix, not an investment. In every case insist on breathable materials, repairs first, and a 25-year SWIGA or insurance-backed guarantee from a TrustMark installer.

Frequently asked

How much does solid wall insulation cost in the UK? The Energy Saving Trust, page updated 18 June 2026, puts internal wall insulation at around £12,000 and external wall insulation at around £15,000 for a typical three-bedroom semi in Great Britain, assuming the whole house is done in one go by a company and, for internal insulation, fully redecorated. Doing it alongside other building work can cut the cost substantially.

How much does solid wall insulation save per year? At July 2026 fuel prices for a gas-heated home the Energy Saving Trust puts the saving at £500 a year for a detached house, £280 for a semi, £220 for a detached bungalow, £150 for a mid-terrace and £140 for a mid-floor flat. All-electric homes save roughly three times as much.

Is external or internal wall insulation better? External costs more, about £15,000 against £12,000, but renews the outside of the house, improves weatherproofing, keeps the room sizes and does not require you to move out. Internal is cheaper and keeps the appearance of the house but takes 50 to 100 mm off each room, needs skirting, sockets and radiators refitted, and can make the wall colder on the outside so moisture management matters more. Neither pays back on bills.

Can I get a grant for solid wall insulation? Possibly. ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme fund insulation for eligible lower-income households and poorly rated homes, and the Warm Homes Plan promises fully funded insulation for low-income households plus low or zero-interest loans for other homeowners. Check eligibility before paying full price. If you had external wall insulation under ECO4 or GBIS, TrustMark's free Find and Fix audit will inspect it and fix defects at no cost.
