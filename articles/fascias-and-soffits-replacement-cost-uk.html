You Are Not Paying for Plastic. You Are Paying to Get Up There

A full fascia, soffit and guttering replacement on a semi runs 1,200 to 3,500 pounds. Most of the argument about materials misses the point, because the scaffolding costs the same whether you paint the boards or replace them, and it is the trips up the ladder that drive the twenty year bill.

1. The access cost, and why it decides everything

Fascias are the boards running along the bottom edge of the roof that the gutter is screwed to. Soffits are the horizontal panels closing the gap underneath. Neither is glamorous and neither gets thought about until water is running down the wall.

Here is the thing every quote hides inside a single line. On a two storey house, nobody is doing this work off a ladder properly. You need a scaffold, or at minimum an edge protected tower moved round the building. On a three bedroom semi that is 450 to 900 pounds, and on a detached house with a complex roofline it is 900 to 1,600.

That cost does not care what you do once you are up there. Painting a metre of timber fascia and replacing a metre of timber fascia both require the same platform for the same number of days. So the material argument, which is where everybody starts, is the smaller half of the decision. The bigger half is how many times you intend to pay for the scaffold over the next twenty five years.

Paint the boards and you are buying about five years. On a south or west facing elevation with driving rain, sometimes four. Replace them in uPVC and you are buying twenty five, with a wash down every couple of years from the ground.

2. Capping, and why the cheap quote is the expensive one

You will get a quote, often the cheapest by a distance, that offers to fit new uPVC over the existing timber rather than removing it. In the trade this is capping or overboarding. It looks identical from the pavement on the day it is finished.

If the timber underneath is completely sound and dry, capping is defensible and saves real money on labour and skip hire. That is a genuine option and a decent installer will tell you so.

If the timber is not sound, capping is the worst thing you can do to a house. Wet wood is now sealed behind plastic with no path for moisture to leave. It carries on rotting quietly, it takes the rafter feet with it, and the first sign is a soffit sagging or a gutter pulling away four or five years later. At that point the repair is not a fascia job. It is a roofline and rafter end job with a roofer involved, and it is easily three times what the honest full replacement would have cost.

So the question to ask is not what the quote is. It is: are you removing the existing timber, and if not, why do you think it is dry. A firm that has actually got up and looked will answer that in one sentence. A firm quoting from the driveway will not.

Full replacement costs two and a half times a repaint on the day and works out at roughly half the cost per year. The break even against repainting arrives at about year nine: you have paid 1,500 pounds more up front, and by year ten you have avoided two repaints worth 1,800.

Capping over genuinely dry timber is the cheapest row in the table. Capping over damp timber is the most expensive. They are the same job and the same quote. The only thing separating them is whether somebody got up there and checked.

4. What a proper quote has in it

Four things, and if any is missing, ask before you compare prices.

The access method and who pays for it. Scaffold, tower or cherry picker, how many days, and whether it is in the price or a provisional sum. A scaffold should come with a handover certificate.

Whether the existing boards are being removed. Stated in writing, not implied. Also whether the rafter feet will be inspected once the boards are off, and what happens to the price if one is rotten. The answer should be a named day rate, not silence.

Ventilation. A roof void needs airflow at the eaves. If the old arrangement was a gappy timber soffit, the gaps were the ventilation, and sealing them behind a solid uPVC panel with no vents is how condensation in the loft starts. The quote should say either continuous eaves vent or a stated number of circular soffit vents.

The guttering. Doing the fascias and leaving twenty year old gutters up is a false economy, because the gutter has to come off anyway to fit the board behind it. Replacing it while it is down costs a fraction of doing it as its own job later, and that is the same scaffold logic as everything else on this page.

5. Worked example: the Doyle house in Kendal

Bernadette Doyle owns a 1930s semi in Kendal. The timber fascias were last painted in 2021 and the paint is already flaking on the gable end, which faces the weather. A decorator quotes 880 pounds including tower hire to prep and repaint.

Two roofline firms quote as well. One offers capping at 1,320 pounds. One offers full strip and replacement, with new gutters, at 2,460 pounds.

The decorator, to his credit, is the one who tells her the gable fascia is soft at the joint. That kills the capping quote on the spot, because the point of capping is that the timber underneath is sound, and hers is not on the elevation that matters.

So her real choice is 880 pounds now, repeated roughly every five years in Cumbrian weather and probably every four on the gable, against 2,460 pounds once. Take the five year interval as generous. Over twenty years repainting is 3,520 pounds, or 176 a year. Replacement is 2,460 pounds, or 98 a year over twenty five.

She replaces. The break even is at about year eight and she has been in the house eleven years with no intention of going anywhere. The number that decided it was not the price of uPVC. It was that she would otherwise pay for tower hire four more times.

One detail worth copying. She asked for the rafter feet to be photographed once the boards came off, and she got the photographs. Two feet needed splicing at 90 pounds each. On a capping quote she would never have known.

Frequently asked

Is uPVC actually maintenance free? No, it is maintenance light. It wants washing every couple of years or it goes green on the shaded elevation, and cheap white board yellows under UV. Better systems use a foamed core with a capped surface and hold colour far longer. Ask for the system name, not just the colour.

Can I do it myself? The boards are not difficult. The height is. Working at eaves level on a two storey house without proper edge protection is how people end up in hospital, and a domestic fall is not covered by most home insurance policies. If it is a single storey extension roofline, it is a reasonable weekend job. Above that, pay someone.

Does it need planning permission or building control? Like for like replacement of fascias, soffits and gutters is repair and maintenance, so neither applies on an ordinary house. On a listed building or in some conservation areas, changing timber to plastic can need consent, and that is worth a phone call before the skip arrives.

Will new fascias fix my damp wall? Only if the wall is wet because a gutter or downpipe was failing, which is common enough to be worth checking. Water running down a wall from a broken joint produces a patch that gets worse after rain. That is penetrating damp and the roofline job is the right fix. A tide mark rising evenly from the floor is a different problem entirely.

The honest verdict

If the timber is sound and you are selling within five years, repaint for about 900 pounds and move on. If you are staying, replace, because the break even lands around year nine and the cost per year roughly halves.

Refuse capping unless the installer has been up and can tell you the boards are dry. Capping over wet timber is the cheapest quote on the day and the most expensive line in the table over twenty five years.
