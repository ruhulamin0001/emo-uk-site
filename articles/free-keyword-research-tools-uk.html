Google's Free Keyword Tool Wants Your Card Details. Then It Rounds Everything

Keyword Planner is the tool every beginner guide recommends, and it is genuinely useful. It is also the source of four persistent misunderstandings that send people after the wrong pages for months. Google documents all four openly, which is the frustrating part.

Ruhul Amin Published 11 September 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Keyword Planner is not open to everyone. Google's own help page states you must complete account setup by entering your billing information before you can access it. Free to use, not free to reach.
  • The Competition column is about advertisers, not about SEO. Google defines it as the number of advertisers showing on that keyword relative to all keywords. A term marked Low can still be impossible to rank for organically.
  • Average monthly searches is a 12 month average by default. A seasonal term that does 60,000 searches in December and nothing in July reads as 5,000 all year round.
  • The figures are rounded, and Google says so. Its help page warns that volumes across multiple locations may not add up as you would expect.
  • Volume is shown for exact match and close variants only. Broad, phrase and exact all display the same historical figure.
  • Top of page bid ranges are the 20th and 80th percentiles from the last 30 days. A commercial intent signal, not a forecast.

1. It is free, but it is not open

Start with the thing the tutorials skip. Google's help page opens with a requirement, not a welcome: to access Keyword Planner you must complete your account setup by entering your billing information.

You do not have to run a campaign, and you will not be charged for opening the tool. But you do have to hand Google a payment method. For a lot of people starting a first site with no budget, that is a meaningful barrier, and it is worth knowing before you have spent an evening trying to work out what you did wrong.

There is a second, softer gate. Accounts with no active spend see volume in wide banded ranges rather than tighter estimates. The data underneath is the same. The resolution is not.

If that trade is not one you want to make, the genuinely open alternatives are Google Trends, Google Search Console once your site has any impressions, and Bing Webmaster Tools, which does keyword research without asking for a card. None replaces Keyword Planner. Together they cover most of what a beginner needs.

2. The Competition column is the most misread number in SEO

This is the one that does real damage. Keyword Planner shows a column called Competition, with values of Low, Medium and High, almost universally read as "how hard will this be to rank for".

It does not mean that. Google's definition is precise: Competition is the number of advertisers that showed on each keyword relative to all keywords across Google, specific to the location and Search Network targeting you selected.

It counts advertisers. It measures how many people want to buy ads against a term. It says nothing about how strong the ten organic results are, how many links they have, or whether the first page is occupied by retailers you cannot displace.

Competition: Low means few advertisers are bidding. It does not mean the first page is weak. Those are different questions with different answers.

The practical failure looks like this. A term is marked Low competition, so a new site writes a page for it, and the page never ranks, because the organic results are all national newspapers and the advertisers stayed away precisely because there is no money in it. Low competition and low commercial value often travel together. Use the column as a read on whether anyone will pay for this traffic. For organic difficulty, open the search results and look, which is free and takes ninety seconds.

3. Three things the volume figure is not

It is not this month. By default, Google averages searches over a 12 month period. Seasonality vanishes into that average. A term that is enormous for six weeks before Christmas and dead the rest of the year shows the same flat monthly number as one that is genuinely steady. Change the date range, or check the shape in Google Trends, before building a content calendar on it.

It is not precise. Google warns that search volume statistics are rounded, which is why volumes for multiple locations may not sum as you expect. Treat the figure as an order of magnitude. The difference between 1,000 and 1,300 is noise. The difference between 100 and 10,000 is signal.

It is not your traffic. The historical figure covers exact matches and close variants only, and Google notes you see the same statistic whether you look at broad, phrase or exact. It understates the family of related phrases a good page captures, while overstating what one page gets from that phrase, since most of those searches never reach position one.

The bid range columns are cleaner than they look. Top of page bid low range approximates the 20th percentile of what advertisers historically paid, high range roughly the 80th, both from the last 30 days. As a proxy for whether there is money behind a search, it is the most honest column on the screen.

4. Worked example: Priya in Leicester picks the wrong winner

Priya is building a small site about home coffee equipment from her flat in Leicester. Keyword Planner offers her two candidates.

Term A. 12,000 average monthly searches. Competition: Low. Top of page bid range 12p to 41p.

Term B. 880 average monthly searches. Competition: High. Top of page bid range £1.10 to £3.40.

Every instinct says write Term A. Fourteen times the volume and, apparently, less competition. Read the columns properly and it inverts. The bid range on Term A says advertisers will pay about 25p for that click, because they have found it does not lead to a sale, and the Low competition label is a consequence of that rather than an opportunity. Term B has advertisers paying north of a pound, repeatedly, which nobody does out of sentiment.

Now apply the arithmetic. At 5% on a £90 basket each sale pays Priya £4.50. Suppose Term B converts at 3% and Term A at 0.2%, the usual gap between a buying phrase and a browsing one.

Term A, ranking well enough to take 15% of 12,000 searches: 1,800 clicks, 0.2% convert = 3.6 sales = £16.20 a month.

Term B, same 15% of 880 searches: 132 clicks, 3% convert = 3.96 sales = £17.82 a month.

Near enough identical money from a term with one fourteenth of the traffic, and a far better chance of holding a page about a specific buying decision than one competing with every publisher in Britain.

The honest verdict

Keyword Planner is worth using and its data is real. The problem is not accuracy. It is that its columns are labelled in the language of advertising and read in the language of SEO.

Read it like this. Volume is an order of magnitude, averaged over a year, for exact match only. Competition is an advertiser count and tells you nothing about ranking. The bid range is the best commercial signal on the page. And the real difficulty of ranking is only visible by opening the search results and looking.

If handing over card details is a blocker, start with Google Trends and Bing Webmaster Tools instead. Given how much of Keyword Planner's precision is rounded away anyway, you lose less than you think.

Frequently asked

Are the paid tools worth it for a beginner? Not in month one. Their advantage is backlink data and crawled difficulty scores, which matter once you are choosing between pages that could both work. Before that you are paying monthly to be told things the first page of results shows you free.

Does Keyword Planner show UK specific data? Yes, if you set the location. Check it every session, because a default of all countries produces figures useless for a UK site and is often why a term looks far bigger than it is.

Why do two tools give different volumes for the same term? Only Google has the search logs. Every other tool models the number from clickstream samples and extrapolation. Disagreement is normal and is not evidence that one is broken.
