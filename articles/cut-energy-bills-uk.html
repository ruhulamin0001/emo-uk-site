Cutting Your Energy Bills in the UK: What Saves Real Money

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

There is endless advice on saving energy, and a lot of it saves pennies while ignoring the pounds. This sorts the changes that genuinely lower a UK bill from the ones that just make you feel busy. It also points to free help many households are owed and never claim.

Where the money actually goes

In most UK homes, heating is by far the biggest cost, followed by hot water. Everything else, lights, gadgets, the kettle, is small by comparison. So the biggest savings come from heating less wastefully, not from unplugging your phone charger.

The changes that save real money

Heating controls used well. Turning the thermostat down by even one degree noticeably cuts a heating bill over a year, and most people do not feel the difference. Heating rooms you use, when you use them, rather than the whole house all day.

Draughts and insulation. Cheap draught proofing on doors and windows, and proper loft insulation, keep the heat you have already paid for inside. This is dull and it works.

Being on the right tariff. Many households drift onto a provider's expensive default rate. Checking whether a better tariff is available can save more in one afternoon than months of small habits.

The changes that barely help

Obsessively unplugging small devices. Standby power is real but tiny. Worth doing, not worth stressing over.

Expensive gadgets that promise to slash your bill. Most save little and take years to pay back, if ever. Be sceptical of anything sold on a dramatic saving.

The free help people miss

This is the part worth reading twice. Depending on your income and circumstances, you may be owed help you are not claiming: support schemes, free or discounted insulation, and payments towards bills for eligible households. Every year money set aside for this goes unclaimed simply because people do not check.

If you are struggling, contact your energy supplier directly. They have a duty to help and can offer payment plans and support you will not find unless you ask.

A sensible order to act

  1. Check whether you qualify for any grants, discounts or support first. It is free money if you do.
  2. Make sure you are on a fair tariff, not an expensive default rate.
  3. Deal with draughts and insulation to stop paying to heat the outside.
  4. Use heating controls well: lower thermostat, heat the rooms and times you actually use.

The honest bottom line

Real energy savings come from heating smartly, sealing your home, and being on the right tariff, not from fretting over standby lights. And before anything else, check what free help you may be owed. Pair this with the everyday cuts in our DIY home savings guide.
