The Scheme That Paid the Whole Insulation Bill Shuts in December, and This Time It Is Not Coming Back

ECO4 was given a nine month reprieve and now ends on 31 December 2026. The government has confirmed there will be no successor obligation on energy suppliers at all. If you have already had external wall insulation fitted under it, there is something else on that page you should read.

The short version

  • ECO4 now ends on 31 December 2026. It was due to end on 31 March 2026 and DESNZ extended it by nine months, confirmed in the government response published 23 January 2026.
  • There is no successor supplier obligation. That was announced at the November 2025 Budget and restated in the same document. ECO, as a levy on energy suppliers, stops.
  • The Great British Insulation Scheme already closed on 31 March 2026, as planned, and was not extended.
  • What is left is grant funded, not levy funded: £1.5 billion of additional grant money for low income households, taking the Warm Homes Plan to just under £15 billion, plus the Warm Homes: Local Grant and the Boiler Upgrade Scheme.
  • The extension is not extra money. DESNZ explicitly refused to raise supplier targets, so the same obligation is spread over a longer period. Expect fewer installs, not more.
  • If you had external wall insulation under ECO4 or GBIS, you are being offered a free on-site audit, and the original installer is expected to put right anything found.

1. What was decided, and when

The Energy Company Obligation has run since 2013. It places a duty on energy suppliers to install measures that cut household energy use, with the cost recovered through bills. Across all versions, around 4.4 million measures have gone into 2.6 million properties to the end of September 2025. Under ECO4 alone, around 949,800 measures in about 280,100 households.

ECO4 started in July 2022 and was due to finish on 31 March 2026. DESNZ consulted in August 2025 on extending it, received 215 responses, and published its response on 23 January 2026. The decision: extend by nine months to 31 December 2026.

Read the reasoning and the mood changes. The extension exists so suppliers have more time to meet targets they already had, and so the supply chain can fix non compliant installations, an issue raised by the National Audit Office and at a Public Accounts Committee hearing in November 2025. DESNZ was asked repeatedly to raise the obligation to match the longer window and refused, saying the extension is solely to allow extra time, not to expand the scheme. So this is not nine months of new work. It is the same work, later.

An extension with no increase in target is a slower scheme, not a bigger one. If you are waiting for a call, that is the sentence to plan around.

2. What is actually left after December

The Great British Insulation Scheme ended as planned on 31 March 2026. DESNZ was blunt about why: delivery had not met expectations and the scheme was difficult to run in its current form.

After ECO4 closes there is no replacement supplier obligation. Instead the money comes from grants. The government committed £1.5 billion in additional grant funding aimed at upgrading low income households in fuel poverty, bringing the Warm Homes Plan to just under £15 billion of capital investment.

For a household today, the routes DESNZ itself points to are these.

RouteStatus
ECO4Open until 31 December 2026, then closed permanently
Great British Insulation SchemeClosed, ended 31 March 2026
Warm Homes: Local GrantRunning, delivered through councils, funding and criteria vary locally
Boiler Upgrade SchemeRunning, for heat pumps and biomass boilers

The important structural change is who you talk to. Under ECO you approached an energy supplier or an installer working for one. Under the Warm Homes: Local Grant you go through your council, and what is available genuinely differs by area.

3. The free audit almost nobody has been told about

This is buried in the same document and is the most valuable thing in it for people who already had work done.

Serious quality problems were identified with solid wall insulation under both ECO4 and GBIS. DESNZ's response says the department is offering on-site audits to every household with external wall insulation installed under ECO4 and GBIS, and expects the original installer to remediate any issues found.

Alongside that, suppliers have been asked to run enhanced desk based and on-site checks, TrustMark has tightened its requirements including a suspension policy and a new risk radar tool, and an update to PAS 2035 and PAS 2030 came into force on 30 March 2025 requiring Retrofit Coordinators to make site visits.

If you had external wall insulation fitted under either scheme and you have damp, cracking, cold spots or render failure, this is the route. Not a private survey you pay for, and not a claims company. Start with the installer and the supplier that funded the work, and reference the audit offer.

Worked example: the Dunlop household in Dumfries

A 1930s semi with solid walls, on a means tested benefit, EPC band E. They were told in 2025 they were on a list for external wall insulation under ECO4 and have heard nothing since.

Where they now stand: the window closes on 31 December 2026, and the extension gave suppliers more time without a bigger target. A quiet list in September is not a promise of a January install, because there will be no January.

What is worth doing this month: ring the supplier that logged them, ask for a written position, and in parallel ask their council about the Warm Homes: Local Grant, which continues after ECO4 has gone. Two routes, one deadline.

What they should not do: pay a lead generation firm to get them onto a list. ECO4 measures are fully funded when you qualify, so a fee to reach a free scheme is money gone.

The second thing to check: their neighbour had external wall insulation under GBIS in 2024 and has had damp in the front bedroom since. That neighbour is inside the audit offer above and does not know it.

4. What to do before the end of December

If you think you might qualify, ask now rather than in November. Eligibility runs on a combination of household circumstances and how poor your home's energy rating is, and the current criteria are on the Ofgem and GOV.UK ECO pages. Your energy supplier is the first call.

Ask your council about the Warm Homes: Local Grant in the same week. It is not affected by the ECO4 end date and it is the route that survives into 2027.

Never pay a fee to be put forward, and get everything in writing. Fully funded means fully funded, and with a hard end date and no successor a verbal promise made in October is worth very little in January.

If you are not eligible, spend small. The cheap measures still return more per pound than most people expect, and our guides on loft insulation, cavity wall insulation and draught proofing set out the real payback periods rather than the sales ones.

5. The honest verdict

For a household that qualifies and has not been booked in, this is a real deadline with a real consequence. Fully funded insulation through a supplier obligation has existed in some form since 2013 and stops on 31 December 2026. There is money after that, but it is grant money routed through councils and it will not look the same.

For a household that already had work done, the most useful line in the whole response is the one offering on-site audits to every home with external wall insulation under ECO4 or GBIS, with the installer expected to fix what is found. That is a live entitlement with almost no coverage. And for anyone waiting quietly on a list: an extension without a bigger target is not good news. Chase it.

Frequently asked

Will another scheme replace ECO4? Not as a supplier obligation. The government confirmed at the November 2025 Budget that there will be no successor obligation, and committed grant funding instead through the Warm Homes Plan.

Can I still apply for the Great British Insulation Scheme? No. It closed on 31 March 2026 and was not extended.

My insulation was done badly. Who pays to fix it? The original installer is expected to remediate. DESNZ is offering on-site audits to every household with external wall insulation installed under ECO4 and GBIS, and the extension exists partly to give the supply chain time to do that work.

Does ECO4 cover Scotland and Wales? ECO operates across Great Britain. Northern Ireland has separate arrangements. The Warm Homes: Local Grant is an England scheme, so check what your own nation runs.
