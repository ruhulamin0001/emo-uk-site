A Slow Cooker Costs 5p an Hour. Your Oven Costs Up to £1.30

The energy gap between a slow cooker and an oven is not marginal, it is a factor of ten or more. Once you put real numbers on it, the case for batch cooking stops being about virtue and becomes straightforward arithmetic.

The short version

  • A slow cooker costs about 3p to 8p an hour to run, typically 5p to 6p. Most draw only 200W to 250W.
  • An electric oven costs 50p to £1.30 an hour. Eight hours of oven use can exceed £5. Eight hours of slow cooker use is under 50p.
  • A full meal in a slow cooker costs roughly 18p to 36p in electricity, around 29p on average at current rates.
  • Batch cooking saves an average UK household around £100 a year, and combining it with efficient oven use has been put as high as £158.
  • The food savings are larger than the energy savings. Slow cooking makes cheap cuts good, and WRAP puts UK household food waste at about £1,000 a year for a family of four.

1. What it actually costs to run

A typical slow cooker draws 200W to 250W, roughly the same as a couple of old fashioned light bulbs. At an electricity rate around 22p to 24.67p per kWh, that works out at 3.2p to 8.1p an hour, with most models sitting at 5p to 6p.

Cooking timeElectricity cost, at about 5.5p an hour
4 hours on highabout 22p
6 hoursabout 33p
8 hours on lowabout 44p
10 hoursabout 55p

The counterintuitive part is that a longer cook is not much more expensive, because the wattage is so low. Leaving it on for ten hours while you are at work costs about 55p. That is the whole appeal.

2. The comparison with an oven

ApplianceCost per hourCost for a 4 hour cook
Slow cooker3p to 8p12p to 32p
Electric oven50p to £1.30£2.00 to £5.20
Electric hob or grillRoughly 50p and up£2.00 and up

An oven run for eight hours can cost more than £5. A slow cooker for the same eight hours costs about 44p. That is roughly a tenfold difference, and it is why this is one of the few appliance purchases that pays for itself quickly.

The payback. A basic slow cooker costs £20 to £35. If it replaces two oven meals a week, at a conservative £1.80 saved each, that is about £187 a year and the appliance pays for itself in about six to ten weeks.

Where the oven still wins: anything that needs browning, crisping or roasting. A slow cooker cannot produce a roast potato. Use both, and use the oven for several things at once when it is on.

A slow cooker left on all day costs about the same as boiling the kettle three times. That single fact is what people get wrong about it.

3. What one meal costs, end to end

A beef stew that feeds four with leftovers.

Ingredients: about £6. Braising steak, onions, carrots, potatoes, stock, tinned tomatoes.

Electricity: eight hours on low, about 44p to 48p.

Total: about £6.45 for four portions plus leftovers, so under £1.70 a head including the electricity.

The same dish in an oven at three hours would cost £1.50 to £3.90 in electricity, so around £8 to £10 total, and the cheap cut would be tougher for it.

Published figures put a slow cooker meal at 18p to 36p in electricity, averaging about 29p. For a household eating four slow cooked meals a week, that is roughly £60 a year in electricity for 200 meals.

4. Where batch cooking saves the most

Batch cooking saves an average UK household around £100 a year, and cooking several things at once when the oven is on has been put as high as £158 a year.

The savings come from four separate places, and people usually only count the first:

  • Energy per portion. Cooking eight portions costs the same electricity as cooking four.
  • Bulk ingredient prices. A 1kg pack of mince is cheaper per kilo than two 500g packs, and cooking in volume is what lets you buy that way.
  • Food waste avoided. The largest one. WRAP puts UK household food waste at about £1,000 a year for a family of four, and cooking to a plan rather than to a mood is what stops vegetables rotting in the drawer.
  • Takeaways not ordered. The real competitor to a slow cooker meal is not an oven meal, it is a £28 delivery on a Wednesday because nothing was ready. A portion in the freezer removes that decision.

The practical version: cook double, freeze half, label with the date. Two cooking sessions a week can cover most evenings.

5. Cheap cuts, which is the real saving

Long, low temperature cooking breaks down connective tissue, which is exactly what makes cheap cuts tough. That means a slow cooker turns the cheapest meat in the shop into the best thing it can be, and this saves more than the electricity does.

Adding a tin of lentils to a mince dish is the single cheapest way to turn four portions into six, and in a slow cooked sauce most people do not notice.

6. A worked month for a family of four

The Hendersons, Sunderland. Two adults, two children. Previously three oven meals and one takeaway a week.

Before. Three oven meals a week at roughly £2.40 of electricity each, so £7.20. One takeaway at £29. Weekly cost of those four evenings: £36.20 plus ingredients.

After. Two slow cooker sessions a week, each cooking double. Electricity 44p a session, so 88p. Four evenings covered from those two sessions, because half goes in the freezer. One oven meal retained at £2.40. No takeaway, because there is always something ready.

Weekly energy and takeaway cost: £3.28. Saving £32.92 a week, though most of that is the takeaway rather than the electricity, and that is the honest breakdown.

Ingredients. Moving to braising steak and chicken thighs cut the meat bill by roughly £6 a week.

Realistic monthly saving: about £120, of which around £16 is electricity, £26 is cheaper cuts, and the rest is the takeaway that no longer happens.

The honest note: if you never ordered takeaways, your saving is the energy and the cheaper cuts, which is closer to £40 a month. Still worth doing, and a third of the headline.

7. Food safety, done properly

  • Do not put frozen meat straight in. It spends too long in the temperature range where bacteria multiply. Defrost in the fridge first.
  • Dried red kidney beans must be boiled hard for 10 minutes before slow cooking. A slow cooker alone does not reach a high enough temperature to destroy the toxin. Tinned beans are already treated and are fine.
  • Fill between a half and three quarters. Too empty burns, too full does not reach temperature.
  • Cool leftovers within two hours and refrigerate. Divide into shallow containers to cool faster.
  • Reheat until piping hot throughout, once only.
  • Freeze in portions and label with the date. Most cooked dishes keep three months.

The honest verdict

The energy case is real and it is not close: 5p an hour against 50p to £1.30. A £25 appliance that replaces two oven meals a week pays for itself in a couple of months and then saves you money indefinitely.

But be clear about where the saving comes from. The electricity is perhaps £100 to £160 a year. The bigger money is in cheap cuts becoming worth eating, food not being wasted, and the takeaway you do not order because something is already in the freezer.

If you buy one, buy a plain one. Timers and searing functions are pleasant and none of them change the arithmetic. The saving is in the method, not the model.

Frequently asked

Is it safe to leave a slow cooker on when I am out? They are designed for exactly that, and at 200W to 250W they run cool. Keep it on a hard heatproof surface away from the wall and anything flammable, and make sure the cable is not trailing.

Does a slow cooker use more electricity than an air fryer? Per hour, no, a slow cooker is much lower. An air fryer draws far more but runs for a fraction of the time, so for a single quick meal an air fryer can work out cheaper. For a long cook feeding several people, the slow cooker wins clearly.

Can I cook rice or pasta in it? Poorly. Both are cheap and fast to cook conventionally. Add them at the end or cook separately.

Is high or low cheaper? The wattage difference is small, so a longer cook on low costs slightly more in total but produces better results with cheap cuts. The difference is a few pence.
