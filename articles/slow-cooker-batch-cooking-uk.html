Slow Cooker Batch Cooking in the UK: The Real Money It Saves

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Batch cooking with a slow cooker is one of the most reliable ways to cut a UK food bill, and one of the few money tips that also saves time. But the savings are real only if you do it right. Here is the honest maths and how to make it work.

Where the saving actually comes from

The big saving is not the electricity. It is what batch cooking stops you spending: the midweek takeaway, the expensive ready meal, the lunch bought out because there was nothing at home. A freezer full of home cooked portions removes the reasons you overspend when tired.

The second saving is the ingredients. Slow cookers turn cheap cuts of meat, dried beans, lentils and root vegetables into good meals. These are the cheapest foods in the shop and the slow cooker is built for exactly them.

The running cost, honestly

A slow cooker uses very little power because it runs at low heat. Cooking a big batch for several hours costs pennies of electricity, far less than running an oven for a single meal. So even the energy side is in your favour, though it is a small part of the total saving.

What to actually cook

  • Chillies, stews and curries using cheap mince or beans. They freeze brilliantly and cost little per portion.
  • Soups from vegetables and lentils. Some of the cheapest food per bowl you can make.
  • Slow cooked cheap cuts of meat, which turn tender over hours and cost a fraction of premium cuts.
  • Big batches of a base sauce you can turn into three different meals across the week.

The method that makes it pay

  1. Cook once, eat many times. Make a large batch, eat one portion, freeze the rest in single meals.
  2. Label and date every container so nothing gets lost and wasted at the back of the freezer.
  3. Shop the cheap ingredients on purpose: dried pulses, frozen veg, cheaper cuts, own brand tins.
  4. Cook on a day you have time, so the busy days are already sorted and no takeaway is tempting.

The mistakes that waste the saving

Buying expensive ingredients defeats the point. The slow cooker saves money because it makes cheap food taste good, so lean into cheap food.

Cooking more than you will eat before it spoils, if you do not freeze it. Always freeze the surplus the same day.

The honest bottom line

A slow cooker is a small one off cost that pays for itself fast, mostly by removing the reasons you reach for takeaways and ready meals. Cook cheap ingredients in big batches, freeze in portions, and the weekly food bill drops noticeably. Pair it with the everyday cuts in our energy bills guide.
