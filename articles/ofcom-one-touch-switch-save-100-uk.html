Ofcom says switching saves over a hundred pounds, so check whether you are the person it means

New figures out this morning put a number on what switching is worth. The number is real, but it belongs to a specific group of customers, and the first job is finding out whether you are one of them.

Ruhul Amin Published 12 September 2026 · 5 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The short version

  • Published this morning, 12 September 2026. Ofcom says nearly 3.5 million people have switched landline or broadband in two years.
  • The £100 figure is specific: out of contract customers taking broadband, landline and pay TV from the same provider could save over £100 a year.
  • One in three of those customers were out of contract last year.
  • One Touch Switch means one call. You speak only to the new provider, and over 300 providers are signed up.
  • Mobile is a text: send PAC to 65075, with no need to speak to your current provider.

1. What Ofcom actually published

Ofcom released new analysis on 12 September 2026, two years to the day since One Touch Switch launched for landline and broadband. The headline figures:

  • Nearly 3.5 million people have used the process in those two years.
  • Many out of contract customers who take broadband, landline and pay TV from the same provider could save over £100 a year by switching.
  • One in three of those triple play customers were out of contract last year.
  • 4 per cent of telecoms customers missed a payment last quarter. Twelve per cent changed a service, 10 per cent cut spending elsewhere, 7 per cent cancelled a service.

Ofcom also launched a Consumer Hub with switching guidance, a free broadband checker for your address, and a Map Your Mobile tool for coverage by postcode.

Read the £100 claim carefully. It is not "everyone saves £100". It is: out of contract, triple play, same provider. A real and large group, and one defined by a condition you can check in two minutes.

2. Are you actually in that group

Three questions, in order.

1. Are you out of contract? This is the one that decides everything. Providers must tell you when your minimum term ends, and your bill or online account will say. Out of contract means no exit fee and, usually, a price that has quietly drifted above what a new customer pays for the same thing.

2. Do you buy more than one service from the same company? The £100 figure is about bundles, where the discount on the original deal was largest and the drift after it therefore steepest.

3. Do you still use all of it? The pay TV element of a triple play bundle is the one households most often stop watching and keep paying for. Cancelling something you do not use is not switching, and it beats switching.

Ian, Norwich. Broadband, landline and a TV package from one provider, two years past the end of his minimum term. He checks the bill: £68 a month. The same provider's current new customer price for the equivalent bundle is on its own website. If the gap is £9 a month, that is £108 a year, which is the Ofcom figure almost exactly, and he has not switched anything yet. He has only found out what loyalty is costing him.

That is the honest framing. The £100 is usually not a reward for switching. It is the removal of a penalty for staying.

3. How the switch works now

The process changed and most people have not noticed.

Broadband and landline. One Touch Switch means you contact the new provider only. You do not have to ring your current one, sit through a retention script, or arrange anything between them. Ofcom says more than 300 providers are signed up.

Mobile. Text PAC to 65075 to get your porting code, again with no need to speak to your current provider. Ofcom's Map Your Mobile tool shows coverage and performance by postcode, which matters more than the tariff if the new network is weak where you live.

Two practical notes. Check the service availability at your actual address before committing, using Ofcom's free checker, because what is sold nationally is not always available on your street. And if you are still inside a minimum term, ask what the early exit charge is before anything else, because it can wipe out a year of savings.

The verdict

The £100 is credible but conditional. It describes out of contract bundle customers, and if you are inside a minimum term it does not describe you at all.

So do the cheap thing first. Find your contract end date, then compare your current monthly price against your own provider's new customer price for the same bundle. That single comparison tells you whether there is £100 on the table, before you go anywhere near a comparison site.

Then, if there is, remember that calling your existing provider to ask them to match it is free and takes ten minutes. Switching is the lever, not always the destination.

Frequently asked

Do I have to tell my current provider I am leaving? Not for broadband or landline under One Touch Switch. Ofcom says you speak only to the new provider. For mobile, texting PAC to 65075 gets your code without speaking to your current provider.

Is the £100 saving guaranteed? No. Ofcom's analysis says many out of contract customers taking broadband, landline and pay TV from the same provider could save over £100 a year. It is a figure for that group, not a promise for every household.

What if I am still in contract? Ask for the early exit charge in writing first. It can exceed the saving. Out of contract is the condition that makes the Ofcom figure apply.
