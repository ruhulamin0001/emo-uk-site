Attendance Allowance: not means-tested, and the door it opens is often worth more than the money

Two rates, £76.70 and £114.60 a week, paid from State Pension age to people who need help or supervision because of a disability or illness. Savings are ignored entirely. And for some households the knock-on effect on Pension Credit is worth more than the allowance itself.

Ruhul Amin Published 6 September 2026 · 9 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Two rates: £76.70 a week lower, £114.60 a week higher (GOV.UK, page updated 4 September 2026). That is £3,988.40 or £5,959.20 a year.
  • It is not means-tested. GOV.UK is explicit: "what you earn or how much you have in savings will not affect what you get". There is no capital limit and no income test.
  • It can unlock more money elsewhere. GOV.UK says you could get extra Pension Credit, Housing Benefit or Council Tax Reduction if you get Attendance Allowance.
  • It is about help and supervision, not mobility. The two rates are defined by how much help or supervision you need during the day and at night. There is no mobility component.
  • You do not need to be getting help already. The test is what help you need, not what you currently receive, which is the single most common reason people wrongly rule themselves out.

1. The two rates, and what each one is for

Attendance Allowance is paid weekly at two rates. Which one you get depends on the level of help you need, and GOV.UK's own descriptions are worth reading closely because the wording is the test.

RateLevel of help you needA year
Lower, £76.70 a weekFrequent help or constant supervision during the day, or supervision at night£3,988.40
Higher, £114.60 a weekHelp or supervision throughout both day and night, or a medical professional has said you are nearing the end of life£5,959.20

The word doing the work in both rows is "need". The claim form asks what help you need, not what help you get. Plenty of people care for themselves with great difficulty, or have a spouse quietly doing everything, and conclude that they do not qualify because nobody is being paid to look after them. That is not the test.

"Supervision" also carries more weight than people expect. Someone who could fall, who becomes confused, or who needs checking on during the night can meet the supervision test even if no physical help is given.

If your circumstances change and you need more help than when you claimed, you can report the change and move to the higher rate. That is a live route, not a one-off decision at the point of claiming.

2. Not means-tested, and what that actually means

GOV.UK states it in one line: "Attendance Allowance is not means-tested - what you earn or how much you have in savings will not affect what you get."

In practice that means all of the following are true.

  • Savings are ignored entirely. There is no £6,000 or £16,000 capital limit as there is on means-tested benefits. Someone with £200,000 in the bank can receive it in full.
  • Your pension income does not reduce it. Occupational pension, State Pension, annuity, rental income: none of it counts.
  • Your partner's income does not count either. It is an individual award.
  • It is not taxable and you do not declare it as income.
  • It is not affected by owning your home.

This is why the "I would never qualify, I own my house" reflex is wrong, and it is probably the single biggest reason the benefit goes unclaimed. Nothing about your money is relevant. Only what help you need is relevant.

One genuine interaction: if you already receive Constant Attendance Allowance, GOV.UK says your Attendance Allowance is reduced by the amount of Constant Attendance Allowance you get.

3. The part that is often worth more than the benefit itself

GOV.UK adds a sentence that gets very little attention: "You could get extra Pension Credit, Housing Benefit or Council Tax Reduction if you get Attendance Allowance."

Here is the mechanism, because "extra Pension Credit" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Guarantee Credit tops your weekly income up to a set line. For 2026/27 that line is £238 for a single person. A single pensioner on the full new State Pension of £241.30 sits above it and gets nothing.

But receiving Attendance Allowance can bring a severe disability addition into the Pension Credit calculation, which lifts that line to £324.05. Suddenly the same person is comfortably below the line and qualifies.

The severe disability addition is not automatic. It generally requires all of the following: you receive a qualifying disability benefit such as Attendance Allowance, you live alone or are treated as living alone, and nobody receives Carer's Allowance for looking after you. That last condition is why a family sometimes has to choose between a carer claiming Carer's Allowance and the disabled person keeping the severe disability addition, and the arithmetic does not always favour the obvious answer.

And Pension Credit is itself a gateway. Getting any amount of it, even a few pence a week, opens the door to the Warm Home Discount core group, Council Tax Reduction, Housing Benefit and a free TV licence at 75 or over.

4. Eileen in Kendal: £76.70 that becomes nearly £8,000

Eileen is 79, widowed, and lives alone in Kendal. She gets the full new State Pension of £241.30 a week and has £14,000 in a building society account. Her daughter helps her wash and dress most mornings and rings at night to check on her. Nobody claims Carer's Allowance.

She has never applied for anything, because she owns her flat and has savings.

Attendance Allowance. She needs frequent help during the day and supervision at night, so she is awarded the lower rate. £76.70 a week, £3,988.40 a year. Her savings and her pension are irrelevant to this award.

Pension Credit, now that she has it. Her income for Pension Credit is her State Pension of £241.30 plus deemed income from her savings. The first £10,000 of capital is ignored and every £500 above that counts as £1 a week, so her £14,000 adds £8. Her assessed income is £249.30.

Because she lives alone, receives Attendance Allowance and nobody claims Carer's Allowance for her, the severe disability addition applies and her Pension Credit line is £324.05. Guarantee Credit tops her up by £74.75 a week, which is about £3,887 a year.

Total: roughly £7,875 a year, plus the Warm Home Discount, plus Council Tax Reduction, plus a free TV licence, from a woman who was certain she would not qualify for anything.

Two honest caveats. If her daughter started claiming Carer's Allowance, the severe disability addition would stop, and the family would need to compare £86.45 a week of Carer's Allowance against £74.75 a week of Pension Credit plus the passported help. And if Eileen lived with an adult son or daughter, the "living alone" condition would usually fail and the Pension Credit half of this would not happen at all. Ring the Pension Service on 0800 99 1234 and have it worked out on your real circumstances rather than assuming this example is yours.

5. What it does not cover, and how to claim

There is no mobility component. Both rates are defined by help and supervision. If getting around is the problem rather than personal care and safety, Attendance Allowance is not the benefit for that.

It starts at State Pension age. Below that age the equivalent is Personal Independence Payment, which works differently and does have a mobility part.

Scotland is administered separately. Disability benefits for people over State Pension age in Scotland are delivered by Social Security Scotland rather than DWP. Check mygov.scot before applying if you live there, because the form and the office are not the same.

Going into a care home changes things. If your care is being paid for by the local authority, the award is usually affected. GOV.UK has a separate page on claiming benefits if you are going into a care home.

How to claim. The form is the AA1, available from GOV.UK or by ringing the Attendance Allowance helpline. It is long, and it is worth taking time over. The single most useful piece of advice is to describe a bad day rather than an average one, and to write down the help you need even where you currently go without it. Age UK and Citizens Advice both help people fill this form in for free, and both are better value than any company charging a fee.

If you are nearing the end of life, there is a separate fast-track route on GOV.UK and the higher rate is paid automatically. Nobody should be filling in the long form in that situation.

The verdict

If you are over State Pension age and you need help washing, dressing, eating, getting to the toilet, or someone keeping an eye on you for your own safety, claim it. Your savings, your pension and your house are all irrelevant, and the test is what help you need rather than what help you already get. Then check Pension Credit again afterwards, because for someone living alone the severe disability addition can be worth more than the Attendance Allowance itself and opens several other doors behind it. Get free help with the form from Age UK or Citizens Advice rather than paying anyone.

Frequently asked

Will claiming Attendance Allowance affect my State Pension? No. It is paid on top and does not reduce your State Pension. It is not taxable and you do not declare it as income.

Do I need a diagnosis or a doctor's letter? No formal diagnosis is required and there is no medical certificate to obtain, though DWP may contact your GP or ask you to attend an assessment. The form is about the practical help and supervision you need day to day, so that is what to describe.

My husband does everything for me. Does that mean I do not qualify? The opposite. The test is the help you need, not who provides it or whether they are paid. A spouse doing everything is exactly the situation the benefit exists for, and it is one of the most common reasons people wrongly decide not to apply.

Can I get it if I still work? Attendance Allowance starts at State Pension age, and earnings are ignored because it is not means-tested. If you are working past State Pension age and need help at home, your wages do not affect the award.
