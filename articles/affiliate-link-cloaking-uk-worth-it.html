Should You Cloak Your Affiliate Links? Read the Three Clauses First

Cloaking plugins are sold as a tidiness feature, and the sales pages rarely mention that the biggest affiliate programme in the UK has written rules that bear directly on them. Those rules are public, short, and more specific than most of the advice written about them.

Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Amazon's Programme Policies clause (v) is the one that matters. You will not cloak, hide, spoof or otherwise obscure the URL of your Site containing Special Links, including by use of Redirecting Links, such that Amazon cannot reasonably determine which site a customer clicked through from.
  • Clause (w) covers link shorteners. You will not use a link shortening service, button, hyperlink or other ad placement in a manner that makes it unclear that you are linking to an Amazon Site.
  • The Commission Income Statement goes further and just disqualifies the sale. Purchases referred by a Redirecting Link, meaning a link that sends users to Amazon via an intermediate site without any affirmative action there, are excluded from qualifying purchases.
  • A separate exclusion covers broken formatting. Any purchase not correctly tracked or reported because your links are not properly formatted does not earn.
  • What the rules do NOT say. They do not ban tidy internal URLs as such, and they do not require you to show a raw amazon.co.uk address on the page. The test throughout is whether the destination is clear and the source site is determinable.
  • Getting this wrong is not a warning. Section 6 lets Amazon suspend an account immediately on material breach, and gives only 7 days to cure other breaches.

1. What cloaking actually means here

Cloaking, in affiliate marketing, usually means replacing a long tagged retailer URL with a short one on your own domain, so that the link a reader sees reads something like yoursite.co.uk/go/kettle rather than a string of tracking parameters. The plugin then redirects the click on to the retailer.

Three reasons get given for doing it: the links look tidier, you can change a destination in one place if a product dies, and you can count clicks. The first is cosmetic, the second and third are real operational benefits.

The problem is that "redirect through my own domain" is exactly the mechanism that two clauses of Amazon's programme rules describe, and it is worth reading those before deciding rather than after.

2. The three clauses, in full

All three are public. Two are in the Amazon.co.uk Associates Programme Policies, and the third is in the Associates Programme Commission Income Statement inside the same document.

Programme Policies, clause (v). "You will not cloak, hide, spoof, or otherwise obscure the URL of your Site containing Special Links (including by use of Redirecting Links) or the user agent of the application in which Program Content is displayed or used such that we cannot reasonably determine the site or application from which a customer clicks through such Special Link to an Amazon Site."

Read the shape of that sentence carefully, because it is doing something specific. The prohibited outcome is that Amazon cannot work out which site the click came from. The word cloak appears, but the clause is not a blanket ban on pretty URLs. It is a ban on breaking Amazon's ability to identify your site as the source.

Programme Policies, clause (w). "You will not use a link shortening service, button, hyperlink or other ad placement in a manner that makes it unclear that you are linking to an Amazon Site."

Again, note the test. It is not "do not shorten". It is "do not make it unclear where the reader is going". A link whose anchor text says the reader is going to Amazon passes that test. A bare short link with no indication of destination does not.

Commission Income Statement, exclusion (e). Qualifying purchases exclude "any Product purchased by a customer who is referred to an Amazon Site by a link that sends users indirectly to an Amazon Site via an intermediate site, without requiring the customer to click on a link or take some other affirmative action on that intermediate site (a Redirecting Link)."

This is the one that does real damage, and it is the one almost never quoted in plugin marketing, because it is not a rule you can be warned about. The sale simply does not qualify. There is nothing to appeal.

There is a fourth clause worth knowing alongside them. Exclusion (g) removes "any Product purchase that is not correctly tracked or reported because the links from your site to the relevant Amazon Site are not properly formatted". A redirect chain that drops your tag lands you here even if nothing else does.

3. What the rules do not say

Being fair to the plugins matters as much as being fair to the rules, so here is the other side.

The rules do not ban internal link management. Nothing in clause (v) or (w) requires the visible href on your page to be a raw amazon.co.uk URL.

They do not ban click tracking. Counting your own clicks is not mentioned.

They do not treat all redirects identically. The Redirecting Link definition in exclusion (e) turns specifically on the customer not taking any affirmative action on the intermediate site. That definition is aimed at a particular pattern, not at every 301 that has ever existed.

What all three clauses share is a single underlying requirement: the reader should know they are going to Amazon, and Amazon should be able to tell they came from you. Any setup that satisfies both is a long way from the conduct these clauses were written for. Any setup that quietly defeats either is a problem whatever the plugin's sales page claims.

One more thing the rules do not do: they do not replace your disclosure duty. Section 5 of the Operating Agreement requires the statement "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases", and the Agreement states that any violation of Section 5 will be deemed a material breach. A tidy link does not carry that disclosure for you. Our guide to affiliate disclosure rules in the UK covers the advertising side of the same duty.

4. Other networks are not Amazon

It is worth saying plainly that none of the above is a general law of affiliate marketing. It is the terms of one programme. Other networks and individual merchants set their own positions on redirects and shorteners, and some are relaxed about practices Amazon is not.

The practical instruction is therefore boring and correct: read the terms of each programme you are actually in, not a blog post about affiliate marketing in general. If you run Amazon alongside a network programme, the strictest set of terms is the one your site has to satisfy, because your site is one site.

If you are weighing up which programmes to be in at all, Amazon Associates versus Awin sets out how differently two UK programmes can be built.

Worked example: Callum in Dundee

Callum runs a small tools and DIY site with about forty product pages. He installs a link manager because he is tired of editing the same dead product URL in nine places.

What he set up. Every outbound link became yoursite/go/{product}, the anchor text stayed as the product name, and there was no mention of Amazon anywhere near the button.

The two problems. First, clause (w): a reader cannot tell from the page that the button goes to Amazon, so the destination is unclear. Second, and worse, the redirect he chose passes the reader straight through with no action on his own site, which is the pattern described in exclusion (e).

What he changed. He kept the internal link management, because that was the actual problem he was solving. He changed the anchor text so every link says where it goes, for example "check the current price on Amazon UK", and he added the required Associate disclosure to the page.

What it cost him. About an hour, and none of the benefit he installed the plugin for. He did not need to choose between tidy links and the rules. He needed to stop hiding the destination.

The honest verdict

The question "is cloaking worth it" turns out to be the wrong question, because the rules are not written about tidiness. They are written about two outcomes: whether the reader can tell they are being sent to Amazon, and whether Amazon can tell the click came from your site.

If your setup preserves both, internal link management is a reasonable operational tool and nothing in clauses (v) or (w) is aimed at you. If your setup defeats either, the risk is not a stern email. Exclusion (e) simply does not count the sale, and Section 6 of the Operating Agreement allows immediate suspension for a material breach with a seven day cure period for lesser ones.

Where this does not help you: if you are already several hundred links deep in a setup you are unsure about, this page cannot audit it for you. Click one of your own links, look at what a reader would have seen before clicking, and check whether your reporting still shows the traffic as coming from your site. Those two checks answer it.

Frequently asked

Is a cloaking plugin against Amazon's rules outright? The rules do not name plugins. They describe outcomes. Clause (v) prohibits obscuring the URL of your site such that Amazon cannot reasonably determine where the click came from, and clause (w) prohibits shortening in a manner that makes the Amazon destination unclear. A plugin that leaves both clear is not what those clauses describe.

What is a Redirecting Link, exactly? The Commission Income Statement defines it as a link that sends users indirectly to an Amazon site via an intermediate site, without requiring the customer to click a link or take some other affirmative action on that intermediate site. Purchases referred that way are excluded from qualifying purchases.

Do I still need the disclosure if my links are cloaked? Yes, and more obviously so. Section 5 of the Operating Agreement requires the statement that you earn from qualifying purchases as an Amazon Associate, and the Agreement deems any violation of Section 5 a material breach. Making the link look less like an ad does not reduce the duty to say it is one.

Do these rules apply to other affiliate networks too? No. These are the terms of the Amazon Associates programme. Other networks and merchants set their own rules on redirects and shorteners, and some permit things Amazon does not. If you run several programmes on one site, the strictest terms are the ones your pages have to meet.
