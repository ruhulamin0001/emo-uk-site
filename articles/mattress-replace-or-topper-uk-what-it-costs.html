A Topper Fixes Comfort. It Cannot Fix Support. That Is the Whole Decision

Putting a £60 topper on a sagging mattress is either a brilliant piece of thrift or throwing sixty pounds at a problem that will still be there in the morning, and which it is depends on one question that nobody at a bed showroom will ask you. Here is the question, and the cost per year of sleep for every way of answering it.

The short version

  • The question is comfort or support. A topper changes the feel of the surface. It cannot restore the structure underneath.
  • A topper is £60 to £200 and buys 2 to 4 years, so roughly £20 to £75 a year of sleep.
  • A mattress is £400 to £900 and buys 7 to 10 years, so roughly £44 to £113 a year.

1. The one question that decides it

A mattress does two separate jobs and they fail at different rates.

Comfort is the top few centimetres. The padding, the quilting, the feel against your body. It softens, compresses and flattens first, usually within the first few years, and it is what people mean when they say a mattress "feels tired". A topper addresses this perfectly, because a topper is literally a new comfort layer.

Support is the core. Springs or foam holding your spine roughly in line. When that fails you get a dip, most obviously in the hip area, and your body sags into it all night. A topper laid over a dip follows the dip. You have added softness on top of a hollow, which in some cases makes matters worse, because now the hollow is less obvious and harder to compensate for.

A topper over a dip does not fill the dip. It upholsters it.

The test, which takes two minutes and costs nothing. Strip the bed completely. Crouch at the foot end and look along the surface at eye level, against a window if you can. Then do it from the side. You are looking for a visible hollow where people sleep, present with nobody on the bed. Then press down hard with a flat palm in the middle of the dip and in a corner that nobody sleeps on, and feel whether the resistance is noticeably different.

  • Flat surface, but it feels hard, lumpy or just unpleasant: comfort layer. Buy a topper.
  • Visible hollow, or a ridge in the middle between two sleepers: support. Replace the mattress.
  • Springs you can feel individually, or any audible metallic noise: support, and the mattress is finished.

One extra check while the bed is stripped: look at the base. A sagging slatted base or a broken slat produces exactly the same symptoms as a failed mattress and costs a fraction to fix. Slats are cheap. People replace £600 mattresses because of a £15 piece of timber more often than anyone admits.

2. Cost per year of sleep

The reason to think in cost per year here is that a bed is used more hours than anything else you own. Eight hours a night is a third of your life, and a mattress that costs £700 and lasts eight years costs less per hour of use than almost any other object in the house.

Topper. £60 to £200, lasting 2 to 4 years before it flattens. £20 to £75 a year.

Mattress. £400 to £900, lasting 7 to 10 years. £44 to £113 a year.

Per night, on a £700 mattress lasting 8 years. About 24p a night.

Two honest observations about those numbers. First, the topper genuinely is cheaper per year, roughly half, provided the underlying mattress still supports you. That is a real saving and it is not a compromise. Second, the difference between the cheapest and most expensive mattress in that range is about £69 a year, which is less than most households spend on takeaway coffee in a quarter. Sleep is the wrong place to be squeezing the last few pounds.

The eight to ten year figure is the conventional replacement interval quoted by the bed industry and by sleep charities. Treat it as a prompt to check rather than a deadline: a good mattress on a good base with a rotation routine can outlast it comfortably, and a cheap one on a sagging divan will not reach it.

4. What to compare instead of a price

Mattress retail is built around discount theatre, and comparing prices between retailers on what is often the same product under different names is close to impossible. So compare the things that are written down, that do not change hourly, and that actually decide whether you are happy in two years.

The sleep trial. How many nights, and what happens at the end of it. This is the single most valuable term, because you cannot tell whether a mattress suits you in a showroom in four minutes with your shoes on. A long trial is a genuine transfer of risk from you to the seller.

Who pays return carriage. A hundred night trial where you pay £70 to send a double mattress back is a different product from one where collection is free. Ask, and get the answer in writing.

What the warranty covers, and what counts as a defect. Most mattress warranties only cover sagging beyond a stated depth, commonly measured with no weight on the bed. Ask what that depth is. Ask whether the warranty is void if you use the wrong base, because many are.

Whether it is one sided or two sided. A two sided mattress you can flip will generally outlast a one sided one, all else equal, because you are using twice the comfort layer.

Delivery and removal. Whether they take the old one away, and at what cost. Disposing of a mattress yourself means a trip to a household waste site and, at some councils, a fee.

Those six things are checkable from a retailer's own published terms, they are the same next week, and between them they are worth more than any star rating. This is the same argument our guide to appliance extended warranties makes about white goods: the terms are the product.

5. Worked example: the Osei bedroom in Croydon

A couple in Croydon with a seven year old pocket sprung double. He wakes with lower back pain, she does not. The mattress has never been rotated. They are ready to spend £800.

The two minute test. Stripped and viewed at eye level, there is a clear hollow on his side and a slight ridge up the middle. Her side looks flat.

Diagnosis. Support failure on one side, which is the classic pattern where one sleeper is heavier and the mattress has never been rotated.

What a topper would have done. £140, and the hollow would still be there under it. £140 for nothing.

What they did. New mattress at £720, with a 200 night trial and free collection if returned.

Cost per year over 8 years. £720 ÷ 8 = £90 a year, or about 25p a night.

The free change that came with it. A diary reminder to rotate it every three months, which is what will decide whether they get eight years or five.

The instructive part is what would have happened had the hollow not been there. Same couple, same seven year old mattress, same back pain, but a flat surface that simply feels too firm: the right answer is a £140 topper at about £40 a year, they keep the £720, and the outcome is just as good. The symptom is identical. The fix is completely different. That is why the two minute test is the most valuable thing in this article.

Frequently asked

How often should a mattress be replaced? The conventional figure is around seven to ten years, but treat it as a reminder to do the eye level test rather than a rule. Condition beats age. A supportive twelve year old mattress on a sound base is fine, and a collapsed four year old one is not.

Do toppers make you hot? Memory foam toppers in particular hold heat, which some people find comfortable in winter and unbearable in July. If you sleep hot, that matters more than the price. Latex and fibre toppers generally run cooler than memory foam.

Does a firmer mattress help back pain? Not reliably, and the assumption that firmer is better for backs is one of the more persistent myths in this market. What matters is that your spine stays roughly in line, which for a side sleeper usually needs enough give at the shoulder and hip. If you have ongoing back pain, that is a conversation for a GP or physiotherapist rather than a bed shop, and this article is not medical advice.

Is it worth buying second hand? Generally no, for hygiene reasons and because you cannot assess how the core has been loaded by a stranger's body for five years. The one exception is an unused ex display or return with a clear provenance and a fire label intact.

What about the fire label? Any mattress sold in the UK should carry a label showing it meets the relevant fire safety requirements. If a seller cannot show you one, walk away. This is also why an unlabelled second hand mattress is a poor idea.

The honest verdict

Strip the bed and look along it at eye level before you spend anything. If the surface is flat and simply feels wrong, buy a topper: at £20 to £75 a year it is roughly half the cost per year of a new mattress and it genuinely solves comfort.

If there is a hollow, a ridge, or springs you can feel, do not buy a topper. It will not work and the money is gone. Replace the mattress, and check the base slats at the same time, because a £15 slat sometimes explains the whole thing.

When you do buy, ignore the discount and compare the trial length, who pays return carriage, and what the warranty defines as a defect. Those three terms are worth more than any headline price, and unlike a price they are the same next week.
