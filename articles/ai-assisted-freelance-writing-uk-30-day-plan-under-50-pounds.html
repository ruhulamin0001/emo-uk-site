A 30-Day Plan to Test AI-Assisted Freelance Writing From the UK for Under GBP 20 a Month: The Hours, the Exact Cost, the First Milestone, and the Day You Stop

Every guide to freelance writing with AI tells you to start. None of them tells you when to stop, which is the number that protects your evenings. This is a 30-day plan with a fixed weekly hour budget, an exact monthly tool cost read from the vendors' pages, one realistic milestone, and a stop condition written down before day one. It does not promise an income, because no reliable figure exists for what a beginner earns in month one, and the commonest answer is nothing.

The short version

  • The budget is six hours a week, 24 hours in the month, which at the GBP 12.71 National Living Wage is GBP 305.04 of your time. That is the real cost of the test and it dwarfs the tools.
  • The tools cost between GBP 0 and GBP 17.71 for the month. Claude Free, LanguageTool free and Toggl Track free cost nothing. The only upgrade in the plan is Claude Pro at USD 20 before tax, about GBP 17.71 with VAT at HMRC's September 2026 rate, and only from week three if you have hit the free limit on real work.
  • The first milestone is one paid piece of at least GBP 50 from a person you spoke to, by day 30. Not a portfolio, not followers, not a profile. Paid.
  • The stop condition: fewer than three paid conversations by day 30, or no paid piece by day 45. Stop, keep the free tools, and try a different route.
  • What usually happens: most people who start this earn nothing in month one, and the ones who earn something earned it from someone they already knew. Plan the month around that, not around cold applications.
  • Tax: up to GBP 1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year sits under the trading allowance; above it you register for Self Assessment by the 5 October after the tax year ends.

1. The rules of the test

A test has to be bounded or it is not a test, it is a hobby with a spreadsheet. Three rules. First, the hour budget is six hours a week and you log every one of them in a timer, so at the end you know what the month actually cost you. Second, the tool budget is under GBP 20 a month and the only paid tool allowed is one Claude Pro plan, and only from week three, and only if you hit the free limit while doing paid or nearly paid work. Third, the milestone and the stop condition are written down on day one and not moved.

The work is AI-assisted writing and editing for small businesses and other freelancers: web page copy, product descriptions, case studies, email sequences, tidying up documents someone else wrote. AI-assisted means you use Claude to research, structure, draft and check, and you remain the person who knows the client, chooses what is true, and signs off. The existing guide to earning a first GBP 1,000 with AI explains why plain AI-written blog content no longer sells; this plan assumes you read that and are not doing that.

3. The four weeks, hour by hour

Week 1, six hours: pick one thing and make one sample. Two hours choosing a narrow service you could describe in one sentence to a stranger: rewriting the About page and three service pages for a trade, or editing a consultant's proposals, or turning call notes into case studies. Two hours making one finished sample of that exact service for a real business you know, unpaid and told so, using Claude to research and draft and you to correct. Two hours setting up the timer, the grammar checker and a one-page description of the service with a price. No website. No logo.

Week 2, six hours: fifteen conversations. All six hours go on reaching people, and the target is fifteen real conversations with people who run or work in a small business you can name, starting with the ones who know you. Not applications on job boards; those have fee structures of their own and cold applicants at the bottom of the pile. The ask is specific: here is a sample, here is the price, is there one page or document you would pay to have done. Log every conversation and its outcome.

Week 3, six hours: do the first paid piece, or the first two. If week two produced one yes, this week is delivery: brief, draft, check, revise once, invoice. If it produced nothing, week three is another fifteen conversations, and this is the week you decide about Pro, based only on whether the free plan stopped you doing something someone was paying for. Time the piece. That number, hours per paid piece, is the one you will need to set a rate, and the freelance rates guide shows what to do with it.

Week 4, six hours: second piece, ask for the referral, tally the month. Deliver the second piece if there is one, ask every paying client for one introduction, and spend the last hour reading the timer. Hours logged, conversations held, pieces paid, pounds invoiced, pounds received. Then read section 4.

4. The milestone and the stop condition

The milestone is one paid piece of at least GBP 50, invoiced and paid, from a person you spoke to, by day 30. It is deliberately small because the purpose of month one is not income, it is finding out whether anyone in your reach will pay for this at all. A profile, a portfolio site and a number of followers are not milestones; they are things people build to avoid asking for money.

By day 30Decision
Fewer than three paid conversations (a real person discussing a real price)Stop. The problem is reach, not the service, and another month will not fix reach
Three or more conversations, no paid piece yetRun to day 45 on the same hours. If still no paid piece, stop
One paid piece, GBP 50 or moreContinue. Double the hours only when the timer shows you are turning work away
Two or more paid piecesContinue, raise the price on the next one, and read the tax section

Our stop conditions. Write them down before day one; people who set them afterwards move them.

Stopping is not failure. It costs you GBP 0 to GBP 17.71 and 24 hours to learn that your immediate network will not buy writing, which is worth knowing before you spend six months on it, and the free tools stay useful for whatever you try next.

5. A worked example in Stoke-on-Trent

Sofia in Stoke-on-Trent works four days a week in a GP practice and wants a fifth day's worth of income. She picks one service: rewriting the About and services pages for small trades, GBP 120 for the set. Week one she makes a sample for her cousin's plastering firm, unpaid, using Claude Free to research what customers ask plasterers and to draft, then rewriting the draft in the firm's voice. Week two she has eleven conversations, not fifteen, mostly at the school gate and through the practice's patients who run businesses. Two say yes in principle. One asks the price and goes quiet.

Week three she does the first paid set for a mobile dog groomer at GBP 120, and the timer says 4 hours 40 minutes including a revision. She hits the Claude Free limit twice while drafting and buys Pro at GBP 17.71 on day 19, which she records. Week four she does a second set for a gardener the groomer introduced, GBP 120, in 3 hours 50 minutes. Month total: 25 hours logged, 13 conversations, two paid pieces, GBP 240 invoiced, GBP 120 received by day 30 and the second GBP 120 on day 36.

Her arithmetic: GBP 240 for 25 hours is GBP 9.60 an hour across the whole month, below the National Living Wage, and GBP 240 for the 8.5 hours of actual paid work is GBP 28.24 an hour. Both numbers are true, and the second one is the one that improves with a third client. She is well inside the GBP 1,000 trading allowance for the tax year, keeps every invoice and the Pro receipt as the side hustle tax rules guide describes, and continues.

6. What usually happens

The commonest outcome is month one earns nothing, and the plan is designed so that costs 24 hours and at most GBP 17.71 rather than a year. The second commonest is one paid piece from someone the person already knew, which is why the plan spends its hours on conversations rather than profiles. Nobody publishes reliable figures for what beginners earn in month one; job platforms publish fees, not outcomes, and people who post screenshots are the ones it worked for. This site does not invent a number to fill that gap.

Two things reliably break the plan. Building a website in week one, because it feels like progress and produces no conversations. And skipping the timer, because without it the month ends with a feeling rather than a figure, and feelings are how people spend a year on GBP 300.

The honest verdict

Run the test if you can name ten people who run small businesses and are willing to ask them for money; it costs 24 hours and between nothing and GBP 17.71, and by day 30 you will know. Do not run it if your plan for finding clients is job boards and a profile, because the month will end with the stop condition met and the reason will be reach, not writing. And whatever happens, keep the timer: the hours-per-piece number it gives you is worth more than the first GBP 120.

Frequently asked

Do I need Claude Pro to start? No. The plan runs on Claude Free for the first two weeks and only allows Pro, at USD 20 a month before tax or about GBP 17.71 with VAT, from week three if the free limit stopped paid work. Pro is included in the under GBP 20 budget.

How much does a beginner earn in the first month? Nobody can tell you and this article does not guess. The commonest outcome is nothing; the next commonest is one paid piece from someone you already knew. The plan's milestone is one paid piece of GBP 50 or more by day 30.

Should I use Upwork or PeoplePerHour instead of asking people I know? The plan spends its hours on direct conversations because cold applicants sit at the bottom of platform piles and platforms take a fee. If you do use one, read its own fee page first; this article does not quote platform fees because their pages could not be verified on the day of writing.

When do I tell HMRC? Not while gross trading income is GBP 1,000 or less in the tax year, under the trading allowance, though you must keep records. Above GBP 1,000 you register for Self Assessment by the 5 October after the tax year in which you started.
