Tax-Free Childcare Pays 20% Up to £500 a Quarter. The 30 Hours Pay Far More. Which to Use, Why You Can Have Both, and the £100,000 Cliff They Share

Two government schemes cut a working family's childcare bill in England, and they are routinely confused with each other. One is a top-up to money you pay in, capped low. The other is a block of funded hours, worth thousands, that started reaching babies from nine months in September 2025. They can be used together, they share the same earnings test and the same income cliff, and the order you use them in matters. Here is what each is worth on an ordinary nursery bill.

The short version

  • Tax-Free Childcare: for every £8 you pay into the online childcare account the government adds £2, up to £500 every three months per child (£2,000 a year), or £1,000 a quarter for a disabled child. Child must be 11 or under (16 if disabled) and the provider signed up. Source: GOV.UK, Tax-Free Childcare, read 5 September 2026.
  • Free Childcare for Working Parents: 30 hours a week of funded childcare for 38 weeks a year for children aged nine months to four years. That is 1,140 hours a year at the provider's funded rate, worth far more than the £2,000 TFC cap on a full-time bill.
  • Both schemes use the same tests: each parent must expect to earn at least 16 hours a week at the National Minimum or Living Wage, £2,643.68 over three months for someone aged 21 or over, and neither is available if either parent's adjusted net income is over £100,000. That is a cliff, not a taper.
  • You can use both together. The 30 hours reduce the bill; Tax-Free Childcare tops up what you still pay. You cannot use Tax-Free Childcare alongside Universal Credit childcare or the old childcare vouchers, so anyone on UC needs to do the sum before switching.
  • Both need you to reconfirm every three months in the childcare account, or they stop. The 38 week rule means term time only unless your provider stretches the hours across the year at fewer per week.
  • Our verdict: claim the 30 hours first, as soon as your child is nine months old and the term after; put Tax-Free Childcare on top for the balance. Watch the £100,000 line, because £1 over it removes both.

1. Tax-Free Childcare: the 20% top-up and the £500 cap

Tax-Free Childcare works through an online childcare account on GOV.UK. You pay money in, the government adds 25% on top of what you paid, which GOV.UK expresses as £2 for every £8, and you pay your childcare provider out of the account. GOV.UK's own example: a £250 nursery bill is met by paying in £200, with £50 added. The provider has to be signed up to the scheme, and most nurseries, childminders and after-school clubs are, but check before relying on it.

The cap is the part people miss. The top-up is limited to £500 every three months per child, which is £2,000 a year, and £1,000 a quarter, £4,000 a year, for a disabled child. So the 20% only applies to the first £2,500 of childcare you pay for in a quarter, about £833 a month. Above that you are paying in full. For a part-time bill the cap rarely bites; for a full-time nursery place it bites every quarter.

The child must be 11 or under, or 16 or under if disabled, so the scheme runs long past the free hours into school-age wraparound care and holiday clubs. The money in the account is yours: you can withdraw it, in which case the government's share goes back. And you must sign in every three months to confirm you are still eligible, or the top-ups stop.

2. The 30 hours: what is funded, and what is not

Free Childcare for Working Parents, the scheme usually called the 30 hours, gives eligible working parents 30 hours a week of funded childcare for 38 weeks of the year for children from nine months old until they start school. GOV.UK states it as 30 hours per week for children aged nine months to four years. The full 30 hours for the nine months to two year olds arrived in September 2025, the last step of the expansion that began in April 2024. The entitlement starts the term after your child reaches nine months and after you have a valid code, so a child turning nine months in October starts in January.

Thirty hours over 38 weeks is 1,140 funded hours a year. On a full-time place of, say, 50 hours a week over 51 weeks, that funds 1,140 of 2,550 hours, about 45% of the bill. Many providers offer to stretch the entitlement across the whole year at about 22 hours a week instead, which suits parents who need cover in the school holidays; the total hours are the same.

Funded does not always mean free. Government pays providers a funded rate per hour, and providers may charge for consumables such as meals, nappies and trips, and for hours outside the funded ones, at their own rates. The Department for Education's guidance says these charges must be voluntary and itemised and cannot be a condition of taking up the funded hours, but the practical bill for a funded day is rarely zero. Ask the provider for the funded-hours price list before you assume.

3. The tests both schemes share, and the £100,000 cliff

Both schemes apply the same two tests, and GOV.UK sets them out on the same eligibility checker. First, earnings: you, and your partner if you have one, must each expect to earn at least the National Minimum or Living Wage for 16 hours a week on average over the next three months. For someone aged 21 or over that is £2,643.68 over three months at the £12.71 rate from April 2026; for 18 to 20 year olds it is £2,256.80; under 18 or an apprentice £1,664. Self-employed parents can average over the tax year, and a business under 12 months old is exempt from the minimum. Parents on maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental or sick leave count as working.

Second, income: neither scheme is available if you or your partner expects an adjusted net income over £100,000 in the current tax year. Adjusted net income is total taxable income less pension contributions and Gift Aid, and it is a cliff, not a taper. A parent on £100,001 loses the 30 hours and Tax-Free Childcare in full, which on a full-time place can be worth more than £8,000 a year. Someone near the line who can make a pension contribution to bring adjusted net income under £100,000 is in the unusual position of being paid to save; our salary sacrifice guide explains the mechanism.

Both are reconfirmed every three months in the same childcare account. Miss the reconfirmation and the code lapses; a provider cannot claim the funded hours against a lapsed code, and the bill for that term lands on you.

4. Using both together, and the schemes you cannot mix

The two schemes stack. The 30 hours come off the bill first, at the provider's funded rate. Whatever you still pay, for extra hours, consumables, holiday weeks or a second child, can be paid through the Tax-Free Childcare account and attracts the 20% top-up, up to the £500 quarterly cap. That is the intended design, and GOV.UK's childcare step-by-step lists both under the help you can get at the same time.

The schemes you cannot mix with Tax-Free Childcare are the older and the means-tested ones. You cannot get Tax-Free Childcare at the same time as Universal Credit, Working Tax Credit, Child Tax Credit or childcare vouchers from an employer. Universal Credit's childcare element pays up to 85% of costs, which for a family entitled to it is usually worth far more than a 20% top-up, so anyone on UC should not open a Tax-Free Childcare account without doing the sum, because opening one ends the UC childcare element. The 30 hours, by contrast, can be used alongside Universal Credit.

Childcare vouchers closed to new joiners in October 2018. A parent still in a voucher scheme can keep it or switch to Tax-Free Childcare, but not both, and the switch is one way.

5. A worked example in Exeter

Josh and Emily in Exeter both work full time, on £38,000 and £31,000, and their daughter Isla is 14 months old in a nursery charging £1,400 a month for a full-time place of 50 hours a week, 51 weeks a year. That is £16,800 a year. Both pass the earnings test easily and neither is near £100,000.

Tax-Free Childcare alone: the top-up on £1,400 a month would be £280, but the cap is £500 a quarter, so they get £500 every three months, £2,000 a year, on our arithmetic. Their bill falls to £14,800.

The 30 hours alone: Isla qualified from the term after she turned nine months, and the nursery funds 30 of her 50 weekly hours for 38 weeks. The nursery's own figures put the funded reduction at £5,700 a year, with a consumables charge of £8 a funded day, about £1,520 a year, on top. Net saving on our arithmetic: £4,180, taking the bill to £12,620. More than double what Tax-Free Childcare delivers, and it is why the hours come first.

Both together: the 30 hours take the bill to £12,620, which is £1,052 a month. They pay that through the Tax-Free Childcare account. The top-up on £1,052 a month is £210, £631 a quarter, which is above the £500 cap, so they get the capped £2,000 a year. Final bill on our arithmetic: £10,620, against £16,800 with nothing. The two schemes together cut it by £6,180, about 37%, and the cap on Tax-Free Childcare is the reason the number is not higher.

The cliff: if Josh took a promotion to £101,000, the household would lose both schemes and the bill would return to £16,800. A £63,000 pay rise would be worth £6,180 less than it looks, before tax. A pension contribution of £1,000 or more to bring his adjusted net income back under £100,000 would restore both. That is the sum to do before accepting the offer, not after.

6. The honest verdict

The 30 hours are worth thousands and Tax-Free Childcare is worth at most £2,000 a child a year. Claim the hours the term after nine months, then put the balance through the childcare account for the 20% top-up.

Both need each parent earning 16 hours at minimum wage and both vanish at £100,000 adjusted net income. The cliff can cost more than a pay rise is worth; a pension contribution can step you back under it.

Do not open a Tax-Free Childcare account if you get Universal Credit childcare without doing the sum first. The 85% UC element usually beats the 20% top-up, and opening the account ends it.

Frequently asked

Can I use Tax-Free Childcare and the 30 hours at the same time? Yes. The funded hours come off the bill first and whatever you still pay can go through the Tax-Free Childcare account for the 20% top-up, up to £500 a quarter per child.

What is the income limit? Neither scheme is available if you or your partner expects an adjusted net income over £100,000 in the current tax year. It is a cliff edge: £1 over removes both. Pension contributions reduce adjusted net income.

How much do I need to earn to qualify? Each parent must expect to earn at least 16 hours a week at the National Minimum or Living Wage on average over three months: £2,643.68 for someone 21 or over from April 2026. Parents on parental or sick leave count as working.

Can I get Tax-Free Childcare on Universal Credit? No. You cannot have Tax-Free Childcare alongside Universal Credit, tax credits or childcare vouchers. The 30 hours can be used with Universal Credit.
