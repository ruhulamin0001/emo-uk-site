Half Your Energy Bill Is Getting a Tax Cut This Winter and Half of It Is Not

From 1 October, VAT on domestic electricity in Great Britain falls from 5% to zero until the end of March. It is a real cut and it happens automatically. It also does not touch gas, does not apply in Northern Ireland, and does not last past 31 March 2027.

The short version

  • Domestic electricity in Great Britain is zero rated for VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. The measure was announced on 21 July 2026 and the policy paper was published on 8 September 2026.
  • VAT stays at 5% on all other domestic fuel across the whole UK. That means gas, and HMRC says so in one sentence.
  • Northern Ireland electricity stays at 5%. The Order applies to England, Wales and Scotland only.
  • Removing 5% VAT takes 4.76% off a VAT inclusive bill, not 5%. The arithmetic runs the other way round from how people expect.
  • You do not have to do anything. HMRC says the reduction is applied automatically by suppliers.
  • Whether you see the full saving depends on your supplier passing it on. HMRC's own impact note says exactly that.

1. What the measure actually says

The instrument is a temporary zero rate of VAT for qualifying supplies of electricity in Great Britain, running from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. It was announced on 21 July 2026 and HMRC published the policy paper on 8 September 2026.

Mechanically, the Order modifies two schedules of the Value Added Tax Act 1994. It restricts the 5% reduced rate in Schedule 7A so that it applies to electricity supplied in Northern Ireland only, and it inserts a new Group 24 into Schedule 8, headed Domestic Electricity: England and Wales and Scotland, at the zero rate. The description of what qualifies is unchanged, so the same supplies that got the 5% rate get the zero rate.

Ofgem has already folded this into the price cap. Its published list of what the cap is built from now includes the line VAT (5%) on gas, 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. There is no matching electricity VAT line for that window, because there is no electricity VAT for that window.

HMRC states the objective plainly: electricity is used more widely than any other fuel type, so cutting electricity bills reaches the greatest number of households. About 100 electricity suppliers have to change their systems on 1 October and change them back on 31 March.

2. What it does not do

Four things, and every one of them matters to somebody.

It does not touch gas. HMRC's wording is that the rate will remain 5% on all other types of domestic fuel UK-wide. If you heat with gas, the larger half of your winter energy bill is completely unaffected.

It does not apply in Northern Ireland. The Order applies to supplies made in Great Britain. Supplies in Northern Ireland continue at the reduced rate of 5% if they qualify. HMRC's impact note says the measure will have no impact on individuals, households and families in Northern Ireland.

It does not last. The zero rate ends on 31 March 2027. HMRC's own administrative impact section describes suppliers updating systems to go from 5% to 0% on 1 October, and updating them again to go back from 0% to 5% on 31 March 2027. It is written into the measure that it stops.

It does not guarantee you the money. HMRC's impact note says households benefit subject to the degree to which these businesses pass the VAT savings on to their customers. That is not a conspiracy theory, it is the government's own wording.

It also does not reduce the unit rate, the standing charge, or anything else in the cap. The cap is a separate mechanism and we have covered what the October 2026 cap does to a typical bill in its own guide.

3. The arithmetic, which is not 5%

This trips up almost everyone, so it is worth doing slowly.

Your electricity bill today already includes 5% VAT. That means the figure you see is 105% of the pre-VAT price. Removing the VAT does not take 5% off the figure you see, it takes the bill back to 100% of the pre-VAT price.

The saving is therefore 1 minus 1 divided by 1.05, which is 4.76% of what you currently pay, not 5%.

On £100 of VAT inclusive electricity, the VAT element is £4.76 and the pre-VAT cost is £95.24. Check it: 95.24 plus 5% is 100. That 0.24 percentage point difference sounds trivial, and on one bill it is. It is worth knowing because it is the difference between a promised saving and the one that arrives.

4. Worked example: Bernadette in Hull

Bernadette heats with gas and cooks with electricity, which is the normal arrangement in most British homes. Over the six months from October to March she expects to spend, at current VAT inclusive prices, roughly £680 on electricity and £520 on gas.

On the electricity. £680 multiplied by 4.76% is £32.38. That is what the zero rate is worth to her across the whole winter, assuming her supplier passes it on in full.

On the gas. Nothing. £520 with 5% VAT still in it, exactly as before. The VAT element in that £520 is £24.76, and she keeps paying it.

Her total saving across the winter is £32.38, on a combined energy spend of £1,200. That is 2.7% of what she actually pays to keep the house warm and the lights on.

That is a real £32 and she should not sniff at it. It is also not the number the headlines imply, and the reason is entirely that she heats with gas. A household that heats with electricity, on a heat pump or on storage heaters, gets a proportionally much larger cut, which is the opposite of how most energy support has been structured in recent years.

5. What to watch on your bill

  1. Check your October statement for the VAT line. It should show 0% on electricity if you are in England, Wales or Scotland. If it still shows 5%, that is a billing error worth raising.
  2. Do not expect a separate refund or credit. HMRC says the reduction is applied automatically and you will not need to do anything differently.
  3. Fixed tariffs are not excluded. The zero rate applies to qualifying supplies, not to a particular tariff type, so a fix does not lock you out of it.
  4. Diarise 1 April 2027. That is when 5% comes back on electricity. If you budget monthly by direct debit, the autumn 2027 review is where that reappears.
  5. If your direct debit does not move, that is not necessarily wrong. Direct debits are set on annual estimates and a 4.76% cut on part of your usage for six months may sit inside the existing estimate.

The honest verdict

This is a genuine tax cut, it is automatic, and nobody has to apply for anything. It is also narrower than the coverage suggests. It is electricity only, Great Britain only, six months only, and worth 4.76% of the electricity part of your bill rather than 5% of your energy bill.

For a gas heated home, which is most homes, the honest number is small. Around £32 across the winter on the usage in our example. If you were hoping this would meaningfully change what you pay to heat the house between October and March, it will not, and no amount of switching or tariff shopping changes that, because the gas half of the bill was never in scope.

Frequently asked

Does the VAT cut apply to gas as well as electricity? No. HMRC's policy paper says the rate will remain 5% on all other types of domestic fuel UK-wide. Only qualifying supplies of domestic electricity in Great Britain are zero rated.

Does it apply in Northern Ireland? No. The Order applies to supplies made in Great Britain only. Electricity supplied in Northern Ireland continues at the reduced rate of 5% if it qualifies.

Do I need to apply or tell my supplier? No. HMRC says the reduction will be applied automatically and households will not need to do anything differently.

When does the 5% come back? 1 April 2027. The zero rate has effect from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, and HMRC's impact note describes suppliers changing their systems back at the end of that period.
