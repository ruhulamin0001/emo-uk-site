Make Money

Same 50,000 Views. £100 in Gaming, £300 in Finance. The Subject Decides Almost Everything.

Search how much YouTubers earn and you get numbers from people with millions of subscribers, which is useless if you are starting from zero in a bedroom in Bolton. Here are the actual UK figures, what you have to hit before earning anything at all, and the honest hourly rate once you count the editing.

The short version

  • You earn nothing until you hit the threshold: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in the last 12 months. That is roughly 240,000 minutes of people actually watching.
  • Shorts are a separate route and a trap for the unwary. 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million Shorts views in 90 days, and Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hour threshold.
  • UK RPM runs £1.50 to £8 after YouTube's 45% cut. Finance, business and tech sit at the top. Gaming, entertainment and comedy sit near the bottom.
  • 50,000 monthly views is £100 a month in gaming and £300 in finance. Same effort, same audience size, triple the money, decided entirely by subject.
  • The honest hourly rate in year one is under minimum wage, often around £2 to £6 an hour once you count filming and editing.
  • Ad revenue is the entry level, not the business. Sponsorships, affiliate links and your own product are where working creators actually earn.
  • Past £1,000 of gross income you must register for Self Assessment, and your camera and microphone are deductible expenses.

1. What you must reach before earning a penny

To join the YouTube Partner Program and run ads you need 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 hours of watch time in the last 12 months. The watch time is the hard part. It means roughly 240,000 minutes of people actually watching, not clicking away.

RouteThresholdThe catch
Long-form video1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 monthsThe watch hours, not the subscribers, are what stop people
Shorts1,000 subscribers + 10 million Shorts views in 90 daysTen million. In ninety days. Read that twice

The detail that catches people out: Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hour threshold. If you post both, only your long videos build that number. Plenty of people spend a year making Shorts, gain subscribers, and discover they are no closer to monetising the normal way.

Most channels take six months to two years to get there, and many never do.

2. What UK views are actually worth

The number that matters is RPM, which is what you keep per 1,000 views after YouTube takes its 45% cut. Not CPM, which is what advertisers pay before the split, and which is the number people quote to make things sound better.

Finance, business, insurance£5 to £8Advertisers pay heavily to reach people about to spend money

Two channels with identical audiences can earn three times differently. Nothing about the effort changed. The advertisers bidding on the audience did.

Say you build a channel to 50,000 views a month, which is a genuine achievement and takes most people a year or more.

The work column assumes twelve videos a month, each taking four hours from filming to upload, which is a conservative estimate for anyone editing properly.

Set that against the National Living Wage of £12.71 from April 2026, which is the benchmark we apply to every side hustle on this site. All three fail it. The finance channel earns half of minimum wage on ad revenue alone, after a year of building.

That is not a reason to quit. It is a reason to understand what ad revenue is: a slow foundation, not the actual business. Anyone showing you a channel earning a living from ads alone is showing you the top fraction of a percent.

4. Where YouTube money really comes from

Ask any UK creator earning a living and ads will be a minority of their income.

  1. Sponsorships. Far better paying. A channel with 10,000 engaged subscribers in a specific niche can charge £200 to £800 for an integrated mention, and that is one video, not a month of them. Note "engaged" and "specific niche" are doing the work in that sentence.
  2. Affiliate links. They work quietly in the background. A tech or money channel linking products it genuinely uses can out-earn its own ad revenue. The rates and the rules are in our Amazon commission rates guide, and the disclosure obligations are not optional: the ASA and CMA require it upfront and in the same medium, so a spoken recommendation needs a spoken disclosure.
  3. Your own product. Courses, templates, printables, a service. The audience is already there and you keep almost everything.
  4. Memberships and Super Thanks. Small for most channels, meaningful for ones with an unusually loyal community.

This is the pattern across every side hustle on this site. Platform payouts are the entry level, and the real money is in what you build on top of the audience.

5. Choosing a subject with the money in mind

Two filters, and you need both.

Filter one: is the viewer close to spending money? Personal finance, software, business tools, home improvement, and anything involving expensive kit all attract higher-paying adverts. That is the RPM table above.

Filter two: could you still film it in month eighteen? Almost every channel that fails does so because the person got bored, not because the algorithm buried them. Eighteen months is roughly when ad revenue starts being worth having, so anything you would abandon before then earns nothing at all.

The overlap between those two, a subject with commercial value that you would happily discuss for free, is where a channel actually works. Picking a high-RPM subject you find dull is the most common expensive mistake, because you quit at month seven having earned £40.

6. Worked example: two channels, same effort

Both start in January. Both publish twelve videos a month.

Channel A: retro gaming. Reaches monetisation in month nine, which is fast. By month fourteen it does 50,000 views a month at an RPM of £2. Ad revenue £100 a month. Sponsors in this niche are mostly small, and affiliate opportunities are thin because the audience buys second-hand. Realistic total after eighteen months: £130 a month.

Channel B: UK small business finance. Grows more slowly and reaches monetisation in month thirteen, because the audience is smaller. By month eighteen it also does 50,000 views a month, at an RPM of £6. Ad revenue £300 a month. One sponsored integration a month from a software company at £400. Affiliate links to accounting tools and hosting add roughly £150. Realistic total: £850 a month.

Same videos published. Same hours. Six and a half times the income. Channel A took four months less to monetise and earns a fraction of what Channel B earns, because the difference was never the effort. It was who wanted to reach the audience.

The honest caveat: if the person running Channel A has no interest in small business finance, Channel B does not exist for them. They would have quit in month five. Enjoying it is not a soft consideration, it is the thing that gets you to month eighteen.

7. The tax side

YouTube pays you as a self-employed individual. Once your total income from it and any other side work passes the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, you must register for Self Assessment. That is gross income, not profit, and it is the mistake people make most often. Full detail in our side hustle tax guide.

Keep receipts for the camera, microphone, lighting, editing software and any props. Those are legitimate business expenses that reduce the profit you pay tax on. If you claim the £1,000 trading allowance you cannot also deduct expenses, so once your kit spending is meaningful, deducting properly wins.

One thing worth knowing: YouTube requires US tax information from creators outside the US, and without it withholding can be applied to your US-sourced earnings at a higher rate. The UK has a treaty that reduces it. Fill the form in when prompted rather than ignoring it.

Should you start?

Start if you want to build an audience and can accept earning nothing for the first year. That is the honest entry fee, and the asset you end up with is one nobody can take away.

Do not start if you want money within three months. For that, our remote jobs guide pays this month rather than next year, and the £12.71 test shows which side hustles clear minimum wage from the first hour.

If you do stick with it, pick the overlap: a subject advertisers pay for that you would talk about anyway. Then treat ad revenue as the least valuable thing the channel gives you, because for almost every working creator, it is.

Frequently asked

How many subscribers do I need to earn a living? The wrong question. A 10,000-subscriber channel in a commercial niche with sponsors and affiliate income out-earns a 200,000-subscriber entertainment channel on ads alone. Subscriber count is the vanity metric; RPM and what you sell alongside are the real ones.

Do Shorts pay anything? Pennies per thousand views. They are a discovery tool for growing an audience you then convert to long-form. Treating Shorts as an income stream is the most common miscalculation on the platform right now.

Does it matter where my viewers are? Considerably. RPM varies by country, and UK, US and Australian viewers are worth far more to advertisers than viewers in many other markets. A channel with a globally scattered audience earns less than the view count suggests.

Can I use AI voiceovers and stock footage? You can, and monetisation policies require content to be original and to add value rather than being mass-produced. Channels built on repackaged material get demonetised regularly. It is a fragile business model, not a shortcut.

How long until monetisation, realistically? Six months to two years for people publishing consistently, and a large share never reach it. Twelve videos a month for a year is the sort of commitment that gets there.
