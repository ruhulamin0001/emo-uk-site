The Leave Entitlement Created So Parents Do Not Spend Their Maternity Leave in a Hospital Corridor

Neonatal Care Leave gives eligible parents up to 12 weeks on top of their other family leave, and it applies from the first day of a job. Two tests decide whether you get any of it, and one of them is a hard cliff edge that turns on a single day.

The short version

  • It is additional leave, not leave taken out of your maternity or paternity entitlement. That was the whole point of the Act. Time your baby spends in neonatal care no longer eats the leave you were going to spend at home.
  • Leave is a day one right. There is no length of service test for the leave itself, so it applies even in your first week in a job.
  • Up to 12 weeks, accrued at one week for every 7 consecutive full days your baby spends in neonatal care.
  • Two gates decide eligibility: the baby must be admitted to neonatal care within 28 days of birth, and the care must last 7 full continuous days or more.
  • The baby must have been born on or after 6 April 2025, and you must be employed in England, Scotland or Wales.
  • Pay is GBP 194.32 a week for April 2026 to April 2027, or 90% of your average weekly earnings if that is lower. It was GBP 187.18 in 2025 to 2026.
  • Pay has extra tests that leave does not: 26 weeks of continuous service and average earnings of at least GBP 129 a week. You can qualify for the leave and not the pay.
  • Take 6 consecutive weeks of it and you gain extra redundancy protection. Leave must be used within 68 weeks of the birth.

1. Why this entitlement exists at all

Before April 2025 a parent whose baby was admitted to neonatal care had a cruel piece of arithmetic to do. Maternity or paternity leave started running from the birth whether the baby was at home or in an incubator. Weeks of leave meant for settling in at home were spent commuting to a hospital ward, and the leave ran out at roughly the point the baby finally came home.

The Neonatal Care (Leave and Pay) Act 2023, brought into force through regulations in 2025, fixes that by creating a separate additional entitlement. Neonatal Care Leave sits on top of maternity, paternity, adoption and shared parental leave rather than coming out of them. That is the single most important thing to understand about it, and it is the thing employers unfamiliar with the rules most often get wrong.

2. The two gates, and the cliff edge at six days

Gate one, the 28 day test. The baby must be admitted to neonatal care within 28 days of birth. A readmission at ten weeks old, however serious, does not open this entitlement.

Gate two, the seven day test. The care must last 7 full continuous days or more. This is the one to be careful about, because it is a genuine cliff rather than a sliding scale. Six days of neonatal care gives you nothing under this entitlement. Seven days gives you a week of leave. There is no partial award for five or six days, and no rounding in your favour.

Past that gate, leave accrues at one week for every 7 consecutive full days in care, up to 12 weeks. The arithmetic uses whole weeks, so a 20 day stay produces two weeks of leave rather than two and six sevenths. The remainder is simply lost.

Two further conditions: the baby must have been born on or after 6 April 2025, and you must be an employee in England, Scotland or Wales. Northern Ireland has its own employment law framework.

Six days of neonatal care entitles you to nothing. Seven entitles you to a week. Nobody designs a rule that way on purpose, but it is the rule, and it is worth knowing what is written on the discharge paperwork.

3. Leave is day one, pay is not

The leave is a day one right. No qualifying service at all. If your baby is admitted to neonatal care in your second week in a new job, you are entitled to the leave.

The pay is a different test, and this is where people are caught out. On top of qualifying for the leave, Statutory Neonatal Care Pay requires 26 weeks of continuous service completed before the relevant week, that you are still employed in the week before the pay period starts, and average earnings of at least GBP 129 a week over the 8 weeks before the relevant week.

So it is entirely possible to be entitled to 12 weeks of leave and none of the pay. That is not a loophole, it is how every statutory family payment in the UK works, but nobody expects it when the leave itself has no service test attached.

Where you do qualify, the rate for April 2026 to April 2027 is GBP 194.32 a week, or 90% of your average weekly earnings if that figure is lower. For the previous year it was GBP 187.18. Many employers top statutory family pay up under their own policies, so check your contract as well as the law.

4. What you keep while you are off

During neonatal care leave you are entitled to the benefit of all the terms and conditions of employment that would have applied if you had not been absent. Holiday continues to accrue, your pension arrangements continue, and your contractual benefits are not suspended.

If you return from neonatal care leave alone, you have the right to return to the same job.

And there is a threshold worth knowing about: take 6 consecutive weeks of neonatal care leave and you gain additional protection from redundancy during the protected period. That is a meaningful legal protection triggered by a specific number of weeks, and it is not something employers volunteer.

All of it has to be used within 68 weeks of the birth, which is generous but not unlimited.

Worked example: the Nowak family in Peterborough

Hannah gives birth on 4 May 2026. The baby is admitted to neonatal care on 6 May, two days old, and is discharged 23 days later. Her partner Tomasz started a new job three weeks before the birth.

Do they get through the gates? Admission at two days old is inside the 28 day window. The stay is 23 continuous days, comfortably past the seven day minimum. Both qualify for the leave.

How much leave? 23 days divided by 7 is 3 full weeks, with two days left over that do not count. So 3 weeks each, on top of Hannah maternity leave and Tomasz paternity leave.

Hannah pay. She has been with her employer four years and earns well above the threshold, so she qualifies for Statutory Neonatal Care Pay: 3 weeks at GBP 194.32, which is GBP 582.96.

Tomasz pay. Three weeks into the job, he fails the 26 week continuous service test. He is entitled to the 3 weeks of leave and none of the pay. Same baby, same ward, same entitlement to be there, nothing in the bank for it unless his employer tops it up voluntarily.

The version that pays nothing at all. Had the baby been discharged after 6 days instead of 23, neither of them would have qualified for a single day of leave or a penny of pay. One day either side of that line changes the whole answer.

What they should actually do: write down the admission date and the discharge date, and keep the hospital paperwork. Those two dates are the entire eligibility test, and an employer working this out for the first time will ask for them.

5. The honest verdict

This is a good law that fixes a real and specific injustice, and if your baby has been in neonatal care since April 2025 you should be claiming it. Up to 12 weeks of additional leave, at a day one right, is not a token.

Be clear-eyed about two things, though. The seven day gate is absolute, so a short stay that was every bit as frightening gets you nothing. And the split between leave and pay means a parent who has recently changed jobs may get all of the time and none of the money, which for many households is the difference between taking the leave and not being able to afford to.

The practical advice is unglamorous: keep the admission and discharge dates, tell your employer in writing early, and if they have never heard of this, send them the GOV.UK employer guide rather than arguing. Acas is free if it becomes a dispute. And check your own staff handbook, because a contractual top-up is where the real money is for anyone failing the earnings or service test.

Frequently asked

Does this come out of my maternity leave? No. It is a separate additional entitlement that sits on top of maternity, paternity, adoption and shared parental leave.

Can both parents take it at the same time? Both eligible parents have their own entitlement, so yes, subject to each meeting the conditions in their own employment.

What if my baby was born before 6 April 2025? The entitlement does not apply. The qualifying date is the baby date of birth, not the date the care happened.

What if the care was split into two separate stays? The test is 7 full continuous days, so how a stay is counted matters. Take the admission and discharge dates to Acas or to the GOV.UK guidance rather than assuming, because this is exactly the kind of detail the regulations pin down.
