The saving, the risk and the warranty are different at every point on that range. 1. What Apple's own refurbished store sells, and at what price

Apple runs a Certified Refurbished section of its UK online store, and on 5 September 2026 it listed iPhone 15, 15 Plus, 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max, 16, 16 Plus, 16 Pro and 16 Pro Max models. The prices that matter for a decision, taken from the store that day: the iPhone 16 128GB at £589, shown against £699, which is also the price Apple charges for a new iPhone 16 today; the iPhone 16 256GB at £669 against £799; the iPhone 15 128GB at £509 against £599; the iPhone 15 Plus 128GB at £599 against £699; and the iPhone 15 Pro 128GB at £679 against £919.

So the refurbished discount on Apple's own store runs from about 16% on a model Apple still sells new, to about 26% on a Pro model it no longer does. Note what is not there: the current iPhone 17 range. Apple's refurbished stock is typically a generation behind, because it is built from returns, exchanges and trade-ins that take months to accumulate. Part of the saving is the discount for buying last year's phone, which Apple applies to its new stock too.

What you get for the money is spelled out on Apple's page. A device with genuine Apple replacement parts fitted where needed, cleaned and inspected, and for iPhones specifically a new battery and a new outer shell. All accessories and cables, a brand new white box, free delivery and free returns, and Apple's standard one year limited warranty, with AppleCare available on top. In practical terms it is a new phone in a plain box.

2. The battery rule: the thing that separates the grades

A phone battery is a consumable. It loses capacity with every charge cycle, and a two year old iPhone that has been charged daily is typically somewhere around 80% to 85% of its original capacity, at which point Apple's own settings app starts suggesting a service. A refurbished phone with a tired battery is a phone that will need a battery replacement within a year or two, at the out-of-warranty fee Apple lists for that model on its support site, and that cost belongs in the price comparison.

This is why Apple's new battery and outer shell promise is the important line on its page, and why the first question to ask any other refurbisher is whether the battery is replaced, and if not, what battery health they guarantee. Reputable resellers state a minimum, often 80% or higher, and replace below it. Marketplace sellers usually say nothing, and the phone's own Battery Health screen, which you can only see after buying, is the only answer.

The second question is the outer shell. Apple replaces it, which is why its refurbished phones look new. Third-party grades describe the shell instead: pristine, excellent, good, fair, in various house names. The grade is cosmetic. It tells you about scratches, not about the battery, the screen's original status, or whether the parts inside are Apple's.

3. Third-party refurbishers and marketplace sellers

The established refurbishers, the ones with their own websites and warranties, sell the same models for less than Apple because they do less to them. A good-grade iPhone 16 from a reputable reseller will normally sit well below Apple's £589, with the gap depending on the day, the grade and the battery policy. We do not quote their prices here because they move constantly and we have not verified them on the day; check the listing and read three things before the price: the battery guarantee, the warranty length, and the returns window.

Your legal rights are the same either way. Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 goods sold by a business must be of satisfactory quality, and a fault that appears within the first six months is presumed to have been present at sale unless the seller proves otherwise. A refurbished phone described as good condition with a working battery has to be that. The rights are against the seller, so a UK business with a returns address is worth more than a cheaper listing from a seller you cannot trace.

Private sales on marketplaces are different. The Consumer Rights Act does not apply to a private seller, the phone can be locked to a network or to a previous owner's Apple ID, and the money is gone if the description was wrong. Apple's Activation Lock check and a network status check take minutes and are the minimum before paying anyone privately. Our budget smartphone guide covers the checks in more detail.

4. The saving over the life of the phone

Take the iPhone 16 128GB. New from Apple, £699. Apple refurbished, £589. The £110 saving on a phone kept for four years is £27.50 a year, or £2.29 a month on our arithmetic, for a device that is new in every part that wears. That is a small but certain saving, with no offsetting risk.

Now the reseller route. Suppose a good-grade iPhone 16 with an 80% battery guarantee and a 12 month warranty is £200 below Apple's refurbished price, so about £389. The saving against new is £310, or £6.46 a month over four years. If the battery lasts, that is the better deal by a distance. If it needs replacing after 18 months, deduct Apple's out-of-warranty battery fee for the model, which Apple publishes on its support site and which we have not quoted here because it changes. On our arithmetic the reseller route stays ahead at any fee under the £310 saving, but the margin is the battery, and the year in which it fails is the year the saving is spent.

The other lever is the model. Apple's refurbished iPhone 15 at £509 is £80 less than its refurbished 16, and the practical difference for most people is small. A buyer who does not need the newest chip saves more by stepping back a generation than by choosing refurbished over new in the same generation. Combine the two, an older model and Apple's refurbished price, and the saving against a new current model runs into the hundreds while the warranty and battery stay new.

5. A worked example in Reading

Kwame in Reading has an iPhone 12 with a battery at 76% and a cracked back, and wants a phone that will last four years. He is not fussed about the newest camera. Three options on 5 September 2026: a new iPhone 16 at £699, an Apple refurbished iPhone 16 at £589, or an Apple refurbished iPhone 15 at £509. All three come with a new battery and a one year warranty.

On our arithmetic the refurbished 15 saves £190 against the new 16, which over four years is £3.96 a month. The refurbished 16 saves £110. He also prices a good-grade iPhone 15 from a reseller with an 85% battery guarantee and a 12 month warranty at £380, £129 below Apple's refurbished 15. That is the cheapest, and it is a reasonable buy, but he weighs it against the 85% battery: on a four year plan he expects to replace that battery once, at whatever Apple's support site quotes for an iPhone 15 battery on the day, and that fee closes much of the £129 gap. He takes Apple's refurbished 15 at £509 for the new battery and the simplicity, and puts the £190 saved against the new 16 towards a case and AppleCare.

Had he wanted the phone for two years rather than four, the reseller's £380 unit would have been the better buy, because the battery would probably have lasted the term. The right answer moved with the ownership period, not with the phone.

6. The honest verdict

Apple refurbished is a new phone in a plain box: new battery, new shell, one year warranty, free returns, and on 5 September 2026 £110 less than a new iPhone 16 and £240 less than a new one if you step back to the 15.

A reputable reseller beats Apple on price by £100 to £200 on the same model, and the battery guarantee is what you are paying less for. Read that line, the warranty and the returns window before the grade.

Buy new only for the current model or a specific feature. For everything else the question is not refurbished or new; it is which battery promise you are buying, and for how many years.

Frequently asked

Does a refurbished iPhone from Apple have a new battery? Yes. Apple's UK refurbished page says refurbished iOS devices come with a new battery and outer shell, genuine replacement parts where needed, all accessories, a new box and the standard one year warranty.

How much cheaper is a refurbished iPhone than new? On Apple's own UK store on 5 September 2026, about 16% on a model still sold new (iPhone 16, £589 against £699) and about 26% on a discontinued Pro model (iPhone 15 Pro, £679 against £919). Third-party resellers are cheaper again, with different battery and warranty terms.

Can I get the newest iPhone refurbished? Usually not for months after launch. Apple's refurbished stock is built from returns and trade-ins and was a generation behind when we checked.

What are my rights if a refurbished phone is faulty? Bought from a business, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 applies: satisfactory quality, and a fault in the first six months is presumed present at sale. Bought privately, those rights do not apply, so check Activation Lock and network status before paying.
