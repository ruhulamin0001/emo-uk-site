Declutter and Sell: Turn Your Clutter Into Real Money

21 July 2026 · 5 min read · Checked July 2026

The short version
  • Vinted charges UK sellers nothing. The 5% plus 70p Buyer Protection fee is paid by the buyer at checkout and does not come out of your payout.
  • eBay private sellers pay no selling fees either, since October 2024. No final value fee, no order fee. The buyer pays a protection fee instead. Business sellers still pay 6.9% to 14.9% plus a per order fee and VAT.
  • Facebook Marketplace is free for local collection and charges 2% on shipped items in the UK.
  • So the platform fee is almost never the thing that decides your profit. Postage and your own time are.
  • The £1,000 trading allowance only matters if you are buying to resell. Selling your own used possessions is not trading and is not taxable, however much you sell.

1. The tax question, answered properly

This causes more confusion than anything else, largely because of headlines about HMRC and online platforms. Here is the actual position.

Selling your own used possessions is not trading. If you clear out your wardrobe, your loft and your kids' outgrown things, that is not income and it is not taxable, no matter how much it comes to. You are selling things you already owned, usually for less than you paid.

Buying things in order to resell them is trading. That is where the £1,000 trading allowance applies. Above £1,000 of trading income in a tax year you must register for Self Assessment.

Since January 2024, platforms including Vinted, eBay and Etsy report seller data to HMRC where a seller passes 30 sales or about £1,700 in a year. That is a reporting threshold, not a tax threshold. Being reported does not mean you owe anything. If you were selling your own belongings, the answer to any HMRC letter is simply that. HMRC now holds a second year of platform data and has started writing to sellers; what a nudge letter actually means, and how to answer it, is covered in its own guide.

The one genuine exception: a single personal item sold for over £6,000 can attract Capital Gains Tax. That covers jewellery, art and antiques, not a used sofa.

4. What is worth listing and what is not

Be honest about the hourly rate. A listing takes roughly eight to twelve minutes to photograph, write, list, answer questions on, package and post.

Resale valueWorth listing individually?
Under £8No. Bundle it or donate it
£8 to £20Only if it posts cheaply and sells fast
£20 to £60Yes, this is the sweet spot
Over £60Yes, and take proper photographs

Bundling is the answer to the low value pile. Ten baby vests sell for £12 as a bundle and are unsellable individually. Same listing time, ten times the value.

Things that consistently sell better than people expect: branded trainers even worn, Lego by weight, cast iron cookware, power tools, vintage Pyrex, board games, printer cartridges, curtains, and phone chargers in bundles.

Things that consistently do not sell: mass market paperbacks, DVDs, old flat screen televisions, mattresses, and anything with a safety marking removed. Donate or recycle those and stop spending time on them.

6. Photos and listings that sell

  • Natural light, plain background. A window and a white duvet beats any lighting kit.
  • Photograph the flaws. The bobbling, the scuff, the missing button. Buyers who see the flaw and buy anyway do not open disputes. Buyers who discover it do.
  • Put the brand and size in the title. That is what people search. "Nike Air Max 90 UK 8 white" beats "lovely trainers".
  • Measure it. Chest, waist, length in centimetres. Sizing varies wildly and measurements prevent most returns.
  • List in the evening. Roughly 7pm to 10pm is when people browse.
  • Relist rather than reduce, at first. Vinted in particular rewards fresh listings in the feed.

7. Postage, the profit killer

With seller fees at zero, postage is now the main thing between you and your money.

  • Know your parcel sizes. Royal Mail Large Letter is dramatically cheaper than Small Parcel. A jumper vacuum squashed into a large letter saves several pounds every single time.
  • Use a courier comparison site for anything above 2kg. The difference between the counter price and a booked collection is often 40%.
  • Reuse packaging. Buyers do not care and it is free.
  • Always get proof of postage. It is free and it is your entire defence in a not received claim.
  • Weigh before you list. Guessing the weight and being wrong turns a £12 sale into a £3 one.

A cheap kitchen scale and a tape measure are the only equipment worth buying, and you probably own both.

Myths that cost people money

"HMRC will tax my Vinted sales." Not if you are selling your own used possessions. Platforms report seller data above 30 sales or about £1,700, but reporting is not taxation. Trading is taxed. Decluttering is not.

"eBay takes a huge cut." It did. For private sellers it now takes nothing on the first 300 listings a month, and the buyer pays a protection fee instead. A lot of advice online has not caught up. The full split, and the point at which HMRC or eBay would treat you as a business seller, is in our eBay private versus business seller fees guide.

"I will get more if I wait for the right buyer." Occasionally true for genuinely rare items. Usually it means the item sits in a bag for a year and you sell it for less. Price it to move.

The honest verdict

This is worth doing if you have a genuine backlog of unused things. £300 to £700 from a proper household clearout is realistic, it is tax free, and the fee changes mean you keep essentially all of it.

This is not a side hustle. It is a one off, because you run out of clutter. If you want it to become recurring income you have to start sourcing stock, and at that point it becomes trading, it becomes taxable, and it becomes a different job with a different skill set.

Nothing to buy here. No app, no course, no subscription. Scales, a tape measure and some reused boxes.

Frequently asked

Do I need to tell HMRC anything? Not for selling your own belongings. If you are buying to resell and your trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of that tax year. This is general information, not tax advice.

What if a buyer says the item never arrived? Proof of postage is your defence, which is why it matters that it is free. Without it you will usually lose the claim.

Is it worth using several platforms at once? Yes, but list each item in one place at a time. Selling the same item twice is the fastest way to a negative rating.

What should I do with what does not sell? Charity shop, or a local Buy Nothing group. A second relisting round almost never works, and the storage cost of keeping it is real even if it is not financial.
