Best AI Tools for a UK Small Business: What Is Worth Paying For

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

The short version
  • For most UK sole traders and micro businesses, one general AI subscription at around £16 to £20 a month is enough. Everything else should be free until you can name the hour it saves.
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot is £16.10 per user per month on an annual commitment, on top of an existing Microsoft 365 licence. It is only worth it if you already live in Outlook, Teams and Excel.
  • ChatGPT Business is about $25 per user per month, roughly $20 billed annually, with a two seat minimum. Claude Team is about $20 per seat.
  • Canva Pro is $18 a month, Canva Business $25 per user. The free tier is genuinely usable and most small businesses never need to upgrade.
  • The honest test: does it save you an hour a month? At a £30 hourly value, a £20 tool must save 40 minutes to break even. Most do not.

1. The only test that matters

Every AI tool sounds essential on its own landing page. Here is the arithmetic that cuts through it.

Work out what an hour of your time is worth. If you charge £40 an hour, an hour is worth £40. Then ask how many minutes a month the tool genuinely saves you.

Tool cost per monthMinutes it must save to break even at £30/hr
£1020 minutes
£2040 minutes
£501 hour 40
£1505 hours

A general assistant that drafts your emails and first drafts clears 40 minutes easily. A niche £50 a month tool that generates social captions almost certainly does not, because writing a caption takes four minutes and you will spend three editing what it produced.

Run the free tier for a month and count. Actually count. Most people upgrade on a feeling and then forget the subscription exists, which is how a small business ends up with £180 a month of software it does not use.

Pick one. They overlap enormously and paying for three is the most common waste in this category.

The Copilot decision is different from the others because of the stacking. It sits on top of a Microsoft 365 licence, so the real monthly cost per person is the Copilot fee plus your existing Business Standard or Premium seat. Compare that total against a standalone assistant before deciding.

3. By job: what actually helps

Writing and email. This is where the time genuinely goes. A general assistant drafting quotes, follow ups, listings and first drafts saves real hours. Do not publish anything unedited. It reads like it, customers notice, and Google is explicit that unedited mass produced content is a spam signal.

Bookkeeping. The AI features inside FreeAgent, Xero and QuickBooks that auto categorise transactions and match receipts are the highest value AI most small businesses will use, and they come bundled with software you already need. No separate subscription.

Design. Canva. The free tier does more than most small businesses need. Pro at $18 a month is worth it if you make graphics weekly and want brand kit, background removal and resizing.

Meeting notes. Genuinely useful if you do several calls a week, worthless if you do two a month. Several tools offer free tiers with a monthly transcription limit. Start there.

Customer support. A chatbot on a small site usually annoys more people than it helps. A well written FAQ page costs nothing and answers the same questions.

Images. Fine for backgrounds, mockups and abstract graphics. Bad for anything a customer will think is a real photograph of your actual product or premises, which is a trading standards problem, not a taste one.

4. What to use free, probably forever

  • Canva free for design
  • The free tier of one general assistant for drafting
  • Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 basics you already pay for
  • Google Business Profile for local visibility, free and undervalued
  • Wave or the free tier of a bookkeeping tool if you are very small

6. Data protection, the part people skip

If you put customer information into an AI tool, you are processing personal data and UK GDPR applies to you.

  • Do not paste customer names, addresses, health information or payment details into a general chat tool. Anonymise first. "Draft a reply to a customer whose delivery is late" needs no names.
  • Check whether your data trains the model. Business and team tiers generally exclude your data from training. Free consumer tiers often do not. This is the strongest argument for paying if you handle client data.
  • Update your privacy notice if AI processing is part of how you handle customer data.
  • Watch where the data is processed. International transfers have rules, and your customers may reasonably ask.
  • You are responsible for the output. If an AI drafted quote contains a wrong price, that is your quote and potentially your contract.

The ICO has clear small business guidance on this and it is free to read. This is general information, not legal advice.

7. Where AI is genuinely bad and costs you money

Being honest about this is more useful than another list of tools.

  • Anything requiring current, specific facts. Prices, legislation, tax thresholds, opening hours. It will produce something plausible and wrong, and confidently.
  • Legal documents. A generated contract that misses a clause costs far more than a solicitor would have.
  • Anything numerical you have not checked. Quotes, measurements, dosages, tax calculations.
  • Bulk website content. Google's scaled content abuse policy targets exactly this. Publishing fifty generated pages is an active ranking risk, not a shortcut.
  • Your actual expertise. If you are a kitchen fitter, the thing that wins you work is that you know what goes wrong in a 1930s semi. No model has that, and outsourcing your voice to one removes the reason people hire you.

Myths that cost people money

"I need AI or I will fall behind." This is marketing. The businesses winning in 2026 are the ones answering the phone, quoting quickly and turning up when they said they would. AI helps at the edges of that. It does not replace it.

"The expensive tier must be better." For small business use the free and entry tiers are very capable. The expensive tiers buy seats, admin controls and compliance features, which matter at fifty people and not at two.

"I should use several so I get the best of each." The overlap is enormous and the switching cost is real. One, used properly, beats three used shallowly.

The honest verdict

Buy one general assistant, around £16 to £20 a month, and only if you write a lot. If you already live in Microsoft 365, Copilot at £16.10 per user makes sense. If you do not, a standalone assistant is cheaper because you are not stacking it on a licence.

Use Canva free until you genuinely need brand kit and background removal weekly.

Cancel everything else that you cannot point at and say what hour it saved you this month.

The realistic outcome here is not new revenue. It is two or three hours a week back on admin, and a subscription bill that is smaller than it was. Both are worth having, and neither is what the marketing promises.

Frequently asked

Is the free tier good enough? For most sole traders, yes. Upgrade when you hit a limit that actually blocks you, not before.

Can I claim AI subscriptions as a business expense? Yes, where they are used wholly and exclusively for the business. Keep the invoices. General information, not tax advice.

Will AI written content hurt my website? Unedited, mass produced content will. Google targets scaled content abuse, not the use of a tool. Content you have edited, fact checked and added your own experience to is fine.

What if the price changes? It will. All the figures here were checked in August 2026 and this category reprices constantly. Confirm on the vendor site before you commit annually.
