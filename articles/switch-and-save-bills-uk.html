One in Four UK Homes Is Out of Contract, Paying £7 to £9 a Month for Nothing

The loyalty penalty is not a metaphor. It is a specific, measurable amount that providers charge customers who did not do anything when their contract ended. Two rule changes in the last two years have made collecting it back easier than at any point before.

1. What the loyalty penalty actually costs

When a broadband contract ends, most providers move you onto a standard price that is higher than any deal they advertise to new customers. Nothing about your service changes. You are simply charged more for having stayed.

Ofcom's pricing research puts 28% of customers out of contract, and roughly one in four broadband customers paying £7 to £9 a month more than in contract customers for the same product. That is £84 to £108 a year for doing nothing, and it is the cheapest money you will ever recover.

The full switching gap is larger, because you are not just returning to your old price, you are moving to the best available one. Households moving from an out of contract deal to a market leading like for like package save £180 to £292 a year. Published averages for people who actively review sit at about £250 a year, with one provider's own subscriber data showing £162.

Nothing about your broadband changes when your contract ends. Only the price does. That is the entire mechanism, and it works because most people never look.

2. Two rule changes that made switching easier

If you last tried this a few years ago, two things have changed and both are in your favour.

One Touch Switch, from September 2024. You contact the new provider only. They tell your existing provider, end that contract and arrange the move. No retention call, no being transferred four times, no risk of being talked out of it. This removed the single biggest reason people did not switch.

The ban on percentage price rises, from 17 January 2025. Ofcom has banned inflation linked and percentage based mid contract increases on new contracts. Any in contract rise must now be set out upfront in pounds and pence.

The second one matters more than it sounds. Previously a two year contract advertised at £26 a month could rise twice by an unknown amount, so the advertised price was not a real comparison. Now the total cost across the contract is knowable at the point you sign, and comparing two deals is arithmetic rather than guesswork.

3. Broadband, the biggest single win

Do this one first because it has the largest saving for the least effort.

  1. Find out whether you are in contract. Check your account online or your last bill. If you are out of contract you can leave immediately with no penalty.
  2. Find your actual speed need, not your current speed. Most households are on more than they use. A family streaming, gaming and working from home is comfortable well below the top tiers, and dropping a tier is often the same saving as switching.
  3. Check what is available at your postcode. Full fibre coverage has expanded quickly and altnet providers are frequently cheaper than the large brands for faster service.
  4. Compare total contract cost, not the monthly headline. Monthly price times contract length, plus setup, minus any cashback or voucher. The now mandatory pounds and pence rise statement goes into this sum.
  5. Sign up with the new provider and stop. One Touch Switch handles the rest.

If you genuinely want to stay, ring and say you are leaving and have a specific competitor price ready. Retention offers exist and are often good. But treat that as plan B, because the advertised new customer deal elsewhere is usually still better.

4. Energy, and why the savings are smaller now

Energy switching is worth doing and it is not what it was. Before 2021 the gap between a standard variable tariff and the cheapest fixed deal could be £300 or more. The price cap now limits how far a standard tariff can drift, which compresses the range.

A realistic planning figure today is around £90 to £250 a year, depending on your usage, your region and what fixed deals are available at the time.

What to check, in order:

  • Are you on a standard variable tariff? If yes, you are on the capped rate, which is the default rather than a good deal.
  • Is there an exit fee? Fixed tariffs usually charge one. Compare the saving against the fee before moving early.
  • Does the fixed deal beat the cap over its whole term? A fix is a bet on where the cap goes. Fixing slightly above the current cap can still be the right call if you value certainty, but call it insurance rather than a saving.
  • Do you have a working smart meter? Accurate readings prevent the estimated bills that make comparison meaningless.
  • Are you on the right payment method? Direct debit is usually cheaper than on receipt of bill.

Two things that are not switching but pay more than switching: turning your thermostat down 1°C, usually around 10% off heating, and draught proofing, which is unglamorous and has the best return per pound in most homes.

5. The order to do this in, one evening

All of this fits in about two hours. Work down in order of saving per minute spent.

The mobile one catches almost everyone. If you bought a phone on a two year contract and never changed anything, you are probably still paying the handset portion of a phone you finished paying for. Check the contract end date before anything else.

Insurance auto renewal is the other big one. Insurers cannot charge existing customers more than a new customer for an equivalent policy, but the quoted renewal is still frequently beatable. Never accept the first renewal figure without comparing.

6. A worked household audit

The Osborne family, Derby. Two adults, two children. One evening, kitchen table, laptop.

Broadband. Out of contract for fourteen months at £41 a month on a 67Mb package. A local full fibre provider offers 150Mb at £26 with the in contract rise stated as £2 in month 13. Saving £180 in year one. Switch done in one form, One Touch Switch handled the rest.

Mobile, adult one. £38 a month, handset paid off eleven months ago. SIM only equivalent £12. Saving £312 a year. This was the largest single number in the whole audit and they had not noticed it.

Mobile, adult two. Still in contract with nine months left. Diarised, no action.

Energy. On the capped standard variable. A fixed deal comes out about £110 a year cheaper on their usage. They take it, noting it is a bet on the cap. Saving about £110.

Car insurance. Renewal quoted at £612. Comparison and a call to the existing insurer brings it to £498. Saving £114.

Direct debits. Three unused subscriptions totalling £31 a month. Saving £372 a year.

Evening total: £1,088 a year, for about two hours of work. That is roughly £544 an hour, which is more than any side hustle in this section of the site, and it repeats every year they do it.

7. Traps in switching

  • Comparing monthly price instead of total contract cost. A £24 deal over 24 months with a £30 setup fee costs more than a £25 deal with none.
  • Cashback you have to claim. Some switching cashback requires a claim in a narrow window months later. Diarise it or treat the cashback as zero.
  • Buying more speed than you use. The upsell to gigabit is the most common way a switch saves less than it should.
  • Forgetting the next contract end date. Everything in this article happens again in 18 or 24 months. Put it in your calendar the day you sign, and this becomes a recurring saving rather than a one off.
  • Cancelling a direct debit without cancelling the contract. The contract continues and you still owe the money, which can reach collections.
  • Assuming energy switching still saves hundreds. It does not, and expecting it to leads people to skip the broadband and mobile checks that do.

The honest verdict

Switching bills is the highest paid two hours available to most UK households, and the two rule changes have removed the friction that used to make it unpleasant. One Touch Switch means no retention call. The pounds and pence rule means the comparison is real.

Start with broadband if you are out of contract, then your mobile if the handset is paid off, then insurance at renewal, then energy, then your direct debits. That order puts the largest savings first in case you run out of patience.

And put the contract end dates in your calendar. The difference between people who save this money once and people who save it every year is a reminder, not a skill.

Frequently asked

Will switching broadband leave me without internet? Under One Touch Switch the changeover is coordinated between providers and is usually same day. Full fibre installations where none exists may need an engineer, which the provider arranges.

Does switching hurt my credit score? Broadband and energy switches generally involve a soft check. Anything with a handset or credit agreement, such as a phone contract, is a credit application and does show.

Can my provider stop me leaving? Not if you are out of contract. They will offer you a deal, and you are free to take it or leave. If you are in contract, check the early termination fee against your saving.

Is it worth switching energy at all now? Usually yes, at £90 to £250 a year, but check exit fees and be clear that a fixed tariff is certainty rather than a guaranteed saving.
