UK Sign-Up Bonuses and Refer-a-Friend: The Honest Money Maths

16 August 2026 · 7 min read · Checked August 2026

You have probably seen the posts. Earn a thousand pounds a month just by opening a few apps. The truth is more useful and less dramatic. Sign-up bonuses and refer-a-friend schemes are real UK money, they are not a scam, and most people leave them on the table. But the honest maths looks nothing like the hype, so here is exactly how it works, what you can really earn, and where the catches are.

Why companies pay you at all

Banks, payment apps and cashback sites spend a fortune winning customers. They can hand that money to Google and Facebook for adverts, or they can hand some of it to you for bringing a friend or opening an account. To them the cost is similar. The only question is whether the money goes to an ad platform or to you. That is the whole idea, and it is why these offers exist.

The honest maths: one-off versus repeatable

This is the part the hype skips. Almost every account sign-up bonus is paid once. You open the account, meet the condition, take the bonus, and that offer is done. So doing all of them once might realistically bring you somewhere in the region of a few hundred pounds, not a few hundred a month.

The repeating money comes from two places. First, cashback on spending you would do anyway. Second, referring other people, where you earn each time someone signs up through your link. That second one is real, but be honest with yourself: it only becomes monthly income if you have an audience or a lot of friends who genuinely want the product. No audience means no recurring referral income, however good the links are.

The one-off bonuses worth knowing

Bonus amounts and the tasks needed change constantly, so always check the current offer on the provider's own page before you start. As a rough guide for 2026, these are the common UK ones.   • Tide business account (referral code 4V9B3E) — around £100, aimed at sole traders and small businesses, usually needs a business account and a first transaction.
  • Revolut (referral code ruhul00) — commonly £80 to £250 depending on the current promotion, for new users completing set tasks.
  • Monzo — around £10 for new customers who open and use the account.
  • PayPal — around £10 after a qualifying first payment.

A quick word on Klarna. It sometimes offers a small joining reward, but Klarna is a buy-now-pay-later service, which is a form of borrowing. Only use it if you would spend the money anyway and can clear it in full. Do not take on debt to chase a ten pound bonus. That is how these schemes stop being free money.

The genuinely repeatable part: cashback

Cashback is the piece that keeps paying, because it rewards spending you were going to do regardless. You click through the cashback site or app first, then shop as normal, and a percentage comes back to you.

  • TopCashback — free to join, pays cashback on shopping, insurance, broadband and switching. Genuinely useful because you were buying those things anyway.
  • JamDoughnut (referral code YVQD) — instant cashback on gift cards for everyday shops, which suits regular supermarket or fuel spending.

Stacked sensibly, cashback on your normal weekly spending can quietly return a modest amount every month, with no extra work once it is set up. It will not make you rich, but it is close to the only truly passive part of this whole exercise.

The catches nobody mentions

  1. Most conditions require a deposit, a spend, or keeping the account open for a set time. Read them before you start, not after.
  2. Some of these are financial products with eligibility or credit checks. Opening many accounts in a short window can show up on your record.
  3. Offers and amounts change without notice. The figure you saw last month may be different today.
  4. Do not open accounts you will never use just for a tenner. Dormant accounts and forgotten cards are their own small risk.

The tax bit

Straightforward bank switch and sign-up bonuses, and cashback on your own spending, are generally not taxable. Referral income is different. Once you are actively earning from referring others and your total side income passes £1,000 in a tax year, that becomes taxable and you may need to register, as set out in our side hustle tax guide. Keep a simple record from the start.

The honest bottom line

Sign-up bonuses and cashback are real UK money that most people ignore, and clearing the sensible ones once can put a few hundred pounds in your pocket over a few weeks. Turning it into a monthly figure depends on referrals, which depends on reach, which takes time to build. Treat it as a smart one-off top-up plus a small ongoing cashback habit, do it without taking on debt, and it is a genuinely worthwhile use of an afternoon. Just ignore anyone promising thousands a month for nothing.
