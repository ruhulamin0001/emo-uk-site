Sign-Up Bonuses Are Real Money. The "£1,000 a Month" Version Needs Something You Probably Do Not Have.

You have seen the posts. Earn a thousand pounds a month just by opening a few apps. The truth is more useful and less dramatic: this is a few hundred pounds once, plus a small ongoing cashback habit, and the difference between those two things is the entire article.

The short version

  • Almost every account sign-up bonus is paid once. Clearing the sensible ones might realistically bring a few hundred pounds over a few weeks. That is a one-off, not an income.
  • Only two things genuinely repeat: cashback on spending you were doing anyway, and referrals, which need an audience.
  • No audience means no recurring referral income, however good the links are. This is the part the "£1,000 a month" posts leave out, and it is the whole difference.
  • Bank switch bonuses are the biggest single items, and the Current Account Switch Service moves everything within seven working days with a guarantee behind it.

1. Why companies pay you at all

Banks, payment apps and cashback sites spend a fortune winning customers. They can hand that money to Google and Meta for adverts, or they can hand some of it to you for opening an account or bringing a friend. To them the cost is similar. The only question is whether the money goes to an ad platform or to you.

That is the whole idea, and it explains both why these offers exist and why they are conditional. The company is buying a customer, so the conditions are designed to make sure they got one: a deposit, a spend, a direct debit, or a period of keeping the account open.

Doing all of the sign-up bonuses once might bring a few hundred pounds. Doing them monthly is not a thing, because you can only open each account once.

The repeating money comes from two places. First, cashback on spending you would do anyway. Second, referring other people, where you earn each time someone signs up through your link.

That second one is real, and be honest with yourself about it: it only becomes monthly income if you have an audience or a great many friends who genuinely want the product. The people posting screenshots of £1,000 months are earning from referrals, which means they are earning from their audience, which means the thing being sold to you is a business model that requires the thing they already have and you do not. 3. Bank switching, the biggest single item

Current account switch bonuses are usually the largest amounts available, and they are the least understood.

The Current Account Switch Service moves your balance, your direct debits and your standing orders to the new bank within seven working days, closes the old account, and redirects payments. It carries a guarantee: if anything goes wrong, the banks refund any charges or interest you incur as a result.

Typical conditions: pay in a minimum amount, set up a number of direct debits, use the debit card a few times, and keep the account open for a set period. Read those before you start rather than after, because a bonus you fail to qualify for cost you the switch and paid you nothing.

Two practical points. Switching offers appear and disappear constantly, so check the current market rather than any list written months ago. And if you are about to apply for a mortgage, opening and closing accounts in the run-up is not ideal, so time it accordingly.

5. Cashback, the part that actually repeats

Cashback is the piece that keeps paying, because it rewards spending you were going to do regardless. You click through the cashback site first, then shop as normal, and a percentage comes back.

  • TopCashback — free to join, pays on shopping, insurance, broadband and switching. Genuinely useful because you were buying those things anyway, and the switching category pairs well with our bills switching guide.
  • JamDoughnut (code YVQD) — instant cashback on gift cards for everyday shops, which suits regular supermarket or fuel spending.

Stacked sensibly, cashback on your normal weekly spending returns a modest amount every month with no extra work once it is set up. It will not make you rich, and it is close to the only truly passive part of this exercise.

Two habits that decide whether it works: click through before you buy, every time, because a purchase made directly earns nothing; and do not buy things to earn cashback, because 5% back on something you did not need is a 95% loss.

6. The catches nobody mentions

  1. Conditions come first, bonus comes later. Most require a deposit, a spend, direct debits, or keeping the account open for months. Read them before you start.
  2. Some are financial products with credit checks. Opening several accounts in a short window leaves a pattern on your file. Space them out, and avoid the months before a mortgage application.
  3. Offers change without notice. The figure you saw last month may be different today. Nothing in the table above should be treated as a quote.
  4. Do not open accounts you will never use. Dormant accounts and forgotten cards are a small security risk and a source of the exact forgotten direct debits our no-spend guide is about.
  5. Cashback is not instant. It typically tracks in days, confirms in weeks, and pays after a holding period that can run to months. Treat it as delayed, not as income.
  6. Missed tracking happens. Ad blockers and cookie settings can stop a click registering. Most sites have a missing-cashback claim process, and it is worth using.

7. Worked example: a realistic six weeks

What "doing all of them" actually looks like

Week one. Opens two app accounts with joining rewards and completes the qualifying tasks. Sets a reminder for the conditions that take 30 days. Time: about an hour. Pending: modest two-figure amounts.

Week two. Switches their main current account for a switch bonus. Seven working days, handled by the Switch Service, direct debits move automatically. Time: twenty minutes. The largest single item of the whole exercise.

Weeks three to six. Joins a cashback site and installs the reminder extension. Buys nothing extra, just routes the shopping they were already doing through it. Renews car insurance through the cashback link, which is the single best-paying category.

The realistic total: a few hundred pounds over about six weeks, most of it from the bank switch, arriving in stages rather than at once.

Then what? Month two produces almost nothing, because the accounts are open and cannot be opened again. Ongoing cashback continues quietly at a small monthly figure. That drop is not a failure. It is what a one-off looks like from the inside, and it is precisely the moment the "£1,000 a month" posts do not show you.

8. The tax bit

Straightforward bank switch and sign-up bonuses, and cashback on your own spending, are generally not taxable, because they are treated as a discount or an inducement rather than income.

Referral income is different. Once you are actively earning from referring others and your total side income passes £1,000 gross in a tax year, that is trading income and you may need to register for Self Assessment. Note it is gross, across all your side activities combined, as set out in our side hustle tax guide.

Keep a simple record from the start: date, source, amount. If referrals ever become meaningful, reconstructing it later is miserable. General information, not tax advice.

The honest bottom line

Sign-up bonuses and cashback are real UK money that most people ignore, and clearing the sensible ones once can put a few hundred pounds in your pocket over a few weeks. The bank switch is usually the biggest single item and takes twenty minutes.

Turning it into a monthly figure depends on referrals, which depends on reach, which takes years to build. Treat this as a smart one-off top-up plus a small ongoing cashback habit, do it without taking on any debt, and it is a genuinely worthwhile use of an afternoon.

Just ignore anyone promising thousands a month for nothing. They are earning from an audience, and the audience is the part they are not selling you.

Frequently asked

Will switching bank accounts hurt my credit score? A full current account application usually involves a credit check and leaves a footprint. One switch is not a problem. Several applications in a short window can be, particularly before a mortgage application.

Can I switch back to a bank I left? Usually not for a second bonus. Most exclude anyone who has previously held an account with them or received a bonus before, and they check.

How long does cashback take to arrive? Weeks to months, depending on the retailer's own confirmation period. Insurance and broadband are typically the slowest and the largest.

Is it worth doing this if I have a thin credit file? The cashback part, yes, since it involves no credit. Multiple account applications with a thin file can produce rejections that themselves leave marks, so go slowly.

What if the bonus never arrives? Screenshot the offer terms when you start. Most disputes are about a condition the customer believes they met and the provider says they did not, and the terms as they stood on the day are your evidence.
