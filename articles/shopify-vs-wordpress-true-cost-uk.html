The Subscription Is the Smallest Number in This Decision

Everyone compares ecommerce platforms on the monthly price, which is the one cost that does not scale with your shop. Here is three years of running the same £60,000 business five different ways, with the fee that quietly becomes the largest line in the table.

The short version

  • Compare three years, not a monthly plan price. A hosted platform front-loads nothing and bills forever. A self-hosted store front-loads setup and bills less afterwards.
  • The line that decides it is the extra transaction fee. Hosted platforms typically charge a percentage on top of card processing if you use an outside payment provider, and nothing if you use theirs.
  • On £60,000 of sales a year, a 2% platform fee is £1,200 a year, which is larger than every subscription in this comparison put together.
  • Self-hosted is not free. Hosting, a theme, plugins, renewals and your own maintenance time are the bill, and the last of those is the one people forget.
  • Crossing £90,000 changes the sums, because VAT registration becomes compulsory and 20% of your prices stops being yours.

1. Why the monthly price tells you nothing

Every comparison of ecommerce platforms starts with the subscription, because it is the one number both sides advertise. It is also the number least likely to decide what you pay.

The real cost of running a shop for three years is made of five things:

  1. The platform subscription, if there is one.
  2. Payment processing, which you pay on every platform.
  3. Any additional platform transaction fee, which you pay on some platforms and not others.
  4. Hosting, themes, plugins, apps and their renewals.
  5. Your own time, at whatever your hour is worth.

Items three and five are where the differences live, and neither appears on a pricing page.

2. The transaction fee, which is the whole argument

Hosted platforms make money in two places: the subscription, and a cut of what you sell. That cut is usually waived if you use the platform’s own payment processing, and charged if you use an outside provider.

Scale that against sales rather than against the subscription and the picture changes. A 2% additional fee on £60,000 of annual sales is £1,200 a year, or £3,600 over three years. That is not a line item. On a small shop it is the largest single cost in the comparison.

Self-hosted software has no equivalent. You pay your card processor and nobody else takes a percentage. What you pay instead is for everything the platform was doing for you.

Judge a platform on what it takes per pound you sell, not per month. One of those numbers grows with your success and the other does not.

Three things fall out of that table.

The cheapest row is self-hosted, and it is also the row with sixty extra hours in it. If your hour is worth £30, those sixty hours are £1,800 and self-hosting stops being cheapest. If you enjoy the work, or your alternative use of the time is not billable, it stays cheapest by a distance.

The second row is the trap. Same platform, same shop, one setting different, and the three year cost nearly triples. If you are on a hosted platform and not using its own payment processing, that is the first thing to check.

The marketplace row is not a failure. £60 per £1,000 of sales is expensive, but a marketplace brings its own buyers. The honest way to read it is as a customer acquisition cost, and for a new seller with no audience it can be the cheapest customers available. The mistake is staying there once you have repeat buyers you could serve directly.

4. What you own, which is not a cost but decides one

On a hosted platform you rent a shop. The software, the updates, the security and the uptime are somebody else’s problem, and so is your ability to leave. Product data usually exports cleanly. Themes, apps, custom code and URL structures usually do not.

Self-hosted, you own the files and the database. Nobody can change your pricing, your terms or your feature set, and moving host is a weekend rather than a rebuild. You also own every security update and every broken plugin at eleven o’clock on a Saturday.

That is the genuine trade, and it is not a money question. It is a question about which kind of problem you would rather have.

5. The point at which the comparison changes

Everything above assumes you are below the VAT registration threshold. Once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, registration is compulsory within 30 days.

Three consequences for this decision:

  • Your platform must handle VAT properly, including different rates and the display of VAT-inclusive prices to consumers. This is where cheap themes and half-configured plugins start costing real money.
  • Your effective revenue drops if you cannot raise prices, because 20% of every consumer sale now belongs to HMRC. A shop that was comfortable at £85,000 can be tight at £95,000.
  • You can reclaim input VAT on hosting, apps and subscriptions, which quietly reduces every cost in the table by a sixth.

Worked example: Sian in Aberystwyth

Sian sells handmade ceramics. Year one was £22,000 of sales on a marketplace. Year three is on course for £60,000, and 40% of orders are now repeat customers who found her originally on the marketplace.

What the marketplace costs her. At roughly 6% of sales all in, that is £3,600 a year on current volumes.

What she moved to. A hosted platform using its own payment processing, so no extra transaction fee. Subscription and apps come to about £630 a year.

The saving. Roughly £2,970 a year, against a one-off setup of about 25 hours.

What she did not do. Leave the marketplace. She kept it for discovery and pushes repeat buyers to her own site, because the 6% is worth paying on a first order from a stranger and not worth paying on the fourth order from a regular.

What she is watching. The £90,000 threshold. At her growth rate she crosses it in about 18 months, and she would rather configure VAT calmly in advance than in the 30 days after it happens.

6. Frequently asked

Is self-hosted really cheaper? In cash, usually yes. In total cost including your time, only if your time is cheap or you enjoy the maintenance.

How do I avoid the extra transaction fee? Use the platform’s own payment processing where it is available in your market. If you cannot, price the fee into your comparison rather than ignoring it.

What about a middle option? Self-hosted on managed hosting sits between the two rows, trading some money for most of the maintenance burden. For a lot of one-person shops it is the right answer.

Can I move later? Yes, and product data usually transfers. Budget for redirects, because losing your URL structure loses your search traffic, which is a much larger cost than the migration.

7. The honest verdict

If you are on a hosted platform and not using its own payment processing, fix that this week. On £60,000 of sales it is worth about £1,200 a year and it is a settings change, not a migration.

If you are choosing now, the decision is not about price. Self-hosted is cheapest in cash and most expensive in hours. Hosted is the opposite. Pick based on whether an evening of plugin troubleshooting is something you can absorb.

And if you are on a marketplace and most of your orders are repeat customers, you are paying a discovery fee for people who already know where you are. That is the clearest case for your own site in this entire comparison.
