1. Why an air fryer is cheaper at all

Start by dismantling the marketing, because it makes the arithmetic obvious.

An air fryer is a small fan oven. It has a heating element and a fan, exactly like a conventional fan oven, and it cooks by moving hot air around food. There is no frying, no special technology and no efficiency breakthrough. What there is, is a much smaller box.

That gives it three genuine advantages, and all three are about physics rather than cleverness.

Less air and metal to heat. A domestic oven cavity is roughly 60 litres. An air fryer basket is commonly 4 to 6 litres. Bringing a tenth of the volume up to temperature takes a fraction of the energy.

Effectively no preheat. Most air fryer recipes start cooking from cold or with two or three minutes of warm-up. An oven typically wants ten to fifteen minutes at full power before the food goes in, and that energy does nothing except heat empty space.

Shorter cooking times. Faster air movement over a smaller quantity of food usually means less time at temperature.

Every one of those advantages shrinks as the quantity of food grows. Hold on to that, because it is the whole of section 3.

2. The arithmetic, with every assumption stated

The formula for any electrical appliance is the same: kilowatts, times hours, times the price per kilowatt hour.

The price is the one number here that is not ours. Under Ofgem's price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026, the capped electricity unit rate for a typical direct debit customer is 26.32p per kWh. Your own rate is on your bill and may differ, particularly if you are on a fixed deal or a time-of-use tariff.

Now the assumptions. These are ours, they are stated so you can change them, and they are the reason no two articles on this subject agree.

AssumptionValue used
Oven rated power2.1 kW
Oven preheat12 minutes at full power
Oven cooking time30 minutes
Oven duty cycle while cooking60%, because the thermostat cycles the element on and off
Air fryer rated power1.5 kW
Air fryer preheatnone
Air fryer cooking time20 minutes
Air fryer duty cycle70%

The duty cycle is the assumption most articles quietly ignore. An oven rated at 2.1 kW does not draw 2.1 kW for the whole cook. Once it reaches temperature the thermostat switches the element on and off, so the average draw is well below the rating. Ignoring this overstates the oven's cost badly, which conveniently overstates the air fryer's saving.

A sensitivity check, because the duty cycle assumption is doing a lot of work. If your oven cycles at 40% rather than 60%, the oven meal falls to 22.1p and the saving drops to 12.9p, which is £33.53 a year at five meals a week. The direction of the answer does not change. The size of it moves by about a third on one assumption.

This is why we show the assumptions rather than just the conclusion. Anybody quoting a single confident figure has made these same choices and not told you.

3. The case where the oven wins

Here is the part that almost no coverage includes, and it follows directly from section 1.

A 5 litre air fryer basket holds roughly one to two portions. An oven holds a tray. So once you are cooking for a family, you are not comparing one air fryer load against one oven load. You are comparing several sequential air fryer loads against one oven load.

Take four portions on the same assumptions. The oven cooks all four in a single 27.6p run, because a full tray costs no more to heat than an empty one. The air fryer needs four loads at 9.2p each.

Cooking four portionsCost
Electric oven, one load27.6p
Air fryer, four sequential loads36.9p
Oven cheaper by9.2p

So the honest rule is not "air fryers are cheaper". It is: the air fryer wins for one or two portions and loses for four, on these assumptions, with the crossover sitting around three portions.

There is a second point. Four sequential loads also takes about 80 minutes rather than 42. The air fryer is sold on speed, and for a family meal the opposite is true.

Worked example: the Okonkwo household in Salford

Two adults, two children, cooking at home most nights. They buy an air fryer for £70 expecting to cut the energy bill.

Weeknight family dinners, four portions, five nights a week. On our figures the oven is cheaper by 9.2p a meal here, so using the air fryer for these actually costs them about £24 a year more, and adds nearly 40 minutes to each evening.

Weekend lunches and after-school food, one or two portions, say four times a week. Here the air fryer saves 18.4p a time, about £38 a year.

Net effect: roughly £14 a year saved, not the £48 headline. The payback on the £70 appliance is therefore about five years rather than eighteen months.

What would change the answer. If they used the air fryer only for the small jobs it is good at and kept using the oven for family meals, they would keep the £38 and avoid the £24, and the appliance would pay for itself in under two years. The mistake is not buying it. The mistake is trying to use it for everything.

4. Payback, and what actually changes your bill

On the straightforward one-or-two-portion case, the payback on a £70 air fryer is:

  • about 1.0 years if you use it daily
  • about 1.5 years at five meals a week
  • about 2.4 years at three meals a week

We will not quote a price for any specific model, so use whatever you actually pay and divide by the annual saving.

The wider point deserves stating. A saving of £30 to £50 a year is real money and it is also small relative to a household energy bill. If cutting the bill is the goal, heating and hot water are where the pounds are, and our guide to cutting UK energy bills covers the changes that move hundreds rather than tens.

Two free ways to improve the numbers above, whichever appliance you use:

Stop preheating for things that do not need it. Preheating matters for baking, where the chemistry depends on hitting temperature fast. For roast vegetables or a jacket potato it mostly wastes the 11p our model spends on it.

Cook more than one thing at once. If the oven is on, the marginal cost of a second tray is close to nothing.

5. If you are buying one

Three things actually matter to the arithmetic above, and they are all on the box.

Capacity, in litres. This is the variable that decides whether you are in the winning case or the losing one. If you regularly cook for four, a 4 litre basket will put you in the four-sequential-loads scenario permanently.

Wattage, on the rating plate. Every figure in section 2 scales directly with it. A 2 kW air fryer costs a third more to run than a 1.5 kW one for the same time.

Whether it will actually get used. The cheapest air fryer is the one you do not buy. If you already have a slow cooker in a cupboard, be honest about whether this one is different.

6. The honest verdict

An air fryer does save energy against an electric oven, and on our assumptions at the October 2026 capped rate the saving is about 18p a meal, or roughly £48 a year at five meals a week. That is worth having and it is not transformative.

The saving comes entirely from heating a smaller space and skipping the preheat, which means it shrinks as the quantity grows and reverses somewhere around three portions. If you cook for a family most nights, buying one to cut your energy bill is likely to disappoint you, and using it for family meals will cost you slightly more than the oven while taking twice as long.

Buy one because you want fast, crisp food for one or two people without warming up a full oven. That case is genuine and the payback is roughly eighteen months at five meals a week. Do not buy one as an energy measure for a household of four, because on these numbers it is not one.

Frequently asked

Does the air fryer's smaller size mean worse results for big meals? For quantity, yes, and that is the same limitation as the cost point. Overloading the basket blocks airflow, which is the entire cooking mechanism, so food steams rather than crisps. The manufacturer's stated capacity is usually optimistic about how much you can cook well at once.

What about a combination microwave or a slow cooker? A microwave is more efficient again for reheating and for anything that does not need browning. A slow cooker draws very little power but runs for hours, so it is not automatically cheaper; it wins on cheap cuts of meat and on not needing attention. Our slow cooker guide covers the batch-cooking case.

Are the manufacturers' running cost claims reliable? Treat them as marketing. They tend to compare full-power oven use with no duty cycle against air fryer use with none of the preheat, which flatters the appliance being sold. The formula in section 2 takes about two minutes with your own rating plates and your own bill.

Does it matter which tariff I am on? Yes, and it is the largest single lever. Every figure here uses 26.32p per kWh. On a time-of-use tariff with cheap overnight electricity, when you cook matters more than what you cook in, though most people cannot move dinner to 2am.
