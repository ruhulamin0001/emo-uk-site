Tap the Wall With Your Knuckle. Plasterers Do It in the First Ten Seconds

A skim coat over sound plaster is 350 to 550 pounds for an average bedroom. Hacking off and starting again is 700 to 1,200. The difference between the two is not a matter of opinion and you can settle it yourself in about a minute.

The short version

  • Skim over sound plaster: 350 to 550 pounds for a 3.5 by 3 metre bedroom, walls and ceiling, two days on site.
  • Overboard and skim: 550 to 900 pounds for the same room. Loses about 15 millimetres off each wall and buries the problem safely.
  • Hack off and re-plaster: 700 to 1,200 pounds, three to four days, and a skip.
  • Skim over blown plaster fails in two to four years, and then you pay for the full job anyway. That is the expensive mistake this page exists to prevent.
  • Per year over thirty years: a skim on sound walls is about 15 pounds, overboarding about 24, a full re-plaster about 32. A failed skim is about 190.

1. The tap test, and what you are listening for

Stand in front of the wall and knock on it with a knuckle, working across the whole surface in a grid, including low down by the skirting and up in the corners.

A solid, dull, slightly dead note means the plaster is still bonded to the masonry behind it. That wall can be skimmed.

A hollow, drummy note, the sort you get tapping a cardboard box, means the plaster has separated from the wall. Plasterers call it blown. The plaster is being held up by friction and by the paint, and nothing you apply on top of it will stick to a surface that is not itself attached to anything.

Two more checks worth a minute each. Press firmly with the flat of your hand in the middle of any suspicious area and see if it flexes. And look at the corners of door and window reveals, which is where blown plaster usually announces itself first as a hairline crack that reopens every time you fill it.

The important part is the proportion. A wall with one blown patch the size of a dinner plate can be cut out, patched and skimmed. A wall that sounds hollow across a third of its area is a hack off, and no plasterer worth using will skim it, because their name is on it when it fails.

2. Skim, overboard, or hack off

Skim. The existing surface is prepared, usually with a PVA or a proprietary bonding agent, and two thin coats of finish plaster are applied and polished. Total thickness about 3 millimetres. Fast, cheap, minimal mess, and completely dependent on what is underneath being sound.

Overboard and skim. Plasterboard is fixed over the existing wall, either screwed to battens or dot and dabbed with adhesive, then skimmed. You lose about 15 millimetres of room in each direction, sockets and switches need extending forward, and skirting and architrave usually come off and go back. In exchange you get a perfectly flat wall without a skip, and you can put insulation behind the board if the wall is cold.

Hack off and re-plaster. Everything comes off down to the brick or block, a base coat goes on, then the skim. It is dusty, noisy, takes three or four days, needs a skip and leaves the room unusable. It is also the only option that deals with the wall itself, which matters if the reason the plaster blew was damp or salt contamination.

That last point decides more cases than cost does. If the plaster blew because water was getting in, overboarding hides the wall without fixing it and the new board will be damp within two winters. Find out why the plaster failed before choosing how to cover it up.

The first row deserves defending. If you are selling in eighteen months and the wall has a few cracks rather than a structural problem, filling and painting for 150 pounds is the right answer and the 41 pounds a year is irrelevant because you will not be there.

4. What changes the price, and what should not

Four things legitimately move a plastering quote and it is worth knowing them so you can tell a fair price from a guess.

Ceiling height and access. Anything above about 2.6 metres needs staging rather than a hop up, and a stairwell can double the labour for the same square metres because of the time spent setting up safe access.

The number of corners, reveals and features. A plain square room is quick. The same area broken up by a chimney breast, two alcoves, a bay window and a picture rail is a different job, because the finish work at every arris takes far longer than the flat.

Preparation. Removing wallpaper, especially the woodchip painted over four times that you find in 1930s houses, is real work and should appear as a separate line. Artexed ceilings pre 2000 may contain asbestos and must be tested, not scraped.

Making good. Whether the price includes taking off and refitting skirting, sockets and radiators, or whether that is your problem. This is the commonest reason two quotes look different and the commonest source of arguments on day three.

What should not move the price is the plaster itself. Materials are a small fraction of a plastering job. Anyone quoting a premium for a brand of finish plaster is charging for something the finished wall will not show.

5. Worked example: the Ahmed bedroom in Dudley

Yusuf Ahmed is redecorating the back bedroom of a 1935 Dudley semi. The wallpaper has been on since before he bought the house and there are cracks around the window reveal that keep coming back.

He strips the paper and does the tap test. Three walls are solid throughout. The external wall under the window sounds hollow across roughly a square metre, and pressing it makes a faint crackle.

Quote one, from a plasterer who did not strip anything, is 430 pounds to skim the room. Quote two, from someone who came after the paper was off and tapped the walls himself, is 640 pounds: cut out and re-render the blown patch, then skim the whole room.

The cheaper quote would have failed. A one square metre blown area under a window is almost always a sign of water tracking in, and skimming over it would have cracked in the first winter. The second plasterer checked the outside first and found the window cill had lost its drip groove, so rain was running back into the wall.

The cill is repaired for 180 pounds before the plastering starts. Total spend 820 pounds against 430. Over thirty years that is 27 pounds a year against a skim that would have needed redoing in year two, putting the cheap route past 170 pounds a year.

The useful detail: the second plasterer was the one who looked outside. That is the single question to ask when a patch has blown. Where is the water coming from.

Frequently asked

How long before I can paint it? New plaster needs to dry, not just look dry. Four to seven days for a skim in a ventilated room, longer for a full re-plaster in winter. The first coat should be a mist coat, which is emulsion thinned with water, because full strength paint sits on the surface and peels. Painting too early is the commonest way to ruin a good plastering job.

Can I skim over artex? Often yes, provided it is not a heavy peaked pattern and provided it predates 2000 only after an asbestos test comes back clear. Never sand or scrape an artex ceiling of unknown age. Overboarding is frequently the safer and barely dearer answer.

Is dot and dab as good as wet plaster? For flatness and speed, yes. It creates a small void behind the board, which can allow air movement and, on an external wall, a slight cold bridge at the perimeter unless the adhesive is applied as a continuous ribbon. Ask for a continuous perimeter bead on external walls.

Does plastering need building control? Plastering on its own does not. If you are dry lining an external wall as part of insulating it, the thermal element rules in Approved Document L can apply, and your installer should tell you so before starting.

The honest verdict

Strip the wall first, tap it yourself, and only then get quotes. On sound walls, skim for 350 to 550 pounds a room and spend the difference on something else, because at about 15 pounds a year it is the cheapest durable finish in this guide.

If more than a patch sounds hollow, find out why before you choose. A wall that blew because of water will blow again under whatever you put on top of it, and that is how a 450 pound job turns into a 1,400 pound one.
