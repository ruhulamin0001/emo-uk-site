Deadline 31 October 2026: Every Pension Scheme Must Be Plugged In, and You Still Cannot Log In. What the Dashboard Will Show, When, and How to Find Lost Pots Now

The pensions dashboard has been promised since 2016. On 31 October 2026 the first hard date arrives: every occupational scheme and provider in scope must be connected to the system, by law. What does not arrive is the dashboard itself. The public service, run by MoneyHelper, is not expected until the 2027/28 financial year, and the government has promised the industry six months' notice of the launch date. Here is what is actually happening on 31 October, what you will see when it opens, the pensions it will not show, and the free way to find a lost pot without waiting.

The short version

  • 31 October 2026 is the connection deadline, set by the Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022 as amended in 2023: all schemes and providers in scope must be connected to the pensions dashboards ecosystem. Source: DWP staged timetable guidance (updated 6 August 2025) and the Pensions Dashboards Programme, read 5 September 2026.
  • It is not the launch. The Pensions Dashboards Programme says the public MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard is currently expected to become available in financial year 2027/28, the launch date is decided by the Secretary of State, and industry will get six months' notice.
  • What it will show: your pensions, including the State Pension, in one place, with the values and contact details schemes already hold, found by matching your name, date of birth and National Insurance number through GOV.UK One Login.
  • What it does NOT do: occupational schemes with fewer than 100 relevant members are out of scope unless they connect voluntarily, it will not consolidate or move pensions, and it will not tell you anything the schemes do not already know about you.
  • You do not have to wait to find a lost pension. GOV.UK's Pension Tracing Service is free, online or on 0800 731 0175, and gives you the contact details of any scheme you name an employer for.
  • Our verdict: put 31 October in the diary as the day the plumbing is finished, not the day the tap turns on. If you have a pot you cannot find, trace it now; the dashboard will only ever show what a connected scheme can match to you.

1. What 31 October 2026 actually is

The Department for Work and Pensions' guidance on connection, updated 6 August 2025, states the legal position: the Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022, as amended by the 2023 amendment regulations, "introduced a single connection deadline of 31 October 2026 for relevant occupational pension schemes to connect to the pensions dashboards ecosystem", and the Financial Conduct Authority made a corresponding rule for personal pension providers. The guidance then sets a staged timetable, largest schemes first, starting with schemes of 20,000 or more members in spring 2025 and working down by size, so that everything in scope is connected before the deadline. The Pensions Dashboards Programme, the Money and Pensions Service body that runs the plumbing, says on its connection deadline page that "pension providers and schemes in scope of the regulations must connect to the pensions dashboards ecosystem by 31 October 2026".

The same page draws the line that most coverage misses: "The final connection deadline is separate from the launch date for the MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard. This will be decided by the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions." The Minister for Pensions has confirmed that industry will get six months' notice of that date, and "the current expectation is that the MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard will become publicly available in financial year 2027/28". So the earliest a member of the public logs in is April 2027, and only if the notice is given by October 2026. Nothing in the regulations promises that.

2. What you will see when it opens

DWP's guidance describes the aim: dashboards "will allow individuals to see their pensions information, including their State Pension, for free in one place online at a time of their choosing", and "will also reunite savers with lost or forgotten pensions". In practice you will prove who you are through GOV.UK One Login, and the system will send your name, date of birth and National Insurance number to every connected scheme, which checks whether it holds a record that matches. Matches come back as a list: the scheme, the administrator's contact details, and the value information the scheme already produces for annual statements. The State Pension figure comes from the same forecast you can see today on GOV.UK.

3. What the dashboard does not do

It does not show every pension. The regulations bring in occupational schemes with 100 or more relevant members; DWP's guidance says smaller schemes "wish to voluntarily connect" may do so under separate Pensions Dashboards Programme guidance, which means a pot in a small employer's scheme from years ago may simply not appear. It does not find pensions the scheme cannot match to you: a maiden name, an old address or a mistyped National Insurance number on a 1990s record can leave a real pot invisible. It does not move, merge or value pensions beyond what the scheme already reports, and it does not give advice. It does not replace the annual statement or the scheme's own online account. And it does not exist yet, in the sense that matters: no member of the public can use it before the launch date is set.

4. How to find a lost pension today, for free

GOV.UK's Pension Tracing Service is the tool the dashboard will eventually automate, and it is free now. Give it the name of an employer or a pension provider and it returns the scheme's current contact details; you then write to the administrator with your name, date of birth, National Insurance number and the dates you worked there. GOV.UK is careful about the limits: the service "will not tell you whether you have a pension, or what its value is", it only finds the door. The line is 0800 731 0175, Monday to Friday 10am to 3pm, and the online form is on GOV.UK.

Two habits make the dashboard work when it does arrive. Tell every scheme your current address and any name change now, because matching is done on the data schemes hold. And check your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK, which is the one part of the dashboard already live, and which our voluntary National Insurance guide shows can be worth thousands to act on before the 5 April deadlines. Anyone born between 2002 and 2011 should also check for a Child Trust Fund, which is not a pension and will never appear on the dashboard.

5. A worked example in Bath

Ruth, 52, in Bath has had six employers since 1994 and can account for four pensions. The two she cannot are a retail job from 1996 to 1999 and a small design agency from 2003 to 2005. She was going to "wait for the dashboard".

On the timetable above, the earliest she could log in is April 2027, and only if the launch notice arrives by this October. The retail employer was part of a large group, so its scheme almost certainly has 100 or more members and must be connected by 31 October 2026; but she married in 2001 and the record will be in her maiden name, so unless she tells the scheme, the dashboard's match may fail. The agency had eleven staff; if it ran its own small trust the scheme is out of scope, and if it used a group personal pension with an insurer the pot sits with that provider under her old name. The Pension Tracing Service finds both doors in an afternoon. She writes to each with both names, her National Insurance number and her dates, and gets a statement for the retail pot within six weeks. Waiting eighteen months for a screen that might not show either pot was the worse plan.

6. The honest verdict

31 October 2026 is a deadline for pension schemes, not for you. It is the day every in-scope scheme must be connected to the dashboard system; the public dashboard follows in the 2027/28 financial year at the earliest, on a date the government has not yet set and has promised six months' notice of.

When it arrives it will show what connected schemes can match to your name, date of birth and National Insurance number, and nothing else. Update your details with every scheme you know about, trace the ones you do not through GOV.UK's free service, and treat the dashboard as a future convenience rather than a reason to wait.

Frequently asked

Can I use the pensions dashboard now? No. 31 October 2026 is the deadline for schemes to connect. The Pensions Dashboards Programme says the public MoneyHelper dashboard is expected in financial year 2027/28, on a date the Secretary of State will set with six months' notice to industry.

Will it show my State Pension? Yes. DWP's guidance says dashboards will show pensions information including the State Pension. You can already see the same forecast on GOV.UK.

Will every pension I have ever had appear? No. Occupational schemes with fewer than 100 relevant members are only included if they connect voluntarily, and any scheme that cannot match your name, date of birth and National Insurance number to its record will not return it.

How do I find a lost pension before then? Use GOV.UK's free Pension Tracing Service, online or on 0800 731 0175, with the employer's or provider's name. It gives you contact details; you then write to the scheme.
