Etsy Fees for UK Sellers: What You Really Pay Per Sale

22 August 2026 · 9 min read · Fees checked August 2026

The short version
  • Six separate charges can land on one sale. Listing, transaction, payment processing, regulatory operating fee, VAT on the fees, and sometimes offsite ads.
  • Realistically expect Etsy to take 11% to 14% of a typical UK sale before you have paid for materials, postage or your time.
  • The transaction fee applies to your postage charge too, which is the one that catches everybody.
  • The UK Regulatory Operating Fee rose from 0.32% to 0.48% on 22 June 2026.
  • Offsite Ads at 12% to 15% are compulsory once your shop passes the sales threshold.

A worked example on a £25 sale

Say you sell a handmade item for £20 with £5 postage. The buyer pays £25.

Now the uncomfortable bit. If that item took you an hour to make, list and pack, you earned £11.18 an hour before tax, with no holiday pay and no pension. That is around the National Living Wage.

And if that sale came through Offsite Ads at 15%, take another £3.75 off, and you are at £7.43, which is below minimum wage for an adult.

This is not an argument against Etsy. It is an argument for knowing the number before you set your price.

The postage trap

This is the single most common mistake UK Etsy sellers make.

Etsy charges the 6.5% transaction fee on your postage charge as well as the item price. So if you charge £5 postage, you pay 32.5p of fee on the postage alone, plus VAT on that fee, plus the payment processing percentage on it.

Two consequences:

  • Free postage is not free. Building postage into the item price does not avoid the fee, because the fee applies to whatever the buyer pays either way. What it does change is how the listing looks in search, and Etsy pushes free-postage listings, so it may still be worth doing for visibility.
  • Overcharging for postage costs you twice. You pay fees on the inflated amount and buyers compare postage across shops.

Weigh and measure your packed items properly. Royal Mail large letter rates are dramatically cheaper than small parcel, and the difference between the two is often a few millimetres of packaging. That is one of the few places in this business where an hour of work saves money on every single order forever.

Offsite Ads, and why you cannot always opt out

Etsy advertises listings on Google, Meta and elsewhere. If a buyer clicks one of those adverts and buys from you within a set window, you pay a percentage of that order.

  • Below the sales threshold: participation is optional and the rate is the higher one. You can switch it off.
  • Above the threshold: participation is mandatory and cannot be turned off, though the rate drops.
  • There is a cap on the fee per individual order, which limits the damage on a large sale.

Whether it is good value depends entirely on whether those buyers would have found you anyway. For a distinctive product that people search for by name, you may be paying for traffic you already had. For a generic product in a crowded category, it is genuinely bringing you sales you would not have made.

Either way, price as though every sale might carry it. A shop that is only profitable when Offsite Ads does not fire is not a profitable shop.

How to price so you are not working for free

A method that survives contact with reality:

  1. Materials at true cost, including the wastage and the bits you buy in bulk and forget to count.
  2. Your time at a real hourly rate. Pick a number you would accept from an employer. Include making, listing, photographing, packing and answering messages.
  3. Add packaging, per order.
  4. Add 25% to cover Etsy's cut. Not 15%, because you need the margin to survive Offsite Ads and returns.
  5. Then sense-check against the market. If the number is far above comparable listings, the answer is usually to change the product or the market, not to accept a wage below minimum.

Two things that genuinely move the numbers: raise the average order value with bundles and add-ons, because most fees are percentage-based but your packing time is not, and cut making time, because that is the largest hidden cost in almost every handmade shop.

VAT, HMRC and the reporting rules

  • The trading allowance covers your first £1,000 of trading income in a tax year. Above that, register as self-employed and file a Self Assessment return. Note it is income, not profit.
  • Etsy reports to HMRC. Under the digital platform reporting rules, marketplaces pass seller data including sales totals to tax authorities. Assuming a small shop is invisible is out of date.
  • VAT on Etsy's fees is charged to you as a UK seller. If you are VAT registered you can normally reclaim it, which is one of the few genuine advantages of registration for a small maker.
  • Your own VAT threshold is separate. Below it you do not charge VAT on sales. Above it you must register, and that changes your pricing entirely.

General information, not tax advice. Once your shop is turning over real money, an accountant is cheaper than the mistakes.

Is Etsy still worth it

The honest case for Etsy is traffic. Millions of people arrive already intending to buy handmade, vintage or personalised things, and building that audience yourself takes years. For that, 11% to 14% is not an outrageous price.

The honest case against is control. Fees have risen repeatedly, Offsite Ads became compulsory above a threshold, the search algorithm changes without warning, and the customer belongs to Etsy rather than to you.

The pragmatic answer most successful UK sellers land on: use Etsy for discovery and build your own channel in parallel. Put a card in every parcel. Collect email addresses lawfully. A small direct channel that takes 3% instead of 14% changes the economics of the whole business, and it is the only asset in this list that you own.

Questions people ask

Do I pay a listing fee if the item does not sell? Yes. Listings cost the fee to publish and again every four months if renewal is automatic. A shop with two hundred unsold listings is paying for them.

Is Etsy Ads worth it? Sometimes, and it is easy to lose money. Start with a very small daily budget, run it for at least a month, and judge it on revenue attributable to the ads rather than on impressions. If you cannot clearly see it paying for itself, switch it off.

Can I avoid the payment processing fee? Not meaningfully. Etsy Payments is required in the UK.

What happens if a buyer opens a case? Etsy can refund the buyer from your funds. Keep proof of postage on everything, because without it you will usually lose a non-delivery claim.

The verdict

Assume Etsy takes about 15% of everything, including your postage, and price from there. Weigh your parcels properly, treat Offsite Ads as inevitable rather than optional, and start collecting a direct audience from your first order. Etsy is a good shop window and an expensive landlord, and treating it as both is how sellers stay profitable.
