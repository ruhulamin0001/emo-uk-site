You Earned It in September. Amazon Pays It at the End of November

Affiliate dashboards show a number going up in real time, which makes it feel like income. It is not income until it clears, and the gap between the two is longer than almost anyone tells you when they are persuading you to start. Here are the actual terms, from the networks' own pages.

The short version

  • Amazon UK pays approximately 60 days after the end of the month in which you earned. Amazon's own Associates Central help page, read 11 September 2026. Money earned on 1 September is paid at the end of November, roughly 90 days later.
  • The Amazon threshold is £25 and you cannot change it. For direct deposit and gift certificate, if Amazon owes you less than £25 the balance rolls into next month's total. For cheque the minimum is £50, and you may set your own higher figure.
  • Amazon will not pay anything until your tax information is on file. Not a delay, a block. The balance sits there.
  • Awin's threshold is £20 for sterling, and it pays twice a month. Your balance must reach the threshold by the 15th or the last day of the month, otherwise it rolls to the next cycle. This one you can raise or lower yourself.
  • Awin pays on confirmed commission only. That word does the heavy lifting: a sale sits pending while the retailer checks it against returns and cancellations, and only then joins your payable balance.
  • Plan your first year assuming you bank nothing for four months. The threshold delay and the payment delay stack on top of each other, and they hit hardest when earnings are smallest.

1. Amazon UK: 60 days after the month ends

Amazon's UK Associates help pages state the schedule plainly, and it is worth quoting because almost nobody repeats it accurately. Commission income is paid by direct deposit, Amazon.co.uk gift certificate or cheque approximately 60 days after the end of the month. Payments are made monthly, if your balance meets the minimum threshold, and Amazon must have your tax information on file before it can pay anything at all.

Sixty days after the end of the month is not sixty days after you earned. Work it through:

You earn a commission on 1 September.

September ends on 30 September.

Roughly 60 days later is late November.

Elapsed time from earning to banking: about 90 days.

A commission earned on 30 September, the last day of the month, waits roughly 60 days. One earned on 1 September waits roughly 90. Same month, same dashboard, thirty days of difference in when it lands.

The reason is not obstruction. It is returns. Amazon's returns window means a sale in early September may still be undone in October, and the payment schedule is built around that fact rather than around your cash flow.

2. The thresholds, and which ones you can change

A threshold is the amount you must have accumulated before a payment is released at all. Below it, nothing moves, and the balance carries forward.

Amazon UK, direct deposit and gift certificate: £25. Amazon's help page is explicit that you cannot change this figure. If Amazon owes you less than £25, your commissions roll into the next month's total.

Amazon UK, cheque: £50 minimum. Here you can set your own threshold, but only upwards. Amazon holds the payment until your earnings reach whatever figure you entered.

Awin, sterling: £20. Awin requires every publisher to set a minimum payment threshold and states the minimums as £20 for GBP, €20 for euro and $20 for USD, with other currencies at the equivalent of $20. This one is adjustable in Account, then Payment Details, at any time.

The threshold matters far more at the start than later, and it compounds with the payment delay in an unhelpful way. A site earning £8 a month on Amazon does not get paid every month for eight pounds. It gets nothing for three months and then £24 is still under the line, so it gets nothing in month four either. The first actual payment arrives once the running total crosses £25 and then waits out the 60 day cycle.

3. Pending, confirmed, declined: the word that costs you money

Awin's threshold applies to confirmed commission. That single word is the difference between the number on your dashboard and the number in your bank.

When someone buys through your link, the sale enters as pending. The retailer then checks it: did the customer return the item, cancel the order, fail a fraud check, use a voucher code that voids commission, or turn out to be an existing customer when the programme only pays for new ones. Only what survives that check becomes confirmed and joins your payable balance.

How long the check takes is set by each retailer, not by the network, and it varies enormously between programmes. Clothing retailers, with high return rates, are usually slowest. The practical consequence is that a new publisher watching a healthy pending balance may be watching money that will never arrive.

A pending balance is a forecast. A confirmed balance is a debt somebody owes you. Only one of them buys anything.

Amazon handles the same problem differently, by simply paying late enough that most returns have already happened, which is why its schedule is a flat 60 days rather than a per retailer validation window. Neither approach is worse. They just move the uncertainty to different places.

4. Worked example: Tom in Sheffield, first six months

Tom launches a small home improvement site in Sheffield on 1 March. He is on the Amazon UK programme with direct deposit, so his threshold is £25 and unchangeable, and his payment lands roughly 60 days after each month closes.

March: earns £6. Balance £6. Under £25, nothing paid.

April: earns £11. Balance £17. Still under, nothing paid.

May: earns £14. Balance £31, over the line. This payment is released roughly 60 days after 31 May, so it lands at the end of July.

Money earned in March, banked in late July. A gap of about four and a half months.

By the end of August Tom has earned £72 in total and has received £31 of it. Nothing has gone wrong. That is the system working exactly as documented.

Tom's hosting, domain and email tool cost him roughly £9 a month from day one. Those bills do not wait for a threshold. So the honest picture of his first five months is that he has paid out about £45 and taken in £31, and the site is still a net cost despite having earned more than it spent on paper.

The honest verdict

None of this makes affiliate income a bad idea. It makes it a slow one, and the slowness is front loaded, which is exactly when most people give up.

Two practical things follow. Get your tax information onto the Amazon account on the day you open it, because that is the one delay that is entirely self inflicted and it blocks payment completely. And if you are on Awin, judge your progress by the confirmed column, not the pending one, however much less fun that is.

Then budget honestly. Assume you will bank nothing for the first four months regardless of what the dashboard says, and treat any money that arrives sooner as a pleasant surprise rather than the plan.

Frequently asked

What happens to my balance if I stop or close the site? It stays on the account and is still subject to the same threshold. A balance below the minimum that never grows is, in practice, never paid. This is the main reason to check your figures before abandoning a project rather than after.

Can I ask Amazon to pay me early? No. The schedule and the direct deposit threshold are both fixed by the programme. The cheque threshold is the only figure you control, and only to raise it.

Is affiliate income taxable in the UK? It is trading income. The first £1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year is covered by the trading allowance, and gross means before you deduct hosting or anything else. Above that you need to register for Self Assessment. Registration for the 2025 to 2026 tax year closes on 5 October 2026.
