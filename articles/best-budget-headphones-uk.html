£59 Now Buys 35 Hours of Battery and Real ANC. What It Still Does Not Buy

Budget headphones got genuinely good, which has made choosing harder rather than easier. Almost everything under £100 now claims noise cancelling and forty hours of battery. The differences that remain are real, and they are not the ones on the box.

The short version

  • Battery is no longer a differentiator. Over 30 hours with ANC on is routine at this price, and many models claim 40 or more. Anything under 25 hours in 2026 is a reason to look elsewhere.
  • Budget ANC reduces low frequency noise, it does not create silence. Traffic, train rumble and aircraft cabin noise, yes. Voices in an office, barely.

1. What budget ANC actually blocks

Active noise cancelling works by producing a sound wave that opposes incoming noise. It is very effective on steady, low frequency sound and much weaker on sudden, high frequency sound.

NoiseBudget ANC under £100
Train and road rumbleStrong. This is what the technology is best at
Aircraft cabin droneStrong
Air conditioning and fan humStrong
Nearby conversationWeak. It reduces it, it does not remove it
A crying child, a barking dog, a keyboardVery weak. Passive isolation from the earcup does more than the ANC

If your reason for buying is an open plan office full of talking, be aware that ANC is not the answer to that problem, and no amount of extra spend fully fixes it either. Good passive isolation and something playing quietly does more.

Two useful distinctions: hybrid or adaptive ANC systems generally perform better than basic feedforward designs, and the difference between the best and worst ANC at this price is larger than the difference between the best budget model and something at £250.

Every set at this price says noise cancelling. The gap between the best and worst of them is wider than the gap between the best of them and headphones costing three times as much.

2. Battery, and why it stopped mattering

Battery life used to be the reason to spend more. That is over. At this price, over 30 hours with ANC switched on is now routine, and plenty of models claim 40 or more. As an example of where the market sits, the Soundcore Space Q45 lists at around £59 with 35 hours of ANC on playback.

What that means practically: a full working week of commuting without charging. So stop using battery as a tiebreaker unless a model is unusually low, and treat anything advertising under 25 hours in 2026 as a signal that the rest of the specification may also be dated.

Two things that do still matter. Quick charge, where five or ten minutes gives you a few hours, is genuinely useful and not universal. And USB C rather than micro USB, so you are not carrying a second cable.

3. Codecs, in plain English

A codec is how audio is compressed to travel over Bluetooth. Which one you can use depends on your phone as well as your headphones, which is why the spec sheet alone can mislead you.

  • SBC. The baseline, on everything. Fine, not great.
  • AAC. What iPhones use. If you have an iPhone, this is your ceiling and LDAC support on the headphones does nothing for you.
  • LDAC. Higher bitrate, Android only. A real improvement if your phone supports it and you listen to good quality files or a lossless streaming tier.
  • aptX and aptX Adaptive. Android and some Windows laptops. Lower latency, which matters for video and gaming.

The honest note: on compressed Spotify streams through £60 headphones on a noisy train, most people cannot reliably tell LDAC from AAC. Treat codec support as a tiebreaker between two otherwise equal options, not as a reason to pay more.

4. Over ear or in ear

This is the decision that actually affects whether you enjoy them, and it is about your life rather than about audio.

Over earIn ear
Passive isolationBetter, the cup physically blocks soundDepends entirely on the tip seal
Battery30 to 60 hours in the headphones6 to 10 hours per bud, plus case charges
Long sessionsMore comfortable for most peopleCan ache after an hour or two
PortabilityBulky, needs bag spacePocketable
Glasses and warm weatherPressure on the arms, hot earsNo issue
Losing themDifficultEasy, and a single bud is expensive to replace

The single most underrated thing about in ear buds: the ear tips decide almost everything. A poor seal loses bass and destroys ANC performance. Try every tip size in the box, and if none seal properly, third party foam tips cost a few pounds and often transform a set you were about to return.

5. The four specs worth checking

  1. Multipoint pairing. Connected to your laptop and your phone at once, switching automatically when a call comes in. Once you have used it you will not go back, and it is not on every model at this price.
  2. A transparency or ambient mode. Lets outside sound in without removing the headphones. Essential for walking near roads and for shop counters.
  3. Physical buttons rather than touch controls. Touch panels misfire with gloves, in rain, and when you adjust the fit. This is a genuine daily annoyance.
  4. Replaceable ear pads on over ear models. Pads perish long before the electronics. If replacements are sold, the headphones last years longer.

Notice what is not on that list: driver size, frequency response range, and the number of microphones. Those numbers are marketing at this price and do not predict how anything sounds.

6. A worked choice for three people

Same budget, £70, three different right answers.

Amir commutes 50 minutes each way by train, Android phone. Over ear. Train rumble is exactly what budget ANC handles well, battery covers the week, and LDAC is worth having since his phone supports it. Multipoint matters because he also uses them for work calls. Priority: ANC quality, then multipoint.

Sophie runs and uses them at the gym, iPhone. In ear, and over ear would be the wrong purchase at any price. She needs a proper seal, an IP water resistance rating, and a secure fit. LDAC is irrelevant on an iPhone. Priority: fit and IP rating, then transparency mode for road safety.

Dan works from home in a house with two children. The honest answer is that ANC will not solve his problem, because children are not low frequency steady noise. Over ear with good passive isolation gets him further than any ANC claim. Priority: passive isolation and comfort for long wear, and realistic expectations.

The point: there is no single best set under £100. There is a best set for the noise you are actually trying to escape, and the three answers above have almost nothing in common.

7. Traps in budget headphone marketing

  • Battery figures measured with ANC off. Always check whether the headline number is with noise cancelling on. The difference is often ten hours or more.
  • "Hi-Res Audio" badges on Bluetooth headphones. Largely meaningless over a wireless connection at this price.
  • Permanent sales. If it has been reduced from £129 to £59 continuously since March, £59 is the price.
  • Reviews of a different model. Marketplace listings sometimes carry reviews from an earlier version. Check the review dates and that they mention the model you are looking at.
  • No replaceable pads. Sealed pads mean the headphones are disposable once they perish, usually in two to three years.
  • Buying for a noise ANC cannot fix. The most common disappointment, and it is a mismatch of expectations rather than a bad product.

The honest verdict

Under £100 is now a genuinely good place to buy headphones. Battery is solved, build quality is decent, and ANC is real if not magic. The upgrade to £200 buys better ANC and better microphones, and for most people it is not necessary.

Choose in this order: over ear or in ear based on how you will actually use them, then ANC quality from independent testing rather than the box, then multipoint if you take calls on a laptop. Everything else is a tiebreaker.

And be honest about the noise you are escaping. If it is a train, budget ANC will delight you. If it is your colleagues talking, no set at any price will, and knowing that before you buy saves both the money and the disappointment.

Frequently asked

Is ANC bad for your ears? No. It works by adding an opposing sound wave, not by pressure. Some people feel a sensation of pressure, which is a known effect and not harmful. The genuine hearing risk is volume, and ANC usually lets you listen at lower volume.

Can I use them for work calls? Check the microphone specifically, because it is the weakest part of most budget headphones. Multipoint pairing matters more than anything else here.

Do I need a headphone amp? No. Bluetooth headphones have their own amplifier built in.

How long should they last? Three to five years is reasonable. The battery and the ear pads fail first. Replaceable pads meaningfully extend that, and are worth checking before you buy.
