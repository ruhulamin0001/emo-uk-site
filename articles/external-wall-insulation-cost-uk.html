Twenty Nine Years Is Not a Payback. It Is a Mortgage

External wall insulation is the most effective single thing you can do to a solid walled house, and for most owners it is still the wrong purchase. Here is the arithmetic, done openly, and the three situations where the answer flips to yes.

Ruhul Amin Published 15 September 2026 · 8 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Energy Saving Trust puts external wall insulation at around 15,000 pounds and internal at around 12,000 for a typical three bedroom semi in Great Britain.
  • On the assumptions set out below, it saves about 510 pounds a year. That is a payback of about 29 years for external and about 24 years for internal.
  • Loft insulation on the same arithmetic pays back in under two years. Do the cheap things first. All of them. Twice.
  • The quality record is the bigger warning. In October 2025 the National Audit Office reported on failures under ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme, and 98 per cent of homes fitted with external wall insulation under those schemes have problems needing fixing.
  • It flips to yes in three cases: the work is grant funded, the walls are being re-rendered or the roof replaced anyway so the scaffolding is already paid for, or the house is genuinely cold and damp in a way that money alone does not measure.

1. What it costs, from the people who publish the figure

Energy Saving Trust is the closest thing the UK has to a neutral published figure, and it gives two numbers for a typical three bedroom semi detached house in Great Britain. External wall insulation costs around 15,000 pounds. Internal wall insulation costs around 12,000 pounds.

Both assume you pay a company, do the whole house in one go, and in the internal case fully redecorate afterwards. That last clause matters more than it reads. Internal insulation means skirting boards, door frames, radiators, sockets and curtain rails all come off and go back, and every room loses a few centimetres in each direction.

Energy Saving Trust also states that around 33 per cent of the heat lost in an uninsulated home escapes through the walls. That is why this measure looks so attractive in a brochure. A third of the loss is a big prize. The trouble is the price of the ticket.

2. What it saves, worked out in the open

Every heat figure on this page is worked the same way, so you can argue with it. Take the U value of what is there now, subtract the U value of what replaces it, multiply by the area in square metres. That gives watts lost per degree of temperature difference. Multiply by 48,000, which is 2,000 heating degree days for a UK heating season expressed in degree hours. Multiply by 0.7, because the heating is not on all winter and because bodies, cooking and sunlight already contribute. Divide by 0.85 for what a gas boiler delivers against what it burns. Then price it at 7.97p per kWh, the Ofgem capped gas unit rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026, checked on 15 September 2026. Change any assumption you like and the arithmetic still works, which is more than can be said for most saving claims.

Now the numbers. Take a three bedroom semi with roughly 90 square metres of external wall once windows and doors are deducted. Solid nine inch brick has a U value of about 2.1 watts per square metre per degree. External insulation to current standards brings that to about 0.30.

The difference is 1.8. Multiplied by 90 square metres that is 162 watts per degree. Times 48,000 degree hours is 7,776 kWh of heat. Times 0.7 for intermittent heating and internal gains is 5,443 kWh. Divided by 0.85 boiler efficiency is 6,404 kWh of gas burned. At 7.97p that is 510 pounds a year.

So: 15,000 pounds divided by 510 pounds a year is 29 years.

Internal insulation performs slightly worse because the floor and ceiling junctions carry on losing heat, so call it a U value of 0.35 and a saving of 496 pounds. 12,000 divided by 496 is 24 years.

Those are honest numbers with honest assumptions, and they do not include the render maintenance at year twenty, or the money you would have earned had the 15,000 pounds sat in a savings account instead.

4. The quality problem nobody mentions in the sales call

In October 2025 the National Audit Office reported on the quality of external and internal wall insulation fitted under government schemes. Energy Saving Trust now states the consequence plainly: 98 per cent of homes fitted with external wall insulation under ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme have problems that need fixing.

In response the government is offering affected households free audits, carried out by TrustMark, with any faults repaired at no cost. If you had this work done under either scheme you should have received a letter from Ofgem in early 2026 carrying a Unique Measure Reference Number. TrustMark will contact you, and you can contact them yourself.

One safety point worth repeating. Only Ofgem and TrustMark will know your Unique Measure Reference Number. Anybody offering you an audit who cannot quote it should not be given your details or let into your house.

That failure rate is not an argument that the technology does not work. It is an argument that the installation is everything, and that a measure which only pays back over three decades has no tolerance at all for being fitted badly. Insulation that traps moisture in a solid wall does not merely underperform, it rots the wall. Energy Saving Trust is explicit that repairs to brickwork, pointing, roofs, overhangs, gutters and downpipes must be finished before any insulation goes on.

5. Worked example: the Haq household in Burnley

Nasreen Haq owns an 1908 stone terrace in Burnley. The walls are solid, the loft was done properly in 2019, and the gas bill last winter was uncomfortable. She gets a quote for external wall insulation on the rear and gable at 11,400 pounds, covering about 62 square metres.

Running the same method on 62 square metres: 1.8 times 62 is 111.6 watts per degree, times 48,000 is 5,357 kWh, times 0.7 is 3,750, divided by 0.85 is 4,412 kWh of gas, at 7.97p that is 352 pounds a year. Payback on 11,400 pounds is 32 years.

She is 54. On that arithmetic alone she should not do it, and nothing about the comfort argument changes the sum.

What changes it is the survey. The render on the gable is blown across about a third of its area and needs replacing within three years regardless, at about 4,200 pounds with scaffolding. Once that cost is coming anyway, the marginal cost of insulating while the scaffold is up drops to about 7,200 pounds. Payback on the marginal figure is 20 years, which is still long but is now a genuine decision rather than an obvious no.

She decides to do it, in 2028, at the same time as the render. That is the correct answer and it is the one no salesperson will ever give her, because it involves not buying anything today.

Frequently asked

Are there grants? Sometimes, and they change the answer completely, because a measure with a 29 year payback at full price can be worth having at a fifth of the price. ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026. Check what is running in your area before you assume there is nothing.

Does it need planning permission? Usually not on an ordinary house, because it counts as improvement rather than extension, but it changes the external appearance and that matters in a conservation area or on a listed building, where it will normally need consent and may be refused. Ask your local planning department before you sign anything.

What guarantee should I expect? Any TrustMark registered installer offers a 25 year guarantee, backed either by an insurer or through the Solid Wall Insulation Guarantee Agency. Get at least three quotes, and check the guarantee is third party backed rather than the company's own promise.

Will it stop my damp? It can reduce condensation on internal wall surfaces, because the wall is warmer. It cannot be installed over an existing damp problem, and doing so makes the damp worse rather than better. Fix the cause first.

The honest verdict

Do not buy external wall insulation on its own as an energy saving measure. At around 15,000 pounds against about 510 pounds a year it pays back in 29 years, and the ECO4 quality record shows what happens when this work is rushed.

Buy it if a grant covers most of it, or if you are already re-rendering or re-roofing and the scaffolding is a sunk cost. In every other case, spend the money on the loft, the draughts and the boiler flow temperature, and come back to this when the walls need work anyway.
