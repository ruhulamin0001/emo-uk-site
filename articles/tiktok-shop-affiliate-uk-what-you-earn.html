TikTok Shop Affiliate Pays Commission On Revenue Minus Refunds, Up To 15 Days After Delivery. Here Is What A £30 Sale Actually Puts In Your Bank.

TikTok Shop affiliate content promises a percentage of every sale. TikTok's own UK creator rules are more precise, and less generous, than the promise: commission is calculated on revenue after refunds, at a rate the seller sets per product, paid 3 or 15 days after delivery and frozen by any dispute. Here is the published formula, the payout mechanics, and the arithmetic on a typical sale, with no invented rates.

The short version

  • The formula is published: Commission = (Revenue minus Refunds) x Commission Rate. Source: TikTok Shop UK Academy, Affiliate Commission and Payout Rules.
  • Rates are set per product by the seller. TikTok's page says only that different products have different commission rates. Any single "typical rate" you read is a third party's estimate, not TikTok's figure.
  • You are paid on the 3rd or 15th day after the order is successfully delivered, depending on the seller's settlement period. Not on the click, not on the order.
  • Disputes, refunds or return requests freeze the commission until they are resolved.

  • Unwithdrawn balances are paid out automatically every 90 days, with a 7 day in-app warning, unless the account has a tax compliance block.

1. The formula, and what "estimated commission" means

TikTok Shop's UK Seller Academy publishes a page called Affiliate Commission and Payout Rules. It is short and it is the primary source for everything below. The core sentence is: "Commission = (Revenue minus Refunds) x Commission Rate." Your final commission, it adds, may differ from the estimated commission because of coupons or refunds.

Two words in that formula do the damage. Refunds means that a sale you were credited for disappears from your commission base if the buyer returns it, and TikTok Shop's returns window means that can happen after you have seen the order in your dashboard. Rate is set by the seller, per product. TikTok's page says only that different products have different commission rates. It does not publish an average, a floor or a ceiling for UK creators, and neither do we, because we could not verify one from TikTok.

The "estimated commission" shown when you add a product to your showcase is, per the page, the product's average discounted price multiplied by the commission rate. The word discounted matters. If the seller runs a coupon at checkout, the revenue on which your commission is calculated is the price the buyer actually paid, not the list price you saw when you chose the product.

2. When you are actually paid

The page states that you should receive your commission on the 3rd or 15th day after the order is successfully delivered, based on the seller's settlement period with TikTok Shop. Then the caveat: commissions may be delayed if there are disputes, refunds or return requests, and cannot be paid until those are resolved.

Work that through for a video posted on 1 September. A buyer orders on 2 September. Delivery takes four days, to 6 September. Commission is then due on the 3rd or 15th day after that, so 9 or 21 September, if nothing goes wrong. If the buyer opens a return request on 12 September, the commission is frozen until the return is resolved. In practice a creator posting in early September is looking at cash in the bank in late September or October, and a chunk of the September dashboard figure will never arrive.

Once commission is settled it sits as a balance. You can withdraw to your UK bank account, or do nothing: TikTok Shop automatically pays out any remaining balance every 90 days to creators with a valid UK bank account and no withdrawal restrictions, and sends an in-app notification 7 days before. The restriction it names is tax compliance, including what it calls MRDP non-compliance, which is the platform reporting regime under which TikTok, like eBay and Vinted, reports UK sellers' and creators' income to HMRC each January.

3. The three conditions, and the one you cannot undo

Before any commission is paid, the page lists three requirements. Verify your name, and it must match the name on your beneficiary account. Provide a valid payout method, meaning UK bank account details. Complete KYC, which TikTok says is required to comply with UK regulations. If the information is inaccurate, payouts cannot be processed and you do not receive your commission.

The line worth reading twice is this: "Your name cannot be changed after submission." Creators who set up under a channel name, a nickname or a partner's account discover that the payout is blocked and the fix is not a form. Use your legal name, matched to the bank account you will actually be paid into, the first time.

Tax is the other condition the page does not spell out but the MRDP reference implies. Affiliate commission is trading income. Above the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year it must be declared through Self Assessment, and TikTok reports creator earnings to HMRC under the digital platform rules. We covered the allowance and the deadlines in the side hustle tax guide linked below.

4. A worked example: Nadia in Croydon

Nadia in Croydon posts a video featuring a £30 kitchen gadget. The seller has set the commission rate for that product at 10%. That rate is our illustration, chosen for round arithmetic; her real dashboard will show the seller's actual figure.

The video generates 40 orders. The dashboard estimates 40 x £30 x 10% = £120.

The estimate was £120. The bank receives £103.50, roughly 14% less, spread over several weeks. Nothing went wrong; that is the formula working as published. On a product with a high return rate, fashion for example, the gap is wider.

Nadia's £103.50 is also taxable income. If her total trading income for the year passes £1,000, it goes on a Self Assessment return, and TikTok will have reported it.

5. How it compares with Amazon Associates

The comparison most UK creators are actually making is with Amazon. Amazon publishes its commission rates by category, pays around 60 days after the end of the month in which the sale occurred, and, since 14 April 2026, requires the product to be shipped and paid for within 180 days and credits onsite commission only for the exact ASIN variant you linked. We cover those changes in the Amazon Associates guide published alongside this one.

TikTok's structure is the mirror image. Rates are unpublished and seller-set, so they can be far higher than Amazon's for the right product. Payment is faster, days after delivery rather than two months after month end. But the refund deduction is explicit and the dispute freeze is absolute. Amazon's model rewards volume across a catalogue; TikTok's rewards picking products that do not come back.

6. The honest verdict

Treat the dashboard estimate as a ceiling, not a forecast. Revenue minus refunds, at the discounted price, is what you are paid, and on a returnable product that is meaningfully less.

Set up your legal name and the matching UK bank account before your first video, and complete KYC. The name cannot be changed afterwards.

Pick products people keep. The formula punishes returns more than it rewards clicks. There is nothing to buy here; the rules are free to read on TikTok's own page.
