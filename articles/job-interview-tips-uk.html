UK Job Interview Tips: How to Prepare and Actually Get the Offer

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Most people walk into interviews hoping to wing it, then wonder why they did not get the offer. Interviews reward preparation more than raw talent. A candidate who prepares well often beats a more qualified one who did not. Here is how to prepare properly for a UK interview.

Do the homework first

Before the day, learn what the company does, who its customers are, and why the role exists. Re read the job advert and note the skills they keep mentioning. Interviewers can instantly tell who has done this and who has not, and it changes how they see you.

The STAR method for answers

Most UK interviews ask you to describe a time you did something. The clearest way to answer is STAR: the Situation, the Task you faced, the Action you took, and the Result. It keeps your answer structured and stops you rambling.

Prepare four or five real examples in advance, covering teamwork, solving a problem, handling pressure, and a mistake you learned from. You can adapt these to almost any question.

Prepare for the common questions

  • Tell me about yourself. Keep it short, relevant, and about why you fit this role.
  • Why do you want this job. Show you understand the company and the role.
  • A weakness. Give a real one and what you do to manage it, not a fake humble brag.
  • A time you faced a problem. Use a STAR example you prepared.

Ask good questions back

When they ask if you have questions, always say yes. It signals real interest. Ask about the team, what success looks like in the role, or what they enjoy about working there. Avoid making your first question about pay and holidays, save those for later.

The small things that decide it

Arrive early, or test your camera and connection well before an online interview. Dress a little smarter than the role needs. Be warm to everyone you meet, not just the interviewer. Bring a couple of copies of your CV. These small signals of care add up.

  1. Prepare STAR examples and rehearse them out loud, not just in your head.
  2. Research the company and role until you can speak about them naturally.
  3. Prepare two or three genuine questions to ask them.
  4. Send a short thank you message afterwards. Few people do, and it is remembered.

The honest bottom line

Interviews are won by preparation, not luck. Research the company, build STAR examples, ask smart questions, and handle the small details with care. Pair this with a strong UK CV, a sharp cover letter, and a tidy LinkedIn profile.
