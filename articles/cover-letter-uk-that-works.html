Writing a UK Cover Letter That Actually Gets Read

21 July 2026 · 5 min read · Checked July 2026

Most cover letters are skimmed in seconds or skipped entirely. That is not a reason to write a bad one. It is a reason to make yours do its one job fast: prove you understand the role and would be easy to say yes to. Here is how, without the tired phrases everyone else uses.

This pairs with our UK CV guide. The CV lists what you did. The cover letter says why it matters for this job.

Open with the point, not with yourself

Skip the line that says you are writing to apply for the role. They know. Open with the single most relevant thing about you for this job. One sentence that makes the reader think, this person gets it.

The opening line decides whether the rest is read. Spend real time on it and cut everything vague.

The three short paragraphs that work

First, why this company and this role, showing you actually read the advert and know something about them.

Second, the proof. One or two concrete things you have done that match what they need, with a real result where you can. Not I am a hard working team player, but the actual thing you did and what happened.

Third, a brief, confident close. You would welcome the chance to talk, and a thank you. Short.

Cut these instantly

  • Tired openers like please find attached and I am writing to apply.
  • Empty claims with no proof: passionate, dynamic, results driven.
  • Repeating your whole CV in sentences. The letter adds meaning, it does not copy.
  • A wall of text. Keep it to under one page, ideally half.

Match the letter to the advert

Read the job advert and note the exact skills and words it uses. Reflect those honestly in your letter. Many employers scan for the language of their own advert, so speak it back to them where it is true for you. A generic letter sent to fifty jobs reads like exactly that.

Before you send

  1. Address a real person if you can find their name. It signals effort.
  2. Read it aloud. If a sentence sounds like a robot, rewrite it plainly.
  3. Check the company name is correct. The wrong name is an instant bin.
  4. Keep it to one page and match its tone to the employer, formal or friendly.

The honest bottom line

A good cover letter is short, specific, and clearly written for this job and no other. Open with your strongest relevant point, prove it once, and stop. That alone puts you ahead of most applicants, who send the same tired template to everyone.
