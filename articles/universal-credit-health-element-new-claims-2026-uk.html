Report A Health Condition On Universal Credit After 6 April 2026 And The Extra Amount Is £217.26, Not £429.80. Who The Law Protects, And Who It Does Not.

Since 6 April 2026 there have been two Universal Credit health elements. People already getting it, people with severe conditions and people who are terminally ill receive £429.80 a month. Almost everyone else who is newly assessed as unable to work receives £217.26, and that figure is frozen until April 2030. Five months in, the rule is settled law and still widely misunderstood. This is what the Universal Credit Act 2025 actually says, what it does not do, and what it means in pounds. General information, not benefits advice.

The short version

  • Two rates since 6 April 2026: £429.80 a month for pre-2026 claimants, severe conditions criteria claimants and the terminally ill; £217.26 for everyone else with limited capability for work and work-related activity. Source: Universal Credit Act 2025, section 2, and GOV.UK.
  • Section 3 freezes both LCWRA rates for 2026-27 to 2029-30. The £217.26 stays £217.26 in cash terms for four years.
  • Sections 1 and 4 protect the other side: the standard allowance must rise by at least CPI plus a set uplift each year to 2029-30, and pre-2026 claimants' combined standard allowance and health element must not fall in real terms.
  • The standard allowance for 2026-27 is £424.90 a month for a single person aged 25 or over. A new LCWRA claimant on their own gets £642.16; a protected one gets £854.70.
  • What it does not do: it does not cut anyone already getting the element before 6 April 2026, it does not change PIP, and it does not change the work capability assessment test itself.
  • The date that decides is when the health condition is reported and assessed, not when the Universal Credit claim started. GOV.UK: "There are different rules if you reported a health condition before 6 April 2026."

1. What the Act says, section by section

The Universal Credit Act 2025 is short, and the four sections that matter are all now in force.

Section 2 amends the table of element amounts in regulation 36 of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013. It inserts a new row: a claimant with limited capability for work and work-related activity, "other than a pre-2026 claimant, a severe conditions criteria claimant or a claimant who is terminally ill", gets £217.26. The existing row is relabelled so that the higher amount applies only to those three protected groups. Subsection (3) brings it into force on 6 April 2026 for assessment periods starting on or after that date.

Section 3 switches off the annual uprating duty in section 150 of the Social Security Administration Act 1992 for the LCWRA and LCW elements in the tax years ending 5 April 2026 through 5 April 2029. In plain terms, neither rate rises with inflation until April 2030 at the earliest.

Section 1 works the other way. It obliges the Secretary of State to set the standard allowance for 2026-27 to 2029-30 at no less than the previous year's figure increased by CPI and then by a further set percentage. That is the above-inflation rise the government promised in exchange for the cut.

Section 4 protects pre-2026 claimants further: for each combination of the protected LCWRA amount and standard allowance, the combined total must rise by at least inflation each year to 2029-30, with the Secretary of State required to top up one or the other to make that true.

The GOV.UK figures for 2026-27: standard allowance £424.90 a month for a single person aged 25 or over; health element £429.80 for a severe condition "unlikely to change" or someone nearing the end of life, £217.26 where the condition "may improve over time"; and the transitional line, "there are different rules if you reported a health condition before 6 April 2026".

2. Who gets £429.80 and who gets £217.26

GroupMonthly health element, 2026-27
Pre-2026 claimant: LCWRA established for a condition reported before 6 April 2026£429.80
Severe conditions criteria claimant: condition unlikely to change, meeting the criteria in the regulations£429.80
Terminally ill claimant£429.80
Any other claimant found to have limited capability for work and work-related activity on or after 6 April 2026£217.26

Three things follow. A couple where both partners have LCWRA still get one element, the higher one if either qualifies. Protected status attaches to the person and their reported condition, so someone on £429.80 with a break in their claim should check the linking rules in Schedule 1 before reclaiming. And the severe conditions test is a legal definition applied through the work capability assessment, not a doctor's opinion.

Combined with the standard allowance, a single person aged 25 or over receives £854.70 a month if protected and £642.16 if not, before housing costs, children or the carer element. The gap is £212.54 a month, £2,550.48 a year, on our arithmetic.

3. What the rule does not do

It does not reduce anyone's existing award. If your LCWRA was established for a condition reported before 6 April 2026, section 2 keeps you on the higher row and section 4 protects the real value of your combined amount to 2030.

It does not touch Personal Independence Payment. PIP is a separate benefit with its own rules; the PIP changes that were part of the original bill were removed before it became law. A person can receive PIP and either rate of the health element at the same time.

It does not change the test. The work capability assessment and the descriptors that decide limited capability are unchanged. What changed is the amount attached to the result.

It does not apply to legacy benefits. Employment and Support Allowance has its own support group rate; the Act is about Universal Credit.

It does not stop the standard allowance rising. Section 1 guarantees above-inflation rises for four years, and that applies to every claimant, health element or not.

4. A worked example in Oldham

Tariq in Oldham is 34, single, renting privately, and has claimed Universal Credit since 2024 with his rent covered by the housing element. In July 2026 he reports a back condition that stops him working. He is referred for a work capability assessment and in October 2026 is found to have limited capability for work and work-related activity.

Because his condition was reported after 6 April 2026 and it is not judged to meet the severe conditions criteria, he is on the £217.26 row. His monthly award, leaving housing aside: £424.90 standard allowance plus £217.26, which is £642.16.

His neighbour Sandra, 52, reported a similar condition in November 2025 and was found to have LCWRA in March 2026. She is a pre-2026 claimant: £424.90 plus £429.80, £854.70. The difference between them is £212.54 a month. Same town, same condition, same decision; the only variable is the date on the form.

Two further points. Tariq's £217.26 does not rise in April 2027, 2028 or 2029; his standard allowance does, so his total moves but the health part is fixed. If his condition deteriorates to meet the severe conditions criteria, he can ask for a reassessment and move to the £429.80 row. That is the one piece of upward movement the rule allows.

5. What to check, and where free help is

If you are already on the health element, check your statement shows £429.80 from your first assessment period after 6 April 2026. If it shows £217.26 and your condition was reported before that date, raise it in your journal and ask for a mandatory reconsideration; that is a decision that can be challenged.

If you are reporting a condition now, do not delay because of the rate. The element starts only once limited capability is established, after a waiting period, so reporting later means it starts later. Upload the fit note.

If you think your condition is severe and unlikely to change, say so in the assessment and provide the medical evidence that shows it. The severe conditions criteria are the difference between £217.26 and £429.80, and the assessment is where they are applied.

Free, named help: Citizens Advice for Universal Credit queries and mandatory reconsiderations; the Turn2us benefits calculator and helpline; Scope's helpline; and the GOV.UK pages, which carry the official figures. Nobody needs to pay a "benefits consultant" to find out which row they are on.

6. The honest verdict

If your health condition was on your claim before 6 April 2026, nothing has been cut and the Act protects the real value of your award to 2030. Check the statement shows £429.80.

If you report a condition now and it is not severe or terminal, the extra amount is £217.26 a month, frozen for four years. That is the law, section 2 and section 3, and no adviser can change it.

The one lever is the severe conditions criteria. If your condition is unlikely to change, make sure the assessment sees the evidence. Free help exists for exactly that.

Frequently asked

I was getting the health element before April 2026. Has mine gone down? No. Section 2 of the Universal Credit Act 2025 keeps pre-2026 claimants on the £429.80 row, and section 4 protects the real value of your combined standard allowance and health element to 2029-30.

Does this affect PIP? No. PIP is a separate benefit and the PIP provisions were removed from the bill before it became law. You can receive PIP alongside either rate of the health element.

Will £217.26 rise with inflation? Not before April 2030. Section 3 disapplies annual uprating for the LCWRA and LCW elements for the tax years to 5 April 2029.

Is this benefits advice? No. It is general information from the Universal Credit Act 2025 and GOV.UK. For your own claim, Citizens Advice, Turn2us and Scope offer free help.
