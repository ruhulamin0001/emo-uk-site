Dropshipping in the UK: The Honest Truth Nobody Tells Beginners

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Dropshipping adverts promise a laptop, a beach, and easy riches. The reality for most people is a store that never makes a sale and a supplier who ships slowly from far away. It can work, but only if you understand what it really is. Here is the honest version for the UK.

What dropshipping actually is

You run an online shop but hold no stock. When a customer buys, you order the item from a supplier who ships it straight to them. You never touch the product. Your profit is the gap between what the customer pays and what you pay the supplier, minus fees and advertising.

Why most stores fail

The model sounds simple, which is exactly why so many people try it and fail. The product is available to everyone, so you are rarely selling anything unique. You compete on marketing, and most beginners spend more on adverts than they ever make back.

Slow shipping is the other killer. Cheap suppliers often ship slowly, customers get frustrated, and refunds and bad reviews eat any profit. The dream of passive income turns into unpaid customer service.

The hidden costs

  • Advertising. This is the real cost, and it can be large before you find a product that sells.
  • Store and app fees, which stack up monthly whether you sell or not.
  • Refunds and returns for slow or faulty items, which come out of your pocket.
  • Your time, spent testing products that mostly do not work.

The UK rules you cannot ignore

You are a real business selling to UK customers, so consumer law applies fully: accurate descriptions, refund rights, and clear delivery times. Importing goods can also bring VAT and customs charges that surprise beginners. Getting this wrong causes disputes and losses.

Dropshipping income is taxable. Past £1,000 of sales in a tax year you register for Self Assessment, as covered in our side hustle tax guide.

Who actually makes money

The winners treat it as a real business, not a shortcut. They find a genuine gap, use reliable suppliers with faster shipping, build a proper brand, and understand their advertising numbers cold. That is skilled work, not passive income.

For most beginners, the honest, lower risk routes on this site, like print on demand or reselling, are far more likely to actually pay.

The honest bottom line

Dropshipping is a real but hard business, not the easy money the adverts sell. If you go in, treat it as a serious venture with real advertising costs and reliable suppliers, and know most stores lose money before any win. Go in with open eyes or start somewhere lower risk.
