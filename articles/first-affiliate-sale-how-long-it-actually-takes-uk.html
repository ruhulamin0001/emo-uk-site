Nobody Warns You About the Gap Between the Sale and the Money

The usual answer to this question is a vague range, three to six months, and then the article moves on. That range is wrong because it measures the wrong thing. What actually stands between publishing your first page and money reaching your bank account is four separate delays, and only the first one has anything to do with how good your writing is.

The short version

  • Amazon's own agreement refuses to promise you anything. Section 4 of the Associates Programme Operating Agreement, updated 15 October 2025, says Amazon makes no representation about the traffic or commission income you can expect, and will not be liable for actions you take based on your expectations.
  • The tracking window is 24 hours, not 30 days. An Amazon Session ends at the first of three things: 24 hours passing, the customer ordering a physical product, or the customer clicking somebody else's Amazon affiliate link.
  • The basket exception buys you 89 days. If the reader adds the item to their basket inside that Session, the order still counts as long as it completes no later than 89 days after the original click.
  • A sale is not a commission. Skimlinks tells its publishers that retailers typically take 60 days to review commissions, with more time after that before they clear, so the normal period is around 90 days.

  • Returns claw it back. Amazon's Commission Income Statement excludes any order where a cancellation, return or refund has been initiated, whether or not you have been paid.

1. Four delays, not one

Ask how long a first affiliate sale takes and you will be given a number that covers exactly one of the four things in your way. Here is the whole list, in the order you meet them.

  1. Time before anyone reads the page. This is the one every guide talks about.
  2. The tracking window. A reader has to buy inside a defined period or the sale belongs to nobody.
  3. The approval period. The retailer reviews the transaction before the network will count it.
  4. The payout threshold. The network holds your balance until it is big enough to be worth paying.

The second, third and fourth are written into contracts you can read tonight. They do not move because you write better. That is the useful part, because it means you can plan around them instead of taking them personally.

It is also worth starting with what Amazon itself says. From Section 4 of the Associates Programme Operating Agreement, updated 15 October 2025: "We do not make any representation, warranty, or covenant regarding the amount of traffic or commission income you can expect at any time in connection with the Associates Program, and we will not be liable for any actions you undertake based on your expectations."

That is the company with the most affiliate data on earth declining to give you a timeline. Every blogger who gives you one with confidence is guessing from a sample of one.

2. Delay one: being found at all

This is the only delay under your control and the only one nobody can put a number on honestly. What is worth saying plainly is that publishing volume on its own is not the lever people think it is.

Google's own documentation on creating helpful, reliable, people-first content, last updated 10 December 2025, lists this as a warning sign that you should reconsider your approach: "Are you producing lots of content on many different topics in hopes that some of it might perform well in search results?"

So the honest framing of delay one is not "publish 50 posts and wait". It is closer to "find the handful of questions you can answer better than the pages currently ranking, and answer those". Our guide to picking an affiliate niche covers how to work out what those are before you write anything.

3. Delay two: the 24 hour window

This is the most commonly misreported figure in UK affiliate advice, and the rule is written down in Amazon's Associates Programme Commission Income Statement, which sits inside the Programme Policies.

A Session begins when a customer clicks your Special Link. It ends at the first of these three to happen:

  • 24 hours pass from that click
  • the customer places an order for a product that is not a digital product
  • the customer clicks through to Amazon on somebody else's affiliate link

That third one matters more than people expect. Your reader is not sitting in a sealed room. If they open another review site on the way to buying, the Session transfers and so does the commission.

There is one genuine extension, and it is worth knowing because it changes what you write. If the customer adds the product to their basket during the Session, the purchase still qualifies as long as they complete the order no later than 89 days after the initial click-through. So the practical instruction to a reader is not "buy this now", it is closer to "put it in your basket while you decide".

Separately, the purchase only counts if, within 180 days of it, the product is shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer. A pre-order that ships late can sit in limbo for months.

4. Delay three: 60 days to approve, about 90 to clear

This is the delay that catches people hardest, because by this point they have done everything right and there is still no money.

Skimlinks tells its own publishers exactly why: "Typically retailers will take 60 days to review commissions, with a further period before individual commissions clear and are paid out, so the normal period is around 90 days."

It also says why the wait exists: "Networks and retailers can cancel commissions at any point in the process for a variety of reasons, fraud, customers returning purchases, other transaction errors."

Amazon's version of the same logic is an exclusion rather than a delay. Its Commission Income Statement disqualifies "any Product order where a cancellation, return, or refund has been initiated". Note the word initiated. The customer does not have to complete the return for the commission to go.

The practical consequence is that a dashboard figure in October is a forecast, not an invoice. Our guide to affiliate network payment terms in the UK goes through how the different networks handle this.

5. Delay four: the payout threshold

Even a fully cleared commission does not move until your balance clears the network's minimum. Skimlinks publishes its thresholds openly: £50 for UK publishers, or $65 and €55 in the other two currencies it quotes, paid at the end of each month once you are past it. UK and US publishers can take direct deposit or PayPal. Everyone else is PayPal only.

Thresholds are the reason so many people report that their first affiliate money arrived as a strangely large single payment months after the sales that produced it. Nothing went wrong. The balance simply sat below the line until enough small commissions piled up behind it.

Worked example: Nadia in Stoke-on-Trent

Nadia publishes her first proper review on 1 March. It starts getting search traffic in late May, which is fast rather than typical.

The click. On 2 June a reader clicks her link and buys a home item at £120. The Amazon UK rate for the Home category is 5%, so the commission is £6.

The approval. Sixty days of retailer review takes that to roughly 1 August, and full clearance to roughly 1 September, in line with the 90 day norm.

The threshold. At £6 a sale, she needs around nine cleared sales to pass a £50 threshold. Even at two sales a month from June, she reaches it in early autumn and is paid at the end of that month.

The result. Sale on 2 June, money in the account in autumn. Nothing failed. Two of those delays are contract terms and the third is arithmetic. Nadia's mistake, if she makes one, would be concluding in July that the page does not work.

6. The month three quitting point

Month three is where most people stop, and the timeline above explains why with no psychology required. Month three is the exact point at which a March starter has traffic arriving, sales showing on a dashboard, and nothing in the bank, because delays three and four have not finished running.

So the decision at month three should not be made on income. It should be made on two other things, both visible far earlier:

  • Is anything ranking at all? Not earning. Ranking. If nothing has an impression after three months, the problem is upstream of money and no amount of waiting fixes it.
  • Is the click-through happening? If people read and never click, the page is not answering a buying question, and that is a writing problem you can fix this week.

If both of those look healthy and the money has not arrived, the money is in a queue, not missing.

Where this does not help you: if your traffic is flat at zero, none of the above applies, and this article will not fix it. Go and look at whether the pages are indexed before you look at anything else.

The honest verdict

There is no honest single number for how long a first affiliate sale takes, and the largest programme in the world has written into its contract that it will not give you one. What there are, are three delays with documented lengths: a 24 hour tracking window with an 89 day basket extension, roughly 90 days from sale to cleared commission, and a payout threshold that is £50 at Skimlinks and similar elsewhere.

Add those up and the gap between your first real sale and your first real payment is realistically three to four months on top of however long it took to get read. That is the number nobody quotes, and it is the one that makes month three feel like failure when it is actually just the middle of the queue.

Judge month three on rankings and click-through, not on your bank balance. The bank balance is measuring something that has not finished happening yet.

Frequently asked

Is the Amazon cookie really only 24 hours? Yes, with one exception written into the same clause. The Session ends after 24 hours, or sooner if the customer places an order or clicks another Associate's link. But if the item goes into the basket during that Session, the order still qualifies as long as it completes within 89 days of the original click.

If someone buys a different product from the one I linked, do I still get paid? Generally yes, within the rules of the Session. Amazon distinguishes between a Direct Qualifying Purchase, in the same product category as the page you linked to, and an Indirect Qualifying Purchase in a different category. Both can be qualifying purchases. What kills it is the Session ending first.

Why did a commission disappear from my dashboard? Most often a return or a cancellation. Amazon's Commission Income Statement excludes any order where a cancellation, return or refund has been initiated, and Skimlinks says openly that networks and retailers can cancel commissions at any point in the process. Reversals are normal, not a sign of a problem with your account.

Does more posts equal a faster first sale? Not reliably, and Google lists producing lots of content on many topics in the hope that some performs as a warning sign in its own documentation. Traffic to a buying question is what produces a sale, and one page that answers a buying question well beats twenty that do not.
