Six Different Statutory Payments, One Weekly Rate, and One Important Exception

Statutory maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental, parental bereavement and neonatal care pay all sit at the same weekly figure for 2026 to 2027. That makes the headline easy and hides the part that decides what most people actually receive, which is the clause about 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower.

The short version

  • £194.32 a week for 2026 to 2027, or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower.
  • Six payments share it: SMP, SPP, SAP, ShPP, SPBP and SNCP.
  • The exception: SMP and SAP pay 90% of average weekly earnings with no cap for the first 6 weeks.
  • If you earn under about £216 a week, the 90% figure is lower, so that is what you get. Not everyone receives £194.32.
  • SMP rates apply from 5 April 2026. All the other rates apply from 6 April 2026.
  • This is pay, not leave. The leave entitlements are separate and longer than the pay in every case.

1. The six payments sharing one rate

HMRC publishes these together, and for 2026 to 2027 they all carry the same weekly figure:

Statutory Maternity Pay (SMP), weekly rate for the remaining weeks after the first 6.

Statutory Paternity Pay (SPP), weekly rate.

Statutory Adoption Pay (SAP), weekly rate for the remaining weeks after the first 6.

Statutory Shared Parental Pay (ShPP), weekly rate.

Statutory Parental Bereavement Pay (SPBP), weekly rate.

Statutory Neonatal Care Pay (SNCP), weekly rate.

All six: £194.32 or 90% of the employee's average weekly earnings, whichever is lower.

One date detail that catches payroll out. The SMP rate applies from 5 April 2026. All the other rates apply from 6 April 2026. A single day, and it exists because maternity pay periods start on a Sunday.

Read down the second column and the point of this article changes. One weekly rate, and a spread from 2 weeks to 39. Thirty-three weeks of SMP at £194.32 is £6,412.56, before the first six weeks at 90% are added at all. Two weeks of Statutory Paternity Pay at the same rate is £388.64. Identical rate, about sixteen times the money, which is why a household that plans around the rate instead of the weeks gets the answer badly wrong.

One more thing before you check your own figure, because GOV.UK does not currently agree with itself. HMRC Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027 gives Statutory Neonatal Care Pay as £194.32. The parent facing guide at gov.uk/neonatal-care-pay-leave still showed £187.18 when it was read on 26 September 2026, which is the 2025 to 2026 figure. A parent reading one page and a payroll clerk reading the other will produce two different numbers for the same week, and the employer rates table is the one that governs. If neonatal care leave has been paid at £187.18 a week in this tax year, that is the gap worth asking about.

2. The words that decide what you get

"Whichever is lower" is doing more work than the £194.32. The rule is not that you receive £194.32. It is that you receive the lower of £194.32 and 90% of your average weekly earnings.

So there is a threshold hidden in that sentence. Work out where 90% of average weekly earnings equals £194.32 and you get average weekly earnings of about £215.91. Below that, the 90% figure is smaller and that is what you are paid.

Average weekly earnings of £600. 90% is £540, which is higher than £194.32. You receive £194.32.

Average weekly earnings of £250. 90% is £225, still higher than £194.32. You receive £194.32.

Average weekly earnings of £180. 90% is £162, which is lower. You receive £162.

This matters most for part time workers, and it is the single most common reason someone reads £194.32 in an article and then receives less. If your earnings are below roughly £216 a week, the headline rate is not your rate.

3. The maternity and adoption exception

Two of the six behave differently at the start, and it is worth a lot of money.

SMP and SAP pay 90% of average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks, with no cap at all. The £194.32 figure does not apply to those weeks. A higher earner receives 90% of their actual earnings for six weeks and then drops to the flat rate.

The other four payments, paternity, shared parental, parental bereavement and neonatal care, have no equivalent higher opening period. They are at the lower of £194.32 and 90% from the first week.

That produces a difference that surprises couples. On the same salary, the parent taking maternity leave receives six weeks at 90% of full earnings. The parent taking paternity leave receives the flat rate from day one. And if the same person converts maternity leave into shared parental leave, those shared parental weeks are paid at the flat rate, not at 90%.

4. Worked example: Aisha in Bradford

Aisha, Bradford. Earns £30,000 a year, which is average weekly earnings of about £576.92. She is taking the full 39 weeks of Statutory Maternity Pay.

First 6 weeks. 90% of £576.92 is £519.23 a week, with no cap. That is £3,115.38.

Remaining 33 weeks. 90% of her earnings is still £519.23, which is higher than £194.32, so she gets the flat rate. 33 weeks at £194.32 is £6,412.56.

Total SMP over 39 weeks: £9,527.94, against a normal 39 weeks of pay of about £22,500.

The cliff. Her weekly income drops from £519.23 to £194.32 in week 7, a fall of 63% overnight. That is the number worth planning around, and it is not the number in the headline.

And after week 39. Maternity leave runs to 52 weeks. The last 13 weeks are unpaid.

5. What the rate does not tell you

Three things the weekly figure says nothing about, and all three change the answer.

  • Pay is shorter than leave in every case. Maternity leave is 52 weeks and SMP is 39. Being told the rate tells you nothing about the unpaid tail, which is usually the harder part of the budget.
  • Eligibility is separate. These are statutory payments with qualifying conditions on length of service and on earnings. If you do not qualify for SMP, Maternity Allowance is a different payment with different rules, and it is where a lot of self employed and recently changed jobs cases land.
  • Your employer may pay more. Plenty of contractual maternity and paternity schemes are more generous than the statutory minimum. The statutory rate is a floor, not a forecast, so read your own policy before you plan.

The honest verdict

A single rate across six payments is genuinely simpler than what came before, and it makes one number easy to remember: £194.32.

The two things that decide what actually arrives are both in the small print. If your average weekly earnings are under about £216, you get 90% of them rather than the headline rate. And if you are taking maternity or adoption leave, the first six weeks are at 90% of full earnings with no cap, so the real event in your budget is week 7, when the payment drops to the flat rate. Plan around the drop and the unpaid tail, not around the headline.

Frequently asked

Do I definitely get £194.32 a week? Only if 90% of your average weekly earnings is higher than that. The rule is the lower of the two. If your average weekly earnings are below roughly £216, you receive 90% of your earnings instead, which will be less than £194.32.

Why do the first 6 weeks of maternity pay differ? Statutory Maternity Pay and Statutory Adoption Pay both pay 90% of average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks, with no cap. The flat rate applies only from week 7. The other four statutory payments have no equivalent higher opening period.

Is shared parental pay worse than maternity pay? For the first six weeks, yes, in the sense that Statutory Shared Parental Pay has no 90% opening period. Converting maternity leave into shared parental leave during those first six weeks means those weeks are paid at the flat rate rather than at 90% of earnings. After week 6 the rates are the same.

Does this apply if I am self employed? No. These are statutory payments made by an employer through payroll. Self employed people are not eligible for Statutory Maternity Pay and would need to look at Maternity Allowance instead, which has its own rules and its own rate.
