The Cheap Quote Is the One Where You Do the Lifting

A skip and a man with a van are not the same product and comparing their prices directly tells you nothing. One is a container. The other is a container plus two people to fill it. Here is the comparison done per cubic yard, and the legal check that matters more than either price.

The short version

  • An 8 yard builders skip is 280 to 420 pounds for a two week hire including delivery, collection and landfill tax. That is about 42 pounds a cubic yard.
  • A man and van clearing 6 to 8 cubic yards is 220 to 400 pounds, which is about 40 pounds a cubic yard, and the labour is included.
  • If the skip goes on the road you need a council permit, commonly 30 to 60 pounds for one or two weeks, plus lights and markings. On your own drive you need nothing.
  • Check the waste carrier licence on the GOV.UK register before you pay anybody. Under the household duty of care, waste traced back to you after fly tipping is your problem, with penalties running to 600 pounds or prosecution.
  • Skips are cheaper only when you load them. Once you price your own weekend at anything, the man and van is usually ahead for a house clearance and behind for a building job.

1. Sizes, and what a cubic yard actually holds

Skips are sold in cubic yards and almost nobody has an instinct for what that means. One cubic yard is roughly a cube a little under a metre on each side. In practical terms, about eight to ten full builders bags, or twenty five to thirty bin bags of light household waste.

The common domestic sizes are the 4 yard mini, which is about right for a bathroom strip out or a small garden clearance, the 6 yard midi, the 8 yard builder, which is the most hired size in the country and takes a kitchen or a loft clearance comfortably, and the 12 yard maxi, which is for bulky light waste only because most operators will not lift a full one of soil or rubble.

That weight limit catches people out. A 12 yard skip filled with plasterboard or hardcore will exceed what the lorry can legally lift, and the operator will refuse to take it or charge to remove some. For soil, rubble, concrete or tiles, use a 4 or 6 yard skip and fill it level, not heaped.

A man and van is sold by volume of the van, usually described as a Luton or a tipper load. A typical single load is six to eight cubic yards, which is why it compares most directly with an 8 yard skip.

3. The permit, and when you need one

This is the single most common surprise on a skip bill.

If the skip stands entirely on your own private land, a driveway or a front garden, you need no permission from anybody. If any part of it stands on the public highway, which includes the road, the verge and the pavement, you need a permit from the local highway authority. Councils typically charge 30 to 60 pounds for one to two weeks, some charge more, and some require the skip company to apply on your behalf rather than you.

The permit comes with conditions: reflective markings, cones, and lamps at each corner from dusk to dawn. The skip must also carry the operator's name and telephone number. Those are the operator's responsibility to supply but they are commonly the householder's problem when a council officer turns up.

Two things worth asking before you book. Whether the permit fee is inside or outside the quoted price, because both are common. And whether a permit is even available on your street, because some councils refuse them on bus routes, on red routes and on narrow terraced streets, which quietly makes the decision for you.

A man and van needs no permit at all, because the vehicle is parked rather than placed. On a street with no driveway and no skip permits, that is often the only workable option.

4. The duty of care, which is the expensive bit

Here is the part that costs more than every price in the table above put together if you get it wrong.

Anyone who takes waste away from your home for payment must be a registered waste carrier. You can check them free on the GOV.UK register of waste carriers, brokers and dealers in about two minutes using the company name or the registration number.

If you hand waste to somebody unregistered and it is later fly tipped, the duty of care means you can be held responsible. Councils routinely go through fly tipped waste looking for envelopes, delivery labels and anything else with an address on it, and they do prosecute. Penalties for a householder who failed to check run to a fixed penalty of up to 600 pounds, and a prosecution can go further.

The practical protection is cheap and takes five minutes. Ask for the carrier registration number. Check it on the register. Ask for a waste transfer note or at minimum a receipt showing the company name, the vehicle registration and where the waste is going. A legitimate operator produces all of that without hesitating, because they are asked for it every day.

A quote that is dramatically below everybody else's, cash only, with no company name on the van, is not a bargain. It is a bill you have not received yet.

5. Worked example: the Kaur clearance in Coventry

Harpreet Kaur is clearing her late father's house in Coventry. A three bedroom terrace, forty years of accumulation, roughly eight cubic yards of furniture, boxes and general household waste. There is no driveway.

Option one: an 8 yard skip at 340 pounds, plus a highway permit at 45 pounds because it has to go on the road. Total 385 pounds, and she and her brother load it over a weekend. Call it fourteen hours between them.

Option two: a man and van at 360 pounds for a full load, cleared in an afternoon with no lifting by her at all.

The van is 25 pounds cheaper and saves fourteen hours of two people's time. On those numbers there is no argument.

What she does before paying is the thing worth copying. She asks the van firm for its waste carrier registration number, checks it on the GOV.UK register, and gets a receipt naming the transfer station. The first firm she rang could not give her a number and said it was not needed for household waste. That is wrong, and she used somebody else.

One extra saving: she listed the dining table, two wardrobes and a chest of drawers on a local selling page before booking anything, and they went in four days for 140 pounds. That also took about a cubic yard and a half out of the load, which is why the single van load was enough.

Frequently asked

What cannot go in a skip? Fridges and freezers, televisions and monitors, tyres, paint, asbestos, batteries, gas cylinders, mattresses in many areas, and anything classed as hazardous. Operators charge separately for most of these and refuse some outright. Ask the list before the skip arrives, not after you have buried a fridge in it.

Can I fill a skip above the sides? No. It must be level with the top, because an overloaded skip cannot legally be sheeted and carried. Operators will refuse to lift it or charge to level it off, and either way you have lost a day.

Is a wait and load skip cheaper? It avoids the permit entirely because the lorry stays with the skip, usually for an hour or two. It costs about the same as a standard hire but it only works if your waste is already bagged and ready, which for a garden clearance it often is.

Will the council take bulky items instead? Most councils run a bulky waste collection at 20 to 60 pounds for three to five items, which is far cheaper than either option here for a sofa and a couple of mattresses. It is slow, often two to four weeks, and it is the cheapest route by a distance if you are not in a hurry.

The honest verdict

For a house clearance with no driveway, use a man and van. At about 40 pounds a cubic yard with the labour included, it beats an 8 yard skip once you add the permit, and it takes an afternoon instead of a weekend.

For a building project, hire the skip, because what you are really buying is fourteen days of somewhere to put things. Either way, check the waste carrier registration on GOV.UK first. That check is free and it is worth more than the price difference between every option on this page.
