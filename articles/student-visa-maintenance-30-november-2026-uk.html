Student Visa Money Rule Rises On 30 November: £1,570 A Month In London, £1,203 Elsewhere. Apply Before Then And The Old Figures Apply.

On 3 September 2026 the Home Office laid Statement of Changes HC 584. Among many changes, three short paragraphs raise the amount of money a Student visa applicant must show for living costs. The new figures apply to applications made on or after 30 November 2026. This is a plain explanation of what changes, by how much, who it does not affect, and how the timing works for anyone applying this autumn. It is general information, not immigration or legal advice.

The short version

  • From 30 November 2026 the monthly maintenance figure rises from £1,529 to £1,570 for study in London and from £1,171 to £1,203 elsewhere. Source: HC 584, paragraphs APP ST8 and APP ST9, amending Appendix Student ST 12.3.
  • Over the 9 month maximum that is £14,130 in London (up £369) and £10,827 outside (up £288). Our arithmetic.
  • The accommodation offset cap rises too, from £1,529 to £1,570 (APP ST10, ST 12.4), so a paid accommodation deposit can reduce the funds shown by up to that amount.
  • Applications made before 30 November 2026 are decided under the rules in force on 29 November, so the old figures apply. That is in the Implementation section of HC 584.
  • What does not change: the 28 day rule for holding the money, the 12 month exemption for people already in the UK, dependants' figures, course fees, and the London definition.
  • Why: the Home Office aligns the figure each year with the maintenance loan available to home students, which the Department for Education reviews annually. Explanatory Memorandum paragraph 5.43.

1. What HC 584 changes, exactly

Appendix Student of the Immigration Rules requires most applicants to show they hold enough money to support themselves for each month of their course, up to nine months, in addition to course fees. The figures are in paragraph ST 12.3: one for study in London, one for study elsewhere. A paid accommodation deposit can reduce the amount shown, up to a cap in ST 12.4.

Statement of Changes HC 584, laid before Parliament on 3 September 2026, makes three substitutions:

RuleNowFrom 30 November 2026HC 584 paragraph
ST 12.3(a), monthly amount, study in London£1,529£1,570APP ST8
ST 12.3(b), monthly amount, study outside London£1,171£1,203APP ST9
ST 12.4, maximum accommodation offset£1,529£1,570APP ST10

Over the nine month maximum that is £14,130 for a London course, up £369, and £10,827 elsewhere, up £288. The rise is 2.7%, which is the same uplift applied to the home student maintenance loan for 2026 to 2027, and that is the stated reason: paragraph 5.43 of the Explanatory Memorandum says the level "is aligned with the maintenance loans available for home students, which is reviewed every year by the Department for Education", and that the Home Office will keep it aligned in future. Paragraph 5.44 notes that the annual uplift was agreed as part of the student route measures announced in May 2024.

The rest of HC 584 is unrelated to money: Erasmus+ participants gain some exemptions in Appendix Student, and there are changes for Skilled Workers recognised as victims of modern slavery, which we cover separately.

2. The 30 November cut-off and how applications straddling it are decided

The Implementation section of HC 584 says that APP ST8 to APP ST10 take effect on 30 November 2026, and that where an application for entry clearance or permission to stay has been made before 30 November 2026, it will be decided in accordance with the Immigration Rules in force on 29 November 2026.

In plain terms: the date that matters is the date you submit the application, not the date it is decided and not the date the course starts. Submit on 29 November and the £1,529 and £1,171 figures apply, even if the decision comes in December. Submit on 30 November and the new figures apply.

The other timing rule is unchanged. GOV.UK's student visa guidance requires the money to have been held for at least 28 consecutive days, with the end of that 28 day period falling within 31 days of the application date. So an applicant working to the old figures needs the funds in place from around the start of November at the latest, and the bank evidence must line up with that window.

3. What the rule does not do

Because this is the Immigration Rules, the limits matter as much as the change.

  • It does not change course fees. The requirement to show fees for the first year, as stated on the Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, is separate and unaffected.
  • It does not change the 12 month exemption. Someone who has been in the UK with valid permission for at least 12 months on the date of application does not have to show maintenance funds at all. HC 584 does not touch that.
  • It does not change the dependants' figures. APP ST8 to ST10 amend only ST 12.3 and ST 12.4. The separate monthly amounts for a partner or child are not in those paragraphs.
  • It does not change the 28 day rule, the 31 day window, or the nine month cap.
  • It does not change what counts as London: the City of London and the 32 London boroughs, as GOV.UK sets out.
  • It does not affect anyone who already holds a Student visa unless and until they make a new application on or after 30 November 2026 that requires funds.

It also does not apply to Child Students at residential independent schools, where boarding fees on the CAS take the place of the maintenance figure.

4. A worked example: Farhana, Manchester, January start

Farhana in Dhaka has an unconditional offer for a one year MSc in Manchester starting on 18 January 2027. Her course fee is £19,500 and she has paid a £1,200 deposit to university accommodation. She has not been in the UK before, so the 12 month exemption does not apply.

If she applies on or before 29 November 2026: the outside-London figure is £1,171 for nine months, £10,539. Her accommodation deposit of £1,200 can be offset in full because it is under the £1,529 cap. Funds to show for living costs: £9,339, plus the £19,500 fee, or whatever remains of it after any fee already paid.

If she applies on or after 30 November 2026: £1,203 for nine months, £10,827, less the same £1,200 deposit: £9,627. The difference is £288.

For the earlier date she needs the money held continuously for 28 days ending within 31 days of the application. Applying on 25 November means the 28 day period must end between 25 October and 25 November, so the funds have to be in place by late October. Her CAS also has to have been issued, and the visa cannot be applied for more than six months before the course start, which for an 18 January start means from 18 July onward, so the window is open.

£288 is not a large sum against a £19,500 fee. What the example really shows is that the timing is controllable: the money, the CAS and the 28 day period can be lined up for November, and there is no reason to let an application drift into December by accident.

5. Where to get free, reliable help

Three sources cost nothing and know this rule well. The international student office at the university that issued the CAS deals with the maintenance rule every week and can check the bank statements before submission. UKCISA, the UK Council for International Student Affairs, publishes plain-English guidance on the money requirement and runs a student advice line. And the GOV.UK student visa pages give the current figures and the 28 day rule in the government's own words. The rule text itself is in Appendix Student on GOV.UK, and HC 584 with its Explanatory Memorandum is published in full.

Nobody needs to pay an adviser to find out the maintenance figure. Where an application is complicated for other reasons, only an adviser registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (the regulator formerly called the OISC) or a solicitor should be giving immigration advice, and the IAA register is free to search.

6. The honest verdict

The change is real, small and dated. £1,570 and £1,203 a month from 30 November 2026, up £41 and £32; £369 and £288 over nine months.

The application date decides which figures apply, not the course date. Applications made by 29 November are assessed on the current amounts.

Nothing else in the money rule moves: not the 28 days, not the 12 month exemption, not dependants, not fees. Anyone told otherwise should ask for the paragraph number.

Frequently asked

I applied in October 2026 and my decision comes in December. Which figures apply? The ones in force when you applied. HC 584 says applications made before 30 November 2026 are decided under the rules in force on 29 November 2026.

Does the change affect the money I must show for my husband or child? No. APP ST8 to ST10 amend only ST 12.3 and ST 12.4, the applicant's own monthly amount and the accommodation offset cap. Dependants' amounts are in separate paragraphs that HC 584 does not change.

I have been in the UK on a visa for over a year. Do I need to show the money? No. The exemption for applicants who have held valid permission in the UK for at least 12 months on the date of application is unchanged.

Is this legal advice? No. It is general information taken from HC 584, its Explanatory Memorandum and GOV.UK. For advice on your own application use your university's international office, UKCISA, or an adviser registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.
