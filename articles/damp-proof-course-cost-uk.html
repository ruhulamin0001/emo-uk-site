The Most Expensive Damp Is the Damp You Did Not Have

An injected damp proof course costs between 300 and 4,000 pounds depending on how many walls get done. The uncomfortable part is that the majority of walls quoted for one are not suffering from rising damp, and the real fault often costs less than a tank of fuel to put right.

The short version

  • An injected chemical DPC costs roughly 60 to 100 pounds a linear metre, so a single wall is 300 to 700 pounds and a whole ground floor is 2,000 to 4,000 once replastering is included.
  • Replastering is the bigger half of the bill. The injection itself is cheap. The sand and cement render that follows, at 40 to 70 pounds a square metre, is what fills out the quote.
  • Most low level damp is condensation or a bridged damp proof course. A blocked airbrick, a raised path or a flowerbed banked against the wall will produce the same tide mark for about 40 pounds of remedy.
  • The payback question here is inverted. Spending 2,400 pounds on a DPC you did not need does not pay back at all, and it usually hides the real fault behind waterproof render for another five years.
  • Get a diagnosis from someone with nothing to sell you. An independent damp and timber surveyor charges 350 to 600 pounds and does not also quote for the work.

1. The four things people call rising damp

A dark band along the bottom of a wall, salts blooming through the paint, skirting board going soft. Four completely different faults produce that picture and only one of them is rising damp.

Condensation is by a distance the most common. Warm moist air meets a cold surface and water comes out of it. It shows worst on north facing walls, behind furniture and in corners, and it is usually accompanied by black mould, which rising damp does not produce. The fix is ventilation and heat, not chemicals.

Penetrating damp comes in sideways through a defect. A cracked downpipe, failed pointing, a leaking gutter, a blocked cavity. It gets worse after rain and better in a dry spell, which is the tell.

A bridged damp proof course is where the house has a perfectly good DPC and something is carrying moisture over it. This one gets its own section below because it is so common.

Genuine rising damp is groundwater drawn up through the masonry by capillary action, where there is either no DPC at all or the original one has failed. It exists. It is simply far rarer than the number of quotes issued for it, and it should be diagnosed by what the wall is actually doing, not by a hand held conductivity meter, which measures electrical resistance and will read high on salt contaminated plaster that is bone dry.

2. The bridged DPC, the commonest cause of all

Approved Document C expects a damp proof course to sit clear of the outside ground level, normally by around 150 millimetres. That gap is not decoration. It is the whole mechanism.

Now think about what happens to houses over forty years. A patio gets laid and the level comes up. A driveway is resurfaced over the old one rather than dug out. A border is dug and the soil is banked against the wall. Render is applied and carried down past the DPC line to finish neatly at the ground. In every one of those cases the moisture now has a path over the top of a DPC that is still working perfectly.

The remedy is to lower the ground, cut the render back above the DPC line, or dig a narrow gravel channel along the wall. That is an afternoon with a spade and a bag of gravel. Call it 40 pounds if you do it and 250 if a builder does. It is the single cheapest fix in this entire guide and it is the one most often skipped, because the firm inspecting the wall is also the firm quoting for the injection.

4. What an injected DPC actually costs, line by line

If the diagnosis really is rising damp, here is where the money goes. The injection is cheap: holes drilled at intervals along the mortar bed and a silicone or cream damp proof cream injected, at roughly 60 to 100 pounds a linear metre of wall including labour. A single 5 metre wall is therefore 300 to 500 pounds.

The expensive half is what follows. The existing plaster is contaminated with hygroscopic salts and will keep pulling moisture out of the air no matter how dry the wall gets, so it has to come off, usually to a metre above the highest tide mark, and be replaced with a sand and cement render containing a waterproofer. That runs 40 to 70 pounds a square metre. Add skirting, architrave, redecoration and the removal of a skip's worth of rubble.

A whole ground floor of a two bed terrace, say 22 linear metres, lands between 2,000 and 4,000 pounds all in. The injection part of that is maybe 1,600. The rest is plaster and making good.

One thing to insist on. A guarantee from the installing firm is only worth anything while the firm exists, and this trade has a high failure rate. Ask whether the guarantee is insurance backed through a third party. If it is not, price it at zero.

5. Worked example: Mrs Byrne in Chesterfield

Eileen Byrne has an 1890s terrace in Chesterfield. The front room has a tide mark about 700 millimetres up the party wall and the paint is lifting. A damp company inspects free of charge, runs a meter along the skirting, and quotes 2,850 pounds for injection and replastering of two walls.

She pays 420 pounds for an independent surveyor instead. He finds two things in ninety minutes. The neighbour's yard was concreted about a decade ago and the new slab sits 90 millimetres above her DPC line, which is bridging it along the party wall. And the front room has no ventilation at all since the chimney was blocked without a vent, which is why the mould is in the corner.

The remedies are a 60 millimetre deep gravel channel cut along the base of the party wall where it is accessible, at 190 pounds, and an air vent cut into the blocked chimney breast at 140 pounds. Total 330 pounds, plus the 420 pound survey, so 750 pounds against a 2,850 pound quote. The wall dried out over the following summer and the plaster was patched for 180 pounds the year after.

The saving is 2,100 pounds. But the part that matters more is that the 2,850 pound job would not have worked, because neither of her actual faults is rising damp, and waterproof render would have hidden both of them until the timber floor gave way.

Frequently asked

Does a damp meter prove rising damp? No. A pin type meter measures electrical conductivity. Salt contaminated plaster conducts well even when it is completely dry, which is why meters read high on walls that have already been fixed. A proper diagnosis uses the pattern of the damp, the external ground levels and, where it matters, a calcium carbide test or a drilled sample sent for gravimetric analysis.

Will my mortgage lender insist on a DPC? A valuer who spots damp will often make the report subject to a specialist damp and timber report, and lenders then retain funds until it is done. That is a request for a diagnosis, not an instruction to inject. An independent report clearing the wall satisfies it just as well, and costs less.

Do the new cream injection systems work better than the old fluids? They are easier to apply consistently, which matters, because the failure mode of injected DPCs is almost always incomplete application rather than the chemistry. That is an argument for watching the work being done, not for paying more.

Should I use a dehumidifier while it dries? For condensation, yes, it treats the actual cause. For a wall drying after remedial work, it speeds things up but it is not the fix. A wall dries at roughly a month per 25 millimetres of thickness and nothing hurries that much.

The honest verdict

Do not buy a damp proof course from the company that diagnosed the damp for free. Pay 350 to 600 pounds for an independent surveyor who does not do remedial work, and in most houses you will find the answer is ground levels, guttering or ventilation, and costs a few hundred pounds rather than a few thousand.

If the independent report does say rising damp, then spend the money without hesitation, budget 2,000 to 4,000 pounds for a ground floor, and insist on an insurance backed guarantee rather than a company one.
