Side Hustle Ideas You Can Start for Under £100 in the UK

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

You do not need money to make money, but a little helps. The best beginner side hustles cost under £100 to start, so a slow month cannot ruin you. Here are the honest options, what each really needs, and roughly what it pays, so you can pick one that fits your life.

Reselling and flipping

Buy low, sell higher. Charity shops, car boots and clearance sales are full of items worth more than their price to the right buyer. Startup cost is tiny, just your first bit of stock, and you learn as you go. Our declutter and sell guide and retro game flipping guide cover this in detail.

Skills you already have

If you can write, design, edit, translate, or build a spreadsheet, people pay for it. Freelancing costs nothing to start beyond the laptop you own. The trick is charging properly from the start, which our rate setting guide explains.

Teaching what you know

Tutoring, coaching a hobby, or teaching a practical skill pays well per hour and costs almost nothing to begin. If you can explain something clearly, there is demand, as covered in our online tutoring guide.

Making and selling

Print on demand lets you sell designs with no stock and no upfront cost, since the product is only made when someone buys. Our print on demand guide shows the real profit per item. Simple handmade goods sold locally or on marketplaces also fit under £100 to start.

Local services

Cleaning, gardening, dog walking, car cleaning and small repairs need little kit and have steady local demand. A few tools and a post in a community group can bring your first customer this week. See our car detailing guide for how one of these works in practice.

How to choose the right one

  1. Be honest about what you have more of, time or money, and pick a hustle that matches.
  2. Choose something you can start this week, not something that needs months of setup.
  3. Pick one and commit. The person doing five ideas badly earns less than the person doing one well.
  4. Track what you spend and earn from day one so you know your real profit, not a guess.

The rules that apply to all of them

Whatever you choose, the tax rule is the same. Once your total side hustle income passes £1,000 in a tax year you register for Self Assessment, as set out in our side hustle tax guide. Doing it right from the start saves stress later.

The honest bottom line

A good side hustle does not need big money, it needs a clear choice and steady effort. Start under £100, pick one idea that fits your time, and give it real weeks rather than a half hearted try. Fit it around a busy life using our side hustle productivity guide.
