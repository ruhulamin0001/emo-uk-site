There is one number that sorts a side hustle from a hobby that pays badly, and it is the National Living Wage. From April 2026 that is £12.71 an hour. If a side hustle earns you less than that after costs and unpaid admin, you would be better off with more hours at work.

1. The £12.71 test, and how to apply it

From April 2026 the National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 an hour. That is the floor an employer is legally required to pay. It is the right benchmark for a side hustle because it is the alternative you always have.

Applying it properly means counting the hours nobody counts:

  • Sourcing. Time in charity shops, time scrolling listings.
  • Preparation. Cleaning, photographing, writing the description.
  • Admin. Messaging buyers, packing, the trip to the post office.
  • Travel. Both ways, and the fuel or fare.
  • Failure. The items that never sell, the client who cancels.

An £8 charity shop jacket sold for £35 looks like £27 profit. Take off Vinted postage handling and packaging, then divide the rest by the 45 minutes of sourcing, cleaning, photographing, listing, messaging and posting, and you are usually somewhere between £15 and £25 an hour. Still a pass, but a long way from £27.

Every side hustle has a headline number and an hourly number. The headline number is what gets shared. The hourly number is what decides whether you are still doing it in March.

3. The four that clear the bar easily

Dog walking. Average UK rates in 2026 run £16 to £25 an hour, with £20 emerging as the norm. Walking several dogs together, walkers typically earn £9 to £18 per dog per hour, so three dogs on one walk is where the money is. Startup is a lead, poo bags, a hi vis and public liability insurance, which is the one thing not to skip. Under £60 all in. Walking needs no licence in England, but boarding a dog overnight does once you pass the £1,000 trading allowance, and the difference is explained in our dog walking costs and licensing guide. The limits are honest ones: it is weather dependent, it is physical, and clients want the same slot every day, so it eats a fixed part of your week.

Freelancing something you already do at work. The highest paying option on the list, and the one people overlook because they assume their skill is ordinary. Bookkeeping, copywriting, spreadsheet building, translation, social media for a local business, basic web fixes. £20 to £60 an hour, and startup is close to zero. Check your employment contract for anything on outside work or conflicts of interest before you start.

Private tutoring. £20 to £40 an hour for a subject you genuinely know, more for A level and for maths and sciences. Startup is a decent webcam if you tutor online, or nothing if you already have one. You will need a DBS check for most in person work with under 18s. What a tutor actually clears after platform commission and no-shows is worked through in our online tutoring income guide.

Reselling on Vinted. The important structural fact: Vinted charges the seller nothing, the buyer pays the protection fee. On a £15 item that difference decides whether it is worth listing at all. Casual sellers at 5 to 10 hours a week land at £200 to £600 a month. Start with your own wardrobe, which has zero cost and teaches you what sells before you risk money sourcing.

4. The three that depend entirely on you

Car cleaning and detailing. £12 to £22 an hour, and the spread is about whether you have somewhere to work and can charge for a proper detail rather than a wash. £70 to £100 gets you a decent starter kit. Winter is quiet, and you are competing with £6 hand car washes on price, so this only works if you sell a better job rather than a cheaper one.

eBay reselling of small electronics. £10 to £20 an hour. Higher margins than clothes, but eBay takes a final value fee and returns are far more common because buyers test things. Works well if you can test and repair. Poorly if you cannot, because you will buy faults you did not spot.

Prolific and paid research. The one survey platform that generally passes. Prolific's own payment rules set a floor of £6 an hour, tell researchers to pay at least £9, and the better academic and commercial studies pay £12 to £15. So it clears the £12.71 test on the good studies, not by default, and the published hourly rate is measured on the researcher's time estimate rather than how long you actually take. We have measured it on median completion time in our guide to whether paid surveys are worth your time. The catch is availability rather than rate: you cannot decide to do four hours, you take what appears. Treat it as filling gaps, not as a schedule.

5. The two that fail, and when they still make sense

General survey apps. Effective rates run £3 to £12 an hour, and the low end is far more common than the high end once you count screen outs, the surveys where you spend six minutes answering questions and are then told you do not qualify. At £3 an hour you are working for a quarter of minimum wage.

Mystery shopping. £6 to £14 an hour once you count the travel, the report writing and the wait for reimbursement. It looks better than it is because the meal or the item feels like extra pay.

When they still make sense. If you were making the trip anyway, or you are on a bus with dead time, the alternative is not £12.71 an hour, it is £0. In that specific situation, low pay beats no pay. What does not work is treating them as a plan.

6. The tax bit, in plain English

  • The trading allowance is £1,000, and it is on gross income, not profit. Sell £1,400 of clothes and make £300 after costs, and you have still crossed the threshold. This catches more people than any other rule.
  • Property income has its own separate £1,000 allowance, so renting a driveway does not eat into your trading allowance.
  • The platforms already report you. Since January 2024, eBay, Vinted, Etsy, Airbnb and similar have been required to collect and report seller data to HMRC. Guessing is no longer a strategy.
  • Selling your own used possessions is normally not trading. Clearing your wardrobe is different from buying stock to resell. The distinction is intent, and it starts from your first sourced item.
  • Keep records from day one. Date, item, what you paid, what you sold it for, postage, fees. A single spreadsheet is enough, and reconstructing it in January is miserable.

7. A worked first month

Marcus, 26, Bristol. £95 to start, eight hours a week.

Spent: public liability insurance £46 a year, leads and bags £14, a phone tripod and ring light for listing photos £35. Total £95.

Dog walking. Two regular clients, three walks a week each, walked together. Six walks at one hour, £18 a walk when doubled up. £108 a week, £432 in the month, for about 8 hours including travel. £13.50 an hour once travel is counted.

Vinted, from his own wardrobe. 23 items sold for £287 gross. No seller fees. Packaging £11. About 9 hours across the month photographing, listing and posting. £30.67 an hour, and this is the number people misread, because it was his own stuff so there was no cost of goods. Repeating it means buying stock, and the rate then drops to roughly £16 to £20.

Month one total: £708 income, £106 of costs, £602 net, about 17 hours a week for the first month. Against £12.71 an hour, he passed. Against the fantasy of £3,000 a month, he did not, and that gap is where most people quit.

The tax position: his own wardrobe clearance is not trading. The dog walking is, at £432 in one month, so he will pass £1,000 of gross trading income within three months and needs to register for Self Assessment. If he sells on a platform, HMRC may write to him first, because it now holds two years of Vinted, eBay and Etsy seller data; what those letters mean and how to answer one is in our HMRC nudge letter guide.

The honest verdict

The best side hustle for you is almost never the trendiest one. It is the one that uses a skill you already have, because that is the only route to £25 an hour and above without a year of learning.

Run the £12.71 test before you start and again after month one with real numbers. If it fails twice, stop, because more hours at £6 an hour is not persistence, it is a worse job.

And start with what you already own. Selling your own wardrobe teaches you what sells, what postage really costs and how much time listing takes, with no capital at risk. Everything after that is the same skill applied to stock you paid for.

Frequently asked

Do I need to tell my employer? Check your contract. Many have clauses on outside work or conflicts of interest. Most employers are relaxed about dog walking and unhappy about you freelancing for a competitor.

Do I need insurance for dog walking? Public liability is not legally required but going without it is a serious risk, since you are responsible for someone's dog in public. It is roughly £40 to £60 a year and it is the one cost not to cut.

Does selling my old clothes count as income? Normally no. Selling your own used possessions is not trading. Buying items specifically to resell is trading from the first sale, and then the £1,000 gross threshold applies.

What is the fastest to first pound? Selling things you already own. No sourcing, no capital, and you can list this evening.
