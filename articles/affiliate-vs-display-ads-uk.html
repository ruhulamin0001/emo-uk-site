Affiliate Links Or Display Ads: The Traffic Number Everyone Quotes Decides Nothing

The standard advice is to run affiliate links until you hit some session threshold, then switch to display ads. Work the arithmetic through and the traffic figure cancels out of both sides of the equation. What is left is a single number, measured per thousand visits, and you can find yours in about a month.

The short version

  • Both models scale linearly with traffic, so when you compare them, traffic cancels out. The advice to "switch at 50,000 sessions" has no arithmetic behind it.
  • The number that decides it is revenue per 1,000 sessions. Work out what your affiliate links earn per 1,000 visits, and that is the display RPM you would need to beat.
  • We are not quoting an RPM figure and that is deliberate. No ad network publishes verified UK RPM data. Every figure circulating online is either network marketing or one publisher's single data point.
  • Amazon Associates UK runs a 24-hour cookie, so a visitor who returns three days later earns you nothing. Display ads pay on the visit itself.
  • Display ads cost you page speed, which is a real trade for a site that depends on search traffic.
  • Running both is normal and is usually the right answer for a site with mixed content.

1. What each model actually pays you for

These two things feel like alternatives because they occupy the same space on a page. They are not remotely the same product.

Display advertising pays you for attention. An advertiser buys impressions on your page. You are paid whether or not the reader cares, clicks, or buys. Your revenue is a function of how many pages get loaded and what advertisers are willing to pay to appear on them.

Affiliate marketing pays you for a completed purchase. Nothing happens unless a reader clicks your link, lands on a retailer, buys, and the purchase sticks through the returns window. Four things have to go right rather than one.

That difference has a consequence people miss. Display revenue is stable and roughly predictable. Affiliate revenue is lumpy, seasonal, and can collapse if a single retailer changes its commission rate. Amazon has cut UK category rates more than once, and every publisher who depended on one category found out on the day.

There is also a timing difference that matters more than most people allow for. Amazon Associates in the UK runs a 24-hour cookie on a standard click. A reader who reads your review on Tuesday and buys on Friday earns you nothing at all. A display ad on that same Tuesday visit paid you on Tuesday.

2. The arithmetic, and why traffic cancels out

Here is the standard advice, in its usual form: run affiliate links while you are small, then when you reach some session threshold, apply to an ad network. It sounds like experience. It is not arithmetic.

Write both models out.

Display revenue equals sessions divided by 1,000, multiplied by your RPM, the revenue you earn per thousand sessions.

Affiliate revenue equals sessions, multiplied by the share of visitors who click a link, multiplied by the share of those who buy, multiplied by the average order value, multiplied by your commission rate.

Now look at what happens when you double your traffic. Both sides double. Triple it, both sides triple. Sessions appear once, linearly, on both sides of the comparison, so they cancel. The winner at 5,000 sessions is the winner at 500,000 sessions, assuming your audience behaves the same way at both sizes.

Which means the real question is not "how much traffic do I have", it is "what does one thousand visits to my site earn under each model". That is a number you can compute.

Take a set of assumptions and hold them constant. Say 3% of visitors click an affiliate link, 8% of those clickers buy something, the average order is £32, and you earn 3% commission. Run that through:

Sessions per monthAffiliate revenue on those assumptionsDisplay RPM you would need to match it
5,000£11.52£2.30 per 1,000 sessions
20,000£46.08£2.30 per 1,000 sessions
50,000£115.20£2.30 per 1,000 sessions

The right-hand column does not move. That is the whole point. These are our own calculations from the stated assumptions, not figures from anybody's research, and if your assumptions differ the answer differs, but the structure does not.

We are deliberately not telling you what UK display RPMs are. No ad network publishes audited RPM data by country or niche. Every figure you have read is either a network's own marketing or one publisher screenshotting one month. Quoting a number here would make this article feel more useful and be less true. What we can tell you is the number you have to beat, and that you can find out your own within a month by simply running the ads.

3. What each one quietly costs you

Display ads cost you page speed. Ad scripts are third-party JavaScript, they load assets you do not control, and they move content around as they render. On a site that lives on search traffic, that is a real cost, not a theoretical one. It is also the cost that publishers systematically underestimate because the revenue shows up in a dashboard and the traffic loss does not.

Display ads cost you control of what appears beside your work. On a money site that matters more than most. You may find yourself hosting an ad for exactly the sort of product your article warns readers away from.

Affiliate links cost you compliance work. Every affiliate link on a UK site has to be labelled so a reader knows it is advertising before they meet it, and the regulator has already ruled against several of the popular wordings. That is covered in full in our guide to UK affiliate disclosure rules.

Affiliate links cost you editorial freedom, subtly. If your income depends on readers buying, there is a permanent quiet pressure to recommend rather than to conclude that nobody needs the product. Display ads do not create that pressure, which is a genuine argument in their favour that almost nobody makes out loud.

4. Worked example: two sites, same traffic, opposite answers

Worked example: Bethan and Callum, both on 18,000 sessions

Bethan runs a UK camera gear review site. Her readers arrive on "best lens for" searches with a purchase already in mind. Roughly 6% click through to a retailer, about 9% of those buy, and the average order is high because lenses are expensive. Even at a 3% commission rate the revenue per thousand sessions is substantial, because the order values are.

Callum runs a UK household budgeting blog. His readers arrive on "how do I" questions. They read, they leave better informed, and they buy nothing, because the honest answer to most of his articles is that there is nothing to buy. His affiliate click rate is under 1% and his conversion on those clicks is worse, because the few products that fit are cheap.

Same 18,000 sessions. Opposite correct answers. Bethan's affiliate revenue per thousand sessions is high enough that display ads would have to be exceptional to compete, and she also loses page speed she cannot spare on competitive commercial keywords. Callum's affiliate revenue per thousand sessions is close to zero no matter how many visitors he gets, so display advertising is the only model that pays him anything at all for work people clearly value.

Neither decision has anything to do with the size of the audience. It is entirely about what that audience came to do.

5. How to measure your own number in 30 days

You do not have to model this. You can measure it.

  1. Get your affiliate revenue per 1,000 sessions. Take last month's affiliate earnings, divide by last month's sessions, multiply by 1,000. That single number is your benchmark. Use a full month; a week is too noisy given how lumpy affiliate income is.
  2. Split it by page type. Roundups and reviews will be dramatically higher than explainers. This is the step most people skip, and it is the one that produces the real answer, which is usually "both, on different pages".
  3. Run display ads on your lowest-earning section only, for a month, and compare like for like. Do not test on your best pages. If you are wrong you have damaged the pages that pay you.
  4. Measure page speed and search traffic on the test section too, before and after. Revenue that costs you rankings is not revenue, it is a loan.
  5. Re-run it after any Amazon rate change. Category rates move, and a cut can flip the answer for a whole section of your site overnight. Our guide to Amazon UK commission rates has the current position.

6. The honest verdict

The traffic threshold is a myth of the genre. Both models scale linearly with visits, so visits cannot be what decides between them. What decides it is intent: whether the people arriving on a given page are in a buying frame of mind or an information-gathering one.

That is why the answer for almost every real site is "both, page by page". Commercial roundups earn far more from affiliate links than any display network will pay. Explainer content earns almost nothing from affiliate links no matter how well it ranks, and display advertising is the only way it pays for itself.

Work out your affiliate revenue per 1,000 sessions by page type. That single number, which nobody can give you and which takes a month to establish, is the entire decision.

Frequently asked

Can I run both at the same time? Yes, and most established sites do. Networks generally do not prohibit affiliate links, though you should read your specific agreement. The practical constraint is layout: stacking display units around affiliate content tends to hurt both.

Does an ad network need a minimum traffic level? Most set entry requirements, and they change frequently. Check the network's own page on the day you apply rather than trusting any published figure, including a recent one, because these thresholds have moved sharply in both directions during 2025 and 2026.

Do display ads hurt SEO? Not directly, but they add third-party scripts and layout shift, both of which affect the page experience signals Google measures. The honest framing is that display ads cost you speed, and speed is worth something. How much depends on how competitive your keywords are.

What about AI Overviews reducing clicks? It affects both models identically, because it reduces sessions, and sessions sit on both sides of the equation. It does not change which model wins for you. We cover the traffic side of it separately in our piece on affiliate traffic and AI Overviews.
