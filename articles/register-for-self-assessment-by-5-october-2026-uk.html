Deadline 5 October 2026: If Your Side Income First Passed £1,000 Last Year, HMRC Wants to Hear From You This Month

The rule is simple and almost nobody who starts a side hustle knows it. If the tax year that ended on 5 April 2026 was the first one in which you needed to send a tax return, you have to tell HMRC by 5 October 2026. That is a month away. Here is exactly who it catches, what actually happens if you miss it, and why the penalty is far less frightening, and far more precise, than the forum posts suggest.

The short version

  • The deadline is 5 October 2026 for anyone who needs to send a return for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 and has either never sent one, or registered before but did not need to file for 2024/25. Source: GOV.UK, Check how to register for Self Assessment, page updated 2 September 2026.
  • The trigger for most side hustlers is the £1,000 trading allowance, measured on gross sales, not profit. Cross it in 2025/26 and you are in scope. The £3,000 reporting threshold that has been talked about is not law and does not apply to this deadline.
  • The penalty for telling HMRC late is a percentage of the tax you owe, not a flat fine. For a non-deliberate failure disclosed voluntarily within 12 months of the tax falling due, the range is 0% to 30% of the tax. Source: HMRC factsheet CC/FS11, updated 19 March 2026.
  • If no tax is due, the penalty is a percentage of nothing. A student under the personal allowance, or someone whose profit is wiped out by expenses, has nothing to pay in penalty terms even if they register late. They still need to register and file.
  • Registering does not mean paying now. The return for 2025/26 is due online by 31 January 2027, and so is the tax. Registering in September simply gets you the reference number in time.
  • Our verdict: if you are anywhere near the line, register this week. It takes about 20 minutes, it costs nothing, and a return that shows no tax due is a far better outcome than a nudge letter next year.

1. Who has to register by 5 October

GOV.UK puts it in one sentence: you must tell HMRC by 5 October 2026 if you need to complete a tax return for the previous tax year, and you have either not sent one before, or registered before but did not need to send one for 2024 to 2025. The previous tax year ran from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. So the question is not whether you have side income now. It is whether you had enough of it in the twelve months to last April.

For most readers of this site the thing that tips you over is the trading allowance. You can earn £1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year without telling HMRC. Not profit, gross. Sell £1,200 of prints on Etsy and spend £400 on materials and postage, and you are over the line, because the £1,200 is what counts. We go through the allowance, and the trading-versus-decluttering distinction that decides whether selling your own old clothes counts at all, in our side hustle tax rules guide.

The same deadline catches a lodger landlord above the Rent a Room limit, and anyone whose untaxed savings interest or dividends first pushed them into owing tax. HMRC's factsheet puts the self-employment case as "within 6 months of the end of the relevant tax year", which is 5 October by another route.

The proposal to raise the reporting threshold to £3,000 is an announcement, not law, and would not change the £1,000 tax-free amount anyway. For 2025/26 the £1,000 figure applies.

2. What the penalty actually is, in numbers

There is no £100 flat fine for registering late; that fixed penalty belongs to filing the return late after 31 January. Registering late is a "failure to notify", and HMRC's factsheet CC/FS11, updated 19 March 2026, sets the penalty as a percentage of the potential lost revenue, meaning the tax the late registration put at risk.

Your behaviourHow HMRC found outPenalty range
Non-deliberateYou told them, within 12 months of the tax being due0% to 30% of tax
Non-deliberateYou told them, 12 months or more after10% to 30%
Non-deliberateThey found it, within 12 months10% to 30%
Non-deliberateThey found it, 12 months or more after20% to 30%
DeliberateYou told them / they found it20% to 70% / 35% to 70%
Deliberate and concealedYou told them / they found it30% to 100% / 50% to 100%

Read the first row again. Miss the date through ignorance, come forward before HMRC writes to you, and with the tax under 12 months overdue the penalty can be zero. HMRC then moves within the range for telling, helping and giving, and someone who registers late, explains why and files a full return has done all three.

And because the penalty is a percentage of tax, a return showing no tax due produces no penalty at all: a student under the £12,570 personal allowance, a part-timer whose expenses ate the profit. The obligation to register still stands, but the sum at the end is nil.

The picture changes when HMRC finds you first. Since 1 January 2024 Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Airbnb and the rest have had to collect seller data, the first reports reached HMRC in January 2025, and the nudge letters that followed are the subject of our nudge letter guide. A letter turns your disclosure from unprompted to prompted, and the floor of the range rises from 0% to 10%. Registering before the letter arrives is worth real money.

3. How to register, and what arrives afterwards

Go to GOV.UK's "Check how to register for Self Assessment" page and press Start now. It asks a handful of questions, picks the route (self-employed, not self-employed, or partner), and takes you into registration. You need your National Insurance number, address history and a few details about the work. About 20 minutes.

A ten-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference then arrives by post, followed by an activation code if you have no online account. GOV.UK's reply-times page shows that can take several weeks in autumn, which is the practical reason to register in September rather than late January. If you registered in a previous year, reactivate rather than register again: two UTRs cause more trouble than a late registration.

4. A worked example in Colchester

Priya lives in Colchester and started reselling refurbished games consoles in June 2025 alongside her job. By 5 April 2026 she had sold £4,800 worth, having bought the stock for £3,100 and spent £260 on postage and packaging. Her employment income was £31,000, so she is a basic-rate taxpayer with her personal allowance already used up on her salary.

Her gross was £4,800, well over the £1,000 allowance, so she must register by 5 October 2026. Deducting actual expenses, £4,800 less £3,360, gives £1,440 of profit; deducting the £1,000 allowance instead would give £3,800, so expenses win. Tax at 20% on £1,440 is £288. No Class 4 National Insurance, because profit is under £12,570, and no Class 2, though below £7,105 she could pay it voluntarily to protect her State Pension record.

Register in September, file by 31 January 2027, pay the £288 on time: no penalty of any kind. Forget, discover the rule in March 2027 and come forward: the tax fell due on 31 January, so she is inside 12 months and the range is 0% to 30% of £288, at most £86.40 and with full cooperation close to zero. Let an HMRC letter reach her first: the floor is 10%, at least £28.80, and the return is late too, which triggers the separate £100 late-filing penalty. That one actually hurts, and registering on time is how you stop the clock on it.

5. What happens after you register

Nothing, for a while. Registration puts you on HMRC's list for a return; it does not create a bill. The 2025/26 return is due online by 31 January 2027, tax the same day, or 31 October 2026 on paper, a route we weigh in our paper deadline guide. If the bill is over £1,000, HMRC also asks for a payment on account towards the following year, half on 31 January and half on 31 July. Keep the records now: a spreadsheet of sales, a folder of receipts and the platform's annual statement are enough at this size.

6. The honest verdict

If your gross side income first went past £1,000 between April 2025 and April 2026, register for Self Assessment by 5 October 2026. It is free, it takes 20 minutes, and it is the difference between a penalty range that starts at zero and one that starts at 10%.

If you are under the line, or you were only clearing out your own belongings, this deadline does not apply to you and you can stop worrying about it. If you are over it and the profit is small, register anyway: a return showing £50 of tax is a far better place to be than a nudge letter next spring.

Frequently asked

I only made £1,100 in sales. Do I really have to register? Yes, if it was trading rather than selling your own belongings. The allowance is £1,000 of gross sales, and £1,100 is over it. The tax on the profit will be small or nil, but the registration is still required, and it protects you from the higher penalty floor if HMRC's platform data flags you first.

Is there a £100 fine for registering late? No. The £100 fixed penalty is for filing the return late after 31 January. Late registration is a failure to notify, charged as a percentage of the tax at stake, and for a non-deliberate failure disclosed voluntarily within 12 months the range starts at 0%.

What if I miss 5 October but file and pay by 31 January 2027? Register as soon as you realise. Because you have come forward, the disclosure is unprompted, and HMRC measures the potential lost revenue by the tax still unpaid on 31 January, so if you file and pay on time the figure is usually nil and so is the penalty. Do not wait for HMRC to write.

Does registering mean I have to pay tax now? No. It gets you a Unique Taxpayer Reference. The 2025/26 return and any tax are due by 31 January 2027, or 31 October 2026 if you file on paper.
