Amazon's Influencer Programme Pays the Same Rates as Associates. The Only Thing You Are Applying For Is a Storefront

Search for the Amazon Influencer Programme and you will find videos promising it is the upgraded, better-paid version of Amazon affiliate marketing. Amazon's own UK pages say something plainer. It is the Associates programme with a different front door, a page of your own on amazon.co.uk, and an entry test based on your social following. Here is what is the same, what is different, and which one to apply for.

The short version

  • The commission is the same. Amazon UK's Influencer page says the amount you earn is based on the products sold and links to the Associates Programme Standard Commission Income Statement, the same table every Associate uses. Source: affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/influencers, read 4 September 2026.
  • The eligibility is different. Associates accepts websites, apps and social channels. The Influencer Programme requires a YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or TikTok account, a business account if it is Instagram or Facebook, and reviews your follower count and engagement. Amazon publishes no minimum number.
  • The product is a storefront: a page on amazon.co.uk with a personalised URL where you publish shoppable photos, videos and livestreams, which Amazon may also surface to its own shoppers. Ordinary Associates do not get one.
  • Everything else carries over: the 24 hour cookie, the Operating Agreement, the disclosure rules and the ban on hand-typing prices or ratings apply to both.
  • Amazon says media companies and brands should apply to Associates instead. A blog with a social account is an Associate; a creator whose audience lives on social is an Influencer. The audience, not the money, decides.
  • Our verdict: if your traffic is a website, join Associates today. If your audience is on social and you can meet the unpublished follower bar, apply to the Influencer Programme for the storefront, and expect the same commission per sale either way.

1. What is the same: the money

Amazon UK's Influencer Programme page answers the question most people come with, how much will I earn, in two sentences. You can start earning commission when your followers make qualifying purchases via your affiliate links, and the amount you earn is based on the products sold. The words commission rates can be found here link to Associates Central and open the Associates Programme Standard Commission Income Statement. That is the same category table every ordinary Associate is paid on, which we set out line by line in our UK commission rates guide.

So the Influencer Programme does not pay more per sale. A sale of a kitchen appliance through an influencer storefront earns the same percentage as the same sale through a blog link. The 24 hour cookie is the same, the definition of a qualifying purchase is the same, and the Associates Programme Operating Agreement, with its rules on disclosure, on not hand-typing prices or star ratings, and on the 180 day window to make three sales, applies to both. We covered those rules in our Associates rules guide, and nothing in them is waived for influencers.

Amazon's help pages elsewhere describe additional earning opportunities for influencers, such as commission when Amazon shows your shoppable videos on product pages. The UK sign-up page mentions that Amazon may surface the content you post to your storefront to Amazon shoppers, allowing you to earn from new customers. It does not publish a separate UK rate for that, so we do not state one. If a figure is quoted to you, ask where on Amazon's site it appears.

2. What is different: the door and the storefront

The first difference is the front door. The Associates programme takes applications from a website with a URL, an app, or a social channel, and the site's beginner route is to build a site and apply. The Influencer Programme takes applications only from a YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or TikTok account, and Amazon says plainly that if you are a media company or a brand wanting to drive traffic to Amazon, you should apply to the Associates programme instead.

The second difference is the storefront. An approved influencer gets a page on amazon.co.uk with a personalised URL, described on the sign-up page as your storefront, where you curate products, and publish livestreams, shoppable photos and videos. Followers who click through to the storefront and buy generate commission on whatever they buy within the cookie window, and Amazon says it may feature selected influencers' content across the site. An ordinary Associate has no page on Amazon; every link goes to a product, search or list page, and the reader leaves your site to get there.

That storefront is the whole product. It is useful for a creator whose audience will not click a blog link but will tap a link in a bio, because a single URL holds every recommendation in one place, and because content Amazon chooses to surface reaches shoppers who have never heard of you. It is of no use to a website, which already has a place to put its recommendations.

3. Who gets in, and the number Amazon does not publish

Amazon's UK page says it accepts applications from all types of influencers provided they have one of the four social accounts, that Instagram and Facebook applicants must use a business account, and that when reviewing an application it looks at the number of followers you have in addition to other engagement metrics. There is no minimum follower count on the page, and Amazon has never published one for the UK. Anyone quoting one is guessing from their own experience.

The UK page gives no decision timescale, and a rejection is not a ban; you can build the account and reapply. Because the programme runs inside Associates Central, an existing Associate applies with the same Amazon account and gains the storefront alongside the links they already have. A rejected influencer can still join Associates as an ordinary affiliate and post ordinary links on the same social accounts, at the same commission rates, without the storefront.

The engagement test is the part worth understanding. A large following that does not click, comment or share is less likely to be accepted than a smaller one that does, because Amazon is estimating how many qualifying purchases the account will produce. That is also the honest measure of what the programme is worth to you: commission comes from purchases, not from followers.

4. A worked example in Plymouth

Jess in Plymouth posts short home organisation videos on TikTok and Instagram and has about 12,000 followers across the two. She has no website. In a typical month 900 people tap the link in her bio, and 3% of them, 27 people, buy something on Amazon within the cookie window. The average order is £28, mostly storage boxes, labels and cleaning products in the home category.

Under Associates, with an ordinary link in her bio to a curated Amazon list, she earns the home category rate on £756 of sales a month. Under the Influencer Programme, with the same link pointing at her storefront, she earns exactly the same rate on the same £756, because the commission table is identical. On our arithmetic the two programmes pay her the same amount for the same traffic; if Amazon's home and kitchen rate on the day is, say, 3%, that is £22.68 a month either way. Check the current table before you assume any percentage.

Where the storefront changes her month is in the traffic, not the rate. Because her videos sit on a page Amazon can show to its own shoppers, some sales arrive from people who never followed her. If that adds 300 storefront visitors a month at the same 3% conversion and £28 basket, that is another 9 orders and £252 of sales, about £7.56 more commission at 3%. Real, but small, and entirely dependent on Amazon choosing to surface her content. Her income is decided by how many people buy through her, and the storefront is a modest lever on that, not a different rate.

Her friend Callum runs a review website for camping gear with 40,000 visits a month and an Instagram account with 800 followers. He applies to the Influencer Programme, is rejected on following, and is annoyed until he reads the sign-up page: Amazon told him where to go. He joins Associates, links from the site, and earns the same rate per sale as Jess. His 40,000 visits are worth far more than her storefront.

5. Which to apply for

Apply to Associates if your audience reaches you through a website, an app, an email list or a mix of channels, or if you are a business rather than a person. It is open, quick, and pays the full table from day one, subject to the three sales in 180 days rule.

Apply to the Influencer Programme if your audience lives on YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or TikTok, you can show a following with real engagement, and a single storefront URL would genuinely replace a dozen scattered links. Expect the same commission per sale; value the storefront for what it is, a page on Amazon and a chance of being surfaced, and not for a rate that does not exist.

Do both if you have both. An Associate with a website and a growing social channel can hold the ordinary links and the storefront in one account. Whatever the door, the disclosure rules are the same: label affiliate links as such, and never hand-type an Amazon price or rating, because both programmes end the account for it.

6. The honest verdict

The Influencer Programme pays the same rates as Associates. Amazon's own UK page links the two to the same commission table. Anyone selling it as the higher-paid tier is wrong.

What you are applying for is a storefront on amazon.co.uk and the chance that Amazon surfaces your content. That is worth something to a social-first creator and nothing to a website.

Website: Associates. Social with real engagement: Influencer. Both: both, in one account. Your income is decided by how many people buy through you, and no door changes that.

Frequently asked

Does the Amazon Influencer Programme pay more than Associates? No. Amazon UK's Influencer page says earnings are based on the products sold and links to the standard Associates commission table. The rate per sale is identical.

How many followers do you need for the Amazon Influencer Programme UK? Amazon does not publish a number. Its page says it looks at follower count and other engagement metrics, and requires a YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or TikTok account, with a business account for Instagram and Facebook.

Can I be in both programmes? Yes. The Influencer Programme runs inside Associates Central. An existing Associate can apply for the storefront with the same account and keep using ordinary links.

I was rejected as an influencer. What now? Join Associates and post ordinary affiliate links on the same social accounts at the same rates. Build engagement and reapply for the storefront later.
