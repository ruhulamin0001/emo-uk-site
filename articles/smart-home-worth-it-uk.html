Is a Smart Home Worth It in the UK? What Actually Saves Money

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Smart home gadgets promise to cut your bills and run your house for you. Some genuinely help, many are toys you set up once and forget. Here is the honest UK view of what is worth buying, what saves money, and what is just spending dressed up as saving.

What genuinely saves money

A smart thermostat is the standout. It lets you heat only the rooms and times you actually use, and stops you heating an empty house. Since heating is the biggest bill in most UK homes, as covered in our energy bills guide, this is the one smart buy that can pay for itself.

Smart plugs and timers, used to stop things running when you do not need them, save a little and cost little. Modest, but real.

What saves time, not money

Smart speakers, lights and video doorbells are convenient and can add security or comfort, but they do not really cut your bills. Buy them because you want them, not because you expect them to pay for themselves. Be honest about which reason applies.

What is mostly a gadget

  • Anything sold mainly on a dramatic saving claim. Most of these save little and take years to pay back.
  • Devices that need a subscription to do anything useful. The monthly fee often outweighs any benefit.
  • A house full of connected things you will set up once and never touch again.

The hidden costs to weigh

Smart devices can lock you into one company's system, need their app, and sometimes a monthly fee. They also collect data about your home, so take basic care with privacy and strong passwords, as in our password manager guide. And when the maker stops supporting a device, it can simply stop working.

Where to start cheaply

  1. Begin with a smart thermostat if you own your home and heating is a big cost. It is the one with a real payback.
  2. Add a couple of smart plugs to control the things that waste power on standby.
  3. Buy comfort and security devices only if you want them, not for savings.
  4. Avoid anything that needs a subscription to be useful.

The honest bottom line

A smart home can save money in exactly one big way, smarter heating, and cut a little more with plugs and timers. Everything else is convenience you should buy only if you value it for itself. Do not let saving be the excuse for spending. Pair the real savings with our switch and save guide.
