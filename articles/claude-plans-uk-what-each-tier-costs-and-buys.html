Anthropic's pricing page quotes every Claude plan in US dollars and says plainly that tax is not included. For someone in the UK that means a currency conversion and 20% VAT before the real monthly cost appears. This guide converts each tier at HMRC's published September 2026 rate, lists what each one includes on the day of writing, and works out how many hours of paid work have to come out of the tool to cover it.

1. The plans and what they cost in sterling

Anthropic publishes one price list for everyone at claude.com/pricing, in US dollars, with a line under the cards that says prices shown do not include applicable tax. That single line does a lot of work for a UK buyer. It means the number you see is not the number your bank statement will show. Two things get added: VAT at the UK standard rate of 20%, which applies to digital services sold to UK consumers, and whatever your card issuer does with a dollar transaction.

To turn the dollar prices into sterling we used the rate HMRC itself publishes for customs valuation, which for September 2026 is USD 1.3554 to GBP 1. It is a monthly figure fixed on 19 August 2026, so your card will not match it to the penny, but it is a public, dated number rather than a guess. Here is the whole list converted, with VAT added.

2. What each tier includes, and what it does not

The mistake most people make is assuming the paid tiers are just more of the free one. They are, but Pro is also where four separate products switch on. On the day of writing the comparison table on the pricing page lists these as Free versus Pro.

Free includes: chat on web, iOS, Android and the desktop app, web search, creating files and running code, memory across conversations, skills, connectors including Slack and Google Workspace, extended thinking, voice mode, incognito chats and artifacts. That is a great deal of capability for nothing, and it is enough to find out whether the tool suits the work you do.

Pro adds: more usage, Claude Code (the command line coding tool), Claude Cowork (the desktop task tool that works in your own folders), Claude Design, Claude Science, Claude in Chrome (the browser extension that clicks and types on pages), Claude for Microsoft 365 and Outlook, unlimited projects, and access to more models. On the models row Free has Sonnet and Haiku, Pro adds Opus, and the newest model, Fable, is available on Pro through usage credits and on Max at half of the weekly limits.

Max adds: the choice of 5x or 20x Pro's usage, higher output limits, early access to advanced features and priority access at busy times. It does not add products that Pro lacks. If the reason you want Max is a product, you already have it on Pro.

For a UK freelancer the practical line is this. If your work is chat, drafting, summarising and checking, Free may carry you for weeks. If you want Claude to act inside your files, your browser or a terminal, Pro is the entry ticket, and the guide to AI tools for a UK small business has the wider comparison against Microsoft and Google's offers.

3. What the usage limits actually mean

Anthropic does not sell a number of messages. The pricing page FAQ explains that every plan has limits that reset on a rolling five-hour session window, that paid plans add weekly limits on top, and that how much you get through depends on the length and complexity of your conversations, the model you pick and the features you use. Pro gives at least five times Free's usage per session. Max gives five or twenty times Pro's.

Three consequences matter for anyone planning to earn with the tool. First, everything you do on the web, desktop, mobile and in Claude Code draws from one pool, so a heavy coding afternoon eats the allowance you wanted for client emails. Second, the Cowork product page says in terms that Cowork consumes limits faster than chat, because agentic tasks run multiple tool calls and sub-agents. Third, when you hit a limit on a paid plan you have three choices: wait for the reset, move up a tier, or switch on usage credits, which bill extra work at standard API rates.

That last option is the one to understand before you buy Max. If you hit the Pro ceiling once a month, credits for that one afternoon will almost certainly cost less than the GBP 70 a month step from Pro to Max 5x. If you hit it every working day, Max is cheaper. You can see where you stand in Settings, then Usage, and the honest advice is to look at that screen for a month before spending more.

A subscription pays for itself when the time it saves, valued at what you actually earn, exceeds the fee. The only inputs are your hourly rate and the sterling cost from the table above. We ran it at three rates: the National Living Wage of GBP 12.71 an hour for 21 and over from 1 April 2026, a modest freelance rate of GBP 25, and GBP 40, which is where the freelance rate guide puts a lot of experienced UK generalists.

The table is deliberately blunt. Pro clears the bar for almost anyone doing paid knowledge work, because 43 minutes a month is one long email thread. Max only clears it if you are billing several hours a week that the tool made possible, and the test is not whether you used Claude for those hours but whether the hours would have been unbillable without it. Most people who upgrade to Max cannot answer that question, which is why the verdict below says to wait.

5. A worked example in Oldham

Farhana in Oldham does part-time copy editing for two marketing agencies, about eight billable hours a week at GBP 22 an hour, so roughly GBP 760 a month before tax. She has used Claude Free for two months to check house style and draft the boring client notes. She now wants to use Cowork to run a repeatable brief-to-first-draft process in her own folders, which needs Pro.

Pro monthly costs her GBP 17.71. To cover it she needs to bill 48 extra minutes a month, or keep 48 minutes she was spending unpaid. Her own log for August shows about three hours a month of formatting and version-chasing that clients do not pay for, so Pro is comfortably covered before she takes on any new work. The annual plan would save her GBP 35.45 over the year, but it commits USD 200 in one hit, and Anthropic's own refund FAQ says UK and EEA customers get a 14-day withdrawal period and otherwise payments are generally non-refundable. She takes monthly for three months first.

The tax side is simple and usually ignored. The subscription is an allowable business expense against her self-employed profit, so on a basic rate profit the real cost to her after tax relief is lower than GBP 17.71. If her total trading income sits above GBP 1,000 she is in Self Assessment anyway; the side hustle tax rules guide covers the registration deadline and the trading allowance in full.

6. The other route: paying per token

The subscriptions are not the only way in. The same pricing page lists API rates per million tokens: Haiku 4.5 at USD 1 in and USD 5 out, Sonnet 5 at USD 2 in and USD 10 out, Opus 5 at USD 5 in and USD 25 out, and Fable 5.1 at USD 10 in and USD 50 out. A token is roughly three quarters of an English word. That route suits a person building a tool for other people to use, which we cover in the guide to earning a first GBP 1,000 with AI, and it is billed on actual usage with no monthly fee.

For a solo freelancer using Claude as a workmate, the subscription is almost always simpler and cheaper, because the plan bundles Cowork, Claude Code and the Chrome extension, which the API does not. The exception is someone whose use is rare but heavy, a few big document jobs a quarter, where a pay-as-you-go balance beats twelve months of Pro.

The honest verdict

Pro at about GBP 17.71 a month is easy to justify for anyone billing more than an hour a month of work it helps with, and Free is good enough to find out whether that is you. Max is a tool for people who already know they hit the Pro ceiling on paid work several times a week, and nobody knows that until they have run Pro for a month and looked at the usage screen. Buy in that order, keep the receipt for your tax return, and re-check the pricing page before any annual payment because Anthropic says in writing that the prices can change.

Frequently asked

Is Claude Pro charged in pounds? No. On 5 September 2026 the pricing page quotes every plan in US dollars and states that tax is not included. UK VAT at 20% is added, and your card converts the dollar amount at its own rate, which may include a non-sterling fee. Our GBP 17.71 figure uses HMRC's September 2026 rate of USD 1.3554 per pound.

Does Pro include Claude Code and Cowork? Yes. The pricing page's comparison table lists Claude Code, Claude Cowork, Claude Design, Claude Science, Claude in Chrome and Claude for Microsoft 365 as included on Pro, Max and Team, and not on Free.

What happens when I hit the limit? Limits reset on a rolling five-hour window, with weekly caps on paid plans. You can wait, upgrade, or on a paid plan turn on usage credits that bill extra work at API rates. Anthropic notes that Cowork uses limits faster than ordinary chat.

Can I get a refund if I change my mind? Anthropic's FAQ says payments are generally non-refundable except where its consumer terms or local law say otherwise, and that customers in the UK and EEA within their 14-day withdrawal period can request a refund in the app.
