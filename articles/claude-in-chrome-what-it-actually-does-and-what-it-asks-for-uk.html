A browser extension that clicks, types and fills forms on your behalf is a different kind of tool from a chat box, and the honest way to describe it is from what its maker publishes rather than from a demo. This guide reads Claude in Chrome from its Chrome Web Store listing and Anthropic's help centre on the day of writing: the user count, the rating, the permissions and why each is requested, the plan it needs, and the warning about hidden instructions on websites that Anthropic prints on its own listing. Then it asks the only earning question that matters: which browser jobs is it worth being paid to run, and which should it never touch.

1. What the listing says

The Chrome Web Store listing for Claude, read on 5 September 2026, shows 15,000,000 users, a rating of 2.8 from 1,600 ratings, version 1.0.91, an update date of 4 September 2026, and a size of 6.44 MiB. The developer is Anthropic at a San Francisco address, marked as a non-trader, which the store notes means EU consumer rights do not apply to the contract. Under Privacy it declares that the extension handles personally identifiable information, personal communications, location, web history, user activity and website content, and that data is not sold to third parties outside Google's approved use cases.

The listing's own description says the extension is what lets Claude see and act in your browser: open the side panel, describe what you are trying to do, and Claude reads the page, clicks, types, navigates and fills forms while you watch. It says the side panel is now Claude Cowork, the same product as the desktop app, with saved sessions and your skills and connectors, available on Max and Team, rolling out to Pro, and on Enterprise where an admin enables it. It adds that the extension is available on all paid plans, is not supported on other Chromium browsers or mobile, and that it works with Claude Code for a build, test and fix loop.

The 2.8 rating is the number people will ask about and the honest answer is that we report it as shown and do not know the cause. Read the recent reviews before installing anything that can act in your browser, as you would for any tool with these permissions. The wider extensions guide compares this listing with Grammarly, LanguageTool and Toggl Track on the same fields.

Debugger is the one to understand. It is the permission that gives an extension the same level of control over a page as the browser's developer tools, and it is why the extension can do what it does and why it should be treated as a capable colleague with your logins rather than a spell checker. Anthropic says so in its own way: the extension works on pages behind a login, using your existing sessions.

3. What it actually does

From the help centre: it manages multiple tabs, and dragging tabs into Claude's tab group lets it work across all of them at once. It has built-in knowledge of how to navigate Slack, Google Calendar, Gmail, Google Docs and GitHub, so commands like schedule a meeting or update the doc work without step-by-step instructions. It keeps working when you switch tabs as long as Chrome is open. You can upload images and screenshots to point at things. Prompts that work can be saved as shortcuts, run by typing a slash, and scheduled daily, weekly, monthly or annually. You can record a workflow by performing it yourself and Claude learns to repeat it. For developers it reads console errors, network requests and DOM state.

Permission modes come from the same article. Ask before acting has Claude produce a plan for approval and then execute within it, asking again only for certain high-risk actions or when something falls outside the plan. The listing also describes an automatic approval mode where a separate safety check reviews each consequential action first and blocks anything that looks wrong. On Team and Enterprise plans admins can enable or disable the extension for the organisation and set allowed and blocked sites. The help article also notes that the desktop app's Claude in Chrome connector works only with Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 or Opus 4.5 at the time of writing, while the extension itself offers a model choice for speed against reasoning.

4. The warning Anthropic prints, and what it means

The listing says, in its own words, that websites can hide instructions that try to redirect Claude's actions, that Anthropic builds and tests defences against this, and that it still recommends starting on sites you know, reading the prompts when they come, and reporting anything Claude does that you did not ask for. The help centre carries a separate article on using it safely and marks browser use as a beta feature that carries inherent risks. This is the plainest statement any AI vendor makes about its own product and it should be read as a rule, not a disclaimer.

For a freelancer the rule turns into a short list. Never run it on your banking, on a payment page, or on a client portal that can move money, because the cost of one redirected click is not yours to absorb. Do not run it on material under a non-disclosure agreement without the client's written agreement, because the extension declares that it handles website content and personal communications. Use ask-before-acting on any site you did not build or do not use daily. And keep it on a Chrome profile with only the logins the job needs, which is free and removes most of the exposure.

6. A worked example in Wrexham

Owen in Wrexham does virtual assistant work for a lettings agency, and four hours of every month is copying property details from three landlord portals into the agency's own spreadsheet, a job with no export button on any of the three. He is on Claude Pro at GBP 17.71 for other work already. He sets up a dedicated Chrome profile with only the three portal logins, installs the extension, and runs the job in ask-before-acting mode the first three times, checking every row. The fourth month he saves it as a shortcut and schedules it for the first Monday, then spends about 40 minutes checking the output against the portals.

Four hours becomes about 40 minutes, so 3 hours 20 minutes saved a month. He charges the agency the same fixed fee as before, GBP 100 a month for the task, which at four hours was GBP 25 an hour and is now GBP 150 an hour for the 40 minutes of checking. The marginal cost is nothing, because Pro was already paid for; if it were not, GBP 17.71 against 3 hours 20 minutes saved at GBP 25 an hour is GBP 83.33 of time for GBP 17.71 of subscription. He does not point it at the agency's rent collection system, which is the same rule the table above sets, and he tells the agency in writing which portals the tool touches.

His fee is trading income like any other and goes on his Self Assessment return; the side hustle tax rules guide covers the thresholds for someone starting from nothing.

The honest verdict

Claude in Chrome is a capable browser agent that Anthropic itself says carries inherent risk, and both halves of that sentence are true. On a paid plan from about GBP 17.71 a month it pays for itself on any recurring portal, research or form job that saves an hour a month, and those jobs exist in most small firms. Run it on a separate Chrome profile with only the logins the job needs, in ask-before-acting mode until you trust it on a site, and never on anything that moves money or sits under an NDA without consent. Read the reviews behind the 2.8 before you install, and re-check the Updated date on the listing every few months.

Frequently asked

Does Claude in Chrome work on the free plan? No. The listing says it is available on all paid plans, and the Cowork side panel inside it is on Max and Team with Pro rolling out. Pro is USD 20 a month before tax, about GBP 17.71 with VAT.

Why does it need the debugger permission? Anthropic's help centre says debugger is what allows Claude to actually control the browser, clicking buttons, typing text and taking screenshots. It is the permission that makes the extension work and the reason to treat it with care.

Is it safe to use on my bank? Do not. Anthropic's own listing warns that websites can hide instructions that try to redirect Claude's actions and recommends starting on sites you know. A banking or payment page is the one place a redirected action costs real money.

Does it work in Edge or Brave? No. Both the listing and the help centre say it is not supported on other Chromium-based browsers or on mobile devices.
