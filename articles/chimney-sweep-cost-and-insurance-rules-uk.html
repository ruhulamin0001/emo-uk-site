A Chimney Sweep Kit Pays For Itself In One Winter. Read Your Policy Before You Buy One

Sweeping your own chimney is legal, cheap and not especially difficult. It also produces no certificate, and the certificate is the entire reason a lot of people pay somebody else. Here is the frequency the trade body actually recommends and the one question that decides which option is right for you.

Ruhul Amin Published 14 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The short version

  • HETAS guidance is once a year minimum for smokeless fuels and at least twice a year for wood. Wood produces more deposit, so it needs more sweeping, and that doubles the annual cost for most stove owners.
  • Timing matters as much as frequency. HETAS advises sweeping just before the heating season and after any prolonged period without use. If sweeping twice, the second should come after the peak of the heating season.
  • Two professional sweeps at roughly 60 to 100 pounds each is about 150 pounds a year for a wood burner.
  • A HETAS approved sweep issues a certificate after the sweep. It is evidence of a professional sweep and your insurer may insist on seeing it if you claim for fire damage.
  • The payback on a DIY rod kit is roughly four months if your policy does not require a certificate: about 40 pounds against 150 pounds a year of sweeping.
  • If your policy does require one, the DIY kit saves nothing and risks a declined claim. One paragraph in your schedule decides this, not the price of the rods.

1. How often, according to the people who set the standard

HETAS, the body that certifies solid fuel appliances and installers in the UK, sets the domestic benchmark, and its guidance is specific about fuel type rather than giving one blanket number.

Chimneys should be swept at least once a year when burning smokeless fuels, and at least twice a year when burning wood. The difference is not arbitrary. Wood, particularly wood that is not properly seasoned, deposits far more tar and creosote on the flue liner than manufactured smokeless fuel does, and that deposit is what catches fire.

HETAS is equally specific on timing. The best moments are just before the start of the heating season, and after the stove has not been used over a prolonged period, which catches nests and debris as well as soot. Where you are sweeping twice, the second sweep should come after the peak of the main heating season rather than at some arbitrary six month interval.

That second point is the one people get wrong. Sweeping in September and again in March is correct. Sweeping in September and again in December, because the diary said six months, leaves the heaviest deposits of the year sitting in the flue all spring and summer.

2. The certificate is the product, not the sweep

Here is the part that actually decides the money. HETAS states that following a successful sweep, a HETAS approved chimney sweep will provide a certificate, that this is evidence the chimney has been professionally swept, and that it can be used for insurance purposes if required.

Read the conditional in that sentence carefully. If required. Home insurance policies in the UK are not uniform on this. Some say nothing about chimneys at all. Some require the chimney to be swept at stated intervals. Some require a professional sweep specifically, which is the wording that makes a DIY kit worthless as far as a claim is concerned.

You are not buying a clean chimney for 75 pounds. A set of rods gives you that for the price of one sweep. You are buying a piece of paper with somebody else's registration number on it.

So the decision is not a judgement about your own competence. It is a five minute reading task. Find the section of your policy wording covering the heating appliance or chimney, and look for the words professional, approved, qualified or registered. If any of them appear, the DIY option is off the table and the arithmetic below does not apply to you.

4. Worked example: the Hargreaves household in Buxton

A stone cottage with a wood burner in the sitting room, lit most evenings from October to March, burning seasoned hardwood from a local supplier.

The current arrangement. Two visits a year from a registered sweep at 78 pounds each. 156 pounds a year.

The DIY option. A rod set with a brush head sized to the liner, about 42 pounds, one off. Payback: 42 divided by 156, which is 0.27 of a year, or about 14 weeks. After the first winter it costs nothing and the rods last twenty years.

What their policy actually said. The schedule required the chimney to be "swept annually by a professional sweep". Not twice. Annually. So the correct answer was neither of the above: one professional sweep in September for the certificate at 78 pounds, and the second sweep in March done with the rods.

Annual cost falls from 156 pounds to 78 pounds, the policy condition is met, and HETAS twice a year guidance is still followed. Saving of 78 pounds a year for a 42 pound kit, so the kit repays in about six and a half months.

The reason that split works is that the policy condition and the safety guidance are two different requirements that happen to overlap. Meeting the stricter one with the cheaper method, and the paperwork one with the professional, is not a loophole. It is reading both documents.

Frequently asked

Is it legal to sweep my own chimney? Yes, in your own home. There is no licence requirement for domestic chimney sweeping. The constraint is contractual, through your insurance policy, not legal.

What happens if I claim after a chimney fire and never swept it? That depends entirely on your policy wording. If the schedule carries a sweeping condition and you cannot show it was met, an insurer is entitled to decline or reduce the claim. If there is no such condition, a chimney fire is generally an insured peril like any other. Find out which you have before October, not after.

Does a stove with a liner need less sweeping? No. A liner is smoother and narrower, which means deposits build in a smaller cross section. HETAS frequency guidance is by fuel, not by flue construction.

What about a chimney I never use? An unused chimney does not need sweeping for soot, but it does attract nests, and a capped and vented unused flue is the right answer rather than a sweep. If it has not been used in years and you are about to light it, have it swept and inspected once by somebody who can see it, which is the situation HETAS specifically flags.

Is wood actually cheaper to burn than gas central heating? Not usually, once you price seasoned hardwood properly rather than by the bag, which we went through in the running cost comparison between a wood burner and central heating.

The honest verdict

Read your policy schedule before you buy anything. One sentence in it is worth more than every price in this guide.

If it requires a professional sweep, book one, keep the certificate somewhere you can find it in a hurry, and treat the 78 pounds as the price of a claim that gets paid.

If it does not, buy the rods. At around 40 pounds against 150 a year they repay themselves before Christmas, and for a wood burner you should be sweeping twice a year anyway, which is exactly the interval professional sweeping makes expensive.
