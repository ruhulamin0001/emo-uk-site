Apple's Event Is On 9 September At 6pm. If You Want An iPhone 17 At £799, The Only Question Is Whether To Wait A Week.

Apple's UK events page now carries the invitation: a special event on 9 September at 6pm UK time. Every year the same question follows, and this year it has a twist, because the reporting says the standard iPhone 18 is not launching this month. Here is what Apple has confirmed, what is only reported, what the current UK prices are, and why the sensible answer for most people costs nothing.

1. What Apple has actually confirmed

Very little, and that is normal. Apple's UK events page says: "Surprise and shine. Watch a special Apple Event on 09/09 at 6 pm. Tune in at apple.com or on the Apple TV app." That is the whole of the official announcement. No products, no prices, no dates for sale.

Apple's September events have, for over a decade, been the iPhone launch, and the sales pattern that follows is consistent: pre-orders open the Friday after the event and phones ship the Friday after that. If the pattern holds, that is pre-orders from 11 September and deliveries from 18 September. But that is pattern, not announcement, and we are labelling it as such.

What is also confirmed is the current price list on Apple's UK store, which we read on 3 September:

2. What is only reported, and why it matters this year

Every year the run-up to the event fills with reporting from Apple watchers, some of it reliable, none of it official. This year the widely repeated claim is that Apple is splitting the launch: the iPhone 18 Pro, 18 Pro Max and a first folding iPhone in September, with the standard iPhone 18 and a cheaper 18e following in spring 2027. Dollar estimates for the folding model start around 2,000 dollars.

We are not treating any of that as fact. But it matters to the buying decision even as a possibility, for one reason. In a normal year the standard iPhone 17 would be replaced by a standard iPhone 18 on 9 September, and Apple would typically cut the outgoing model's price to make room. If there is no standard iPhone 18 this month, there is no direct replacement for the iPhone 17, and the usual reason for an immediate price cut on it weakens.

Put plainly: if the reports are right, the iPhone 17 at £799 may be the standard iPhone Apple sells until spring, and waiting for the event may change nothing about its price. If the reports are wrong and a standard iPhone 18 appears, the iPhone 17 is likely to get cheaper on 9 September. Either way, the person who waits six days knows, and the person who buys today does not.

On the folding phone: no UK price exists. A 2,000 dollar estimate converted at today's exchange rate and with 20% VAT added is a very different number from the same estimate converted without VAT, and neither is Apple's price. Anyone quoting a pound figure for it before 9 September is guessing.

3. Current UK prices and what usually happens to them

Three things reliably happen to iPhone prices around a September event, and only one of them is guaranteed.

Guaranteed: Apple removes some older models from its own store and repositions others. The models that stay on sale stay at or below their previous price. Apple does not raise the price of an existing model at a launch event.

Usual: the outgoing standard model, if it has a direct successor, drops by around £100 on Apple's store. Retailers and networks follow within days, sometimes with deeper cuts to clear stock.

Common but not reliable: the refurbished market softens. Apple's own certified refurbished store and the major refurbishers list more of last year's model at lower prices in the weeks after a launch, as trade-ins arrive.

None of those helps you if you buy on 3 September. All of them are available to you on 10 September. That asymmetry is the entire argument, and it holds whether or not the reports about the line-up are right.

There is one exception worth naming. If your current phone is broken and you need a working one this week, the £100 you might save by waiting is worth less than a week without a phone. Buy the iPhone 17, or a cheaper model, and stop reading.

4. A worked example: Leon in Norwich

Leon in Norwich has an iPhone 13 with a battery that dies by mid-afternoon. He has £800 set aside and has been looking at the iPhone 17 at £799.

Option A, buy today. £799. If the iPhone 17 is cut on 9 September, he has lost the difference. Apple's returns window is 14 days, so in practice he could return and rebuy, but only by doing the admin.

Option B, wait until 10 September. He pays either £799 or less, and he knows what else is on sale. Cost of waiting: seven days of a phone that needs charging at lunchtime.

Option C, the one nobody advertises. Apple's out-of-warranty battery replacement for an iPhone 13 is a fixed price published on Apple's UK support site, a small fraction of a new phone; check the figure for your model. The iPhone 13 still receives iOS updates. Leon keeps most of his £800 and revisits the question next year, when the standard iPhone 18 is reportedly due anyway.

We are not telling Leon which to choose. We are pointing out that Option A is the only one that cannot be improved by waiting six days, and that Option C is the one that saves real money.

5. The honest verdict

Do not buy an iPhone between now and 6pm on 9 September unless yours is broken. Nothing gets more expensive after the event, and something may get cheaper.

Ignore every pound price for the iPhone 18 or a folding iPhone until Apple publishes one. They are dollar guesses, mostly without VAT.

If your current iPhone still gets updates, the cheapest upgrade is a battery. There is nothing to buy here this week, and we have no affiliate link for that advice.
