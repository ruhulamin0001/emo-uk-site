Cheap Paint Needs Three Coats. That Is Exactly Why It Is Not Cheap

A tin of paint has a price on the front and two numbers on the back that decide what it will actually cost you. Multiply them out on a real room and the bargain tin stops being a bargain somewhere during the second coat.

The short version

  • Two numbers on the tin decide everything: coverage in square metres per litre, and how many coats it needs. The price on the front tells you almost nothing.
  • Budget contract matt: about 10 square metres a litre in practice, three coats. Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt: a stated 17, two coats.
  • On a 32 square metre room both land at about £28.70 in materials. Identical money, and the cheap one costs you an extra full coat of labour.
  • Divide by the years before you repaint and the gap opens. Budget paint in a hallway is about £0.30 per square metre per year. Trade vinyl matt is about £0.15.
  • Over thirty years in one hallway that is £220 and twenty lost coats of painting. The coats are the larger number.

1. The two numbers on the tin, and the one that is missing

Coverage is printed as square metres per litre, and it is always stated as an up to figure measured on a sealed, smooth, previously painted surface. New plaster, bare filler, a strong colour change or a textured wall will all reduce it, commonly by twenty to thirty per cent. Treat the printed figure as a ceiling, not an expectation.

Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt is stated at around 17 square metres per litre, and Dulux Trade Supermatt, which is designed for new plaster and large ceilings, at around 18. Budget own-brand contract matt is typically claimed at 12 or so, and in practice on anything other than a perfect surface it behaves like 10.

Number of coats is the multiplier nobody applies. A paint with good opacity covers in two. A thin one needs three, and sometimes three is still patchy over a dark colour. Three coats instead of two is fifty per cent more paint and fifty per cent more work, and that is where the apparent saving on the tin goes.

The missing number is the recoat interval, which no manufacturer prints because it depends on where you put it. A contract matt on a hallway wall that gets touched, scuffed and wiped will look tired in about three years. A durable washable matt in the same place will hold up for eight to ten. That difference does more to the cost per year than anything else on this page, and you have to supply it yourself from where the wall is.

Rows one and two are the finding. £28.80 against £28.61. The cheap paint and the trade paint cost the same money for the same room, because the cheap one needs half again as much of it. Anyone who has painted a room knows this and almost nobody costs it out before buying.

Now read the last column. £0.300 against £0.149. The trade paint is exactly twice as good per year, entirely because it survives twice as long before it needs doing again.

Rows two and four are worth comparing carefully, because they land in almost the same place: £0.149 and £0.147. The durable washable matt costs sixty per cent more in materials and lasts sixty per cent longer, so on money it is a wash. Where it wins is that you can actually clean it, which in a hallway with children is the whole point. Buy it for the wipeability, not for the arithmetic.

3. The coat you are not counting is the expensive one

Everything above counts only what is in the tin. The bigger cost is the coat itself.

A decorator charges roughly £180 to £250 a day in most of the UK. Painting a room is commonly a two day job with two coats and becomes closer to two and a half with three. On a £200 day rate that third coat is somewhere around £100, which is more than three times the entire material saving you were chasing.

If you are doing it yourself the cash cost is zero and the real cost is a day of your weekend, plus moving the furniture back out again, plus the masking. Most people who have done it once will tell you the third coat is where the job stops being enjoyable.

So the honest total for one room looks like this. Budget paint: £28.80 and three coats. Trade paint: £28.61 and two coats. There is no version of that comparison where the budget tin wins.

Two things that genuinely do save money, neither of which involves buying better paint. Seal new plaster with a mist coat, which is emulsion thinned with water by about a tenth, because bare plaster drinks full-strength paint and you will lose a whole expensive coat into it. And buy the 5 litre tin rather than 2.5 where you will use it, because paint is priced with a steep quantity discount and leftover emulsion keeps for years if the lid is properly sealed.

4. Worked example: Gethin in Stockport

Gethin is painting a hallway, stairs and landing. He measures it properly: 58 square metres of wall, a lot of it above the stairs where a ladder is involved.

His first plan is four 5 litre tins of supermarket own-brand at £15 each, so £60. At 10 square metres a litre and three coats he needs 17.4 litres, so four tins is right with a little spare.

He prices the alternative: Dulux Trade Vinyl Matt at £38 for 5 litres. At 17 square metres a litre and two coats he needs 6.82 litres, so two tins, £76.

Sixteen pounds more. Then he counts what he is buying with it: one fewer coat over 58 square metres including the stairwell, which is most of a day on a ladder, and a wall that will not need doing again for six years instead of three.

Cost per square metre per year: budget is £60 divided by 58, divided by 3 years, which is £0.345. Trade is £76 divided by 58, divided by 6 years, which is £0.218.

Over thirty years in that hallway the budget route means ten repaints at £60, so £600 and thirty coats. The trade route means five repaints at £76, so £380 and ten coats. He saves £220 and, more to the point, twenty coats of painting a stairwell.

He spends part of the difference on a decent roller and a 1.5 metre extension pole rather than the cheapest ones, which is the other place people lose a day for no reason.

5. The honest verdict

Buy trade emulsion. It costs the same as budget paint once you count the third coat, and it lasts twice as long. There is no arithmetic under which the cheap tin comes out ahead.

Buy durable washable matt for hallways, stairs and children's rooms. It ties with trade vinyl matt on cost per square metre per year, and it is the only one you can wipe, which is what those walls actually need.

Mist-coat new plaster before anything else. Skipping it costs you a full coat of expensive paint and you will not get it back.

And measure the room before you go. The commonest expensive mistake in decorating is buying by guesswork and making a second trip.

If you are buying

The coverage figure and the recommended number of coats are both printed on the tin and on the manufacturer's data sheet. Those two numbers, not the price, are what to compare. A cheap extension pole and a decent roller sleeve will do more for the finish than trading up a grade of paint.

Frequently asked

Is trade paint actually different, or is it the same paint in a plainer tin? It is different. Trade ranges generally carry a higher proportion of solids and pigment, which is what produces opacity in fewer coats, and they are formulated for people who are paid by the job and cannot afford to apply three coats. The plainer tin is a consequence, not the product.

Can I buy trade paint without a trade account? Yes. Trade counters at the large merchants sell to the public, and the same products are stocked by most decorating shops and online. You may pay a little more than an account holder and it will still beat the retail own-brand on cost per year.

How much paint do I need for a ceiling? Measure the floor area, since the ceiling is the same. A 4m by 3.5m room is 14 square metres, which at 17 square metres a litre and two coats is 1.65 litres. Ceilings usually need two coats even in a good paint because they are viewed in raking light, which shows everything.

Does leftover emulsion keep? For several years if the lid is hammered down flat and the tin is stored somewhere that does not freeze. Wipe the rim before closing. A frozen and thawed tin is ruined and will roll on like cottage cheese, so a garage in an unheated house is not the right place.
