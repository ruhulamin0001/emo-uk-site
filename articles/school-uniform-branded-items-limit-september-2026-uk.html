Three Branded Items, Four With a Tie: The School Uniform Limit Now in Force in England, and What It Does Not Cap

For the first time, the number of logo-bearing items a school can make you buy is written into an Act of Parliament. From the start of the 2026 to 2027 school year, a school in England may require a primary pupil to have no more than three branded items and a secondary pupil no more than three, or four if one is a tie. This is the rule as section 35 of the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 and the Department for Education's statutory guidance state it, what counts as branded, the things the law leaves alone, and what a parent can actually do about a school that has not changed its list.

The short version

  • The limit is three branded items for primary pupils and three for secondary pupils, or four if one of them is a tie. Middle schools use the secondary limit for all pupils. Source: Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, section 35, inserting section 551ZA into the Education Act 1996.
  • Branded means two things: an item with the school's name or logo on it, or an item that, because of its colour, design or fabric, is only available from particular suppliers. A blazer with a badge and a sweatshirt with a special-colour trim both count.
  • Bags count. PE kit counts. Summer-only items count. The Act defines school uniform as "a bag and any clothing" required for school or any school activity, and the DfE guidance says a branded winter skirt plus a branded summer dress are two items, not one.
  • In force from September 2026, per DfE statutory guidance "Cost of school uniforms", updated 6 July 2026. Schools were told to have policies compliant for the 2026 to 2027 school year.
  • What it does NOT do: it does not cap the price of any item, does not apply in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, does not force schools to refund kit bought earlier, and does not stop a school specifying plain colours for non-branded items.
  • Our verdict: count the logos on your school's current list. If there are more than three (four with a tie) for a secondary pupil, the school is in breach of a statutory duty, and the route is a written complaint to the governing body, then the DfE.

1. The rule, in the Act's own words

Section 35 of the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 inserts a new section 551ZA into the Education Act 1996. It says the appropriate authority of a relevant school in England "may not require a primary pupil at the school to have more than three different branded items of school uniform for use during a school year", and "may not require a secondary pupil at the school to have more than three different branded items of school uniform (or more than four different branded items of school uniform if one of those items is a tie)". Middle schools apply the secondary limit to every pupil.

The Department for Education's statutory guidance "Cost of school uniforms", updated 6 July 2026, turns that into a date: "From September 2026: schools must limit the number of compulsory branded items of uniform and PE kit to 3 or fewer; secondary and middle schools must limit the number of compulsory branded items of uniform and PE kit to 4 or fewer, if one is a tie." The guidance covers academies, maintained schools, pupil referral units and non-maintained special schools, which is nearly every state-funded school in England. Independent schools are not covered.

2. What counts as branded, and what counts as an item

The Act defines a branded item two ways. First, anything with the school name or logo on or attached to it, or for an academy the trust's name or logo. Second, and this is the part that closes the obvious loophole, any item that "as a result of its colour, design, fabric or other distinctive characteristic, is only available from particular suppliers". A plain navy jumper you can buy anywhere is not branded. A navy jumper with a thin gold trim that only the school's appointed supplier stocks is branded, logo or not.

An item is "required" if the pupil must have it for general use at school, for travelling to and from school, or to take part in any lesson, club, activity or event the school facilitates, including after-school clubs. The DfE guidance adds the two rules parents ask about most. Branded bags, including book bags and rucksacks, count towards the limit. And items required only at particular times of year count separately: its own example is that a secondary pupil could not be required to have a branded winter skirt and a branded summer dress alongside a branded blazer, tie and PE top, because that would be five. Where a school lets pupils choose between two branded items, for example a branded skirt or branded trousers, the guidance treats that as one item. Optional branded items do not count, but only if there is a genuine non-branded alternative; a school cannot label an item optional while requiring it in practice. Safety equipment such as high-visibility vests is outside the count.

3. What the law does not do

It does not cap what any single item costs. A school can require three branded items and the supplier can charge what it likes for them; the guidance says schools "should" prioritise cost and value for money and avoid single-supplier contracts, but "should" is guidance, not a legal duty. It does not apply outside England: Wales has its own statutory guidance, Scotland its own national guidance, and Northern Ireland its own grant scheme, and none of the three has a numerical limit in law. It does not require a school to refund or replace kit bought before the change. It does not stop a school specifying colours, styles or "no logos" for the non-branded items, which is exactly what the guidance encourages. And it does not change the position on sixth forms, which are covered when they are part of a relevant school but not when they are separate colleges.

4. If your school has not complied

Start by counting. Take the school's published uniform policy, which the guidance says must be on its website and must make clear which items are compulsory, and list every item that is either logoed or only available from the named supplier. Include bags and PE kit. If a secondary list has five compulsory branded items and one is a tie, the school is one over. Write to the headteacher first, citing section 551ZA of the Education Act 1996 and the DfE guidance, and ask which items will become non-branded. If nothing changes, the formal route is the school's own complaints procedure to the governing body or trust, and after that a complaint to the Department for Education, which can direct a maintained school and can act against an academy trust through its funding agreement. The guidance also says schools must make second-hand uniform accessible, which is the cheaper fix while the argument runs.

5. A worked example in Gloucester

Helen in Gloucester has a daughter starting Year 7 this month. The school's 2025 list required a badged blazer, a crested tie, a jumper with a trim colour sold only by the school shop, a branded PE polo, a branded PE hoodie and a branded rucksack: six branded items. Under the new limit the school may require four, because one is a tie. The 2026 list, republished in July, keeps the blazer, tie, PE polo and rucksack as branded and makes the jumper plain navy from any retailer and the PE hoodie any plain black hoodie. That is four, and it complies.

What Helen saves depends on the school shop's prices against the supermarket's, which the law does not touch. If the branded jumper was £24 and a plain one is £8, and the branded hoodie £28 against £12, she is £32 better off per child on the two items, before the second-hand rail the school now has to run. If the school had kept all six, her complaint would cite the Act and the guidance, and the first ask would be for the jumper and hoodie to lose their branding, which is what the school ended up doing anyway.

6. The honest verdict

From September 2026 a state school in England can make you buy at most three branded items, or four at secondary if one is a tie, and bags, PE kit and summer-only items all count. That is law, not guidance, and it is the first time the number has been fixed.

It is a cap on logos, not on prices, and it does nothing outside England. Count the items on your school's list today. If it is over, a written complaint citing section 551ZA is the route, and the school's legal duty to make second-hand uniform available is the saving you can use straight away.

Frequently asked

How many branded items can a school require from September 2026? Three for primary pupils. Three for secondary pupils, or four if one of the items is a tie. Middle schools use the secondary limit. Source: Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, section 35.

Does a school bag count? Yes. The Act defines school uniform as a bag and any clothing required for school or school activities, and DfE guidance says branded book bags and rucksacks count towards the limit.

Does the limit cap the price of uniform? No. It limits the number of branded items. Guidance says schools should prioritise value for money and avoid single-supplier contracts, but there is no legal price cap.

Does this apply in Wales or Scotland? No. The Act's section 35 applies to schools in England. Wales and Scotland have their own guidance without a numerical limit in law.
