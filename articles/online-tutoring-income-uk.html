Making Money From Online Tutoring in the UK: What It Really Pays

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Online tutoring is one of the better paid flexible incomes in the UK, and you do not need to be a qualified teacher to start. If you know a subject well enough to explain it clearly, there is demand. Here is the honest picture of what it pays and how it works.

What it realistically pays

Rates vary a lot by subject and level. General primary and secondary tutoring pays a solid hourly rate, and specialist subjects, exam preparation and higher levels pay more. Going independent, once you have a reputation, pays better than platforms because there is no cut taken.

The honest catch is that you are paid only for the hours you teach, and building a full schedule takes time. Early on it is a few hours a week. Established tutors with a waiting list earn a genuinely good part time or full time income.

Platforms versus going independent

Tutoring platforms find students for you, handle payment, and give you a profile. In return they take a share of every hour, sometimes a large one. They are the easiest way to start and get your first reviews.

Going independent, tutoring students you find yourself, keeps all the money but means you handle finding students, scheduling and payment. Most tutors start on a platform, build confidence and reviews, then move students independent over time.

The checks and rules you need

If you tutor children, expect to need an enhanced background check, a DBS check, and reputable platforms will require one. Parents will ask too. It is a normal, one off step and it builds trust.

Tutoring income is taxable. Once your total earnings pass £1,000 in a tax year you register for Self Assessment, as covered in our side hustle tax guide. Keep records from the first paid hour.

What actually makes a tutor in demand

Clear explanation, patience, and reliability beat fancy qualifications. Parents and students return to tutors who turn up on time, prepare, and make hard things feel simple. Good reviews then bring the next students with no effort.

Specialising helps. Being the person who is excellent at one exam or one subject is easier to market than being generally available for everything.

Getting your first students

  1. Start on an established platform to get your first paid hours and reviews.
  2. Sort your background check early, since it unlocks the most work.
  3. Pick one or two subjects and levels you can teach brilliantly, not everything.
  4. Ask every happy family for a review and a referral. Word of mouth fills a tutoring schedule fastest.

The honest bottom line

Online tutoring is a flexible, well paid UK income for anyone who can explain a subject clearly and turn up reliably. Start on a platform, get your checks and reviews, then keep more by going independent. If teaching is your strength, it pairs naturally with the content routes in our YouTube earnings guide.
