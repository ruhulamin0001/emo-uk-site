Bank Rate has not moved since the summer. You have seven weeks until it might

The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 17 September 2026. The next decision is Thursday 5 November, and it comes with a full Monetary Policy Report. That gap is the useful part.

The short version

  • Bank Rate is 3.75%. The MPC held it there on 17 September 2026.
  • The next decision is Thursday 5 November 2026, and it comes with a Monetary Policy Report, which historically is when the Committee has more room to move.
  • It has now been held at 3.75% at three consecutive meetings: 18 June, 30 July and 17 September 2026.
  • A held rate does not mean held savings rates. Banks cut easy access rates between decisions, and often do it quietly.
  • After 5 November the next one is 17 December, so this is the longer of the two remaining gaps this year.

1. Where the rate actually is

Bank Rate is 3.75%. The Monetary Policy Committee maintained it at that level on 17 September 2026, and the Bank of England's own page lists the next decision as 5 November 2026.

It is worth noting how settled this has become. Looking at the Bank's published meeting record for this year, Bank Rate was maintained at 3.75% at the meetings on 18 June, 30 July and 17 September 2026. Three consecutive holds is not a pause in a moving story. It is the story.

For a household, a flat rate over several months is actually useful information, because it means the variable in your finances is no longer the Bank of England. It is your own provider.

2. Why 5 November is the meeting to watch

The MPC meets eight times a year, and four of those meetings are accompanied by a Monetary Policy Report. The Report is the Bank's full quarterly assessment of the economy, with updated forecasts for inflation and growth.

The remaining 2026 dates are Thursday 5 November, which has a Monetary Policy Report, and Thursday 17 December, which does not. The 2027 calendar then starts with 4 February, also with a Report.

None of that tells you what the Committee will do. It tells you when it has the most information in front of it, and when any change is most likely to be explained in detail rather than in a short summary. If you are going to read one MPC statement this year, 5 November is the one with the most in it.

3. The thing to check this month: your savings rate, not the base rate

Here is the part that costs people real money, and it has nothing to do with the Bank of England.

When Bank Rate is flat, savings providers do not have to do anything, and many use the quiet period to drift rates down on older accounts while advertising a competitive rate on a new one. An easy access account opened two years ago at a headline rate is very often not paying that rate now. Nothing was announced. The rate simply changed, with notice buried in a message you did not open.

So the single most useful thing to do before 5 November is not to predict the MPC. It is to log in and read the actual interest rate on your own savings account today, then compare it to Bank Rate at 3.75%.

The same applies to fixed rate bonds, but in the opposite direction. A fixed rate bond taken out when the base rate was higher may now be paying more than anything available today, in which case leaving it alone is the right answer. Rate chasing is not automatically correct. The question is always whether the rate in front of you is reasonable relative to the base rate, not whether it could theoretically be beaten.

If your instant access account is paying materially less than the base rate while the base rate has sat still for three meetings, that gap is your provider's decision, not the Bank's.

4. Borrowing: what a flat rate does and does not change

On a tracker mortgage, a held Bank Rate means your payment does not change. That is the direct link.

On a fixed rate mortgage, Bank Rate today is almost irrelevant. Fixed mortgage pricing follows swap rates, which move on expectations of where Bank Rate will be over the next two or five years, not where it is this morning. This is why fixed rates sometimes fall in the week the MPC holds, and sometimes rise. They are pricing a different question.

On credit cards and overdrafts, the link is weak in practice. Those rates are driven far more by the lender's own risk pricing than by the base rate.

So if you are on a fix that ends after November, the 5 November decision is interesting but it is not the thing that sets your next deal. The forecast in the Monetary Policy Report has more bearing on that than the rate decision itself.

5. A worked example: what checking one rate was worth

Yvonne is in Bristol and has £12,000 sitting in an easy access account she opened in 2024.

She checks the account and finds it is paying 1.90%. Bank Rate is 3.75%. She is not comparing her account to a best buy table, she is comparing it to the base rate, which is the fairer test of whether her provider is passing anything on.

At 1.90%, £12,000 earns £228 in a year before tax.

If she moved it to an account paying 3.60%, close to but below Bank Rate, £12,000 would earn £432 in a year before tax.

The difference is £204 a year, for one afternoon of admin, and it does not depend on what the MPC does on 5 November. If the Committee cuts in November, both rates would likely fall and the gap would largely remain. If it holds, the gap remains. Yvonne gains either way.

That is the honest point of this article. Nobody reading this can predict 5 November, including us. But the money most households leave on the table between now and then is not lost to the Bank of England's decision. It is lost to a savings rate nobody has looked at in two years.

One caveat worth stating plainly. If your savings are already in a competitive account paying near the base rate, there is nothing here for you to do, and moving for a few basis points is not worth the afternoon. This article does not help everyone.

Frequently asked

What is Bank Rate right now? 3.75%. It was maintained at that level by the Monetary Policy Committee on 17 September 2026.

When is the next Bank Rate decision? Thursday 5 November 2026, accompanied by a Monetary Policy Report. The final decision of 2026 is on Thursday 17 December.

Will my mortgage change on 5 November? If you are on a tracker, a change in Bank Rate feeds through directly. If you are on a fix, your payment does not change until your fix ends, whatever the MPC does.

Should I wait until after 5 November to move my savings? There is no mechanical reason to wait. If your current account pays well below the base rate, that gap exists now and waiting seven weeks simply means seven more weeks of earning less.
