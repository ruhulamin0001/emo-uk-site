Awin Wants £5 Before You Earn Anything: Which Affiliate Network Actually Pays a Small UK Site

Every comparison of UK affiliate networks leads with the commission rate. That is the least useful number of the lot. What decides whether a network is worth joining is the joining cost, the payment threshold and the lag before the money reaches your bank, and on all three the ranking is close to the opposite of what the rate cards suggest.

The short version

  • Awin charges a one-off £5 deposit to join, and it is only credited back to you when you reach your first payment threshold. Join, earn nothing, and the £5 stays gone.
  • Amazon Associates costs nothing to join but has a 24-hour cookie, pays most categories 1% to 4%, and pays out 60 days after the end of the month you earned in.
  • Amazon also gives you 180 days to make three qualifying sales. Miss that and the application is withdrawn, and Amazon will not reinstate the ID.
  • Skimlinks keeps 25% of every commission and its payment threshold is set in dollars at $65, so the sterling amount you need moves with the exchange rate.
  • On the worked example below, Amazon at 3% beat an Awin retailer at 5%, because conversion mattered more than the rate. That is the whole point.

1. What each network costs before you earn a penny

This is the section that gets skipped, and it is the one that decides whether joining is sensible at your size.

Amazon Associates UKFree£25 by bank transfer, £50 by cheque24 hours
Awin£5 deposit, refundable£20 by BACS, £100 by international wire30 days on most retail, 60 to 90 on some travel and finance
SkimlinksFree, but keeps 25% of commission$65, set in dollarsDepends on the underlying merchant programme

The Awin deposit deserves a straight explanation, because the network's own wording is gentler than the practical effect. Awin takes £5 at sign up to verify your identity and keep the publisher base clean. If your application is rejected, you can ask their compliance team to refund it to your card. If your application is accepted, the £5 is credited to your publisher account and comes back to you when you first hit the payment threshold.

Read that again. The refund is tied to reaching £20 in approved commission, not to being accepted. A site that joins Awin, is approved, and then earns £11 over the next two years has paid £5 to be an affiliate and will not see it again. That is a small sum, but it is the only UK network where being unsuccessful has a price tag, and almost nobody says so.

Amazon's cookie lasts 24 hours. Awin's usually lasts 30 days. That single difference matters more than the gap between 3% and 5%, and it does not point the way most people assume.

2. Why the commission rate is the wrong number to compare

The Amazon UK rate card sits mostly between 1% and 4%, with home and everyday categories around 3% to 4% and a handful of outliers such as luxury beauty at 10%. Put that next to an Awin retailer offering 5% or 8% and Amazon looks poor. We covered the full card in our guide to Amazon affiliate commission rates.

What that comparison ignores is that commission is the last term in the calculation, not the first. What you actually earn is:

clicks × conversion rate × average order value × commission rate

Amazon converts extremely well because the person arriving is usually already logged in, already has a card saved, and already has an address on file. A specialist retailer you found on Awin often has to build an account from scratch. A conversion rate that is three times higher will beat a commission rate that is 67% higher, every time.

The 24-hour cookie cuts the other way, and honestly so. Amazon's window closes the same day. Awin's typical 30 days will catch the person who reads your review on a Tuesday and buys on the following Sunday. For considered purchases with a long decision gap, that is a real advantage.

3. Worked example: Priya in Leicester

A home and garden blog, 4,000 sessions a month

Priya runs a small home and garden site. It gets 4,000 sessions a month, and about 3% of visitors click an affiliate link, which is 120 outbound clicks.

Route A, everything to Amazon. Amazon converts those clicks at roughly 8%, so 9.6 orders. At an average basket of £32 that is £307.20 of order value. At the 3% home rate, she earns £9.22 a month, or £110.60 a year.

Route B, everything to one Awin garden retailer at 5%. The retailer converts the same 120 clicks at 2.5%, so 3 orders. Its baskets are bigger, £48 each, giving £144 of order value. At 5% she earns £7.20 a month, or £86.40 a year.

Route C, Skimlinks on the same traffic. Assume it monetises much like Route B, but Skimlinks keeps 25%. She is left with £5.40 a month, or £64.80 a year.

The 3% network beat the 5% network by £24.20 a year on identical traffic. The higher headline rate lost, because conversion is a bigger lever than commission.

Now the thresholds. Amazon needs £25, so she crosses it in month three. Awin needs £20, also month three, and that is when her £5 comes back. Skimlinks needs $65, which on her earnings takes roughly nine to ten months. Her first Skimlinks payment would arrive some time the following summer.

Two honest caveats on that example. Conversion rates vary enormously by niche, and a finance or travel programme with a £60 flat bounty changes the picture completely. And Priya's real answer is not one route at all, it is Amazon for the everyday items and Awin for the two or three specialist retailers her readers actually buy from.

4. When the money actually reaches your bank

Affiliate income is slow in a way that catches people out, and the delay is different at each network.

  • Amazon Associates pays approximately 60 days after the end of the month in which the commission was earned. A sale in January is paid at the end of March. Add the time it takes to cross £25 and a small site's first payment is commonly four to six months after its first sale.
  • Awin waits for the advertiser to approve the transaction, which takes on average 30 to 45 days, then pays on the next payment day, either the 1st or the 15th depending on the frequency you chose. Faster than Amazon once running, but only after approval.
  • Skimlinks is slowest for a small site, purely because of the dollar threshold sitting above what a modest site earns in a month.

There is one deadline that is more serious than any of this. When you apply to Amazon Associates you have 180 days to refer three qualifying sales. Miss it and your application is withdrawn, and Amazon states it cannot reinstate the account or the Associates ID after rejection. You can reapply later with an established site, but the tag is gone.

The practical consequence: do not apply to Amazon before you have traffic. Applying early costs nothing in money and everything in clock. Build the site, get the visitors, then apply.

5. Which to join, and in what order

For a UK site under roughly 10,000 sessions a month, this order works.

  1. Amazon Associates, but only once traffic exists. Free, high conversion, broad catalogue, and the £25 threshold is reachable. The 180-day clock is the reason to wait rather than rush.
  2. Awin, once you know which two or three retailers your readers actually buy from. The £5 is only wasted if you never earn £20, and if you already know the merchants, you will. The 30-day cookie earns its keep on considered purchases.
  3. Skimlinks, mostly not yet. Its case is convenience on a site with hundreds of old posts full of unmonetised outbound links. On a small, deliberate site, giving away 25% to automate something you can do by hand is a poor trade.

One more thing that applies whichever you pick. Affiliate income is taxable and counts towards the £1,000 trading allowance, so keep records from the first payment rather than the first tax return. Our guide to side hustle tax rules covers where the thresholds sit.

Frequently asked

Is the Awin £5 ever genuinely refunded? Yes, in two situations. If your application is rejected you can ask compliance to refund it to your card. If you are accepted, it is credited to your account and released when you first reach the payment threshold, which for a GB publisher on BACS is £20.

Can I use Amazon Associates and Awin at the same time? Yes, and most UK sites should. They are not exclusive. Point each link at whichever merchant genuinely suits that product, rather than picking a network and forcing everything through it.

Does Amazon's 24-hour cookie mean I only get paid for that one product? No. Within that window you earn on qualifying items the customer adds to their basket, not only the item you linked to. The limitation is the time, not the product.

What if my site gets almost no traffic yet? Then none of these are worth joining today. Joining Awin costs £5 you may not recover, and joining Amazon starts a 180-day clock you cannot pause. Write more, get visitors, then apply. Our beginners guide covers the traffic arithmetic first.

The honest verdict

Compare networks on cost of entry, threshold and payment lag, not on the rate card. Amazon Associates is free to join, converts best, and pays about 60 days after month end once you clear £25. Awin costs £5 you only get back by earning £20, but the 30-day cookie and higher rates make it the right home for the specific retailers your readers use. Skimlinks takes a quarter of everything and sets its threshold in dollars, which makes it a poor first network for a small UK site.

On the worked example, a 3% programme out-earned a 5% one by £24.20 a year. If a comparison article ranks networks by commission percentage alone, it has not done the arithmetic.

And if your site has no traffic yet, the honest answer is that joining any of them today makes you poorer, not richer. Wait.
