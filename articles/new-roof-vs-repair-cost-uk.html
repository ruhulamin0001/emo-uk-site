Stop Comparing £800 Against £9,000. Divide Both by the Years They Buy

A roof repair and a roof replacement are not the same kind of purchase, so putting their prices side by side tells you almost nothing. One buys a few years, one buys several decades. Divide each by the years it buys and the answer usually becomes obvious, including the cases where the obvious answer is to keep patching.

The short version

  • Replacement is £5,000 to £12,000, repair is £300 to £1,500. Those numbers are not comparable until you divide each by the years it buys.
  • A new concrete tile roof at £9,000 over 50 years is £180 a year. That is the number to beat.
  • A £900 repair that buys five years is £180 a year too. Exactly break even. Anything that buys fewer years than that is losing.
  • Coverings differ enormously. Natural slate can run past 100 years, clay 60 to 100, concrete tile 40 to 60, EPDM and fibreglass 25 to 30, felt 10 to 20.
  • Replacing a roof covering is notifiable work. GOV.UK lists replacing roof coverings on pitched and flat roofs as work that may need building regulations approval.
  • Three repairs in five years is the signal. Not the cost of any one of them. The frequency is what tells you the covering has reached the end.

1. The only sum that matters

A roofer gives you two prices. Patch the slipped tiles and re-bed the ridge for £800, or strip and re-cover the whole thing for £9,000. The instinct is to take the £800 because it is eleven times smaller. The instinct is sometimes right and sometimes very wrong, and the way to tell is one division.

Cost per remaining year = price ÷ years of service it buys.

Replacement: £9,000 ÷ 50 years of concrete tile = £180 a year.

Repair: £800 ÷ the years before the next one.

So the question becomes: how many years does £800 buy? If the roofer tells you honestly that this covering has about five years left in it, that repair is £160 a year and it wins. If the covering is at the end and you will be back within two years, it is £400 a year and it loses badly.

The repair is not cheap because it is small. It is cheap only if it is the last one.

This is also why the honest answer is frequently "repair". A roof with twenty years of covering life left, that has lost a few tiles in a storm, should be repaired. Replacing it would be throwing away twenty years of an asset you already own. Any roofer who reaches for replacement in that situation is not doing arithmetic.

2. How long each covering actually lasts

These are the service lives published by the roofing trade. Two caveats before you use them. First, the figures come from roofing contractors and manufacturers, who sell roofs, so treat the upper ends as best case. Second, installation quality moves every one of these numbers substantially, which is exactly why the National Federation of Roofing Contractors publishes workmanship standards at all.

  • Natural slate: 100 years plus, and figures up to 150 are quoted. Slate roofs usually fail at the nails and battens long before the slate does, which is why a re-slate often reuses the original slates.
  • Clay tile: 60 to 100 years.
  • Concrete tile: 40 to 60 years. They fail by the surface eroding until the tile goes porous and brittle.
  • EPDM rubber and fibreglass, flat roofs: 25 to 30 years.
  • Mineral felt, flat roofs: 10 to 15 for traditional, 15 to 20 for modern high performance.

The most useful thing in that list is the slate entry, because it changes what you should be buying. If your Victorian terrace has original slate that is sound, the job is very often to strip, re-batten, re-felt and re-lay the same slates, adding new ones only where they have broken. That is a different and cheaper proposition from buying a roof's worth of new covering, and it is worth asking for it explicitly rather than accepting a quote for concrete tiles on a slate house.

That is the crossover. It is not a price. It is a frequency.

4. The building regulations point nobody mentions

GOV.UK's list of work that may need building regulations approval includes replacing roof coverings on pitched and flat roofs. Most homeowners have no idea, because the roofer handles it or, too often, does not.

The practical consequences are the same as for any other notifiable work, and they are set out plainly on the same page. The person doing the work could be prosecuted and fined for non compliance. The building control body could make you pay to fix faulty work. And, most relevant to a household budget, without approval you will not have the certificates of compliance you may need when you want to sell your home.

Note also that from 1 October 2026, GOV.UK says applications for building control approval and initial notices for certain residential buildings may be subject to the Building Safety Levy. If your job needs an application, ask whether the quote allows for it.

A repair is different. Replacing a handful of slipped tiles is maintenance, not re-covering, and does not trigger this. The line between the two is roughly a quarter of the roof area, and if your job is near that line the person to ask is your local authority building control department, not your roofer.

5. Worked example: the Adeyemi roof in Leicester

A 1978 detached house in Leicester with the original concrete tile roof. That makes the covering 48 years old, which is inside the 40 to 60 band but towards the top of it. Over the last four years they have paid for three repairs: £420, £680 and now a quote for £750 after two more tiles came off in a gale.

What they have spent on repairs so far. £420 + £680 = £1,100 over four years, or £275 a year.

Add the new quote. £1,850 over five years, or £370 a year, and rising.

Replacement quote. £9,400 for a full strip and re-tile, concrete, with new felt and battens.

Cost per year if it lasts 50 years. £9,400 ÷ 50 = £188 a year.

Cost per year if it only lasts 40. £9,400 ÷ 40 = £235 a year. Still cheaper than what they are paying now.

So for the Adeyemis the answer is replace, and the reason is not the £750. It is that three repairs in four years on a 48 year old concrete covering means the surface has gone porous across the whole roof and they are now buying time in twelve month blocks at £370 a year, against £188 a year for a roof that outlives them.

The number that would have changed the answer is age. Identical symptoms on a 20 year old covering would mean storm damage on a sound roof, and the right answer would have been to repair and forget about it.

Frequently asked

Will my insurance pay for it? For storm damage to an otherwise sound roof, often yes. For a covering that has simply worn out, no. Buildings insurance covers sudden damage, not gradual deterioration, and "the roof is old" is the standard and correct reason for a declined claim. This is another reason not to let a worn covering run on: the longer it runs, the more of the eventual failure is deemed wear.

Should I take the scaffolding down between jobs? If you are having other work done at height, do it at the same time. Scaffolding is a significant share of a roofing bill and erecting it twice is money burnt. Gutters, fascias, chimney repointing and solar all belong in the same visit.

What about spray foam under the roof? Treat any offer to spray foam the underside of your roof with real caution, particularly if it is pitched as an alternative to re-covering. It has caused serious mortgage and resale problems for many households, and it can hide rather than fix a leaking covering.

How do I get comparable quotes? Insist all three price the same specification: same covering, whether battens and underfelt are included, whether scaffolding is included, what happens to the old covering, and what guarantee is offered and by whom. A guarantee from a sole trader is worth what the sole trader is worth in ten years.

The honest verdict

Do the division before you decide. Replacement cost divided by the covering's expected life gives you a number, usually between £110 and £250 a year. Then ask the roofer, in writing, how many years the repair buys. If the repair costs more per year than the replacement does, replace.

If your covering has plenty of life left and you have simply lost tiles in a storm, repair it and stop reading. That is the cheapest line on the whole table and there is nothing clever to do beyond it.

The signal to replace is not a big quote. It is the third repair in five years. At that point you are renting a roof at several hundred pounds a year, and buying one costs less.
