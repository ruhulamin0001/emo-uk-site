Before You Chase a Switching Bonus, Price the Hoops

Bank switching offers are one of the few genuinely free bits of money in UK personal finance, and the mechanics behind them work remarkably well. The catch is never the switch itself. It is the list of conditions attached to the payment, and whether you can meet them without changing how you actually live.

The short version

  • The switch itself is genuinely reliable. In the first quarter of 2026, 99.8% of switches completed within seven working days, across 53 participating banks and building societies, according to Pay.UK.

1. How the switch service actually works

The Current Account Switch Service is run by Pay.UK and is why switching stopped being frightening. Under a full switch covered by the guarantee, the new bank moves your balance, redirects payments, transfers direct debits and standing orders, and closes the old account, on a date you choose.

The performance figures are better than most people assume. In the first quarter of 2026 there were 319,529 switches, of which 99.8% completed within seven working days, across 53 participating banks and building societies. Over the preceding twelve months the service handled 1,151,244.

Satisfaction ran at 90%, 68% of that quarter's switchers preferred their new account and only 2% said it was worse. Awareness sat at 76%, so roughly a quarter of UK adults still do not know the service exists.

Two details worth holding on to. The guarantee covers a full switch, which closes the old account. And payments sent to your old details are redirected, so a payroll department that has not updated its records cannot strand your salary.

2. The conditions are where the money is won or lost

Nobody fails to get a bonus because the switch broke. They fail on the terms. The conditions that recur:

  • Minimum pay in, by a deadline. The trap is the word credited. A transfer from your own savings sometimes counts and sometimes does not.
  • A minimum number of active direct debits. Often two or three, and often they must be transferred as part of the switch rather than set up afterwards. This catches out anyone whose bills are paid from a different account.
  • Stay open for a qualifying period. Closing early can claw the payment back, and it sets the earliest date you could switch again.
  • Register for online or mobile banking, and log in. Trivial, and trivially easy to forget inside a deadline.
  • New customer only. The big one. Many offers exclude anyone who has held an account with that bank, or any bank in the same group, sometimes stretching back several years.

Read the exclusion clause first, not last. If you held an account with that group in 2019, everything else on the page is irrelevant to you.

There is also a hard credit search in most cases, because you are opening a credit facility even if you never use an overdraft. One search is not a problem. Several before a mortgage application is, and is worth timing around.

3. How to price any offer in four steps

Rather than a list of offers that would be stale by the weekend, here is the calculation to run against whatever is on the table.

One. Check the exclusion clause. If you have ever banked with that group, check eligibility before spending another minute. This single step removes most wasted effort.

Two. Count the hoops in hours, honestly. Opening the account and starting the switch, about thirty minutes. Moving direct debits that are not already on the account you are switching from, an hour or more. Remembering deadlines, another thirty minutes spread over a month.

Three. Add the account's own cost. A packaged account at £12 a month costs £144 a year. A bonus smaller than that is not a gain, it is a discount on a product you may not want.

Four. Divide. Bonus, less fees over the qualifying period, divided by the hours. That is your effective hourly rate, and the only number that compares a switch against any other use of the same afternoon.

On tax, be careful of confident answers in either direction. HMRC's Capital Gains Manual at CG13026 onwards treats cashback style inducements from financial institutions as not a capital sum derived from an asset, and so not chargeable to capital gains tax, with income treatment arising only in certain circumstances, normally where the payment is received in the course of a trade or employment. The same manual tells HMRC staff to refer difficult cashback cases to its Financial Products Team, so there is no simple universal rule. For a household switching a personal current account, tax generally does not arise, but this is not tax advice.

4. Worked example: Michelle in Belfast does the sum

Michelle sees an offer paying £175 for a full switch: two direct debits transferred with the switch, £1,500 paid in within 60 days, mobile banking registered, account kept open six months, no monthly fee.

Eligibility. Never held an account with that group. Clear.

Hours. Her account already carries four direct debits, so they move automatically. Opening and switching, 30 minutes. Mobile banking, 10 minutes. Her salary is well above £1,500 and redirects anyway, so the pay in costs nothing. Total: about 45 minutes.

Costs. No monthly fee. One hard credit search, and she has no borrowing planned. £0.

Effective rate. £175 for 45 minutes is about £233 an hour.

Now change one fact. Suppose Michelle's direct debits all run from the joint account she shares with her partner. To meet the condition she must move her phone and gym payments across and then live with that split. Call it an extra hour, plus a standing untidiness in the household budget. Same £175, now about £100 an hour, and a permanent small complication in a system that was working. Still probably worth it, but noticeably less obviously so, and that is the calculation the offer page will not do for you.

The honest verdict

Switching for a bonus is one of the genuinely good deals available to a UK household. The service works, the seven working day guarantee is met 99.8% of the time, and the money is unconditional once the conditions are.

The discipline is to check eligibility first, read the direct debit condition carefully because it turns an easy job into a fiddly one, and ignore any offer attached to a fee paying account unless you want that account.

One thing this does not help with. If you are overdrawn, switching is not an escape. The new bank decides whether to offer you an overdraft, and if it does not, the balance still has to be settled. Deal with that first, separately, using the free debt advice linked below.

Frequently asked

Will switching hurt my credit score? Opening the new account normally involves a hard search, visible to lenders for around a year, with a small and short lived effect. Closing the old one removes an ageing account from your file. Neither matters much alone. Several switches in quick succession before a mortgage application is the scenario to avoid.

Can I switch if I have an overdraft? You can apply, but the new bank decides whether to give you an overdraft and on what terms. If it offers less than you use, you cover the difference. Check before starting the switch, not during it.

What if something goes wrong with the switch? The guarantee commits the banks to refunding any interest and charges on either account caused by a failure in the switch. Raise it with the new bank, since it runs the switch.
