Nobody Has Ever Saved Money by Buying a Bifold Door

A three panel bifold runs from about 2,000 pounds in uPVC to about 6,000 in aluminium, fitted. The energy saving over the patio door it replaces is real but small, and the difference between the two frame materials is worth roughly two pounds a year. So choose on something else.

The short version

  • Fitted price bands: uPVC three panel 2,000 to 3,200 pounds, aluminium three panel 3,800 to 5,500, aluminium four panel 4,800 to 6,500. Structural opening work is extra and often 900 to 2,500 on top.
  • Replacing an old single glazed patio door saves about 71 pounds a year on a 3 metre by 2.1 metre opening, worked from Ofgem gas rates below.
  • The aluminium versus uPVC energy difference is about 2 pounds a year. Two. It is not a reason to choose either one.
  • A 2,300 pound upgrade to aluminium never pays back on energy, so the case for it is sightlines, glass area, span and lifespan, and the article says so plainly rather than pretending otherwise.
  • The threshold decides more than the frame. A low or flush threshold looks right and is the commonest source of water ingress complaints. Ask what the drainage detail is before you ask about the colour.

1. The heat arithmetic, done properly

Bifold doors are sold on light, on the garden, on the feeling of the room opening up. Then somewhere near the end of the pitch comes a line about energy efficiency and A ratings, and that line is doing work it has not earned. Here is what it is actually worth.

Every heat figure on this page is worked the same way, so you can argue with it. Take the U value of what is there now, subtract the U value of what replaces it, multiply by the area in square metres. That gives watts lost per degree of temperature difference. Multiply by 48,000, which is 2,000 heating degree days for a UK heating season expressed in degree hours. Multiply by 0.7, because the heating is not on all winter and because bodies, cooking and sunlight already contribute. Divide by 0.85 for what a gas boiler delivers against what it burns. Then price it at 7.97p per kWh, the Ofgem capped gas unit rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026, checked on 15 September 2026. Change any assumption you like and the arithmetic still works, which is more than can be said for most saving claims.

Take a typical opening of 3.0 metres by 2.1 metres, which is 6.3 square metres. An aluminium patio door from the 1980s with no thermal break and single glazing has a whole door U value of about 5.0. A modern bifold, aluminium or uPVC, will be around 1.4.

The difference is 3.6. Times 6.3 square metres is 22.7 watts per degree. Times 48,000 degree hours is 1,089 kWh. Times 0.7 is 762. Divided by 0.85 is 896 kWh of gas. At 7.97p that is 71 pounds a year.

Seventy one pounds. Against a fitted cost that starts at 2,000 pounds and climbs past 6,000. On the cheapest possible door the payback is 28 years, and on an aluminium four panel it is over 90. This is not a saving measure and nobody should sell it as one.

Now the comparison people actually agonise over. A good uPVC bifold might hit a whole door U value of 1.3, an aluminium one 1.4, because uPVC insulates better as a material even though the fatter frames mean less glass. Take that 0.1 difference. Times 6.3 is 0.63 watts per degree. Times 48,000 is 30 kWh. Times 0.7 divided by 0.85 is 25 kWh of gas. At 7.97p that is 2 pounds a year.

Two pounds. If a salesperson tells you the frame material will show up on your energy bill, they have either never done this sum or they are hoping you have not.

2. Aluminium against uPVC, without the sales script

Since energy is not the deciding factor, here is what actually is.

Sightlines. Aluminium is far stronger per unit of thickness, so the frames are slimmer and you get noticeably more glass in the same opening. On a three metre opening the difference is roughly the width of a hand across each panel. Some people cannot unsee it. Others genuinely do not notice.

Span and panel count. uPVC struggles beyond about three metres and beyond three or four leaves, because the sashes get heavy and the frames flex. Aluminium handles six metres and six or seven panels without complaint. If your opening is wide, the choice may be made for you.

Lifespan and finish. A powder coated aluminium frame holds its finish for 25 years or more. uPVC in white is stable, but the fashionable anthracite grey foils on cheaper systems can chalk and fade on a south facing elevation within a decade, and they cannot be repainted successfully.

Hardware. This is the part that fails, on both. Bifolds run on rollers in a track and the running gear takes the whole weight of the leaf every time it moves. Ask what the guarantee on the running gear is separately from the guarantee on the frame. Ten years on the frame and two on the hardware is a common and unhelpful split.

4. The costs that are not in the quote

Almost nobody is replacing a door in an opening that is already the right size. Widening a wall to take a three metre bifold means a structural opening, which means a steel beam, a calculation from a structural engineer, and a building control application. That is commonly 900 to 2,500 pounds on top, and up to 4,000 if the wall is loadbearing above two floors.

Then there is the threshold. A flush or low threshold, level with the floor inside and the patio outside, is what everybody wants and it is the single biggest source of leaks. Water needs somewhere to go. Ask the installer to explain the drainage channel, the fall on the paving outside and where the water actually exits. If the answer is vague, get a different installer, not a different door.

Finally, replacing a door is controlled work under the building regulations in England, and it must meet the standard in the Approved Document L edition that applies to the job. A FENSA or CERTASS registered installer self certifies it. If you use someone who is not registered, you need a building control application yourself, and the certificate will be asked for when you sell.

5. Worked example: the Ferreira extension in Taunton

Marco and Helena Ferreira are opening the back of their Taunton semi into the garden. The existing opening is a 1986 aluminium patio door, 2.4 metres wide. They want three metres of glass.

Two quotes. uPVC three panel at 2,750 pounds fitted, or aluminium three panel at 4,900. Both need the opening widened, which is a steel and a building control application at 1,850 either way.

So the real decision is 2,150 pounds. The energy difference between the two doors, on the arithmetic above, is 2 pounds a year. Over thirty years that is 60 pounds against a 2,150 pound premium.

They choose aluminium, and they are right to, but not for the reason the brochure gave. The uPVC system quoted maxes out at three metres with visibly chunkier stiles, and Helena works at the table by that window. Over thirty years the aluminium costs them about 183 pounds a year against 156 for the uPVC. Twenty seven pounds a year to get the view they wanted is a reasonable thing to buy. Calling it an energy saving would not have been.

What they also did, which cost nothing, was ask both firms to put the running gear guarantee in writing. One offered ten years on the frame and two on the hardware. The other offered ten on both. That answer moved more money than the frame material ever would.

Frequently asked

Do bifolds need planning permission? Replacing a door like for like does not. Forming a new or wider opening in a rear wall is usually permitted development on a house, but it is still building control work, and it is not permitted development on a flat or in some conservation areas.

Are bifolds less secure than a wall? They are as secure as their locking points and their glass. Look for a multi point locking system and laminated rather than toughened glass on the outer pane, and check the door carries PAS 24 certification, which is what insurers and building control recognise.

Do they work in winter? A modern bifold at a U value of 1.4 loses roughly a third of what the door it replaced did, so the room will be warmer than it was. It will still be the coldest wall in the house, because six square metres of glass always will be. Thermal curtains or a well fitted blind over the opening are worth more per pound than anything in the door specification.

Sliding or folding? Sliding gives a larger uninterrupted pane and fewer moving parts to fail. Folding gives you the whole opening cleared. Sliders are quietly the more sensible purchase for most households and the less fashionable one.

The honest verdict

Buy a bifold because you want the room to open onto the garden. Do not buy one expecting it to pay for itself, because at about 71 pounds a year against the door it replaces it will not, and the difference between aluminium and uPVC is worth about two pounds a year.

Choose aluminium if the opening is over three metres or the sightlines matter to you. Choose uPVC if neither applies and you would rather have the 2,000 pounds. Spend the argument on the threshold drainage and the hardware guarantee, because those are what actually go wrong.
