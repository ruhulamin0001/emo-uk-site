An Electric Shower Costs About A Third More Per Shower Than A Gas Mixer, And Uses Half The Water. Here Is When Each One Wins.

Electric showers are sold in 7.5 to 10.5 kilowatt ratings and every one of those kilowatts comes from the grid at 26.32p a unit under the October 2026 cap. A mixer shower fed by a gas combi heats its water at 7.97p a unit, three times cheaper, but pushes out nearly twice as many litres a minute. Both facts matter, and so does whether you pay for water by the cubic metre. We priced eight minutes under each, then a year for one, two and four people, then added the meter.

1. What the kilowatt rating means, and what it costs per minute

An electric shower is a heating element in a box. Cold water passes over it and comes out warm, and the only thing that decides how warm and how fast is the element's power. Triton, one of the two big UK makers, lists its range in ratings from 7.5 kW to 10.5 kW, with 8.5, 9.5 and 10.5 the common choices, and explains on its own guide that the higher ratings heat water faster. That is the whole specification.

Price it. A 9.5 kW shower running flat out draws 9.5 kWh an hour, so 0.158 kWh a minute. At the Ofgem cap unit rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026, 26.32p per kWh, that is 4.2p a minute, or 33p for an eight-minute shower. An 8.5 kW unit is 3.7p a minute; a 10.5 kW unit 4.6p. The thermostat on a modern unit cycles the element in summer when the incoming water is warmer, so these are winter figures and the summer cost is lower.

The physics also tells you why the shower feels weak in January. Heating water takes 4.186 kilojoules per litre per degree. A 9.5 kW element delivers 9.5 kilojoules a second, so to raise water by 25 degrees, from a winter mains of 15 to a shower of 40, it can manage 9.5 divided by 4.186 divided by 25, which is 0.091 litres a second, or 5.4 litres a minute. In summer, with mains water at 20 degrees, the same unit gives 6.8 litres a minute. There is no setting that changes this; the flow you get is the flow the element can heat.

That low flow is the electric shower's saving grace as well as its weakness. Eight minutes at 5.4 litres a minute is 43 litres. Hold that number for the water meter section.

2. What a gas mixer shower costs, and why it uses more water

A mixer shower blends hot water from the boiler or cylinder with cold, and the flow is set by the mains pressure and the boiler's hot water rate, not by an element. A combi boiler typically supports 10 to 12 litres a minute at a useful temperature; we use 10, which is on the cautious side for the mixer.

Our arithmetic. Eight minutes at 10 litres a minute is 80 litres. Raising 80 litres by 25 degrees takes 80 times 4.186 times 25 kilojoules, which is 8,372 kJ, or 2.33 kWh of heat. A modern condensing combi turns about 90 per cent of its gas into hot water, so that is 2.58 kWh of gas, which at the October 2026 cap rate of 7.97p is 21p. For a shower of the same length, the gas mixer costs about two thirds of the electric unit, despite delivering nearly twice the water, because gas is 3.3 times cheaper per unit of heat.

If the mixer runs off a hot water cylinder heated by the boiler, the per-shower gas figure is similar but there is a standing loss from the cylinder whether you shower or not; the Energy Saving Trust puts insulating a cylinder to 80 mm at £45 a year, which tells you the scale of that loss. If the cylinder is heated by an immersion, the mixer is an electric shower with extra steps and costs more than the box on the wall, because it heats 80 litres electrically instead of 43. We have covered that sum separately.

The honest per-shower comparison is therefore 33p of electricity for 43 litres against 21p of gas for 80 litres. Which is cheaper depends on whether the litres cost you anything.

4. The water meter twist, and the cost of switching

If you are on a meter, the extra 37 litres a shower is charged twice, once as water in and once as sewerage out, and the combined rate is printed on your bill per cubic metre. We are not quoting a national figure because there is not one; rates differ by company and change every April. Take the litres from the table, divide by 1,000 for cubic metres, multiply by your combined rate, and subtract from the energy saving. For a family of four on a typical 2026 combined rate the water takes back somewhere between a third and a half of the £185, and the mixer still wins, more slowly. For a single person it can wipe the saving out entirely.

If you are not on a meter, water is free at the margin and the energy saving is the whole story. That is also the household where our water meter article says to check whether a meter would cut the bill, which is a separate sum.

The cost of switching. Electric to mixer needs a hot water supply at the shower, which means a combi or cylinder with enough flow and pressure, a plumber to run hot and cold to a mixer valve, and an electrician to disconnect and make safe the shower's dedicated circuit. £300 to £800 all in is a reasonable planning range and the table uses £500. Going the other way, mixer to electric, needs a new high-current circuit from the consumer unit and is often dearer.

When the electric shower is the right answer anyway. A home with no gas. A cylinder that runs cold by the third shower. A boiler that cannot deliver 10 litres a minute in winter. A second bathroom used rarely, where the standing losses of keeping a cylinder hot outweigh the per-shower premium. In all of those the electric shower's independence is worth its 12p a shower.

5. Worked example: Amara in Nottingham

Three-bed semi, gas combi fitted 2022, two adults and two teenagers, 9.5 kW electric shower over the bath, water meter

The four of them shower daily, the teenagers for longer, call it an average of ten minutes each. The combi is rated for 12 litres a minute and the bathroom is above the kitchen, so the pipe run is short. A plumber has quoted £450 for a thermostatic mixer and the making safe.

Electric now. Four showers at ten minutes is 40 minutes a day at 0.158 kWh, 6.33 kWh, £1.67 a day, £608 a year. Water: 40 minutes at 5.4 litres, 216 litres a day.

Gas mixer. 40 minutes at 10 litres is 400 litres a day, 2.5 times the electric shower's; the heat is 11.6 kWh, the gas 12.9 kWh, £1.03 a day, £376 a year. Energy saving £232 a year.

The meter. Extra water 184 litres a day, 67 cubic metres a year. Amara reads the combined water and sewerage rate off her bill and multiplies; at any plausible 2026 rate it is a meaningful sum, and she finds it takes back roughly half the energy saving. Net saving somewhere around £110 to £130 a year.

Payback. £450 divided by about £120 is just under four years, against two years if she had not been on a meter. She goes ahead, because the combi is new and the teenagers will be there for years, and fits a shower timer at the same time, which is where the bigger saving was hiding.

6. The honest verdict

If you have a gas combi that can deliver ten litres a minute and three or more people showering daily, a mixer shower is cheaper to run than an electric one by £140 to £280 a year at the October 2026 cap, and the plumbing pays back in two to four years, longer on a water meter because the mixer uses nearly twice the water. Do it when the bathroom is next being worked on.

If you live alone, are on a meter, or have no gas, keep the electric shower: the energy gap is £46 a year, the water gives most of it back, and the unit's low flow is doing you a favour. Whoever you are, the cheapest change is two minutes off the shower, which saves more than either technology.

Frequently asked

How much does an electric shower cost per minute? A 9.5 kW unit at full power draws 0.158 kWh a minute, which is 4.2p at the October 2026 cap rate of 26.32p; 8.5 kW is 3.7p and 10.5 kW is 4.6p. An eight-minute shower on a 9.5 kW unit is about 33p.

Is a mixer shower cheaper than an electric shower? On energy, yes, if the hot water comes from a gas boiler: about 21p for eight minutes against 33p, our arithmetic at 7.97p gas and 90 per cent boiler efficiency. But the mixer uses about 80 litres to the electric shower's 43, so on a water meter the gap narrows and for a single person it can close entirely.

Why is my electric shower cold in winter? Because the element can only add so much heat per litre. A 9.5 kW unit raising water by 25 degrees can manage about 5.4 litres a minute; when the mains arrives at 8 degrees instead of 15 it must either slow the flow or run cooler. That is physics, not a fault.

How much does it cost to change an electric shower to a mixer? Typically £300 to £800: a plumber to run hot and cold to a mixer valve and an electrician to make the shower circuit safe. It needs a combi or cylinder able to supply around 10 litres a minute of hot water; a weak boiler makes a poor mixer shower.
