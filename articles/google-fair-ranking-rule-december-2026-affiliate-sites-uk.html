Google Has Until 17 December To Rank UK Search Results Fairly. What The CMA Rule Actually Gives A Small Affiliate Site, And What It Does Not.

On 17 June 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority imposed its first conduct requirement on Google Search under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act. Google has until 17 December 2026 to comply. Most coverage has been written for news publishers. This is for the person running a UK affiliate site from a spare room, and it is honest about the limits.

The short version

  • The fair ranking conduct requirement was imposed on 17 June 2026. Google must comply by 17 December 2026. Source: CMA summary published 4 August 2026.
  • It covers organic results and AI features in the UK, not adverts. Google must rank on objective, non-discriminatory criteria and must not favour its own services, advertisers, or sites that have not opted out of AI use.
  • Ranking changes you could act on need 30 business days' notice. Changes likely to shift rankings significantly, where Google can explain what and why, need 15 business days.
  • There is a complaints route for manual actions and for material changes that distort UK markets. Fifty publishers complaining within 30 business days triggers a report to the CMA.
  • It does not guarantee anyone a ranking, does not remove AI Overviews, and does not apply outside the UK. A six month exemption exists for live-testing new organic features.
  • What to do now costs nothing: Search Console set up, manual action history kept, and the CMA's own feedback address noted.

1. What was imposed, and when it bites

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 lets the CMA designate a firm as having strategic market status in a digital activity and then impose conduct requirements on it. Google was designated for general search and search advertising, the CMA consulted on four proposed requirements between 28 January and 25 February 2026, and on 17 June 2026 it imposed the first of them, fair ranking, alongside a data portability requirement. The compliance deadline in the CMA's own summary is 17 December 2026.

The requirement applies to organic search results in the UK, including how organic results are shown relative to search features and AI features such as AI Overviews. It does not apply to paid results. So the ten blue links and the way Google's own boxes sit around them are in scope. The adverts above them are not.

A conduct requirement has teeth: breach can mean fines of up to 10% of global turnover. It is enforced by the CMA, not by you, which matters for the rest of this article.

2. The three parts, in plain terms

Part one, objective and non-discriminatory ranking. Google must rank organic results using objective, non-discriminatory criteria. It must not take into account whether a site advertises with Google or has any other commercial relationship with it, whether a site has opted out of its content being used for Google's AI, or whether a site has exercised legal or contractual rights against Google. It must treat its own services the same as competitors' in rankings. There is a limited exemption of up to six months where Google needs to live-test a new organic feature.

For an affiliate site the second bullet is the one to read twice. Sites will shortly be able to opt out of AI Overviews under a separate publisher requirement. The fair ranking rule says Google cannot punish that opt-out in the ordinary rankings.

Part two, transparency. Google must publish clear information on how it ranks organic results, and must give notice of material changes to its ranking criteria, policies or procedures. The notice periods are the concrete gain: no fewer than 30 business days before a change where publishers could take action to avoid or reduce the impact, and no fewer than 15 business days before a change that may cause significant shifts in ranking, where Google can explain what is changing and why. Six weeks' notice of an update you can prepare for is more than any affiliate site has ever had.

Part three, non-distortion and complaints. Google must run clear, accessible processes for publishers to give feedback on material changes that may distort UK markets, and to request reconsideration of manual actions affecting organic ranking. If 50 different publishers give feedback on one change within the first 30 business days of it taking effect in the UK, Google must report that to the CMA. And there must be an alternative dispute mechanism for publishers manually excluded from the search index altogether.

3. What the rule does not do

This is the part the SEO newsletters leave out, so it goes in full.

It does not guarantee any site a position. "Objective criteria" can still rank your review of a pressure washer below a retailer's, a national newspaper's and a forum thread's. Algorithmic demotion of thin affiliate content is objective if it is applied to everyone.

It does not remove or shrink AI Overviews. It requires that organic results and AI features be presented on non-discriminatory terms; it does not say the organic result must appear above the AI answer.

The complaints route is for manual actions and for material changes. An ordinary core update that costs you 40% of traffic is a material change you can give feedback on, and if 49 other UK publishers do the same the CMA hears about it. That is a reporting trigger, not a reversal. Nothing in the rule requires Google to restore anyone's rankings.

The dispute mechanism is only for sites manually excluded from the index entirely. A site that is indexed but invisible is not covered by it.

It is UK-only. If your traffic is from the United States, the rule changes nothing you will see.

And the live-testing exemption means a new organic feature can run as a test, without the notice periods, for up to six months.

4. A worked example in Leicester

Aisha in Leicester runs a baby-products review site, about 140 pages, roughly 60% of traffic from the UK. In March 2026 a core update took her from 900 to 400 visits a day, and a comparison page was hit by a manual action for what Search Console called "thin content with little or no added value". She reconsidered, was rejected once, and the page has been out of the index since.

Under the rule from 17 December: if the same core update happened again, she would have had at least 15 business days' notice that a significant change was coming, with Google's own explanation of what it targets, and possibly 30 if the change is one she could act on. She could give feedback through the new process, and if her trade body or 49 other UK sites did too, Google would have to report that to the CMA. Her traffic would not come back because of it.

The manual action is different. She would have a formal reconsideration process with defined standards, and because the page was excluded from the index, access to the dispute mechanism. Whether the page deserves to rank is still Google's call under objective criteria. Whether Google followed its own process is now the CMA's.

Her arithmetic: at £0.03 a visit, the 500 lost daily visits were about £15 a day, £5,475 a year. The rule gives her notice and a voice, not the £5,475.

5. What to do before 17 December

Three things, none of which cost money.

First, have Search Console verified for every property, because the transparency and notice obligations will almost certainly be delivered through it and through Google's Search Central channels. A site not in Search Console will not see the notices.

Second, keep a dated record of every manual action, every reconsideration request and every response. The complaints route will ask for it, and "we sent something in 2025" is not a record.

Third, note the CMA's own feedback address, searchsms@cma.gov.uk, and the fact that it invites comments via trade associations. Fifty publishers is the trigger. One publisher writing to the CMA directly is still evidence, and the CMA said it welcomes it.

Do not buy anything sold as "CMA compliance" for websites. The obligations sit on Google.

6. The honest verdict

From 17 December you get notice, an explanation and a complaints process. That is real, and it is more than UK sites have ever had from Google.

You do not get a ranking, a refund or a smaller AI Overview. Anyone selling this as the end of affiliate traffic loss has not read the 4 August summary.

Set up Search Console, keep records, and know the feedback address. Then go back to writing pages that would rank under objective criteria anyway.

Frequently asked

Does this apply to my site if most of my visitors are in the UK? Yes, the requirement covers Google's general search services in the UK. It does not apply to results served outside the UK.

Can I sue Google if it breaks the rule? No. Conduct requirements are enforced by the CMA. You can give feedback and use Google's new complaints processes, and you can write to the CMA.

Does the rule stop AI Overviews? No. It requires organic results and AI features to be ranked and presented on objective, non-discriminatory criteria. A separate publisher requirement deals with opting out of AI features.

What happens on 17 December 2026? That is the date by which Google must comply. The CMA will monitor compliance through reporting notices it issued on 17 June 2026.
