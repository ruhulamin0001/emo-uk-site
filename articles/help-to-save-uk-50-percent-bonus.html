Help To Save Pays A 50% Bonus And Closes To New Accounts In April 2027. Mind The Year 3 Trap.

No bank pays 50%. The government does, to a specific group: people receiving Universal Credit who earned at least £1 of take-home pay in their last assessment period. The scheme has been extended to April 2027 and the eligibility test was loosened in 2025. The bonus rules are simple until year 3, where one withdrawal can wipe out the second bonus. Here is exactly how the money works.

The short version

  • You can pay in £1 to £50 a calendar month and the government adds 50p for every £1 saved, paid as two bonuses at the end of year 2 and year 4. Source: GOV.UK.
  • Maximum £2,400 in, £1,200 of bonus out over four years.
  • Eligibility is now £1 of take-home pay in your last monthly Universal Credit assessment period. The old 16-hours-at-minimum-wage test went in April 2025. Source: HMRC, 17 April 2025.
  • The scheme is extended to April 2027. HMRC's press release says accounts can be opened until then; after that, nothing has been confirmed.
  • The year 3 trap: the final bonus is 50% of the amount your highest balance in years 3 and 4 exceeds your highest balance in years 1 and 2. Withdraw in year 3 and you have to rebuild past the old high before earning a penny more.
  • Savings of £6,000 or less, including this account, do not affect Universal Credit, and the bonuses themselves never do. Source: GOV.UK.

1. How the account works

Help to Save is a government-backed savings account run by HMRC. GOV.UK describes it in one line: it allows certain people receiving Universal Credit to get a bonus of 50p for every £1 they save over four years.

The mechanics are deliberately simple. You can pay in between £1 and £50 in each calendar month, by debit card, standing order or bank transfer. You do not have to pay in every month. Once you have paid in £50 in a month, you cannot pay in again until the next month. Withdrawals go only to your own bank account. The account closes four years after you open it, you keep the money, and you cannot open another.

Eligibility changed on 17 April 2025. Before then you had to be on Universal Credit or Tax Credits and earning the equivalent of 16 hours a week at the National Living Wage. Now, according to GOV.UK, you can open an account if you receive Universal Credit and you, with your partner on a joint claim, had take-home pay of £1 or more in your last monthly assessment period. HMRC estimated the change opened the scheme to 550,000 more people. Couples can each open one, applying separately.

If you stop claiming Universal Credit after opening the account, you keep it and keep earning the bonuses.

2. How the two bonuses are calculated

This is where most guides stop at "50%" and where the detail matters.

BonusWhenHow much
FirstEnd of year 250% of the highest balance you reached in years 1 and 2
FinalEnd of year 450% of the amount by which your highest balance in years 3 and 4 exceeds your highest balance in years 1 and 2

Both bonuses are paid into your bank account, not into the Help to Save account, and both are tax free. Notice that the bonuses are based on your highest balance, not your closing balance. You get any bonus you have earned even if you later withdraw. That is generous, and it is also the root of the year 3 problem.

Save the maximum £50 every month and the numbers are: £1,200 saved by the end of year 2, first bonus £600; £2,400 highest balance by the end of year 4, final bonus 50% of (2,400 minus 1,200), which is £600 again. Total £1,200 of bonus on £2,400 of savings. HMRC says 93% of savers pay in the full £50 a month.

3. The year 3 trap, with numbers

GOV.UK puts it plainly: if your highest balance does not increase, you will not earn a final bonus.

Suppose you save £50 a month for two years. Highest balance £1,200, first bonus £600, paid to your bank. In month 25 the boiler goes and you withdraw £1,000. Your balance is now £200.

Every pound you save in years 3 and 4 now goes towards getting back to £1,200 before it counts for anything. At £50 a month, that takes 20 months. By the end of year 4 your highest balance in years 3 and 4 is £1,400, which exceeds the old high by £200, so your final bonus is £100. Without the withdrawal it would have been £600.

The withdrawal did not cost you the £600 you had already earned. It cost you £500 of the bonus you were going to earn. The lesson is not "never withdraw". It is that withdrawals in years 1 and 2 are cheap, because the first bonus locks in your highest balance, and withdrawals in years 3 and 4 are expensive, because you have to climb back over that locked-in figure before the final bonus starts.

If you know you will need the money, the honest arithmetic often favours taking it out in year 2 after the balance has peaked, rather than in year 3.

4. A worked example: Aisha in Bradford

Aisha works 14 hours a week in a Bradford care home and receives Universal Credit. Under the old rules her hours were below the 16-hour test and she could not open an account. Under the current rule, her take-home pay is well above £1, so she qualifies.

She sets up a £50 standing order for the day after her Universal Credit payment. After 24 months she has £1,200 in the account and HMRC pays £600 into her bank account. She continues for another 24 months, reaches £2,400, and at the end of year 4 receives a further £600. Total: £2,400 of her own money back, plus £1,200 from the government, over four years, with no tax to pay on any of it.

For comparison, £50 a month into a savings account paying 4% for four years would earn roughly £200 of interest, before any tax. The Help to Save bonus is six times that. There is no product on the UK market that competes with it for the people who qualify.

5. Benefits, tax and the April 2027 date

GOV.UK states that if you or your partner have £6,000 or less in personal savings, including the Help to Save balance, it does not affect how much Universal Credit or Housing Benefit you get, and the bonuses themselves never affect either. Above £6,000 in total savings, the normal Universal Credit capital rules apply, so if you already hold savings elsewhere, count them together.

The bonuses are tax free and the account pays no interest, so there is nothing to declare.

On timing: HMRC's April 2025 announcement said the scheme is extended to April 2027. That is the date by which an account must be opened under the current rules. Whether it is extended again, made permanent, or closed has not been confirmed on GOV.UK as at 3 September 2026. Reports that it will be made permanent from 2028 are reports, not published policy, and we have not relied on them. If you are eligible, the safe course is to open the account well before April 2027, because opening it costs nothing and you are not obliged to pay anything in.

6. The honest verdict

If you are on Universal Credit with any take-home pay, open the account. Even £10 a month earns 50%. Nothing else does.

Pay in early in the month and leave it alone in years 3 and 4. The final bonus only counts new ground above your year 2 peak.

Watch your total savings against £6,000 if you have money elsewhere, and do not wait for an extension that has not been announced. There is nothing to buy here; the account is on GOV.UK and the HMRC app.

Frequently asked

Who can open a Help to Save account? Anyone receiving Universal Credit who, with their partner on a joint claim, had take-home pay of £1 or more in their last monthly assessment period, and who lives in the UK. Each partner in a couple can open their own account.

How much can I get? Up to £1,200 of bonus on £2,400 saved over four years: £600 at the end of year 2 and up to £600 at the end of year 4.

Can I withdraw money? Yes, at any time, to your own bank account. Bonuses already earned are safe. Withdrawing in years 3 and 4 makes the final bonus harder to earn because it is based on how far you exceed your year 1 and 2 peak.

Does Help to Save affect my Universal Credit? Not if your total savings, including the account, are £6,000 or less. The bonuses never affect Universal Credit or Housing Benefit.
