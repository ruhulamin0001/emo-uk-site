Budget Laptop Buying Guide UK: What to Get and What to Skip

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Laptop shops love to sell you power you will never use. For most people, browsing, documents, video and a side hustle, a modest laptop does everything, and the money saved is large. Here is what actually matters when buying a budget laptop in the UK.

The specs that genuinely matter

Memory, often called RAM, is the one that affects everyday smoothness most. Enough of it lets you have several tabs and apps open without the laptop crawling. This matters more than a fast processor for normal use.

A solid state drive, an SSD, rather than an old style hard drive. This single thing makes a laptop feel fast: quick to start up and open programs. Never buy a laptop with the old slow drive to save a few pounds.

Battery life and a screen you can actually read comfortably. You will use these every day, so they matter more than headline numbers.

The specs that are mostly marketing for you

The very fastest processor. Unless you edit video or play heavy games, a mid range chip feels identical for browsing and documents.

A dedicated graphics card. Only worth it for gaming or serious creative work. For everything else it just adds cost and drains battery.

Huge storage numbers, when a modern smaller SSD plus free cloud storage covers most people fine.

When refurbished wins

A refurbished business laptop is often the best value buy in the UK. These were built to last, come tested with a warranty from good sellers, and cost far less than new. A refurbished machine frequently beats a brand new budget laptop on build and speed for the same money.

How much to actually spend

For everyday use and a side hustle, you do not need to spend a fortune. A sensible new budget laptop, or a better specified refurbished one, covers it. Spend more only if you have a specific heavy need, like video editing, that genuinely requires it.

  1. Prioritise enough memory and an SSD above a fast processor.
  2. Seriously consider a refurbished business laptop with a warranty.
  3. Ignore graphics cards and top processors unless you game or edit video.
  4. Check the return window and warranty before you buy.

The honest bottom line

A good budget laptop needs enough memory, an SSD, and a decent screen and battery, not a top processor or graphics card. Refurbished business models are often the smartest buy. The same buy what you need thinking runs through our budget smartphone guide.
