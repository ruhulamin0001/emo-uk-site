Boiler Cover Insures A £225 Repair You Might Need Once Every Five Years, And Charges £228 A Year For It

Boiler cover is sold on the fear of a January breakdown, which is a real fear. The question is whether the premium is a fair price for that fear. Split the plan into its two parts, the service and the insurance, and the answer becomes arithmetic rather than nerves.

Ruhul Amin Published 10 September 2026 · 8 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The short version

  • Entry level boiler cover is about £19 a month with a £60 excess, so £228 a year. Zero excess plans run to £28 a month, or £336.
  • About £100 of that is the annual service, which you should have anyway and can buy standalone for £80 to £120. So the insurance part is roughly £128 a year.
  • The average boiler repair is £150 to £300. Call it £225. On a boiler failing roughly once every four or five years, the expected cost is about £50 a year.
  • £128 of premium against £50 of expected loss, less a £60 excess on top. That is a poor deal as insurance, and it is meant to be, because insurers price in their costs.
  • A sinking fund of £128 a year covers the average repair in 1.8 years and after five years holds £640, more than any single repair on the list.

1. Split the plan into the two things you are buying

Every boiler cover plan bundles two products that have nothing to do with each other, and the bundling is what makes it hard to judge.

Product one is an annual service. A Gas Safe engineer checks combustion, flue, pressure and safety devices, and issues a record. This is not optional in any meaningful sense. Most manufacturer warranties are void without an annual service, most landlord obligations require an annual gas safety check, and a boiler that is not serviced fails sooner. Bought standalone it is £80 to £120. British Gas list theirs at around £109.

Product two is breakdown insurance. If the boiler fails, an engineer comes out and parts and labour are covered, subject to the excess and to a list of exclusions.

Now do the subtraction. If the plan is £228 a year and the service inside it is worth about £100, then you are paying about £128 a year for the insurance. That is the number to judge, and it is never the number on the advert.

A boiler breaking down three times in five years is a boiler that should be replaced, and no cover plan will replace it for you.

£225 divided by £128 a year is 1.8 years. That is how long the sinking fund needs before it can absorb the average repair without flinching, and it is the payback figure for this decision.

3. What cover does not do, which is the part that matters

The thing people are actually frightened of is not a £200 repair. It is a £2,800 replacement in the middle of February. Boiler cover does not protect you from that, and the policy documents are clear about it even if the marketing is not.

Beyond economic repair. Every plan reserves the right to declare a boiler beyond economic repair, at which point cover ends and you buy a boiler. Some plans offer a contribution towards a replacement. It is typically a few hundred pounds against a bill of thousands.

Age limits. Many insurers will not take on a boiler over a certain age, and some will not cover a boiler they have not inspected. Once your boiler is old enough for cover to look attractive, it is often old enough to be refused.

Pre-existing faults and the initial exclusion period. There is usually a window of two to four weeks after you take the policy in which claims are not paid. Taking cover out because the boiler is making a noise does not work.

Sludge, scale and system problems. A great many "boiler" faults are actually a dirty system, and plans frequently exclude power flushing or charge separately for it.

Parts availability. If parts are no longer made for your boiler, the plan cannot fix it, and you are back to beyond economic repair.

Read the excess and the exclusions before the price. A £19 plan with a £60 excess and a long exclusion list is worse than a £24 plan with none, and the difference will not show up until the day you need it.

4. The three cases where cover is the right call

This page is not an argument that insurance is always bad. It is an argument that you should know what you are buying. There are three situations where the arithmetic tips.

One: you could not absorb a £300 bill without borrowing. This is the honest and legitimate reason and it has nothing to do with expected value. Insurance is for converting an unpredictable large loss into a predictable small one, and if a £300 January bill would mean a credit card at 24 per cent, then paying £128 a year to remove that risk is rational. Do not let anyone tell you the maths says otherwise. The maths only says otherwise if you have the cash.

Two: your boiler is over eight years old, out of warranty, and you intend to keep it. Failure rates rise sharply with age. Once a boiler is failing more than once every three years the expected loss climbs past the premium. Check first that an insurer will actually take it.

Three: you are a landlord. A gas safety check is a legal obligation, a tenant without heating is a repair you must complete quickly, and the cost of cover is a deductible expense against rental income. The calculation is different and cover often wins on speed alone.

Outside those three, the sinking fund wins. And if you take the sinking fund route, actually open a separate account and set up the standing order. A fund that exists only as an intention is not a fund.

5. Worked example: Desmond in Wrexham

Desmond has a six year old combi, still inside a ten year manufacturer warranty on the condition that it is serviced annually. He is quoted £21 a month for cover with a £60 excess, so £252 a year.

His local Gas Safe engineer charges £95 for a service including the record he needs for the warranty.

Insurance component: £252 minus £95, so £157 a year.

But his boiler is under warranty. Parts and labour on a manufacturing fault are already covered by the manufacturer for another four years. So what the £157 buys him is cover for the things the warranty excludes, which is largely wear items and system faults, and cover on a boiler statistically unlikely to fail at six.

He sets up £13 a month into a separate account instead. After the first year he has £156. After the second, £312, which is more than the top of the typical repair range. After four years, when the warranty ends and the boiler is ten, he has £624 and a real decision to make about cover, made from a position of strength rather than fear.

If the boiler had failed in year one with a £225 bill, he would have been £69 down against the cover, less the £60 excess he would have paid anyway, so about £9. That is the size of the bet.

6. The honest verdict

If you have £500 you could reach in a hurry, do not buy boiler cover. Buy the annual service on its own for £80 to £120, put the difference into a separate account by standing order, and you will be ahead within two years and considerably ahead within five.

If you could not, buy the cover and do not feel bad about it. That is what insurance is for. Read the excess, the exclusion period and the beyond economic repair clause before you sign, and understand that none of these plans will buy you a new boiler.

Whatever you decide, get the boiler serviced every year. That part is not optional and it is the part that actually reduces the number of breakdowns.

Frequently asked

Does home insurance already cover my boiler? Standard buildings insurance covers damage from an event, such as a leak causing water damage, not the boiler failing of old age. Some policies sell home emergency cover as an add-on, typically £40 to £70 a year, which is cheaper than standalone boiler cover but usually caps the call-out at a fixed amount and does not include a service.

Is a service the same as a gas safety check? No. A gas safety check is a safety inspection of every gas appliance and is what a landlord legally needs annually. A service is a more thorough clean and check of the boiler itself. Many engineers do both together, but confirm which you are paying for.

What if I have a heat pump rather than a boiler? Different market and different arithmetic. Heat pump service plans are typically £150 to £250 a year, but heat pumps come with long manufacturer warranties and have fewer moving parts, so the case for third-party cover in the early years is weaker still.

Do I lose the manufacturer warranty if I use an independent engineer? No, provided they are Gas Safe registered and complete the service record in the benchmark logbook. Manufacturers require an annual service by a competent person, not by their own staff. Keep every receipt.
