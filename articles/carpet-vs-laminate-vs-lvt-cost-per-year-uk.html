Every flooring shop quotes a price per square metre. Nobody quotes the number that decides the purchase, which is what a square metre costs you for each year you keep it. Work that out and the ranking changes completely.

1. The sum, and why the sticker price misleads

A floor is not a purchase, it is a rental you pay up front. The only sensible way to compare two floors is:

Cost per square metre fitted, divided by the number of years it will actually last in that room, equals cost per square metre per year.

That sounds obvious and almost nobody does it, because shops quote the first number and never the second. The result is that people buy the cheapest carpet in the shop, replace it in six years, buy another one, and spend more over twenty years than the person who bought LVT once.

Two things make the sum honest. First, the fitted price has to include everything: the material, the underlay, the gripper or the levelling compound, the fitting, and taking the old floor away. A £12 a square metre carpet is not a £12 floor. Second, the lifespan has to be the realistic one for the room, not the manufacturer's warranty. A hallway kills a floor perhaps twice as fast as a spare bedroom.

Everything below uses UK trade price ranges for 2026 and lifespans in the middle of the published ranges. Your quotes will differ. The method will not.

Paying about £7.50 a square metre more for a 0.55mm wear layer instead of a 0.3mm takes the cost per year from £3.75 down to £2.63, a 30 per cent improvement, because the thicker layer roughly doubles the life. That upgrade is the single best value decision in the table and it is the one people skip.

3. The right answer changes room by room

The table above assumes a busy room. Lifespans move a lot with traffic and water, and so the right floor differs by room. This is where a whole-house quote in one material usually costs you money.

Hall, stairs and landing. The hardest wear in the house and the place cheap carpet dies fastest. Either a heavy-duty carpet you accept replacing, or LVT with a good doormat run. Never budget laminate on stairs: the nosings chip and the edges lift.

Kitchen, bathroom and utility. Water decides this. LVT or sheet vinyl only. Laminate is a wood fibre board with a picture on it, and a dishwasher leak turns it into a swollen ruin in a day. This is the one room where the extra cost of LVT is not a preference, it is the correct answer.

Living room. Genuine choice. Carpet is warmer underfoot and quieter, hard flooring is easier to keep clean and lasts longer. If you go hard, budget for rugs, which is a real cost and also a real benefit on a cold floor.

Bedrooms. Lowest traffic in the house, so lifespans stretch. A mid-range carpet that would last ten years in a living room will do fifteen in a bedroom, which drops it to about £2.33 a square metre a year and makes it competitive. This is the room where carpet wins on the arithmetic as well as on comfort.

A cold ground floor either way. If the floor is cold, a floor covering alone will not fix it. Our page on floor insulation payback deals with what is happening underneath, and doing that at the same time as replacing the floor costs a fraction of what it costs later.

4. Worked example: Nkechi in Loughborough

Nkechi is doing a three-bedroom semi: hall and stairs 14m², living room 20m², kitchen diner 18m², three bedrooms 34m². Total 86m².

The one-material quote. Budget carpet throughout at £22.50 fitted is £1,935. It looks like the cheap option and on the day it is. In the hall and kitchen it will be finished in about four years and stained long before that. Blended across the house call it a seven year life, so £276 a year.

The room-by-room quote.

Hall and stairs, 14m² of 0.55mm LVT at £52.50: £735, 20 year life, £36.75 a year.
Kitchen diner, 18m² of the same LVT: £945, 20 years, £47.25 a year.
Living room, 20m² of AC4 laminate at £26.50: £530, 15 years, £35.33 a year.
Three bedrooms, 34m² of mid-range twist pile at £35: £1,190, 15 years in low traffic, £79.33 a year.

Total up front: £3,400. Total per year: £198.66.

So the room-by-room plan costs £1,465 more today and £77 a year less to own. Over the twenty years she plans to stay, the one-material carpet costs about £5,520 in repeat purchases and the mixed plan costs about £3,973. She is £1,547 better off, and she never has a swollen kitchen floor.

She buys good doormats for £60. On the trade rule of thumb that a proper barrier mat run removes most of the grit that wears a floor, that is the cheapest life extension available anywhere in this article.

5. The honest verdict

Do not floor a whole house in one material to save money, because it does not. Put LVT with a 0.55mm wear layer where there is water or heavy traffic, laminate where there is neither, and carpet in bedrooms where low traffic lets it live long enough to be good value.

Never buy the cheapest carpet for a hall or stairs. At £3.75 a square metre a year it is the worst value floor in this table and it will look tired before the second Christmas.

If you buy LVT, pay the extra for the thicker wear layer. About £7.50 a square metre buys roughly double the life. Nothing else in flooring returns that.

Frequently asked

Is laminate really cheaper per year than carpet? On these figures yes, by a wide margin, because it lasts roughly two and a half times as long for a similar fitted price. What it does not give you is warmth or quiet, and it is unforgiving of water, so the arithmetic is not the whole decision.

Can I fit laminate or LVT myself? Click laminate over an existing sound floor is a realistic weekend job and saves the fitting cost, which is a meaningful share of the total. Glue-down LVT is not: it needs a properly levelled subfloor and mistakes are permanent. Price the levelling compound before deciding.

Does underlay really matter that much? Yes, more than the carpet grade in many cases. Underlay absorbs the compression that flattens pile, so a good one keeps a mid-range carpet looking acceptable for years longer. Fitting an expensive carpet on cheap foam is the classic false economy.

What about the floor being cold underfoot? Hard floors feel colder because they conduct heat away from your feet faster, even at the same air temperature. That is a comfort issue rather than a heat loss issue, and a rug fixes it. Actual heat loss through the floor is a separate problem with a separate answer.
