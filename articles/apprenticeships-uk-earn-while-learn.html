Apprenticeships in the UK: Earn While You Learn a New Trade

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

An apprenticeship lets you earn a wage while you train for a real job and gain a recognised qualification, with no student debt. It is one of the best routes into a new career in the UK, and not just for school leavers. Here is how they work and who they suit.

What an apprenticeship actually is

You are a paid employee doing a real job, and part of your week is spent training toward a qualification. Your employer and the government cover the training cost, so you learn a trade or profession without paying tuition and without falling into debt. You come out with experience, a qualification, and often a permanent job.

They are not just for teenagers

This is the biggest misunderstanding. Apprenticeships are open to adults too, and are a genuine route for changing careers later in life, as covered in our career switching guide. If you want to move into a new field without going back to full time study, an apprenticeship can pay you while you retrain.

What you get paid

Apprentices earn a wage, with a legal minimum apprentice rate that rises as you progress and depending on your age and year. It is lower than a full salary while you train, but you are being paid to learn rather than paying to study, which is the whole point. Many apprentices move onto full pay once qualified.

The levels, from beginner to degree

  • Intermediate and advanced, good starting points into a trade or profession.
  • Higher apprenticeships, equivalent to college or foundation level study.
  • Degree apprenticeships, where you earn a full degree while working and being paid, with no tuition debt.

That last one is remarkable: a degree, real work experience, and a wage, instead of years of tuition fees.

How to find and apply

  1. Use the official government apprenticeship service to search vacancies by field and area.
  2. Treat the application like any job: a strong CV and a tailored cover letter.
  3. Prepare for the interview as you would any role, using our interview guide.
  4. Be clear about why you want that trade, since employers value commitment.

The honest bottom line

An apprenticeship is one of the smartest routes into a new career in the UK: you earn while you learn, gain a real qualification, and avoid debt, at any age. If you want to change direction without the cost of full time study, it is well worth a serious look.
