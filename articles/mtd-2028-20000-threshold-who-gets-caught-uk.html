The Making Tax Digital threshold drops to £20,000, and the year being measured is this one

April 2028 sounds comfortably far off. It is not, because the income HMRC will test against the £20,000 threshold is the income in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is running right now.

The short version

  • From 6 April 2028, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £20,000 must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.
  • The qualifying income is measured in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is the one running now.
  • Qualifying income is turnover, not profit, and income from multiple sources is added together.
  • This is the step that catches side hustles. £20,000 of turnover is a modest online shop or a single let property.
  • The step before it is April 2027 at £30,000, affecting around 970,000 people.

1. The rule, and the year that is actually being measured

HMRC's published policy paper is titled "Reduction of the mandation threshold from £30,000 to £20,000 from April 2028", and the substance is exactly that.

From 6 April 2028, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £20,000 must keep digital records and report quarterly to HMRC through compatible software.

Here is the part that changes how urgent this is. The qualifying income for that test is measured in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. That tax year began on 6 April 2026 and it is running as you read this.

So the income deciding whether you are mandated in 2028 is the income you are earning today. There is no future year to adjust in. The measurement is already under way.

2. Turnover, not profit, and why that catches people

Qualifying income is gross income from self employment and property, before deducting expenses. It is not taxable profit.

This trips up more people than any other part of the rule, and it does so in a specific way. Someone running a small business with high costs and thin margins can have qualifying income comfortably over £20,000 while their actual profit is a few thousand pounds. The threshold does not care about the margin.

The other half of the trap is aggregation. If you have more than one source of self employment or property income, they are added together for the test. Two small streams that each look harmless can combine into a mandated total.

3. Who gets caught at £20,000 that did not at £30,000

The April 2027 step at £30,000 brings in around 970,000 people, on top of roughly 780,000 with income over £50,000 who came in from April 2026. Those are HMRC's own published estimates.

The 2028 step down to £20,000 reaches a different population. £20,000 of turnover is not a substantial business. In practice it describes things like:

  • a single let property at around £1,700 a month in rent
  • a part time online shop turning over a few hundred pounds a week
  • a trade done at weekends alongside a full time PAYE job
  • a freelance sideline that has grown quietly over a couple of years

Many people in that group do not think of themselves as running a business at all. They file a tax return once a year and otherwise do not think about HMRC. Quarterly reporting is a different rhythm entirely.

4. A worked example: a side hustle that crosses the line

Ewan works full time for a logistics firm in Dundee and is taxed through PAYE. Alongside that he sells refurbished bikes, and he rents out a room arrangement that qualifies as property income rather than falling under rent a room relief.

His figures for the 2026 to 2027 tax year are shaping up like this.

  • Bike sales, gross takings: £14,800
  • Property income, gross: £7,300

Qualifying income: £14,800 plus £7,300, which is £22,100.

That is over £20,000, so on these figures Ewan is heading for mandation from 6 April 2028.

His salary does not count towards this test, even though it is much larger than either figure and appears on the same tax return. The test looks only at self employment and property income.

His profit on the bikes, after parts, listing fees and postage, is a fraction of £14,800. That does not matter either. The test is on the £22,100.

What Ewan should notice is that he is only £2,100 over the line. If his bike sales are slightly lower than expected this year, he may fall under it. That is a real possibility rather than wishful thinking, and it is a reason to work out his actual figure rather than assume either way.

Two details that change the answer for some people. If a property is jointly owned, it is your share of the gross rental income that counts towards your own threshold, not the whole property's rent. And HMRC's guidance sets out exemptions and exclusions, including for people it accepts are digitally excluded, so if you genuinely cannot use digital tools, that is a recognised category rather than something you have to solve alone.

5. What is genuinely worth doing now, and what is not

Worth doing now: add up your gross self employment and property income for the tax year to date and project it to 5 April 2027. That single number tells you whether 2028 applies to you. It takes ten minutes with your bank statements and it is the only thing that actually resolves the question.

Worth doing if you are over the line: start keeping records digitally at the next natural break, such as the start of a tax year. HMRC publishes a list of compatible software, and there are free and low cost options on it. Building the habit while nothing is at stake is much easier than building it against a deadline.

Not worth doing: buying software now for a 2028 obligation. The market will change in two years, prices will change, and HMRC's compatible list will change with it.

Also not worth doing: panicking. Quarterly updates are summaries submitted from software, not four tax bills. Your payment dates of 31 January and 31 July do not change, your tax rates do not change, and what counts as an allowable expense does not change.

And if your gross self employment and property income is nowhere near £20,000, this rule does not apply to you and there is nothing here you need to act on. That is a genuine answer, and it covers a lot of people who have been made anxious by coverage that does not mention the threshold at all.

Frequently asked

Which tax year decides whether I am caught in 2028? The 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is running now. Qualifying income in that year is what the £20,000 threshold is tested against.

Does my salary count towards the £20,000? No. The test looks at gross income from self employment and property. Employment income, pensions, dividends and savings interest do not count towards it.

Is £20,000 profit or turnover? Turnover, or more precisely qualifying income before expenses. A business with £22,000 of takings and £18,000 of costs is over the threshold.

Will I have to pay tax four times a year? No. Quarterly updates are information submissions. The payment deadlines of 31 January and 31 July are unchanged.
