Amazon Flex: £13 an hour, and what is left after the car takes its cut

Amazon advertises £12 to £15 an hour for Flex blocks in the UK. That number is real. It is also a gross figure paid to a self-employed contractor who supplies the vehicle, the fuel, the insurance and the tyres. Here is the whole sum.

Ruhul Amin Published 6 September 2026 · 9 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Amazon's own site advertises £12 to £15 an hour for Prime Now blocks and £13 an hour for standard deliveries (flex.amazon.co.uk, read 6 September 2026).
  • Blocks run 1 to 4 hours and future blocks are "allocated randomly", in Amazon's words, based on expected volume and how many drivers are available. You cannot plan a week's income.
  • HMRC's flat mileage rate rose to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, up from 45p before 6 April 2026 (GOV.UK, page updated 2 September 2026). That is HMRC's own view of what running a car costs.
  • At 45 miles a block, a £13 hour becomes about £6.81 before tax and about £5.45 after basic rate tax. The National Living Wage is £12.71.
  • Your ordinary car insurance almost certainly does not cover this. Delivering parcels for payment needs hire and reward cover. Check before your first block, not after.

1. What Amazon actually advertises

Straight from flex.amazon.co.uk, read on 6 September 2026:

  • "Earn 12 to 15 per hour for Prime Now and 13 per hour for deliveries." Two different rates for two different kinds of work.
  • Blocks run from 1 to 4 hours. Amazon explains why: 1 and 2 hour blocks are for Prime Now, where the addresses sit close to the delivery station. Same Day and One Day parcels cover a wider area, so those blocks tend to be 4 hours.
  • "Future blocks are allocated randomly, based on expected volume and availability of Delivery Partners." You can grab a same-day block if one is free, or set your availability, but you are not scheduled.
  • You are self-employed. Amazon's own FAQ: "As a self-employed contractor you need to file a self-assessment tax return with HMRC each year and to pay tax and national insurance contributions on your profit after expenses."
  • You need a 4-door car, a van, or a scooter with a trunk, plus a UK driving licence, an Android or iOS phone, the right to work in the UK, and a background and criminal record check. No bicycles, no 2-door cars, no deliveries on foot.
  • It runs in 17 named places: Birmingham, Bristol, Croydon, Dunstable, Hayes, Hemel Hempstead, Liverpool, Leeds, London (scooters with trunks only), Manchester, Newcastle, Northampton, Nottingham, Portsmouth, Sheffield, Edinburgh and Glasgow.

Amazon is also unusually blunt about what this is not. Asked whether Flex is full-time work, the site answers "No", describes it as "supplementary income", and says the available blocks "may fluctuate week to week and are not guaranteed".

Credit where it is due: that is a more honest set of statements than most gig platforms put on a landing page. The problem is not what Amazon says. It is what the number leaves out.

2. The three things the advertised rate does not include

The car. You supply it, fuel it, insure it, service it, tyre it and absorb its depreciation. None of that comes off Amazon's figure, because Amazon is not your employer. This is the single largest deduction and it is the one people underestimate, because fuel is visible at the pump and tyres are not.

HMRC publishes a flat rate for exactly this purpose. Its simplified expenses page, updated 2 September 2026, lets a self-employed person claim a flat amount per business mile "instead of the actual costs of buying and running your vehicle, for example insurance, repairs, servicing, fuel". For the 2026 to 2027 tax year that rate is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles, then 25p. It was 45p before 6 April 2026, so it has gone up by 10p a mile this tax year and a lot of drivers have not noticed.

Two uses for that 55p. It is what you deduct on your tax return, which is genuinely valuable. And it is HMRC's own working estimate of what a mile in a car costs, which makes it a reasonable yardstick when you are asking yourself whether the block was worth doing.

The overrun. Amazon says earnings estimates are "based on delivering a number of parcels across an estimated length of time" and adds that "the actual time taken per delivery may vary ... so the earnings per hour may vary." Read that carefully. You are paid for the block, not for the hours. Finish a 4-hour block in 3 and you have earned £13 an hour on paper and more than that in practice. Take 5 hours and you have earned £52 for 5 hours, which is £10.40.

The unpaid bits. Driving to the delivery station, waiting for your parcels to be loaded, and driving home afterwards. Those miles are business miles for tax, which helps, but they are not paid time.

3. Ravi in Nottingham: £13 an hour, worked all the way down

Ravi has a full-time job and picks up two 4-hour Same Day blocks a week, 48 weeks a year, in Nottingham. Nottingham is one of the 17 places on Amazon's list.

Gross: 4 hours at £13 is £52 a block. Two blocks a week is £104. Over 48 weeks that is £4,992 for 384 hours of work.

Mileage: assume 45 miles a block, including the run to the station and home again. That is our assumption, not Amazon's figure, and yours will differ. Ninety-six blocks at 45 miles is 4,320 business miles, comfortably inside the 10,000 mile band, so all of it is claimable at 55p. That is £2,376.

Profit: £4,992 minus £2,376 is £2,616. Across 384 hours that is £6.81 an hour.

After tax: Ravi's Personal Allowance is already used up by his day job, so the whole £2,616 is taxed at 20%, which is £523.20. He keeps £2,092.80, or £5.45 an hour. His self-employed profit is well under £12,570 so there is no Class 4 National Insurance to pay on it.

Mileage is the variable that decides everything, so here is the same year at three different distances.

For scale, the National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour. Every row above is below it, and legally that is fine, because a self-employed contractor is not entitled to the minimum wage.

One honest counterweight, because this cuts both ways. HMRC's 55p bundles depreciation and servicing into a single average. If you are running an old, paid-off, economical car that you were keeping anyway, your real cash outlay per mile may be well under 55p, and you get to claim 55p regardless. In that case your actual pocket money is better than the table shows, and the tax deduction is doing real work for you. If you are running a newer car on finance, 55p may be flattering it.

4. Insurance, tax and the things that are legally yours

Insurance is the one that can ruin you. A standard social, domestic, pleasure and commuting policy does not cover carrying goods for payment. That is a separate class of use, usually called hire and reward or courier cover. Driving a Flex block on an ordinary policy is very likely driving uninsured, which means points, a fine, and no cover at all if you hit someone. Ring your insurer and get the answer in writing before your first block, not after your first claim.

You must register for Self Assessment. Amazon says so on its own FAQ. If crossing £1,000 of gross self-employed income was new for you in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, the deadline to tell HMRC is 5 October 2026.

Keep a mileage log from day one. Date, start point, end point, miles. Without it you cannot substantiate the 55p claim, and at 4,320 miles that claim is worth £2,376 of deduction, which is £475 of tax at basic rate. A note in your phone at the end of each block is enough. Reconstructing a year of it in January is not.

You get none of the employment protections. No holiday pay, no sick pay, no pension contribution, no notice period, no redundancy, no minimum wage floor, and no guaranteed hours. Amazon states plainly that blocks are not guaranteed.

You cannot choose the work. Amazon's FAQ confirms delivery partners cannot select specific delivery locations or choose what is in the parcels. You take the block you are given.

5. Who this actually suits

Flex works for a fairly narrow set of people, and it is worth being precise about who they are rather than pretending it works for everyone.

It suits you if you already own a car that is paid for and that you would be running anyway, you live close to a delivery station so your unpaid mileage is short, you want money in genuinely spare hours rather than instead of a job, and you are comfortable with a week where no blocks appear at all.

It does not suit you if you would need to buy or finance a car for it, if the nearest station is a long drive, if you need a predictable income to budget against, or if you are looking for something that will build into full-time work. Amazon itself says it is not that.

The verdict

The advertised £13 an hour is a true number and a misleading one at the same time. It is what Amazon pays. It is not what you earn, because you are running a vehicle out of it. Using HMRC's own 55p a mile for 2026 to 2027, a driver doing 45 miles a block is on roughly £6.81 an hour before tax and £5.45 after, against a National Living Wage of £12.71. If your car is old, cheap and already yours, and the station is close, the real figure lands nearer the top of our table and Flex can be a sensible way to convert dead hours into cash. If any of those things are not true, do the mileage sum for your own postcode before you sign up, and sort the hire and reward insurance first either way.

Frequently asked

Is Amazon Flex worth it if I already have the car? That is exactly the case where it can work, because the marginal cost of a mile in a car you already own and insure is lower than the 55p flat rate. Run our table with your own mileage per block. The closer you live to a delivery station, the better it looks, because unpaid mileage is the difference between a good block and a bad one.

Do I get paid more if the block takes longer? No. You are paid for the block, not for the hours. Amazon says as much: earnings estimates are based on a number of parcels across an estimated block length, and "the actual time taken per delivery may vary ... so the earnings per hour may vary." Overrunning reduces your effective rate.

Can I do Amazon Flex on a normal car insurance policy? Almost certainly not. Carrying goods for payment is a separate class of use, usually described as hire and reward or courier cover. Confirm with your own insurer in writing before you take a block. Getting this wrong is not a technicality, it can mean driving with no valid insurance at all.

Does Flex income affect Universal Credit? Self-employed earnings are reported monthly for Universal Credit and reduce the award through the taper, so yes. The figure you report is profit after allowable expenses, which is where the mileage claim matters. Report it monthly rather than annually, because Universal Credit does not work on tax years.
