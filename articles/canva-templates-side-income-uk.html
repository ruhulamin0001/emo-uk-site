The Canva Clause That Decides Whether You Have A Business Or A Breach

Nearly everyone selling Canva templates believes a paid Canva subscription buys the right to sell what they make. The Content Licence Agreement says close to the opposite: Free Content can go into templates you sell to third parties, and Pro Content explicitly cannot. Here is what the agreement actually permits, in its own words, and where the line falls.

The short version

  • Pro Content is licensed for one design. Canva calls it the One Design Use Licence. You may not copy, download or distribute Pro Content as a standalone item, and each new design needs a new licence, including a Magic Resized one.
  • Only Free Content may go into templates you sell. The agreement permits Free Content in templates for distribution or sale to third parties, then says: if your design contains any Pro Content, you cannot use it for these purposes.
  • Selling a template for use on Canva is permitted. The permitted uses list includes design templates solely for use on Canva. Selling the shared link is the safe route.
  • Branded, Education and Disney content are personal use only. Branded Content is not for any commercial or promotional purpose. Education Content, badged Resource, is non-commercial.
  • Mixing categories applies the strictest one to the whole design. Most to least restrictive: Education, Branded, Pro, Free.
  • A Canva Education account cannot be used commercially at all. Canva says you need a separate account for commercial designs.

1. Why Pro Content is the restricted one

The assumption behind most Canva template businesses is that the subscription is what unlocks commercial use. Read section 3 of the Content Licence Agreement and that assumption falls apart.

Canva grants Pro Content under what it names the One Design Use Licence. The wording is not ambiguous: you may use Pro Content or a Pro Template in a single Canva Design, and you may not copy, download or distribute the Pro Content as a standalone item.

Section 4 sharpens it further. A licence is issued when you export a design, for each piece of content in it. Each Pro Content Licence allows use in one design, so you must obtain a new licence each time you use the same piece of content in a new design, including a design that has been Magic Resized. If you have a subscription, that new licence is issued automatically on each export at no extra cost. The point is not that it costs money. The point is that the licence attaches to one design, not to you.

Now put that next to what a template buyer does. They take your file and make their own designs with it, over and over. Every one of those is a new design containing your Pro Content, and the licence for it was never issued to them. That is the structural problem, and no amount of careful wording in your product description fixes it.

Section 9 closes the remaining doors. You cannot sub-license, re-sell, rent, lend, assign, gift or otherwise transfer or distribute the content. You cannot use content in a manner that competes with Canva's business, including offering content for sale. And you cannot incorporate content in any product that results in a re-distribution or re-use of the content, or is otherwise made available in a manner such that a person can extract or access or reproduce the content as an electronic file. That last clause is the one that kills selling a Canva design as a downloadable pack of images or a flattened PDF with Pro elements inside it.

2. What Free Content actually lets you do

Here is the part that reverses the usual assumption. Section 6 is headed additional permitted uses for Free Content only, and it grants exactly what template sellers need.

With Free Content you may use it in more than one design without an additional licence, download it on a standalone basis, and, in Canva's words, use the Free Content in templates for websites, social networking websites, documents, projects or otherwise for distribution and/or sale to third parties. It goes on to permit use in design template applications intended for resale, naming website templates, business card templates, greeting card templates and brochure design templates.

Then the line that settles the argument, in the same section: if your Canva Design contains any Pro Content, you cannot use it for these purposes.

So the licence does not care what you pay. It cares which library the elements came from. A free account user building only with Free Content has broader resale rights over what they make than a Pro subscriber who dropped one Pro icon into the corner.

The practical method follows from that. Build the template entirely from Free Content, and check each element rather than assuming. You can see the source by hovering over an item in the Elements panel and clicking the three dots, which Canva calls the content source information. It will also tell you if an element is CC0 or public domain, in which case the relevant CC0 licence applies instead and is usually more generous still.

One warning that survives all of this: if a design mixes categories, the most restrictive category applies to the whole design. Canva lists them from most to least restrictive as Education Content, Branded Content, Pro Content, Free Content. Ninety nine Free elements and one Branded one makes the whole thing Branded.

3. Selling templates for use on Canva, which is the safe route

There is a route that works regardless of the above, and it is hiding in section 5's list of permitted uses for all content. Item five on that list is design templates solely for use on Canva.

That is the model most successful Canva template sellers use without necessarily knowing why it is the safe one. You do not sell a file. You sell a share link that opens a copy of your template inside the buyer's own Canva account. The elements stay inside Canva's ecosystem, the buyer's own account issues their own licences on export, and nothing is distributed as a standalone file that someone can extract elements from.

It also solves a delivery problem you would otherwise have. A buyer who receives an editable Canva link can actually edit it. A buyer who receives a flattened PDF cannot, which is usually not what they paid for.

There is one separate route for client work that is often confused with selling templates. Section 4A lets you transfer a design containing content to a client for the client's own use, on three conditions: a written agreement with the client requiring them to comply with these terms, you remaining solely responsible and liable for their compliance, and the design going to a single client only. Canva then spells out that this does not let you transfer or sub-license content to your client outside a design or on a standalone basis. That is a route for doing design work for one customer. It is not a template business, and the phrase about remaining liable for your client's compliance deserves a slow read.

4. Four traps that are not in any tutorial

Branded Content. Canva provides some elements in collaboration with third party brands, labelled Branded Content in the source information. Unless stated otherwise it is for personal use only and may not be used for any commercial or promotional purpose. You cannot use it on merchandise for sale, or in any way implying endorsement by the brand.

Education Content. Badged Resource, and available in both the Free and Pro libraries. It is for educational, non-commercial use only. It sits in the Free library, which is precisely why it catches people who have correctly learned to filter for Free.

Education accounts. If you are a teacher using Canva Education, section 8 says you may only use Pro Content for educational, non-commercial purposes, and that you need to create a separate Canva account for commercial designs. Selling templates from a free-for-teachers account is a breach even when everything else is right.

Print on demand. Section 9 prohibits using or displaying content on a standalone basis on websites designed to induce or involve the sale of on demand products, listing postcards, mugs, t-shirts and posters. If your plan is Canva design to print on demand marketplace, read that clause in full before you build a catalogue.

Two smaller ones. Fonts have their own restrictions in section 9A, including that you may not use the font software other than on Canva or as part of an exported Canva design, so an exported font file is out. And section 10 says Canva monitors downloads and exports and may suspend or terminate an account for breach, in which case you lose the rights to the content and the designs and forfeit all fees paid.

None of this is a reason not to sell Canva templates. It is a reason to spend twenty minutes in the source information panel before you spend twenty hours designing.

5. Worked example: Marcus in Swansea, two ways to sell the same pack

Marcus is a freelance marketer in Swansea. He builds a pack of twelve Instagram templates for small hospitality businesses and wants to sell it at £24.

Version one, the way he first built it. Canva Pro account, a mix of whatever looked good: eight Pro photographs, several Pro graphic elements, one element that turned out to be Branded Content. Delivery as a downloadable zip of editable files plus flattened PNG previews.

This version fails on four separate clauses. The Pro Content is licensed for one design each and may not be distributed standalone. The zip lets a buyer extract elements as electronic files, which section 9 prohibits directly. The Branded element makes the entire pack personal use only under the mixing rule. And the whole product is arguably content offered for sale in competition with Canva's own business.

Version two, rebuilt. Every element checked in the source information panel and swapped for Free Content or his own photographs, which he took himself at two client venues and therefore owns outright. The Branded element replaced. Delivery as a single Canva share link that copies twelve templates into the buyer's account.

Version two sits inside section 5's permitted use for design templates solely for use on Canva, and also inside section 6's Free Content resale permission. It took him about four hours to rebuild, mostly swapping photographs.

The money, so the effort has a number attached. At £24 on Gumroad's direct pricing of 10% plus 50 cents, he nets roughly £21.10 per sale. His four hours of rebuilding is recovered on sale five. Gross income crosses the £1,000 trading allowance at 42 sales, at which point he must tell HMRC. He gets reported to HMRC earlier than that, at 30 sales, which is £720 of income and no tax to pay. Those two numbers are explained in our guide to selling Notion templates in the UK, and they work the same way here.

What version two really bought him is not four hours of tidiness. It is that his product does not depend on Canva never looking at it.

The honest verdict

If you sell Canva templates, the single most valuable thing you can do this week is open the Content Licence Agreement and read sections 3, 5, 6 and 9. It takes fifteen minutes and it will probably contradict something you have been told confidently.

The headline is that the free library is the permissive one. Free Content may go into templates for distribution and sale to third parties. Pro Content is licensed for one design at a time, may not be distributed standalone, and its presence in a design removes those resale permissions entirely. Paying more buys you better elements, not broader rights.

The safest model is to sell a share link for use on Canva, which the permitted uses list covers directly, built from Free Content or your own work. Check every element in the source information panel, because Branded and Education content hide inside the free library and the strictest category governs the whole design.

This is a real side income and the arithmetic works: at £24 a template you cross the £1,000 trading allowance at 42 sales. It is just worth being one of the sellers whose product survives being looked at.

Frequently asked

Does a Canva Pro subscription give me commercial rights? It gives you Pro Content Licences at no extra cost each time you export a design, which is commercial use of your own designs. It does not give you the right to resell or redistribute Canva's content, and section 6's resale permissions apply to Free Content only, explicitly excluding designs that contain any Pro Content.

Can I sell a Canva template as a PDF or an image pack? That is the riskiest format. Section 9 prohibits incorporating content in any product that results in redistribution or re-use, or is made available so that a person can extract, access or reproduce the content as an electronic file. Selling a share link for use on Canva avoids that problem entirely.

How do I check whether an element is Free or Pro? Hover over the item in the Elements panel and click the three dots in the top right corner for the content source information. That panel also tells you if something is CC0, public domain, Branded Content or Education Content, which is the check that matters most.

What happens if I get it wrong? Canva says it monitors downloads and exports and may suspend or terminate an account without notice for a breach, and that on termination you lose all rights to the content and designs and forfeit fees paid. There is also the ordinary risk of a complaint from a rights holder. This article is general information, not legal advice; if you are building a real business on this, get the licence reviewed properly.
