Nobody Told Oil Heated Homes They Get an Extra £1,500

Everyone knows the heat pump grant is £7,500. Far fewer people know that if your home runs on oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection, there is another £1,500 on top until March 2027. At £9,000 off, a heat pump and a replacement oil boiler can end up costing roughly the same to install, which turns the whole decision into a question about running cost instead.

The short version

  • The grant is £9,000, not £7,500, for oil and LPG homes off the gas grid. GOV.UK states an extra £1,500 on top of the £7,500 air source or ground source grant, available until March 2027, if the property is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection.
  • That closes most of the installed price gap. A replacement oil boiler is commonly £4,000 to £5,000 fitted. An air source heat pump at £13,000 becomes £4,000 after £9,000 of grant.
  • The break even kerosene price is about 80p a litre. Below that, oil is cheaper to run. Above it, the heat pump is. That single number replaces every vague claim on the subject.
  • The heat pump makes heat at about 8.8p per kWh at the October 2026 electricity cap of 26.32p and a realistic seasonal efficiency of 3.0.
  • There is no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, which flatters the heat pump side of this sum for exactly six months. Say so out loud when you do your own numbers.
  • Do not do this with your existing radiators unchecked. The single most common reason a heat pump disappoints is radiators sized for 70C water being asked to work at 45C.

1. The £1,500 almost nobody mentions

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is the government grant for replacing a fossil fuel heating system in England and Wales. The headline numbers, taken from GOV.UK on 13 September 2026, are:

  • £7,500 towards an air source heat pump
  • £7,500 towards a ground source heat pump, including water source and shared ground loops
  • £5,000 towards a biomass boiler
  • £2,500 towards an air to air heat pump

And then the line that changes the sum for a few hundred thousand rural households:

Until March 2027, you can get an additional £1,500 towards an air source or ground source heat pump if both of these are true:

your property is heated by oil or LPG, and you do not have a mains gas connection at the property.

Source: GOV.UK, Apply for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, "What you can get", read 13 September 2026.

Both conditions have to hold. An oil heated house that happens to have a capped gas main at the boundary does not qualify for the uplift. A house with no gas connection that runs on electric storage heaters does not qualify either, because it is not oil or LPG heated. It is specifically aimed at the homes for which oil is the only realistic incumbent.

One grant per property, and you cannot use it to replace a heating system that is already low carbon. So if you have already put a heat pump in, this is not a route to a newer one.

3. Running cost, and the break even oil price

This is where most comparisons cheat, by quoting an oil price from whenever the article was written and presenting it as a fact. Heating oil is not regulated and not capped. Its price moves with crude oil, the exchange rate and the season, and it is usually at its worst in the weeks when everyone orders. Any single number we printed here would be wrong within a month.

So instead of quoting an oil price, here is the number that does not go stale: the oil price at which the two options cost the same to run.

Step one, the heat pump side. Electricity under the Ofgem cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026 is 26.32p per kWh on direct debit, England, Scotland and Wales average. A well installed air source heat pump in a normal British winter achieves a seasonal coefficient of performance of around 3.0, meaning three units of heat per unit of electricity. Some do better, poorly commissioned ones do worse, and 3.0 is a deliberately unheroic assumption.

Cost of one kWh of heat from a heat pump. 26.32p ÷ 3.0 = 8.77p.

Step two, the oil side. A litre of kerosene contains about 10.35 kWh of energy. A modern condensing oil boiler running properly converts about 88% of that into useful heat, so about 9.11 kWh of heat reaches your radiators per litre.

Cost of one kWh of heat from oil. price per litre ÷ 9.11.

Set the two equal. 8.77p × 9.11 = 79.9p per litre.

Below about 80p a litre, kerosene is cheaper to run. Above it, the heat pump is. Go and look at what you last paid.

That is a number you can check against your own delivery note in ten seconds, which is the point. It does not depend on us guessing an oil price, and it will stay usable until the electricity cap moves.

Three honest qualifications on it. First, it uses an SCOP of 3.0; if your installer is promising 3.5 the break even drops to about 68p a litre, and if the system ends up at 2.5 it rises to about 96p, so the assumption matters more than anything else in the calculation. Second, the electricity rate carries no VAT between 1 October 2026 and 31 March 2027, so if VAT returns at 5% on 1 April 2027 the break even oil price rises by roughly 5% too. Third, an old non-condensing oil boiler is nowhere near 88% efficient, and if you are comparing against one of those rather than against a new one, the heat pump wins far more easily.

4. What else separates them, beyond money

Running cost is not the only thing that decides this, and some of the non-money differences are worth more than the money.

Oil is a thing you have to buy in advance, in bulk. A 1,000 litre order is a several hundred pound cash outlay in one go, usually in the coldest month. Electricity is billed monthly in arrears. For a household on a tight budget that timing difference can matter more than the unit rate.

The tank is a liability. Oil tanks have a life, they need bunding in many locations, and a leak is an environmental incident that can be genuinely expensive. A heat pump has no fuel storage at all and frees up the space the tank was standing on.

A heat pump needs your house to cooperate. It makes heat at a lower temperature, so it needs bigger radiators or underfloor heating, and it works far better in a house that holds heat. If your loft is thin or your walls are uninsulated, deal with that first. Our cavity wall insulation payback guide and floor insulation guide both cost a fraction of the heating system and improve whichever one you choose.

Oil boilers are simple and every rural plumber knows them. Heat pump servicing and fault finding is still a thinner market in remote areas. That is improving, but it is a real consideration if you are twenty miles from anywhere.

5. Worked example: the Pritchard farmhouse near Brecon

A stone farmhouse outside Brecon, no mains gas, oil fired condensing boiler from 2011 that is starting to need parts. They use roughly 2,400 litres of kerosene a year.

Heat delivered per year. 2,400 litres × 9.11 kWh = about 21,900 kWh of heat.

What that heat costs from a heat pump. 21,900 ÷ 3.0 = 7,300 kWh of electricity, × 26.32p = £1,921 a year.

What the same heat costs from oil, at the 80p break even price: 2,400 × 80p = £1,920. Identical, as it should be, because that is what break even means.

If they are paying 90p a litre: oil costs £2,160, so the heat pump saves them £239 a year.

If they are paying 70p a litre: oil costs £1,680, so the heat pump costs them £241 a year more.

Now the capital side. A new oil boiler quoted at £4,600. Heat pump quoted at £13,400 including two larger radiators, less £9,000 of grant, leaves £4,400. The heat pump is £200 cheaper to install than the boiler.

So for the Pritchards the decision does not hinge on the capital at all, which is a sentence that would have been absurd three years ago. It hinges entirely on what they expect to pay for kerosene over the next fifteen years, plus how much they value never ordering oil again. At 90p a litre the heat pump wins on both counts. At 70p it wins on capital and loses on running cost, and they would be choosing convenience over about £240 a year.

Frequently asked

Is the £1,500 uplift automatic? It is applied through the same application, which your installer makes on your behalf, but both conditions have to be met and evidenced: oil or LPG heating, and no mains gas connection. Confirm with your installer that they have claimed it before you sign, because it is the part most likely to be left off a quote.

What happens after March 2027? GOV.UK gives March 2027 as the end date for the additional £1,500. The £7,500 base grant is not stated as ending then. If you are on the fence and you qualify for the uplift, the deadline is a genuine reason to move rather than a manufactured one.

Does this apply in Scotland or Northern Ireland? No. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is England and Wales. Scotland has its own support through Home Energy Scotland, including interest free loans, and the Northern Ireland Boiler Replacement Scheme is closed. Check your own nation's scheme before using any figure on this page.

Can I keep the oil boiler as a backup? Not and take the grant. GOV.UK is explicit that a hybrid system combining a fossil fuel boiler with a heat pump is not eligible.

The honest verdict

If your home is oil heated, off the gas grid, and your boiler is near the end of its life, get a heat pump quote before you get a boiler quote. With £9,000 available the installed costs are close enough that you are no longer paying a large premium to switch, and that was the entire reason most people did not.

Decide the running cost question with one number: look at your last oil delivery. Above about 80p a litre the heat pump is cheaper to run at current electricity prices. Below it, oil still wins on running cost and you are buying convenience.

Do not do it if your house is poorly insulated and you are not willing to fix that first, and do not do it on a quote that has not looked properly at whether your radiators can deliver at a lower flow temperature. Those two things, not the technology, are what turn a good heat pump into a disappointing one.
