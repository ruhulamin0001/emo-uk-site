Amazon Affiliate Commission Rates 2026: The UK Card, and the Countries That Pay More

22 August 2026 · 9 min read · Checked August 2026

The short version
  • The UK rate card tops out at 6% for fashion and luxury. Books, automotive, furniture, home, kitchen, tools and jewellery pay 5%. Everything else is 3%.
  • Amazon deliberately pays least where prices are highest. That is the single most useful thing to understand about the programme.
  • The rate is set by the category of the item the reader actually buys, not the item you linked to.
  • Several marketplaces pay two to four times the UK card for the same category. The Netherlands, Belgium and Ireland top out at 14%.
  • UK payment threshold is £25 by bank transfer, and money arrives roughly 60 days after the end of the month you earned it.

The UK rate card in full

Amazon Associates does not pay you a percentage of the price. It pays you a percentage that it has set, on purpose, lowest in the categories where prices are highest. Once you see that, most beginner advice about promoting expensive products falls apart. It is also why chasing costly products backfires, which we work through in why high ticket items are a trap on Amazon.

Here is the standard UK card. It is shared with Germany, France, Italy and Spain, which is why the Simplified OneLink tier treats them as one group.

Two things in the operating agreement decide which of those rates you actually get, and neither is on the rate card itself. The first is direct against indirect: a purchase counts as direct only when the item bought sits in the same product category as the product page your link pointed at. Send someone to a book page and they buy a saucepan, and that is an indirect qualifying purchase, which is why a link to a category page or the Amazon home page can never be direct. The second is the rule nobody quotes. If Amazon decides you are mainly promoting free Kindle ebooks, you earn no commission income at all for any month in which 10,000 or more free Kindle ebooks are ordered and downloaded through your links and at least 80 percent of all Kindle ebooks ordered through them are free. That is the whole paragraph, in plain terms: a free-ebook roundup site can hit both conditions and be paid nothing for the month. Amazon is not the only programme worth testing against your own traffic, and what the eBay Partner Network pays on a sale is built on a different model entirely.

Standard UK Commission Income rates, checked against Amazon's live fee schedule on 21 August 2026. Amazon can change this card without notice, so verify before you build a site on any single number.

Compare that with the US card, where the catch-all is 4%, books, kitchen and automotive pay 4.5%, and Luxury Beauty pays 10%. The UK card is simpler and, across most of the catalogue, weaker. That is worth knowing before you assume an American guide applies to you.

Two rules that decide what you are actually paid

Rule one: the item purchased sets the rate, not the item you linked. You can write a careful 3,000 word review of a £2,500 television, the reader clicks your link, and then buys a £30 HDMI cable and a week's groceries. You are paid at cable and grocery rates. Because the link is doing that much of the work, people ask whether to route it through a redirect or a tidier URL: what cloaking an affiliate link actually gains and costs. This cuts the other way too, which is the useful half: a reader who arrives through a kitchen link and fills a basket earns you commission on the whole qualifying cart.

Rule two: the click window is 24 hours, the cart window is 89 days. Cookie windows differ sharply between networks, so do not carry the 24-hour assumption anywhere else. A session ends when 24 hours pass, when the customer orders a non-digital product, or when they click through another Associate's link. But anything added to the basket inside that first 24 hours stays attributed to you for up to 89 days. Amazon's consumer-facing help page rounds this to "usually after 90 days"; the contract says 89, so plan around 89.

The strategic consequence is not subtle. The highest-yield format on Amazon is not the single-product review. It is the build guide: the garage gym, the first-flat kitchen, the camera kit, the car audio install. It also decides where your hours go, because a build guide that already ranks is usually worth more attention than a new one: refreshing affiliate content against writing new posts. Content that legitimately puts six items in one basket, and gives the reader a reason to add to basket today even if they buy in six weeks.

The international rates almost nobody uses

The UK card is not the best card. It is one of the weaker ones. If your audience is global, or if you can genuinely choose which market to write for, this table is worth real money. Stated plainly: a £250 handbag on Amazon.nl pays 14%. The same handbag on Amazon.co.uk pays 6%, and on Amazon.com it pays 4%. A kitchen appliance in Australia pays 10% against 5% here.

But read the catch-all column before you get excited. Canada and Australia have the worst catch-all rates in the whole programme at 2%. Anything not explicitly listed there earns you almost nothing. A high top band with a terrible catch-all is a trap if your content is broad.

Two practical constraints. First, every marketplace outside the Simplified group needs its own Associates account, its own tax and payment setup, and its own three-qualifying-sales threshold. Open accounts in five countries on day one and you will fail the threshold in all five. Second, OneLink calculates earnings at the destination country's rate card, so redirecting a UK visitor off a US link moves them onto the weaker UK rates, not the stronger US ones. Earning across marketplaces also means being paid in more than one currency, and what actually lands in a UK account when you are paid in dollars can quietly remove the advantage you chased.

Bounties: flat fees that beat most commissions

These are flat payments for a signup, with no purchase required, and almost every beginner ignores them. In the UK, a full-price Audible membership pays £8. An Amazon Music Unlimited trial pays £4.50.

Put that next to the rate card. A £8 bounty is what you would earn on a £160 kitchen sale at 5%, or a £267 camera sale at 3%. One Audible signup from a books, study or commuting article is worth more than most product sales you will make in your first year.

One caveat specific to the UK and Canada: registry bounties only start paying once you already have three qualifying purchases on the account. That is not true of the Audible and Music bounties.

Getting paid: thresholds and timing

  • Threshold: £25 by bank transfer, £50 by cheque. Take the transfer.
  • Timing: payment lands roughly 60 days after the end of the month you earned it. January's commission arrives in late March.
  • Commission accrues only after the order ships, not when it is placed.
  • Returns reverse your commission, deducted from future earnings, sometimes months later.
  • Subscribe and Save renewals earn nothing. Only the initial order pays.

If you also sell into the US, EU or other Simplified-tier markets, one store ID and one tax setup covers Canada, the UK, Germany, France, Italy and Spain, with consolidated earnings. Anything beyond that group is a separate account with its own threshold to clear.

The 2026 commission-cut reports, honestly

In May 2026, Adweek, eMarketer and Shopifreaks reported that Amazon had cut affiliate commissions for publishers by as much as 50%, removed milestone bonuses and withdrew SKU-level reporting data. Amazon's response was that the changes affect "a tiny fraction of associates". The Influencer programme pays on a different basis again, set out in the Amazon Influencer programme against Associates.

Here is what can be verified. The public rate card is unchanged. The evidence points to cuts in negotiated rates for large publishers, not the standard card a new site sits on. So if you are starting now, those reports do not describe your situation.

They do describe the direction of travel, though, and that is the strongest argument in this whole subject for never building a business that depends on Amazon alone. A programme that can rewrite your income overnight, with no notice and no negotiation, is a revenue stream you rent rather than own.

What I would actually do with this

  1. Pick a niche inside the 5% band. Kitchen, automotive, tools, home and furniture. Decent rate, real ticket sizes, and readers who buy in multiples.
  2. Avoid the categories the card punishes. Televisions, computers, games consoles and grocery are traffic bait. They look impressive and pay badly.
  3. Write build guides, not single-product reviews. The 89-day basket rule rewards content that fills a basket.
  4. Add one bounty angle. If your topic touches books, studying, commuting or driving, an Audible article at £8 a signup is the fastest realistic income on the UK card.
  5. Do not open five country accounts. Clear three sales in one market first.

Questions people ask

Can I just put the US rates on my UK site? No. Your commission is set by the marketplace the purchase happens on. A UK reader buying from Amazon.co.uk earns you UK rates whatever card you read.

Is 3% worth bothering with? On a £40 product, that is £1.20, and you would need to send Amazon a lot of buyers to make that a living. On a £900 kitchen basket at 5%, it is £45. The rate matters less than the basket, which is the whole argument for build-guide content.

Can I write the price in my article? Not by hand. Amazon's operating agreement only allows price display where Amazon serves the link containing the price, or where you pull it from their API. Manually typed or scraped prices are a violation, and so is any price-tracking feature on your site. Say "check the current price" and link.

How long before this pays anything? Realistically, longer than you want. You need three qualifying sales within 180 days just to keep the account, then £25 of earnings to trigger a payment, then a 60 day wait. Plan for six months before money moves.

The verdict

The UK card is workable, not generous. Build inside the 5% band, write content that fills a basket rather than sells one item, take the bounties seriously, and treat Amazon as one income stream among several rather than the business itself. Anyone promising more than that from a 3% catch-all rate is selling you something.
