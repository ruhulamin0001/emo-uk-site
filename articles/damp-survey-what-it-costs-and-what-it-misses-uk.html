Who Pays the Surveyor Decides What the Survey Finds

Search for damp and you will be offered a free survey within about ninety seconds. It is free because the person carrying it out works for the firm that sells the treatment, and often earns commission on it. That is not a scandal, it is a business model, but it means the free report and the paid one are answering two different questions, and only one of them is yours.

The short version

  • An independent damp and timber report costs about £150 to £500. More for a large property, or where damp and timber are both in scope.
  • The free survey is done by the firm that sells the cure. The surveyor is employed by, or paid commission by, the company that will quote for the treatment.
  • The commonest wrong answer is rising damp. Condensation and a simple building defect account for a great deal of what gets treated as rising damp, and the treatment for those two is not a chemical injection.
  • An injected damp proof course is the product being sold. If every free survey you get recommends the same product, that is information about the surveys, not about your wall.
  • The paid report often recommends the cheaper fix. Ventilation, clearing a bridged cavity, lowering raised ground levels, repointing. None of which anyone earns much money installing.
  • Pay for the survey when the quote is over about £1,500. Below that the report can cost a meaningful share of the job. Above it, a £300 second opinion is cheap insurance.

1. Three different things all called damp

Almost all of the money wasted in this field comes from treating one of these as if it were another.

Condensation. Warm moist air inside the house meets a cold surface and water comes out of it. Symptoms are black spotted mould, usually in corners, behind furniture on external walls, around window reveals and in bathrooms and bedrooms. It is by a wide margin the commonest damp problem in British homes and it is worst in well sealed, under ventilated, under heated properties. The cure is ventilation, heating and reducing moisture production. It costs very little and nobody makes a living from it.

Penetrating damp. Water is getting in from outside through a defect: a cracked render, a failed gutter, a blocked downpipe soaking a wall, a bridged cavity, raised external ground levels above the damp proof course, a leaking roof, defective pointing. The patch is usually localised, worse after rain, and it maps onto something you can see outside. The cure is to fix the defect. That is a builder's job, not a damp specialist's. Two other specialist reports get sold the same way and are worth pricing before anyone starts work: what an asbestos survey and removal actually cost in a house built before 2000, and what a Japanese knotweed treatment plan costs where growth is near a boundary.

Rising damp. Groundwater moving up through masonry by capillary action, which needs an absent or failed damp proof course. It presents as a tide mark low on the wall, typically under about a metre, with salts in the plaster. It is genuinely real and it is genuinely much rarer than the volume of injected damp proof courses sold in this country would suggest.

The diagnosis decides the bill. Condensation is a hundred pounds of ventilation. Rising damp is several thousand pounds of injection and replastering.

2. Who pays, and what that changes

A free damp survey is a lead generation visit. The company offering it makes its money installing treatment, and the person who comes to your house is either their employee or paid on commission for work booked. Their report is a quotation with a diagnosis attached.

This is not an accusation of dishonesty. Most of those surveyors believe what they write. It is an observation about incentives: if the only tool you are paid to sell is an injected damp proof course, rising damp becomes a very available explanation. Consumer bodies and independent surveyors have been pointing at the over diagnosis of rising damp for years, and the reason the criticism keeps recurring is that the incentive keeps recurring.

An independent damp and timber surveyor is paid for the report. They do not carry out the work and do not take commission on it, so recommending "improve the extractor fan and clear the gutter" costs them nothing. That is the entire difference, and it is worth about £300.

In fairness, and this is the part the internet leaves out: most of the online material recommending independent surveys, including the sources listed at the bottom of this page, is published by firms that sell independent surveys. They have a commercial interest too. The reason to believe the argument is not the source, it is that the incentive structure is checkable by anyone, and you can test it yourself by asking one question, which is in the FAQ below.

4. When to pay and when not to bother

A simple rule, because the survey has to be proportionate to the job.

Do not pay for a survey if the mould is black spotted, appears in cold corners and behind furniture, is worse in winter, and you know your house is under ventilated. Buy a decent extractor fan for the bathroom, use the kitchen one, open windows after showering, do not dry washing on radiators in a sealed room, and keep some background heat on. If that clears it in a winter, you have saved several thousand pounds and the diagnosis was free.

Pay for a survey if a treatment company has quoted you more than about £1,500, or if you are buying the property and the damp is affecting your offer, or if there is a tide mark with salting low on a solid wall that has not responded to ventilation, or if there is any suggestion of rot in timbers. At those stakes a £300 independent report is cheap.

Get a builder to look outside first, whatever else you do. Blocked gutters, a downpipe discharging against a wall, and garden soil built up above the damp proof course are three of the commonest causes of what gets diagnosed as rising damp, and all three are visible from the ground in five minutes.

5. Worked example: the Novak flat in Bristol

A ground floor Victorian conversion flat in Bristol. Black mould in the bedroom corner on the external wall and around the window. A free survey from a national damp firm diagnoses rising damp, recommends an injected damp proof course to the front and side walls with hacking off and replastering to a metre, and quotes £4,300.

Independent damp and timber report. £320.

What it found. Moisture readings consistent with condensation, no salt contamination in the plaster, mould pattern following the cold bridge at the window reveal. External inspection found a gutter joint dripping onto the side wall.

Recommended. Repair the gutter joint, fit a humidity controlled extractor in the bathroom, keep background heating on in the bedroom.

Cost of that work. Gutter repair £120, extractor fan supplied and fitted £280. Total £400.

Running cost of the fan. A humidity controlled fan on trickle uses a handful of kWh a year. At the Ofgem cap rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026 of 26.32p per kWh, that is under £5 a year.

The arithmetic. They spent £320 on the report and £400 on the work, a total of £720, against a £4,300 quote. The report paid for itself thirteen times over, and it did so specifically because the person writing it had nothing to sell.

Worth stating the other outcome honestly: if the independent report had confirmed rising damp, they would have been £320 down and would still have paid the £4,300. Paying for a second opinion is not a guaranteed saving. It is buying a diagnosis from someone whose income does not depend on the answer.

Frequently asked

What is the one question to ask a free surveyor? "Does your company or do you personally receive any payment if I go ahead with the work you recommend?" Ask it politely, in writing if you can, and note the answer. It does not make them wrong. It tells you how to weight what follows.

Are damp meters reliable? The handheld pin type meters measure electrical conductance, which is affected by salts in plaster as well as by water. A reading that is high because of salt contamination from a historic, long resolved problem can look identical to an active one. This is a well known limitation and a competent surveyor will say so unprompted.

Does a damp proof course guarantee mean anything? Only as long as the company issuing it exists, and many of these guarantees are worth checking for an insurance backing. A twenty year guarantee from a firm that dissolves in year four is a piece of paper. Ask specifically whether it is insurance backed and by whom.

I am a landlord. Does any of this change? The obligation to deal with damp and mould in rented property is a legal one, not a discretionary one, and the ventilation and heating advice above is only part of the answer when a tenant cannot afford to heat the property. Our guide to the EPC C requirement for private rented homes covers the related efficiency rules.

The honest verdict

A free damp survey from a firm that sells the treatment is a sales visit with a clipboard, and you should treat its diagnosis as a proposal rather than a finding.

Before you pay anyone, spend nothing: look at your gutters, your downpipes and the ground level against your walls, and improve your ventilation for one winter. That resolves a large share of real household damp at almost no cost, and no business model exists to tell you so.

If a treatment quote is over about £1,500, buy the independent report. At £150 to £500 it is the cheapest way to find out whether you are being sold a cure for a disease you do not have. And if it confirms the diagnosis, you have lost a few hundred pounds and gained the confidence to spend several thousand.
