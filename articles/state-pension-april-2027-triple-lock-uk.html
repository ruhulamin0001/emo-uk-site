The Number That Sets Your April 2027 State Pension Lands On 15 September. Under Every Outcome It Crosses The Tax Threshold.

The triple lock is simple to state and widely misreported. The State Pension rises each April by the highest of earnings growth, September inflation, or 2.5%. The earnings figure the government uses is published by the ONS on 15 September. The inflation figure follows on 21 October. Here is what the full rate is now, what each leg would do to it, and the fact that the coverage keeps missing: even the 2.5% floor pushes the full new State Pension above the frozen personal allowance.

1. What the State Pension is now

GOV.UK states that the full rate of the new State Pension is £241.30 a week. Over 52 weeks that is £12,547.60. You get the full rate with 35 qualifying years of National Insurance if your record started after April 2016; if you were contracted out before 2016 you may need more. Many people get less than the full rate and some get more, through a protected payment.

The old basic State Pension, for people who reached pension age before 6 April 2016, is a lower weekly figure and rises by the same percentage each April. Everything below uses the full new rate because it is the number most people search for, but the mechanism is identical.

2. How the triple lock actually works, and when each number lands

GOV.UK describes the annual increase in one sentence: the new State Pension increases each year by whichever is the highest of earnings, prices, or 2.5%. Earnings means the average percentage growth in wages in Great Britain. Prices means CPI.

What the sentence does not say is which month's figures are used, and that is where the dates come from.

By the evening of 21 October, all three numbers exist and the outcome is arithmetic. The government confirms the figure formally in the autumn, usually around the Budget, and the new rate takes effect from the first full week of April 2027.

That is why 15 September matters. The May to July earnings figure is the first of the two variable legs to land, and in most recent years earnings growth has been the leg that won. If the ONS prints a number above 2.9% on 15 September, the floor is irrelevant and the October inflation print has to beat earnings to matter.

4. The threshold nobody is talking about

The personal allowance is £12,570 and is frozen until 5 April 2031. The full new State Pension is £12,547.60. The gap is £22.40 a year.

A rise of just 0.18% would close it. The triple lock guarantees at least 2.5%. So from April 2027, a person receiving the full new State Pension and nothing else will have taxable income above the personal allowance under every possible outcome of the triple lock. At the 2.5% floor the excess is £291, and 20% of that is about £58 of tax. At 4% it is £480 of excess and about £96 of tax.

The State Pension is paid without tax deducted. HMRC collects the tax through a PAYE code on any other pension or employment income, or, if there is none, through a Simple Assessment letter after the tax year. For a pensioner with only the State Pension, that means a letter in 2028 asking for a small sum. It is not a mistake and it is not a scam, but it will surprise people, and the amount is small enough that the surprise is the main cost.

This is not an argument about policy. It is a mechanical consequence of a frozen allowance meeting a rising pension, and it has been visible in the numbers for two years. The Autumn Budget on 28 October could change the allowance, but nothing announced so far does.

5. A worked example: Joan in Carlisle

Joan is 71, lives in Carlisle, and receives the full new State Pension with no other income. In 2026 to 2027 she gets £12,547.60, sits £22.40 under the allowance, and pays no income tax.

Suppose earnings growth published on 15 September is 4.0% and September inflation comes in below that. From April 2027 Joan receives £250.95 a week, £13,049.50 a year. Her income above the allowance is £479.50, and the tax on it at 20% is £95.90. Because nothing deducts it at source, HMRC writes to her after April 2028 with a Simple Assessment for roughly that amount.

Her pension has gone up by £501.90 a year. After the tax, she keeps about £406. The triple lock has done what it says. The frozen allowance has taken a fifth of the increase back.

6. The honest verdict

Watch 15 September for the earnings figure and 21 October for inflation. The higher of the two, or 2.5%, is your April 2027 rise. Nobody can tell you the number before then, whatever the headline says.

If the State Pension is your only income, expect a small tax bill for 2027 to 2028. It arrives by letter, it is genuine, and it is the direct result of the frozen allowance. Budget for it rather than being caught by it.

There is nothing to buy and nothing to apply for. Check your forecast on GOV.UK if you have not reached pension age, and check your NI record for gaps. That is the whole to-do list.

Frequently asked

How much is the full new State Pension now? £241.30 a week for 2026 to 2027, according to GOV.UK, which is £12,547.60 a year.

When will the April 2027 increase be known? The earnings figure is published by the ONS on 15 September 2026 and the September inflation figure on 21 October 2026. The increase is the highest of those two or 2.5%, and the government confirms it in the autumn.

Will I pay tax on the State Pension? If your total income exceeds the £12,570 personal allowance, yes. From April 2027 the full new State Pension on its own exceeds it under every triple lock outcome. Tax is collected through a PAYE code on other income or by Simple Assessment.

Does the basic State Pension rise by the same amount? It rises by the same percentage, applied to its own lower weekly rate. Protected payments and additional State Pension rise with CPI only.
