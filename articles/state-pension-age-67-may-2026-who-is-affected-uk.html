State Pension Age Started Rising To 67 In May. If You Were Born In 1960 Or Early 1961, Here Is Your Month, And What Each Month Of Waiting Costs.

Anyone born on 5 April 1960 or earlier got their State Pension at 66. Anyone born a day later did not, and the first of them reached pension age in May 2026. The rise to 67 is phased one month at a time until April 2028, which means people born a few weeks apart retire months apart. Here is the exact table, the arithmetic at this year's full rate, and the other entitlements that quietly move with the date.

The short version

  • The rise from 66 to 67 began on 6 May 2026 under the Pensions Act 2014 and completes on 6 April 2028. Source: DWP State Pension age timetable.
  • Born 6 April 1960 to 5 March 1961: your pension age is 66 plus a set number of months, from one month up to eleven. Born on or after 6 March 1961: it is 67.
  • The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week for 2026 to 2027, which is £12,548 a year. Each month of extra waiting is about £1,046 forgone at the full rate, on our arithmetic.
  • Pension Credit and the English bus pass move with State Pension age. They are not fixed at 66.
  • The next rise, 67 to 68, is legislated for 2044 to 2046. A third review of the age, launched 21 July 2025, has not reported. Nothing about 68 has changed yet.
  • Check your own date on GOV.UK, then your National Insurance record. Those two checks are the whole to-do list.

1. The table: your birth month and your pension age

The Pensions Act 2014 brought the rise to 67 forward by eight years. Rather than a single date on which everyone's age jumps, the increase is phased by birth month. The DWP's published timetable gives the exact bands:

Date of birthState Pension age
6 October 1954 to 5 April 196066
6 April 1960 to 5 May 196066 years and 1 month
6 May 1960 to 5 June 196066 years and 2 months
6 June 1960 to 5 July 196066 years and 3 months
6 July 1960 to 5 August 196066 years and 4 months
6 August 1960 to 5 September 196066 years and 5 months
6 September 1960 to 5 October 196066 years and 6 months
6 October 1960 to 5 November 196066 years and 7 months
6 November 1960 to 5 December 196066 years and 8 months
6 December 1960 to 5 January 196166 years and 9 months
6 January 1961 to 5 February 196166 years and 10 months
6 February 1961 to 5 March 196166 years and 11 months
6 March 1961 to 5 April 197767

So the first people affected, born 6 April to 5 May 1960, reached pension age from 6 May 2026 onwards. The last of the phased group, born in February 1961, reach it in January 2028. From 6 March 1961 the answer is simply the 67th birthday.

The DWP's own footnote handles the awkward cases: someone born on 31 July 1960 is treated as reaching 66 years and 4 months on 30 November 2026, because there is no 31 November. If your birthday falls at the end of a month, the same one-day adjustment applies.

2. What each month of waiting costs

The full new State Pension for 2026 to 2027 is £241.30 a week, after the 4.8% April rise. That is £12,547.60 a year, or £1,045.63 a month on our arithmetic (£241.30 times 52, divided by 12).

So relative to a person born on 5 April 1960, who got the pension at 66:

Extra monthsPension not received, full rate
1£1,046
3£3,137
6£6,274
9£9,411
12£12,548

Those figures assume the full rate, which needs 35 qualifying years of National Insurance under the new State Pension rules. Many people get less, and some get more through protected amounts from the old system. Your own figure is on your State Pension forecast at GOV.UK, and that is the number to multiply.

There is a second, smaller effect. The State Pension is taxable income. Someone still working full time at 66 who also draws the pension pays income tax on both, so for a higher earner the "cost" of a later pension age is partly offset by not paying tax on it yet. That is not a reason to be glad about it, but it is a reason the net figure is less than the gross for anyone above the personal allowance.

3. What else moves with the date

The State Pension age is a reference point for several other things, and they move with it rather than staying at 66:

  • Pension Credit. The qualifying age is State Pension age. If yours is 66 and 7 months, that is when Pension Credit becomes possible, not before.
  • The older person's bus pass in England. Issued from State Pension age. London, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own schemes with their own ages.
  • National Insurance. You stop paying employee Class 1 NI from State Pension age, so a later date means more months of NI on earnings.
  • Winter Fuel Payment. This one is set by a date of birth each winter rather than by pension age directly. For winter 2026 to 2027 the cut-off is being born on or before 27 June 1960, which corresponds to reaching 66 years and 3 months by the end of the qualifying week. People born from 28 June 1960 wait a year.

What does not move: workplace and personal pension access. The normal minimum pension age for private pensions rises from 55 to 57 on 6 April 2028, on a separate timetable, and it is not tied to the State Pension age.

4. A worked example in Bedford

Kamal in Bedford was born on 20 October 1960. Under the table his State Pension age is 66 years and 7 months, so he reaches it on 20 May 2027. His brother, born in March 1960, got his pension on his 66th birthday in March 2026.

Kamal's forecast shows the full £241.30. Seven months at £1,045.63 is £7,319 he will not receive compared with someone born seven months earlier. He works as a delivery driver on £27,000 a year and plans to carry on to 67, so for those seven months he also keeps paying NI on his wages.

Two practical points come out of his forecast. First, it shows a two-year gap in his NI record from 2009 to 2011. If those years are needed to reach the full rate, voluntary contributions for recent years are still possible and the forecast tells him whether they would change anything. Second, if he does keep working past May 2027 and does not need the pension immediately, deferring it increases the weekly amount by 1% for every nine weeks deferred, roughly 5.8% a year. Whether that is worth it depends on how long he lives, and nobody can tell him that.

5. What is not changing, and the review that might

The rise to 68 is already legislated for 2044 to 2046 under the Pensions Act 2007, affecting people born from 6 April 1977. Two earlier government reviews looked at bringing it forward and neither resulted in a change. A third review was launched on 21 July 2025, with an independent report led by Dr Suzy Morrissey feeding into a decision the Secretary of State must make by March 2029. As of today nothing has been announced, and any change to the 68 date would need an Act of Parliament and years of notice.

So if you were born in 1960 or 1961, your date is fixed and is in the table above. If you were born in the late 1970s or after, the honest answer is that 67 is the law, 68 is the law for 2044 onwards, and the review is where any change would first appear.

6. The honest verdict

If you were born between 6 April 1960 and 5 March 1961, find your line in the table and write the date down. Nobody writes to tell you. The gap between neighbours in the table is a month, and each month is about £1,046 at the full rate.

Then check your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK. It gives your real weekly figure and shows any NI gaps while there is still time to fill them.

Do not plan around a change to 68. The review exists, it has not reported, and nothing before 2044 is in law.

Frequently asked

I was born on 6 April 1960. When do I get my State Pension? At 66 years and 1 month, so on 6 May 2026. You are in the first band of the phased rise.

Does the rise to 67 affect the amount I get? No. The amount depends on your National Insurance record. The rise changes only the date from which it is paid.

Can I get Pension Credit at 66 if my State Pension age is later? No. The Pension Credit qualifying age tracks State Pension age, so it moves with your date.

Is the rise to 68 being brought forward? Not as of 4 September 2026. It is legislated for 2044 to 2046. A third review was launched in July 2025 and has not reported.
