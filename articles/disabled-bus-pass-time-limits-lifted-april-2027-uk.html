The Bus Pass in Your Pocket Does Not Work Before 9:30am. From April 2027 It Will

England is lifting the time restrictions on disabled person's bus passes, which is a straightforwardly good change and has been reported as if it happens imminently. It does not. The date is 1 April 2027, and the gap between now and then is the useful thing to plan around.

The short version

  • The change is real and the date is 1 April 2027. Not 2026. Announced by the Prime Minister on 17 August 2026.
  • What it does: from that date a disabled person's bus pass in England works for free travel on local buses at any time of day, any day.
  • What it replaces: passes are currently valid only between 9:30am and 11pm on weekdays, unless a local authority chooses to fund an extension.
  • England only. The announcement covers England. It is backed by £60 million, made up of £40 million from DWP and £20 million from DfT.
  • Some places already have it. Greater Manchester Combined Authority lifted the time restrictions permanently in March 2026. If you live somewhere that already funds an extension, this changes nothing for you.
  • Eligibility is not changing. The seven statutory categories in the Transport Act 2000 still decide who gets a pass, and your local council still assesses you against them.

1. What is changing, and the date that matters

On 17 August 2026 the Prime Minister announced that England will lift the time restrictions on concessionary disabled person's bus passes. The GOV.UK release is clear about when: from 1 April 2027, people who hold a disabled bus pass can travel on buses for free no matter the time of day.

That date is worth saying twice, because the announcement has been widely covered in a way that reads as though it has already happened. It has not. Between now and 1 April 2027 the current rules still apply.

The current rules are these. A disabled person's bus pass in England is valid for travel between 9:30am and 11pm during weekdays. Outside those hours you pay. Local authorities have always been able to fund an extension out of their own budget, and some do. Greater Manchester Combined Authority permanently lifted the time restrictions in March 2026, which is the example the government itself uses.

The funding is £60 million, and it is worth knowing where it comes from because it is unusual: £40 million from the Department for Work and Pensions and £20 million from the Department for Transport. DWP paying towards bus travel tells you how the change is being justified, which is as employment support rather than as a transport measure.

2. Who actually qualifies for the pass

Nothing about eligibility is changing here. What is changing is what an existing pass lets you do. It is still worth setting out who qualifies, because a good number of people who would get one have never applied.

Eligibility is set out in the Transport Act 2000, which defines seven statutory categories. According to GOV.UK these include people who are:

  • blind or partially sighted
  • profoundly or severely deaf
  • without speech
  • unable or virtually unable to walk because of a disability
  • without arms, or unable to use both arms
  • people with a learning disability
  • people who would be refused a driving licence on medical grounds, other than because of drug or alcohol misuse

Local authorities administer the scheme and assess applications against these criteria. That means the application goes to your council, not to central government, and the evidence they ask for varies by area.

One thing the announcement does not address, and we are not going to pretend otherwise: it says nothing about companion passes, which some councils issue so that a person who cannot travel alone can bring someone with them. If that applies to you, that is a question for your own council rather than something this announcement answers.

3. What lifting the restriction is worth

The honest answer is that it depends entirely on whether you need a bus before 9:30am, and for a lot of pass holders the answer is no. If your travel is shopping, appointments and seeing people, you are already inside the window and this change is worth nothing to you in cash.

Where it is worth real money is early starts. A shift that begins at 8am, a college course, a hospital appointment at the front of the day, a caring run. Those are exactly the journeys the restriction blocked.

To put a number on it you need a fare, and the relevant one is the cap. The same GOV.UK announcement refers to the Prime Minister capping bus fares at £2 throughout 2027. So a single is capped at £2 and a return at £4 on the days you currently have to pay.

You will see a £3 figure quoted in places for January 2027. We are not using it, because it does not appear on the primary source for this announcement and we are not going to build your arithmetic on a number we cannot stand behind. Use £2 as the capped single, and use your own actual local fare if it is lower.

4. Worked example: Gordon in Grantham

Gordon is 58, lives in Grantham and holds a disabled person's bus pass. He works four mornings a week and has to be there for 8am, so he catches the 7:15 bus. It is outside the 9:30am window, so he pays. Coming home at 2pm is inside the window, so that leg is already free.

What he pays now. One capped single at £2, four days a week. That is £8 a week.

Over a working year, allowing for five weeks off: 47 weeks x £8 = £376 a year.

What changes on 1 April 2027. That £376 goes to zero. His return leg was already free, so the whole saving sits in the outbound morning journey the restriction was blocking.

What he still pays between now and then. From today to 31 March 2027 is roughly 28 working weeks once holidays come out, so about £224 more at the capped fare before the change lands. That is the real cost of the lead time, and it is the number the coverage leaves out.

One check worth making. If Lincolnshire were already funding an extension locally, Gordon would be paying nothing today and the announcement would be worth nothing to him. That is a phone call to the council, not a guess.

5. What to do between now and April 2027

Check whether your area already lifted the restriction. Greater Manchester did it in March 2026 and it is not the only one with the power to. Councils fund these extensions locally and they do not always publicise them well. One call answers it.

If you might qualify and have never applied, apply now. The pass takes time to process and the categories above are broader than most people assume, particularly the last one, which covers anyone who would be refused a driving licence on medical grounds. Having the pass in April 2027 requires applying well before April 2027.

Do not rearrange your life around this yet. It is an announcement backed by funding, not yet a scheme with rules published. The commitment is clear and the money is allocated, but the date is over six months away and the detail will be in the regulations rather than the press release.

If the morning fare is a genuine barrier to working now, the fare is not the only lever. Access to Work exists and can cover travel costs to work where a disability makes public transport difficult, and it is a separate scheme with its own route in.

The honest verdict

This is a good change that has been reported with the wrong date attached, and the date is the whole story for anyone planning around it. A bus pass that does not work before 9:30am is a bus pass that does not work for a job, and fixing that is worth doing. But it is fixed on 1 April 2027, and everyone paying morning fares pays them for another six and a half months.

For most pass holders the cash value is genuinely zero, because most journeys already sit inside the window. Be honest with yourself about which group you are in before treating this as good news for your own budget. If your travel is daytime, nothing here changes your money.

The one action worth taking today is the boring one. Ring the council and ask two questions: has this area already lifted the restriction locally, and if I have never applied, do I qualify under the seven categories. Both answers are free and one of them might be worth a few hundred pounds a year before April 2027 arrives at all.

Frequently asked

When exactly does this start? 1 April 2027. The announcement was made on 17 August 2026, which is why some coverage reads as though it is already in force. It is not.

Does this apply in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland? The GOV.UK announcement covers England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own concessionary travel schemes with their own rules, and this £60 million does not apply to them.

I already travel free before 9:30am. Why? Your local authority is probably funding an extension out of its own budget, which it has always been able to do. Greater Manchester did this permanently in March 2026. If that is your situation, the April 2027 change makes no difference to you.

Does the pass cover trains or trams? This announcement is about local bus travel. Some areas include other modes in their local concessionary offer, and some do not. That is set locally, so check with your council rather than assuming.
