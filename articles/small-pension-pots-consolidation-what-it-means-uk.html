Thirteen million pots under a thousand pounds, and one of them is probably yours

Every job change leaves a pension behind. The government has legislated to sweep the smallest ones together automatically, but the sweeping has not started, and what you do in the gap is up to you.

1. Why the pots pile up

Automatic enrolment works. That is the cause, not a criticism. Every time you start a job you are enrolled into that employer's scheme, and when you leave, the pot stays where it is. Do that six times across a career and you have six pensions, several of them small, at least one of which you have forgotten.

DWP's figures: there are now 13 million small pots holding £1,000 or less, and the number is growing by around one million a year.

The damage is not that the money disappears. It is that small pots get eaten. DWP's framing is that having several pots "can stop them getting a good return on their savings if they have to pay multiple flat rate charges". A flat monthly administration charge is trivial against £40,000 and material against £400. Overseeing all these pots also costs the pensions industry around £225 million a year in admin.

DWP estimates the reform is set to boost retirement savings for the average worker by around £1,000.

2. What the law now says, and does not yet do

The Pension Schemes Act passed into law on Wednesday 29 April 2026. DWP's announcement that day described reform to the UK's £2 trillion of pensions, with new rules aimed at benefiting over 20 million workers and a headline estimate of a £29,000 retirement boost for an average earner across the package as a whole.

Be careful with that £29,000. It is a figure for the whole Act, not for small pots consolidation, which DWP separately estimated at around £1,000. Articles that attach the larger number to the pots reform are conflating two things.

On the mechanism itself, the design as set out by DWP is that each individual's small pots will be brought together into one scheme certified as delivering good value, and that individuals will retain the right to opt out.

And now the part that decides whether any of this affects you this year: it has not started. Royal Assent is the beginning of the process, not the end. The consolidation mechanism requires regulations, authorised consolidator schemes and the plumbing to move money between providers, and no commencement date for automatic small pots consolidation has been published. Anyone telling you your pots are about to be merged is ahead of the evidence.

3. What to do in the gap

Grace, Plymouth. Four jobs since 2017, four pensions. Three of them are small. She could wait for the machinery, or she could do the part that will still be useful either way.

  1. Find them. The free GOV.UK Pension Tracing Service finds contact details for a scheme from an employer name. It gives you the provider, not a balance.
  2. Get a current value in writing for each one, and the charges. The charge is the number that matters on a small pot, not the growth rate.
  3. Check for anything unusual before you move anything. Older policies can carry guarantees or a protected pension age that a transfer destroys. On a £400 pot that is unlikely. On a £40,000 one it can be the whole value.
  4. Write the list down somewhere your family can find it. Unclaimed pensions are a real and large problem, and the fix is a piece of paper.
  5. Do not pay a percentage to a company that offers to "find and combine" your pensions. The tracing service is free. Consolidation, when it arrives, will be automatic and free too.

Should you consolidate manually now rather than wait? Honestly, for pots of a few hundred pounds, the arithmetic barely moves either way, and the automatic route will do it for nothing. For larger pots the answer is not a small pots question at all, it is a full transfer question, with guarantees and exit terms to check first.

The verdict

This is a genuinely good reform aimed at a genuinely stupid problem, and it is not running yet. The Act is law as of 29 April 2026. The consolidator is not switched on, and nobody has published a date.

So the useful work this month is the boring half: trace the pots, write down the provider and the value of each, and keep the list somewhere findable. When automatic consolidation does start, you will be able to tell whether it worked, and you will know whether opting out is worth considering. Without the list you will not even notice it happened.

Frequently asked

Will my pensions be merged automatically? That is the intention of the reform, for pots of £1,000 or less, but it has not commenced. DWP's design retains an individual right to opt out.

Is the £29,000 figure about small pots? No. DWP used £29,000 for the Pension Schemes Act package as a whole when it became law on 29 April 2026. The separate small pots estimate was around £1,000 for the average worker.

How do I find an old pension? Use the free GOV.UK Pension Tracing Service, which gives you contact details for the scheme from an employer or scheme name. Never pay a percentage of a pot to a tracing company.
