The DWP can now ask a court for your driving licence, and the clock started in June

New debt recovery powers are in force and enforcement begins rolling out next month. Most coverage has the date wrong and skips the exemptions, which are the part that decides whether this touches you at all.

Ruhul Amin Published 12 September 2026 · 5 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • The powers came into force on 24 June 2026, not October. Enforcement rolls out gradually from October 2026.
  • A driving ban needs a court. Direct deductions from a bank account do not.
  • The debt must be at least £1,000 still outstanding before the DWP can even apply.
  • A suspended order comes first. You lose the licence only if you break the repayment terms without reasonable excuse.
  • No ban at all if you need a licence to earn a living or have another essential need. Maximum 2 years.

1. The dates, which most coverage gets wrong

Plenty of "money changes coming in October" lists put these powers in October 2026. Not so. SI 2026/601 brought the relevant provisions of the Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 into force on 24 June 2026, and DWP published a press release the same day.

What happens in October is enforcement. In DWP's words, enforcement will be gradually rolled out from October 2026, giving debtors a final window from 24 June to pay or agree an affordable plan. The press release puts that window at four months. So the practical deadline is around the end of October, and what stops the process is a phone call, not payment in full.

2. Who this is aimed at

This is narrower than the headlines suggest. It targets people who owe DWP money, are no longer on benefits and are not in PAYE employment, a group DWP says was hard to pursue because the usual routes are deductions from benefit or wages. If you are claiming, or on PAYE, this is not the mechanism you will meet.

Two separate powers get mixed together in coverage. Direct deduction orders let DWP go to a bank without a court order. Disqualification from driving does need a court, and is described as being for the most serious cases, where someone persistently and deliberately evaded repayment and the court is satisfied they had the means to repay and did not, without good reason.

3. How a disqualification actually works

The detail sits in Schedule 6, which inserts Schedule 3ZB into the Social Security Administration Act 1992. Two stages, with the safeguards written into the legislation.

  1. Threshold. At least £1,000 must still be outstanding before the Secretary of State can apply.
  2. Suspended order first. The court sets out repayment terms and states that an immediate order may follow if they are broken. Nobody loses a licence at this stage.
  3. Immediate order only on breach, and only if the court is satisfied, on the balance of probabilities, that the person failed without reasonable excuse to comply.
  4. One missed instalment does not on its own count as failing to comply.
  5. Hard exemptions. The court may not make an immediate order if it considers the person needs a licence to earn a living or has another essential need. DWP gives couriers and caring responsibilities as examples.
  6. Length. Such period as the court considers likely to result in payment, and no more than 2 years from the day the order is made.

The court is a magistrates' court in England and Wales, a sheriff or summary sheriff in Scotland. If an order is made without you being heard and you had good reason for not attending, you have 21 days from notice to apply to set it aside.

4. What to do if a letter arrives

Sharon, Hull. She stopped claiming three years ago, is self employed, and owes £2,400 from an overpayment she disputed and then stopped opening post about. A letter arrives in July. On the licence her position is better than she fears, because she drives for work, an express statutory bar on an immediate order. On the money it is worse, because the direct deduction route needs no court and is blocked by none of that. So:

  • Contact DWP inside the window. The press release says these powers can be avoided entirely by getting in touch, and staff can point people to free debt advice.
  • Ask for an affordable plan rather than agreeing to a figure you cannot sustain. An order you then breach is what moves this to stage two.
  • Challenge the debt if it is wrong. A dispute is a separate track and does not resolve itself by silence.
  • Get free advice from Citizens Advice, StepChange or National Debtline. Do not pay a company to talk to DWP for you.

The verdict

The driving ban is the eye-catching part and the least likely to touch anyone: £1,000 threshold, a court required, a suspended order first, one missed instalment not enough, needing a licence to earn a living a hard bar, two years maximum.

The part that will actually affect people is quieter. DWP can now go straight to a bank account without a court order. If you have an old DWP debt and no longer claim, the four month window that opened on 24 June is the cheap moment to deal with it. A call and an affordable plan removes the whole chain. Silence starts it.

Frequently asked

Can the DWP take my licence without going to court? No. Disqualification requires a court, which must make a suspended order first. Direct deductions from a bank account are the power that needs no court order.

I drive for work. Can I still be banned? The legislation says the court may not make an immediate disqualification order if it considers you need a licence to earn a living or have another essential need.

How long can a ban last? Whatever period the court considers likely to result in payment, and no longer than 2 years from the day the order is made.

What if the debt is not mine or is wrong? Dispute it, and get free advice from Citizens Advice, StepChange or National Debtline. Recovery and a dispute about whether the debt is correct are separate things.
