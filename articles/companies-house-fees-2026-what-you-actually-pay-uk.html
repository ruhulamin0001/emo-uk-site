Filing on Paper at Companies House Costs You More Than Double on Some Fees

Companies House publishes every fee it charges in one place, and the list has an obvious pattern once you put the columns side by side. Paper costs more than online on almost everything, sometimes by more than double. Here is the whole set as at 2 July 2026, and the one fee that is worth more than all the others combined to avoid.

The short version

  • Incorporation: £100 online, £124 on paper. Same day is £156 and is software only.
  • Confirmation statement: £50 online, £110 on paper. Once per 12 month payment period, not per filing.
  • Change of name: £20 online, £30 paper, or £85 same day.
  • Voluntary strike off: £13 online, £18 paper. Registering a charge: £14 online, £24 paper.
  • Administrative restoration is £341, paper only. This is the one to avoid, and it is avoidable.
  • Searching the register is free. You only pay for certified copies and certificates, at £22, or £65 same day.

1. The fees a normal small company actually meets

Most limited companies will only ever encounter a handful of these. The full list below is taken from the Companies House fees guidance, updated 2 July 2026.

Incorporation. £100 online, £100 by software, £124 paper. Same day: £156, software only.

Confirmation statement. £50 online or software, £110 paper. Charged with your first statement in the 12 month payment period.

Change of name. £20 online or software, £30 paper. Same day £85.

Voluntary strike off. £13 online, £18 paper.

Registration of a charge. £14 online or software, £24 paper.

Administrative restoration. £341, paper only.

Removing personal details from the public register. £34, charged per document suppressed.

Two of those carry a detail worth reading twice. The confirmation statement fee is once per 12 month payment period, not once per filing, so a company that files several statements in a year pays the fee with the first one. And the £34 suppression fee is per document, so an address that appears on eight filings is eight fees, not one.

2. The online and paper gap, and the software-only catch

Set the two columns against each other and the pattern is consistent.

Incorporation: paper costs 24% more.

Confirmation statement: paper costs 120% more, £110 against £50.

Change of name: paper costs 50% more.

Registration of a charge: paper costs 71% more.

Voluntary strike off: paper costs 38% more.

Now the catch that trips people up. Same day incorporation at £156 is listed under software only, not online. It is not something you can buy through the standard web service, which means going through a formation agent or filing software. If you have told a client you can have a company registered today, that is the route, and it is worth knowing before you promise it rather than after.

The same applies to same day change of name at £85, which is available online and by software but not on paper, and to same day reduction of capital by solvency statement at £89, which is upload service only.

3. Worked example: Tomas incorporating in Coventry

Tomas, Coventry. Setting up a limited company for a consultancy. Year one, doing everything himself online.

Incorporation online: £100.

First confirmation statement: £50.

Year one total: £150.

The same year on paper: £124 plus £110, which is £234. A gap of £84 for identical outcomes, purely on the filing route.

What if he wants his home address off the register? £34 per document suppressed. If his address appears on the incorporation documents and two later filings, that is £102, not £34. Worth using a registered office address from the start rather than suppressing later.

What if he later closes the company? Voluntary strike off online, £13. The cheapest thing Companies House does, and the correct way to close a company you no longer need.

4. The £341 fee, and how it happens

Administrative restoration costs £341, is paper only, and is the largest fee a normal small company is ever likely to face. It exists to undo something.

The sequence is always roughly the same. A company stops filing, usually because the director has moved on mentally before moving on administratively. Companies House begins the strike off process, the company is struck off the register, and then somebody discovers a reason it needed to still exist: a bank account with money in it, a contract, an asset, a claim.

Restoring it costs £341 in Companies House fees alone, before any accountancy or legal cost of bringing the filings up to date. Against that, the confirmation statement that would have kept it alive costs £50 a year online, and a voluntary strike off, if you genuinely wanted to close it, costs £13.

That is the whole practical message of the fee schedule. The expensive fees are corrections. The routine ones are cheap.

5. What is free, and what is not

Searching the Companies House register is free. You can look up any company, its filing history, its officers and its accounts without paying anything, and a great many services that charge for this are simply reselling free data with a subscription attached.

What you pay for is documents with legal standing, and copies delivered to you:

Certified copy of a document: £22 by post, £65 same day.

Certificate of incorporation: £22 by post, £65 same day, £16 for an additional certificate for the same company.

Copy of a company document by email: £3. Hard copy of a document not on microfiche, by post: £9.

A missing image added to a company's filing history: £3.

The £22 certified copy is the one that catches people out, because a bank or a buyer asking for a "certified" document means this, not a printout of the free search result. If somebody has asked you for a certified copy, the free download will not satisfy them.

The honest verdict

Companies House is cheap for routine things and expensive for repairs, and the gap between the two is the only strategic point in the whole fee schedule.

Three things follow from it. File online, because paper costs between 24% and 120% more for identical outcomes. Do not let a company drift into being struck off, because £50 a year keeps it alive and £341 brings it back. And if you want your home address off the public register, sort it at incorporation rather than paying £34 per document to suppress it afterwards. Everything else on the list is a few pounds either way.

Frequently asked

Is the confirmation statement fee charged every time I file one? No. The fee is charged with your first confirmation statement in the 12 month payment period. Filing additional statements within that period, for example to record a change, does not attract another fee.

Can I get same day incorporation online? The published fee schedule lists same day incorporation at £156 under the software column only, not online. In practice that means using filing software or a formation agent rather than the standard web service.

How much does it cost to search the register? Nothing. Searching the Companies House register, including filing histories and accounts, is free. Fees apply to certified copies, certificates and some delivery formats, not to looking things up.

Why is administrative restoration so expensive? It is a corrective process rather than a routine filing, it is paper only, and it undoes a strike off. At £341 in Companies House fees alone it costs nearly seven times an annual confirmation statement, which is why keeping filings current is the cheaper path by a wide margin.
