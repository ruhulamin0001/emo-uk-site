HMRC Is Sending P800 Letters Until March. Four Tells Separate A Real Refund From A Fake One.

Every autumn two things arrive together: genuine HMRC tax calculation letters telling employees they overpaid or underpaid in the year to 5 April, and a wave of texts and emails pretending to be them. The real process is slow, paper based and specific. Here is exactly how a P800 works, what the refund timings are, what happens if you owe, and the four tells that separate HMRC from a scammer.

The short version

  • A P800 is a tax calculation letter for people who are employed or get a pension and are not in Self Assessment. Source: GOV.UK, tax overpayments and underpayments.
  • Letters are sent between June and March of the following tax year, so 2025 to 2026 calculations are arriving now and will continue until March 2027.
  • If you are owed money, you claim online with the reference on the letter and your National Insurance number, or through the HMRC app. Payment arrives within 5 working days online, or 6 weeks if you ask HMRC to post a cheque.
  • If the letter says HMRC will send a cheque, you do nothing. It arrives within 14 days of the date on the letter.
  • If you owe less than £3,000 and pay tax through PAYE, HMRC collects it through your tax code in 12 monthly instalments from the next tax year. You do not have to pay it up front.
  • HMRC does not text or email a link to claim a refund. The letter comes by post. Anything else asking for bank details is a scam.

1. What a P800 is and who gets one

GOV.UK explains it in two sentences. If you have paid too much or too little tax by the end of the tax year on 5 April, HMRC will send you either a tax calculation letter, also known as a P800, or a Simple Assessment letter. The letter tells you how to get a refund or pay what you owe.

You only get one if you are employed or receive a pension. If you are registered for Self Assessment, your return picks up any difference and no P800 is issued.

The common causes GOV.UK lists are a wrong tax code (for example, HMRC had the wrong information about your income), starting or finishing a job partway through the year, having more than one job or pension, or receiving taxable benefits. The 2025 to 2026 tax year is the one being reconciled now, and the letters go out between June and March of the following tax year. That is a nine month window, which is why a genuine letter can arrive any time from now until spring.

A Simple Assessment letter is the cousin of the P800. HMRC uses it when it cannot collect what you owe through a tax code, most commonly for pensioners whose State Pension has pushed them over the personal allowance. We covered why that group is about to grow in the state pension guide linked below.

2. If you are owed money: the two routes and their timings

The letter itself tells you which route applies. There are two.

What the letter saysWhat you doWhen the money arrives
You can claim onlineGo to GOV.UK or the HMRC app with the reference number from the letter and your National Insurance number. Choose bank transfer or request a cheque.5 working days by bank transfer; 6 weeks if you ask for a cheque
HMRC will send you a chequeNothing. Do not contact HMRC.Within 14 days of the date on the letter

Those timings are GOV.UK's own. The bank transfer route needs a UK bank account and can also be done through your personal tax account. If you are owed money from more than one year, you get a single cheque for the whole amount.

Notice what is missing. There is no route where you click a link in a message. There is no route where HMRC asks you to confirm your card details. There is no fee, no processing charge and no deadline to "release" the money. The reference number is on the paper letter and nowhere else.

3. If you owe money: the £3,000 rule

The other kind of P800 says you underpaid. GOV.UK sets out what happens next, and it is gentler than most people expect.

HMRC will usually collect the tax you owe by changing your tax code, so more tax comes off your wages or pension in equal instalments over 12 months from the start of the following tax year. That happens automatically if you pay Income Tax through an employer or pension provider, earn enough over your personal allowance to cover it, and owe less than £3,000.

So an underpayment of £480 for 2025 to 2026 is not a demand for £480 now. It becomes £40 a month less in your pay packet from April 2027 to March 2028. You can pay it off early if you prefer, but you do not have to. If HMRC cannot collect it that way, it writes to you with payment options, and a Simple Assessment letter has its own deadline printed on it.

If you think the calculation is wrong, GOV.UK says to contact HMRC and say which amounts you believe are incorrect and what they should be. The most common error is an employer's figures for a job you left, so keep your P45 and P60.

4. Four tells that separate HMRC from a scammer

HMRC publishes examples of phishing texts and emails every year and the pattern rarely changes. Four things give a fake away.

The channel. A P800 is a letter. HMRC does not text, email or WhatsApp you a link to claim a refund. It may send a text telling you a letter is on its way, but that text never contains a link to a claim form.

The urgency. A genuine refund does not expire in 24 hours. The real window for claiming an overpayment is four years from the end of the tax year.

The details requested. The GOV.UK claim asks for the reference on your letter and your National Insurance number, then your bank details to pay you. It never asks for your card number, your card's expiry date, your mother's maiden name or a "verification fee".

The address. The only claim address is on gov.uk. Anything on a lookalike domain, however official the logo, is not HMRC. If in doubt, type gov.uk into the browser yourself and search for "tax calculation letter" rather than following anything sent to you.

Forward suspicious emails to phishing@hmrc.gov.uk and texts to 60599. Both are free.

5. A worked example: Gareth in Wrexham

Gareth left a warehouse job in Wrexham in July 2025 and started a new one in September. His new employer put him on an emergency tax code for two months because his P45 arrived late, so he was taxed as if he had no personal allowance for those months.

In August 2026 a P800 arrives. It shows total pay of £27,400 for 2025 to 2026, tax paid of £3,412 and tax due of £2,966, so an overpayment of £446. The letter says he can claim online.

He goes to GOV.UK, enters the reference from the letter and his National Insurance number, chooses bank transfer, and £446 lands in his account four working days later. Total cost: nothing. Total time: ten minutes.

Three days after the letter arrived he also received a text saying "HMRC: you are due a tax rebate of £446.20, claim within 48 hours at" followed by a link. The amount was close enough to feel real. It was not HMRC. HMRC does not know his mobile number for this purpose and would not have sent a link if it did. He deleted it. That coincidence, a real letter and a fake text in the same week, is exactly how these scams work, because the fakes are timed to the season the real letters go out.

6. The honest verdict

If a P800 arrives saying you are owed money, claim it on GOV.UK or in the HMRC app. Five working days, no fee, no middleman. There is nothing to buy here, and there are firms that will charge you a third of your refund to fill in the same form.

If it says you owe less than £3,000, it will come out of your tax code next year. Check the figures against your P60 and P45, then let it happen.

If it arrived by text, email or WhatsApp with a link, it is not a P800. Delete it, or forward it to 60599.

Frequently asked

When does HMRC send P800 letters? Between June and March of the following tax year, according to GOV.UK. Calculations for 2025 to 2026 are being sent from June 2026 until March 2027.

How long does a P800 refund take? 5 working days by bank transfer if you claim online, 6 weeks if you ask HMRC to send a cheque, or within 14 days of the letter date if the letter says HMRC will send a cheque automatically.

Do I have to pay an underpayment straight away? Usually not. If you owe less than £3,000, pay tax through PAYE and earn enough over your personal allowance, HMRC collects it through your tax code over 12 months from the next tax year.

Will HMRC text me about a refund? HMRC does not send texts or emails with links to claim refunds. A P800 is a paper letter. Report suspicious texts to 60599 and emails to phishing@hmrc.gov.uk.
