Recruiters Find Half Their Candidates on LinkedIn. A Complete Profile Raises Your Odds 71%

Most people use LinkedIn as a place to apply for jobs, which is the least valuable thing it does. Its real function is as a searchable database that recruiters go through looking for people who are not applying to anything. Being findable is a different job from being impressive.

1. Why the profile matters more than the applications

72% of recruiters use LinkedIn when hiring, 67% consider LinkedIn hires higher quality, and recruiters source more than 50% of candidates through it. Roughly seven people are hired every minute on the platform, over three million a year.

The important consequence is that a large share of those hires never involved an application. A recruiter searched, found a profile, and made contact. If your profile does not appear in that search, you are invisible for those roles no matter how good you are.

This matters more in a slack market. UK unemployment has risen to about 5.1 to 5.2%, a four year high, and there are now 2.4 to 2.5 jobseekers per vacancy against 2.0 a year ago. When advertised roles attract hundreds of applicants, being contacted directly is the route that bypasses the pile.

2. How recruiters actually search

Recruiters use keyword search with filters. They are not browsing. They type a job title, add a location and sometimes a skill, and work down the results.

That means your profile is competing on whether it contains the words being searched, in the fields that are indexed.

  • Your headline is the most heavily weighted field. Leaving it as your current job title wastes it.
  • Job titles in your experience section should be the ones the industry uses, not your employer's internal invention. If your official title is "Customer Delight Ninja", write "Customer Service Team Leader" and explain the internal title in the description.
  • The skills section is a filter, not decoration. Recruiters filter on it. Fill all of it with the terms that appear in adverts for the job you want.
  • Location must be set, and set to where you are willing to work. A blank or wrong location removes you from most searches.
  • Open to work, the internal setting, puts you in the recruiter only pool. That is separate from the green photo frame.
A recruiter with a shortlist to fill types four words into a search box. Your profile either contains those words or it does not exist.

3. The six fields that do the work

A complete profile carries a 71% higher chance of an interview. Complete means these six, done properly.

  1. Photo. A clear head and shoulders, your face taking up most of the frame, neutral background, taken in the last few years. It does not need to be professional, it needs to look like you at work.
  2. Headline. 220 characters, the most valuable space on the profile. Job title, specialism, and one number. "Operations Manager, manufacturing, 12 years, process improvement and cost control" beats "Operations Manager at Acme Ltd".
  3. About. Three short paragraphs. What you do, one concrete achievement with a figure, and what you are looking for. Written as you speak, not in third person.
  4. Experience. Every role, with dates. Three bullets each for recent roles, describing outcomes rather than duties. Older roles can be one line.
  5. Skills. Fill it. This is a search filter, and an empty skills section removes you from filtered searches entirely.
  6. Recommendations. Two or three is plenty. Ask former managers and colleagues directly, and offer to write theirs first, which is usually why people say yes.

Set a custom profile URL as well. It takes ten seconds, and it goes on your CV.

4. Open To Work, public or private

The green Open To Work photo frame increases recruiter messages by 40%. That is a large effect for a setting that takes one click.

The obvious worry is your current employer seeing it. There are two versions and most people do not know that.

  • Public. The green frame, visible to everyone. Highest response, and your employer will see it.
  • Recruiters only. No frame, visible only to recruiters using LinkedIn Recruiter. LinkedIn cannot guarantee your employer's own recruiters will not see it, so treat it as low risk rather than no risk.

If you are unemployed or your search is not secret, use the public frame. Turning down a 40% increase in inbound contact to avoid an awkwardness that does not apply to you is the wrong trade.

5. Activity, without becoming a LinkedIn person

You do not need to post motivational threads. But a profile with no activity at all looks dormant, and dormant profiles get contacted less because recruiters assume they are stale.

The minimum that works, about fifteen minutes a week:

  • Comment on three posts. Something substantive, from people in your field. A useful comment is seen by more relevant people than most posts.
  • Follow the companies you want to work for. You see their vacancies early, and their recruiters see engaged followers.
  • Connect with five relevant people. Always with a note. Recruiters in your sector, people doing the job you want.
  • Post once a month if you can face it. Something you learned, something you finished. It does not need to be profound.

What not to do: engagement bait, reposting other people's content without comment, or applying to everything with the one click button and nothing else. The last one is common and produces almost nothing, because a one click application with a stale profile is the weakest possible signal.

6. Messaging a stranger so they reply

Most connection requests and messages are ignored because they ask for something before giving a reason. A structure that gets replies:

  • One line saying why them specifically. Their role, their company, something they posted.
  • One line saying who you are, with the relevant fact only.
  • One small ask. Not "can you get me a job". Something like "would you be open to a ten minute call about how your team is structured".
  • Under 100 words. Long messages from strangers do not get read.

Message the hiring manager rather than the recruiter where you can identify them, and message after you have applied so there is something to point at.

7. A worked profile rewrite

Daniel, 36, warehouse team leader in Leicester, looking to move into operations. Three months of applications, no replies, no recruiter contact.

Before. Headline: "Team Leader at Midland Logistics". About: empty. Experience: job titles and dates only, no descriptions. Skills: three, added automatically. Location: blank. Photo: a cropped wedding photo.

Why no recruiter ever found him. Blank location removed him from location filtered searches. Three skills removed him from skill filtered searches. His headline contained his employer's name rather than what he does. In practice his profile was unsearchable.

After, forty minutes of work. Photo replaced with a plain head and shoulders taken on his phone against a white wall. Headline: "Warehouse Team Leader, 8 years, stock control and process improvement, Leicester". About: three paragraphs including the line that unplanned downtime fell from 14 hours a month to under 4 after he rebuilt the maintenance schedule. Experience: three outcome bullets per role. Skills: filled with the terms from twelve operations adverts he had already read. Location set. Open To Work set to recruiters only, since he is still employed.

Six weeks later: four recruiter messages, two of which were for roles that were never advertised publicly. He did not become better at his job in six weeks. He became findable.

The honest verdict

Treat LinkedIn as a database entry rather than a social network and it becomes far more useful. The single highest value hour you can spend is filling in the six fields properly, because a complete profile carries a 71% higher interview rate and an incomplete one is often invisible to search.

After that, fifteen minutes a week of genuine activity keeps you from looking dormant. That is the whole commitment. You do not need to post, build a personal brand, or have opinions about hustle.

And turn Open To Work on. Public if your search is not secret, recruiters only if it is. A 40% increase in inbound contact for one click is the best return available anywhere in job hunting.

Frequently asked

Do I need LinkedIn Premium? No. Premium shows you who viewed your profile and lets you message outside your network. Useful during an active search, not necessary. Use the free month if you want to test it, and diarise the cancellation.

Will my employer know I am looking? Not from the recruiters only setting, though LinkedIn cannot guarantee it. Profile updates can appear in feeds, so turn off share profile updates in settings before you make changes.

Does LinkedIn matter for trades and hourly work? Less. It is strongest for office, professional and technical roles. For trades, local groups, word of mouth and trade specific boards do more.

Should my LinkedIn match my CV exactly? Dates and job titles must match, because inconsistencies get checked. The content can differ, since your CV is tailored per application and LinkedIn is one general version.
