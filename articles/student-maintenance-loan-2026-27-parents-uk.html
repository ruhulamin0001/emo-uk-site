Your Child's Maintenance Loan Assumes You Will Pay Up To £5,782 A Year. Nobody Sends You The Bill.

Freshers' week is here and the first maintenance loan instalments are landing. Most parents know the loan is means tested on their income. Fewer know what that means in pounds: the system reduces the loan by £1 for every £6.47 you earn above £25,000 and assumes you make up the difference. That £25,000 line has not moved since 2008. Here is the arithmetic the Student Loans Company never puts in a letter.

1. The 2026 to 2027 figures

GOV.UK publishes the maximum maintenance loan for new full-time undergraduates from England each year. For the 2026 to 2027 academic year the maximums are:

Where the student lives2025 to 20262026 to 2027
With parents£8,877£9,118
Away from home, outside London£10,544£10,830
Away from home, in London£13,762£14,135

Those are the numbers most articles stop at, and they are only true for households with income of £25,000 or less. The word "up to" on the GOV.UK page is doing a great deal of work.

The tuition fee loan, by contrast, is not means tested. It rose to £9,790 for 2026 to 2027, is paid straight to the university, and every eligible student can borrow the full amount regardless of what their parents earn.

2. How the taper works, and the £6.47 rule

The House of Commons Library's briefing on the value of student maintenance support, updated in March 2026, sets out the 2026 to 2027 taper for a student living away from home outside London. The maximum loan of £10,830 is available at household incomes below £25,000. Above that, the loan falls until it reaches a minimum of £5,048 at incomes of £62,410 or more.

The Library expresses the taper as a parental contribution: it increases by £1 for every £6.47 of household income over £25,000, up to a maximum of £5,782. That £5,782 is exactly the difference between the maximum and minimum loan, and it is the amount the system assumes the family will provide.

The middle rows are our own calculations from the Library's £6.47 figure and will differ by a few pounds from the Student Loans Company's assessment. The shape is what matters. A household on £45,000 is assumed to find about £3,000 a year per child at university. Nothing tells them so.

Household income for this purpose is the parents' taxable income from the tax year two years before the course starts, so a 2026 entrant is assessed on 2024 to 2025. If income has fallen by 15% or more since, the student can ask for a current year assessment.

3. Why £25,000 is the number that matters

The Library states plainly that the household income level at which a student qualifies for the maximum level of support has remained unchanged in cash terms since 2008 at £25,000.

In 2008 that was a modest household income. In 2026, after 18 years of wage growth, a couple each working full time on the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour earn roughly £49,600 between them before tax, well above the threshold. The real level of income at which support begins to fall has therefore dropped substantially, and the Library notes that this means parents in higher income households need to make larger contributions to bring support up to the maximum.

The Library also records a real cut of around 10% in the maximum support package across 2022 to 2023 and 2023 to 2024, because increases were set on forecast inflation that undershot actual inflation and were never corrected. The IFS described the government's framing of one of those increases as "at best highly misleading". That cut is baked into the 2026 to 2027 figures above.

Two things are changing, neither of them soon. Maintenance grants return in 2028 to 2029 for students from low income households on certain courses, worth up to £1,000. And GOV.UK notes that student finance is changing for courses starting on or after 1 January 2027. Neither affects a student who started this month.

4. A worked example: the Okafors in Leicester

The Okafors live in Leicester. Their combined taxable income in 2024 to 2025 was £48,000. Their daughter starts a three year degree in Sheffield this month, living in halls.

Her maximum possible loan is £10,830. Their income is £23,000 over the £25,000 threshold. Dividing by 6.47 gives an assumed contribution of about £3,555, so her loan is about £7,275 a year, paid in three termly instalments of roughly £2,425.

Suppose her room in halls costs £7,000 for the year, a figure you should replace with the actual contract. The loan covers accommodation and leaves about £275 for everything else for a year. The system assumes the Okafors put in £3,555, or around £296 a month. Nobody has written to them to say so, and the letter their daughter received simply states the loan amount.

Over three years that is about £10,665 the family is expected to find, on top of whatever they choose to give. Knowing the number in September is worth more than discovering it in a phone call in November.

5. What parents can and cannot do about it

You cannot opt out of the means test except in specific cases: the student is over 25, has been married, has supported themselves for three years, or is estranged from their parents or a care leaver. From 2026 to 2027, care leavers are assessed without household income and can borrow the maximum.

You can ask for a current year assessment if household income has fallen by at least 15% compared with the tax year used. A redundancy or a drop in self-employed profits in 2025 to 2026 qualifies.

Pension contributions reduce assessed income. Household income for student finance is taxable income after pension contributions, so salary sacrifice or personal pension payments in the assessment year lower the figure. This is a legitimate and well known effect, and it works two years in advance, so it helps younger siblings rather than this year's entrant.

The student can appeal the assessment if the income figures are wrong, and should check the online account rather than assume the Student Loans Company has the right year.

6. The honest verdict

Work out the assumed contribution before the first term, not after. Take household income minus £25,000, divide by 6.47, and that is roughly what the system expects from you this year. There is nothing to buy and no form to fill; it is just a number you deserve to know.

If your income has fallen by 15% or more, apply for a current year assessment now. That is the only lever that changes this year's loan.

Do not wait for the £25,000 threshold to move. It has not since 2008, and nothing announced changes that for 2026 entrants.

Frequently asked

What is the maximum maintenance loan for 2026 to 2027? £9,118 living with parents, £10,830 away from home outside London, £14,135 in London, according to GOV.UK. The maximum applies only where household income is £25,000 or less.

How much do parents have to contribute? Legally, nothing. But the loan is reduced by about £1 for every £6.47 of household income above £25,000, to a minimum of £5,048 outside London, and the Commons Library describes that reduction as an assumed parental contribution of up to £5,782.

Which year's income is used? The tax year two years before the course starts, so 2024 to 2025 for a September 2026 entrant. If income has fallen by 15% or more since then, the student can request a current year assessment.

Are maintenance grants coming back? Partly. The Commons Library says grants return in 2028 to 2029 for students from low income households on certain subjects, worth up to £1,000. That does not apply to students starting in 2026.
