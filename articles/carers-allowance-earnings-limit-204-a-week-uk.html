The Cliff Edge at £204: How Carer's Allowance Stops Dead if You Earn £204.01, and the Sums That Keep You Under It

Most benefits taper. Carer's Allowance does not. Earn £204 a week after tax, National Insurance and allowable expenses and you get the full £86.45. Earn £204.01 and you get nothing, for that week, with an overpayment demand if you did not notice. This is the rule as GOV.UK states it on 5 September 2026, the arithmetic at the minimum wage, the deductions that pull earnings back under the line, and the things the rule is often said to do that it does not.

The short version

  • Carer's Allowance is £86.45 a week for at least 35 hours of care for someone who gets a qualifying disability benefit. Source: GOV.UK, Carer's Allowance, read 5 September 2026.
  • The earnings limit is £204 a week "after tax, National Insurance and expenses". Go over by a penny and the whole £86.45 stops. There is no partial payment and no taper.
  • At the £12.71 National Living Wage, 16 hours a week is £203.36 gross, under the line even before deductions. A 17th hour takes gross pay to £216.07, and unless deductions bring it back under £204 the entire allowance is lost for £12.71 of extra pay.
  • What comes off before the test: income tax, National Insurance, half of any pension contributions, work equipment and travel between workplaces, and up to half your earnings if you pay someone other than a close relative to look after the disabled person or your children while you work.
  • What the rule does NOT do: it does not count occupational or private pension income as earnings, it does not apply in Scotland (Carer Support Payment has its own rules), and it does not pay on top of a State Pension of £86.45 or more, though the underlying entitlement can still unlock extra Pension Credit.
  • Our verdict: if you work and care, put the £204 line in your diary, average over the pay period your employer uses, and treat a pay rise or an extra shift as something to check against the line before you say yes.

1. The rule, and why it is a cliff

GOV.UK's eligibility page for Carer's Allowance lists the conditions: you are 16 or over, you spend at least 35 hours a week caring for someone who gets a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance, the middle or higher rate of DLA care, or the daily living part of PIP, you are not in full-time education or studying 21 hours a week or more, and "your earnings are £204 or less a week after tax, National Insurance and expenses". The allowance itself is £86.45 a week, paid weekly in advance or every four weeks, and it carries a Class 1 National Insurance credit for every week you get it, which protects your State Pension record.

The word that matters is "or less". The £204 figure is not a point at which the allowance starts to reduce. It is a test. Pass it and you get £86.45; fail it and you get nothing. Earnings are averaged over the period you are paid for, so a monthly-paid carer is judged on the monthly figure divided into weeks, and a one-off bonus or a month with an extra shift can tip a whole pay period over. Where the DWP later finds earnings were over the limit, it recovers the weeks concerned as an overpayment, which is how carers have ended up with four-figure demands for going a few pounds over for a few months.

At today's National Living Wage, 16 hours is the natural ceiling, and it is a tight one: 64p of headroom before any deductions. A carer earning £13.50 an hour crosses the line at 15.1 hours; at £15 an hour it is 13.6 hours. Because the test is on net earnings, the deductions in the next section are the difference between 16 hours and 18 or 19 for many people, and the reason it is worth doing the sum properly rather than stopping at 16 out of caution. When the minimum wage rises in April 2027, to between £13.02 and £13.34 on the Low Pay Commission's own range, 16 hours moves to £208.32 at the bottom of that range, over the line, unless the £204 limit is uprated at the same time. It usually has been, but it is a separate decision, and it is worth checking in March.

3. What comes off before the £204 test

GOV.UK defines earnings as income from employment and self-employment "after tax, National Insurance and expenses", and lists what counts as an expense: 50% of your pension contributions; equipment you need to do your job, for example specialist clothing; travel costs between different workplaces that your employer does not pay for; and business costs if you are self-employed. There is one more, and it is the big one for parents. If you pay a carer to look after the disabled person or your children while you work, you can treat care costs of up to 50% of your earnings as an expense, provided the carer is not your spouse, partner, parent, child or sibling. GOV.UK's own example: earnings of £100 a week after tax and £60 of care costs lets you deduct £50.

The pension deduction is the lever people miss. Paying £40 a week into a workplace or personal pension takes £20 off your assessed earnings. For a carer sitting at £215 net, that £40 a week of pension saving is the difference between keeping £86.45 a week and losing it, and the money is still theirs. Payments that do not count as earnings at all include occupational and private pension income, and the first £20 a week plus half the rest of any income from a lodger.

4. What the rule does not do

It does not apply in Scotland, where Carer Support Payment has replaced Carer's Allowance with its own earnings rules set by Social Security Scotland. It does not taper; there is no reduced rate for earning £210. It does not pay on top of a full State Pension: GOV.UK says if your State Pension is £86.45 a week or more you will not get a Carer's Allowance payment, but the "underlying entitlement" still counts and can add a carer amount to Pension Credit, which we set out in our Pension Credit guide. It does not let two people claim for the same person. And it does not count savings; there is no capital test at all.

One thing it does do, which surprises people: Carer's Allowance is taxable income, and it counts in full as income for Universal Credit and Pension Credit. A carer on Universal Credit usually does better claiming the UC carer element instead, and Citizens Advice or Carers UK will run both sums for free.

5. A worked example in Chelmsford

Sam in Chelmsford cares for his mother, who gets Attendance Allowance, for around 40 hours a week, and works 16 hours a week at a supermarket on £12.71 an hour. Gross pay £203.36. Below the personal allowance and the National Insurance threshold, so nothing comes off, and he is 64p under the line. He keeps £86.45 a week, £4,495 a year, plus the National Insurance credit.

His manager offers two more hours a week. At 18 hours he grosses £228.78, still under the tax and NI thresholds, so net earnings are £228.78: £24.78 over. The extra £25.42 a week of pay would cost him £86.45 of allowance, a net loss of £61.03 a week. Two ways back under. He could pay £50 a week into the workplace pension, half of which, £25, is deductible, taking assessed earnings to £203.78, under by 22p, and the £50 is still his. Or, if he paid a neighbour £30 a week to sit with his mother during the extra shifts, that £30 is an allowable care cost, taking him to £198.78. Either way he keeps the allowance and the extra hours. Without one of them, the honest advice is to say no to the shifts.

6. The honest verdict

Carer's Allowance is £86.45 a week and it stops entirely when net earnings pass £204. At the minimum wage that is 16 hours, with 64p to spare, and every extra hour above that costs more than it pays unless a deduction brings you back under.

The deductions are real and underused: half of pension contributions, work travel and equipment, and up to half your earnings in paid care costs. Do the sum on net earnings over your actual pay period, watch the April 2027 minimum wage rise against the limit, and if you are in Scotland or over State Pension age, read a different page, because this one is not yours.

Frequently asked

How much is Carer's Allowance in 2026/27? £86.45 a week, for at least 35 hours a week of care for someone who gets a qualifying disability benefit. Source: GOV.UK, read 5 September 2026.

Is the £204 limit before or after tax? After. GOV.UK says earnings must be £204 or less a week after tax, National Insurance and expenses, including 50% of pension contributions and allowable care costs.

If I earn £210 do I get a reduced amount? No. Carer's Allowance is a cliff edge, not a taper. Over £204 in a pay period means no payment for that period, and any paid weeks can be recovered as an overpayment.

Does it apply in Scotland? No. Scotland has Carer Support Payment, administered by Social Security Scotland with its own earnings rules.
