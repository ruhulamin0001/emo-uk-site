Your Fix Ends This Autumn. Lock A Rate Now Or Wait For The Bank On 17 September? The Standard Variable Rate Is 7.13% And It Settles The Argument.

Hundreds of thousands of households are coming off fixed deals priced under 3% this year, and a lot of them are looking at the 17 September Bank Rate decision and wondering whether to wait. This piece does not predict the vote. It puts numbers on the three things you can actually do: secure a rate now, wait and re-fix after the decision, or let the mortgage roll onto the lender's revert rate while you decide.

1. Where rates stand, from the primary source

Moneyfacts publishes a monthly UK Mortgage Trends Treasury Report, and the August edition, published on 10 August 2026, is the latest we could read from Moneyfacts itself. It recorded the first monthly rise in average fixed rates since April: the average two year fix moved from 5.52% to 5.63%, the five year from 5.52% to 5.66%, and the Moneyfacts Average New Mortgage Rate climbed 0.12 points to 5.59%. Moneyfacts' Rachel Springall attributed the reversal to swap rate volatility after the re-escalation of conflict in the Middle East, with lenders repricing quickly: the average shelf-life of a deal fell to 11 days, against a record low of eight in April.

Two other numbers from the same report matter more than the averages. The average standard variable rate, the rate you revert to when a fix ends and nothing replaces it, held at 7.13%. And Moneyfacts quotes the Bank of England's estimate that nearly 750,000 households on fixes under 3% reach the end of their deal this year, facing rises of about £170 a month on average.

Several comparison sites report Moneyfacts' early September data as a two year average between 5.52% and 5.59% and a five year between 5.63% and 5.64%. We could not confirm those from a Moneyfacts page inside this run, so this article uses the 10 August figures and says so. The direction of the argument below does not change either way.

2. The three choices, priced

Take a £180,000 repayment mortgage with 25 years to run. Our arithmetic, standard annuity formula, no fees:

2.49%, the deal that is ending£807£312 less
5.38%, a two year fix 0.25 points lower£1,092£27 less
5.63%, August average two year fix£1,119
5.66%, August average five year fix£1,122£3 more
7.13%, average standard variable rate£1,287£168 more

Choice one, secure a rate now. You pay £1,119 from the day the old deal ends. Most lenders let you apply up to six months ahead and, crucially, let you drop the offer for a cheaper one before completion. So securing a rate is not a bet against rates falling; it is insurance against them rising, at no cost.

Choice two, wait for 17 September and fix afterwards. If the market has already priced the decision, which it usually has, you fix at roughly the same rate a few weeks later. If lenders cut by 0.25 points more than expected, you save about £27 a month, £645 over a two year deal. If they rise, the reverse. The gain is capped and the loss is not.

Choice three, let it roll onto the SVR while you think. This is the one people do by accident. Every month at 7.13% instead of 5.63% costs about £168 on this loan. Two months of drifting wipes out the entire upside of a correctly guessed quarter-point cut.

3. What 17 September can and cannot do to your rate

Bank Rate is 3.75%. The July vote was 6 to 3, with three members wanting a rise to 4%. Our separate guide on the September decision covers the savings and tracker arithmetic, so the point here is narrower.

Fixed mortgage rates are priced off swap rates, which reflect where the market expects Bank Rate to be over two or five years, not where it is today. Moneyfacts' own explanation of the August rise was swap volatility, not a Bank Rate move, because there was no Bank Rate move. That means the announcement on 17 September changes your fixed rate only to the extent it differs from what swaps already assume. A hold that was expected moves nothing. A surprise either way moves fixes in the following days, and the 11 day shelf-life tells you how fast lenders reprice.

A tracker or SVR is different: it follows Bank Rate directly. If you are sitting on the SVR waiting, a rise to 4% on 17 September would push 7.13% higher, not lower.

4. A worked example in Slough

The Okoro family in Slough have £180,000 left on a mortgage fixed at 2.49% until 31 October 2026, 25 years remaining. Their lender's SVR is 7.13% and its best two year fix for their loan-to-value is 5.63%.

Route A. They apply on 5 September for the 5.63% deal to start on 1 November. Payment goes from £807 to £1,119, a £312 a month rise, which is the real story and no decision changes it. If a 5.38% deal appears after 17 September, they ask to switch to it before completion. Cost of holding the option: nothing.

Route B. They wait for the decision, then apply on 20 September. Their deal still ends on 31 October, and a remortgage typically needs four to six weeks, so they are cutting it fine. If the application slips into November they pay £1,287 for a month, £168 more than Route A, to chase a possible £27.

Route C. They do nothing until the SVR bill lands. £480 more a month than they are used to, and every month of indecision costs £168 against the fix they could have had.

The Okoros' honest risk is not the Bank. It is the calendar.

5. Who should wait, who should not

Do not wait if your deal ends inside three months. Secure a rate, keep the right to switch, and let the Bank do what it does.

Waiting is defensible only if your deal ends after February 2027, because you cannot secure a rate more than six months out anyway, and you lose nothing by watching.

Never wait on the SVR. At 7.13% against 5.63%, the revert rate is the most expensive place to be undecided, and a rate rise on 17 September makes it worse, not better.

And the number to hold onto: on this loan the Bank's quarter point is worth £27 a month. The gap between the deal that ends and the deal that replaces it is £312. Shopping across lenders and using a broker moves the second number. Nothing you do moves the first.

6. The honest verdict

If your fix ends by the end of the year, apply now and keep the right to switch. It is free, and a month on the 7.13% SVR costs more than any plausible cut would save.

The 17 September decision is worth about £27 a month per quarter point on £180,000. The jump from a sub-3% deal is £300 a month. Budget for the second number; do not gamble on the first.

Frequently asked

Can I lock a mortgage rate before my current deal ends? Most lenders let existing customers secure a product transfer rate up to six months before the deal ends, and most allow you to switch to a cheaper deal if one appears before completion. Check your own lender's terms.

Will fixed rates fall if the Bank of England cuts on 17 September? Only if the cut is bigger than the market already expected. Fixes are priced off swap rates, which reflect expectations. Moneyfacts attributed August's rise to swap volatility with no Bank Rate change at all.

What is the standard variable rate? The rate your lender charges when a fixed or tracker deal ends and you have not moved to another. Moneyfacts put the average at 7.13% in August 2026.

Two year or five year fix? In August 2026 the averages were 5.63% and 5.66%, so almost identical. That is a personal choice about certainty versus flexibility, and we are not advisers; a broker can compare your actual quotes.
