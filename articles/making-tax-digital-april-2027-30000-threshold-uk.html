970,000 people join Making Tax Digital in April 2027, and most of them do not know yet

From 6 April 2027, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 must keep digital records and report to HMRC quarterly. The threshold is measured on income, not profit, and that is where people get caught.

The short version

  • From 6 April 2027, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.
  • Around 970,000 people join at this step, on top of the roughly 780,000 with income over £50,000 who joined from April 2026.
  • Qualifying income is turnover, not profit. A landlord with £33,000 of rent and £9,000 of costs is over the threshold, even though the profit is well under it.
  • It is quarterly updates plus digital records, using compatible software. It is not four tax bills a year.
  • The threshold drops again to £20,000 from 6 April 2028, measured on the 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is running now.

1. Who is actually caught from April 2027

The rule is set out in HMRC guidance on GOV.UK, and it is short. From 6 April 2027, if you are a sole trader or a landlord and your qualifying income is over £30,000, you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

HMRC's own published estimate is that around 970,000 people join at this step. That is in addition to roughly 780,000 people with business or property income over £50,000 who were brought in from April 2026.

Note what the test is not based on. It is not based on whether you use an accountant, whether you have a company, or whether your affairs feel complicated. It is based on a single number.

2. The trap: qualifying income is not profit

This is the single most important sentence on this page. Qualifying income is your gross income from self employment and property before you deduct expenses. It is not your taxable profit.

People assume the £30,000 test is about what they earn in the sense of what they keep. It is not. If you are a landlord receiving £33,000 a year in rent, your qualifying income is £33,000, even if mortgage interest, letting fees, insurance and repairs mean your actual profit is a fraction of that.

The same applies if you have more than one source. If you run a small trade with £19,000 of turnover and also let a flat producing £14,000 of rent, your qualifying income is the two added together, which is £33,000. You are over the threshold, despite neither source being anywhere near it on its own.

This is the mechanism that will surprise the most people in April 2027, and it is barely mentioned in most coverage, which tends to say "if you earn over £30,000" and leave it there.

3. What actually changes, and what does not

Two obligations, essentially.

Digital records. You must keep your business and property records digitally, in compatible software, rather than in a shoebox or a handwritten book. A spreadsheet can form part of this if it is linked to compatible software, but a spreadsheet alone is not enough.

Quarterly updates. You send HMRC a summary of your income and expenses four times a year through that software, then finalise the year afterwards.

What does not change is as important. You do not pay tax four times a year. The quarterly updates are information, not payment demands. The payment deadlines you already know, 31 January and 31 July, are not altered by Making Tax Digital. Nor are the tax rates, the allowances, or what counts as an allowable expense.

4. What Making Tax Digital does not do

Worth stating plainly, because a lot of software marketing blurs this.

  • It does not change how much tax you owe.
  • It does not change the 31 January payment deadline.
  • It does not apply to employment income, pensions, dividends or savings interest for the purpose of the threshold test. The test is on self employment and property income.
  • It does not mean HMRC sees your bank account. You submit summary figures from software, as you do now with a tax return.
  • It does not require the most expensive package on the market. HMRC publishes a list of compatible software, and free and low cost options appear on it.

5. How to work out your own qualifying income in five minutes

You do not need software or an accountant to do this test. You need last year's tax return.

Take the turnover figure from your self employment pages, before any expenses. Take the total property income figure from your property pages, again before expenses. Add them together. That total is what the threshold is measured against.

Ignore your employment income, your pension, your dividends and your savings interest for this test. They do not count towards the threshold, even though they may well be on the same tax return.

If the total is comfortably over £30,000, assume you are in from April 2027. If it is close to the line, remember that the test looks at a specific tax year, so a good year can pull you in. There are also exemptions and exclusions set out in the HMRC guidance, including for some trustees and for people HMRC accepts are digitally excluded, so if you think one applies to you, check the guidance page linked below rather than assuming.

6. A worked example: a landlord who thinks he is under the line

Dilip lives in Leicester and lets two flats. He also does occasional consultancy.

His numbers for a year look like this.

  • Rent received on flat one: £13,200
  • Rent received on flat two: £11,400
  • Consultancy invoiced: £7,600

Total qualifying income: £13,200 plus £11,400 plus £7,600, which is £32,200.

His costs across the two flats, including mortgage interest treated under the current rules, letting agent fees, insurance, gas safety checks and a new boiler in one flat, come to about £16,000. So his profit is in the region of £16,200.

Dilip's assumption is that he is comfortably under £30,000 and therefore not affected. He is wrong. The test is on the £32,200, not the £16,200. He is mandated from 6 April 2027.

The practical consequence is that he needs compatible software and a digital record keeping habit in place before the start of the 2027 to 2028 tax year, not in the January after it. Setting that up while there is no deadline pressure costs him an afternoon. Setting it up in a panic costs considerably more.

And if Dilip were well under the line, there would be nothing here for him to do at all. This rule genuinely does not affect most people. It affects a specific group who are easy to identify by one calculation, which is why doing that calculation now is worth five minutes.

Frequently asked

Is the £30,000 based on profit or turnover? Turnover, or more precisely qualifying income, which is gross income from self employment and property before expenses. This is the point most people get wrong.

Do I have to pay tax quarterly under Making Tax Digital? No. The quarterly updates are information submissions. Your payment deadlines of 31 January and 31 July are unchanged.

What if I have two small income sources? They are added together. Two sources of £16,000 each produce qualifying income of £32,000, which is over the threshold.

What happens in 2028? From 6 April 2028 the threshold falls to £20,000, and qualifying income for that test is measured in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is running now.
