Fit An Automation Kit To Your Own Gate And The Law Says You Just Manufactured A Machine

Powered gates are classed as machinery, and the rules that follow from that are the reason a proper installation costs four figures while a kit online costs a few hundred. This is not a trade cartel. It is the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations.

The short version

  • Powered gates are machinery under the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations 2008, which implement the European Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC in UK law.
  • Converting an existing manual gate to power makes the installer the manufacturer, with every manufacturer obligation including conformity marking and a Declaration of Conformity. Fit a kit yourself and that is you.
  • Compliance is demonstrated through a safety assessment, correct safeguarding, and where relevant force testing to BS EN 12445. EN 12453 sets the performance rules for force limits, obstacle detection and safety device response.
  • Typical installed cost runs 2,000 to 8,000 pounds depending on gate weight, swing or slide, groundwork and the safety devices required. The kit is the small part.
  • The payback is not financial and this guide says so plainly. A gate does not pay for itself. Price it as security and convenience, and keep the insurance conversation separate.
  • The honest cheaper option for most driveways is not a gate at all. Lighting, a visible camera and a physical post cost a fraction and address the same risk.

1. Why a gate is a machine

A powered gate is a heavy object that moves under its own power in a place where people and children walk. UK and European law treats it accordingly. The European Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC set the safety requirements for powered gates, and it is transposed into UK law as the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations 2008.

What that means in practice is a set of duties that have nothing to do with how well the gate looks or how smoothly it opens. There has to be a safety assessment. There has to be appropriate safeguarding, meaning the physical measures that stop the gate crushing, shearing or trapping. And where force limitation is the safety measure being relied on, there has to be evidence that the forces are within limits, demonstrated through force testing to BS EN 12445, against the performance rules in EN 12453.

EN 12453 covers force limits, hazard control, safety device response times, obstacle detection and safe operation. It is the standard that decides whether the safety edges and photocells on a gate are adequate or decorative.

2. The sentence that catches DIY installations

Here is the part that the automation kits sold online do not put on the box.

Where installers assemble a powered gate from component parts, including where existing manual gates are converted to power operation, the installer becomes the product manufacturer and takes on all the obligations of a manufacturer, including conformity marking and issuing a Declaration of Conformity.

Read it again with yourself in the role of installer. Bolt a 400 pound motor kit onto the wooden gates you already have, and in law you have manufactured a machine. The obligations that follow, the technical file, the risk assessment, the conformity marking, the declaration, do not evaporate because you did it at a weekend on your own property.

This matters in three practical ways. It is the reason a compliant installation costs several times the kit price. It is what an insurer will ask about if the gate ever injures somebody. And it is what a buyer's solicitor will eventually ask about, because a powered gate with no documentation is a liability attached to the house.

HSE publishes guidance on legal responsibilities for powered gates, and the door and hardware federation publishes the sector guidance installers work to. Both are worth ten minutes before you commit to anything.

4. Worked example: the Sinclair household in Ipswich

A four bedroom detached with a shingle drive, two cars, a van that visits twice a week and a lane that people cut through on foot.

What they were quoted. Automated timber swing gates, 3.6 metre opening, underground motors, safety edges, photocells, intercom and keypad, groundwork and power run from the house. 5,400 pounds, including force testing and documentation. Servicing quoted at 120 pounds a year.

Cost per year over a 15 year life: 5,400 divided by 15 = 360 pounds, plus 120 pounds servicing, so 480 pounds a year.

What they compared it with. Two motion sensor floodlights and a visible camera at the drive entrance, 420 pounds fitted, plus a heavy removable bollard at 180 pounds. Total 600 pounds, about 66 pounds a year over ten years with no subscription.

The gate costs roughly seven times as much per year and does one thing the alternative does not: it physically stops a vehicle leaving with anything.

The Sinclairs bought the lighting and the bollard, and are revisiting the gate in a few years. That is the right order, because the cheap measures address the common risk and the gate addresses the rare one.

One caution on the insurance argument, which is how these are usually sold. Gates rarely reduce a home insurance premium by a meaningful amount, and an insurer is far more interested in door locks, window locks and alarm type. Do not let a quote include an assumed premium saving unless your own insurer has confirmed it in writing.

Frequently asked

Is my existing automated gate legal? If it was installed by a competent firm you should have a Declaration of Conformity and a safety file. If you have never seen either, and particularly if the previous owner fitted it, get it assessed by a qualified installer. Retrofitting safety edges and photocells to an existing gate is far cheaper than replacing it.

Do I need planning permission for gates? Generally not for gates up to one metre high next to a highway used by vehicles, or two metres elsewhere, under permitted development in England. Listed buildings, conservation areas and properties with removed rights are different. Check with your local planning authority before ordering.

How often should an automated gate be serviced? Annually as a minimum, and the service should include re checking the force settings, not just greasing the hinges. Forces drift as gates settle and as motors wear.

What about a gate on a shared or communal drive? That moves you into workplace and public access territory, where the duties are stricter again and the Workplace Regulations can apply. It is not a domestic job and should not be priced as one.

The honest verdict

Do not fit an automation kit to an existing manual gate yourself. Not because the wiring is hard, but because the law makes you the manufacturer of a machine, and that is a liability you cannot insure your way out of afterwards.

If you want an automated gate, buy it from a firm that hands you a Declaration of Conformity and a safety file, and treat any quote that does not mention force testing as incomplete.

And be honest about what you are buying. On the Sinclairs' numbers, lighting and a post cost 66 pounds a year against 480 for the gate, and address the risk that actually happens. A gate is a fine thing to want. It is not a saving.
