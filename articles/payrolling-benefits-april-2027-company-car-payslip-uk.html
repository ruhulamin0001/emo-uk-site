From 6 April 2027 Your Company Car And Health Cover Are Taxed On The Payslip, Not The P11D. What Actually Changes In Your Pay Packet.

Millions of employees get something from work that is not cash: a car, fuel, private medical cover. Today the tax on it is usually collected a year late through a tax code, after a P11D form in July. From 6 April 2027 the biggest of those benefits must be put through payroll every month like salary. HMRC confirmed the phased timetable on 13 July 2026. Here is what it means on the payslip, and the one check that stops you paying twice.

1. How benefits are taxed now, and why it lags a year

A benefit in kind is taxed as if it were pay. A company car is valued at a percentage of its list price set by its CO2 emissions; private medical insurance is valued at what it costs the employer. The tax on that value is due from you at your marginal rate.

The collection has always been the odd part. Under the PAYE Regulations, an employer reports each benefit on a P11D form after the tax year ends, by 6 July. HMRC then adjusts your tax code to collect the tax on next year's pay, and often to collect an estimate of the current year at the same time. The result is that a car you got in May 2025 might first affect your take-home pay in the summer of 2026, and a car you hand back keeps being taxed until the code catches up. Employers have been able to put benefits through payroll voluntarily since 2016, and many large ones do, but it was optional.

2. What moves in April 2027, what waits, what is left out

The measure was first announced in January 2024, delayed on 28 April 2025 to give employers more time, and confirmed in phased form in HMRC's policy paper of 13 July 2026. The timetable:

FromBenefits that must go through payroll
6 April 2027Company cars, vans, car fuel, van fuel, employer-provided medical benefit
6 April 2028Most remaining benefits in kind and taxable expenses
Not yet mandatedEmployer-provided loans, living accommodation, which stay on the P11D route until brought in later

"Must go through payroll" means the employer works out the taxable value each pay period, adds it to your taxable pay in the Real Time Information submission to HMRC, deducts the income tax, and pays the Class 1A National Insurance on it in real time too. The P11D for those benefits disappears.

The paper is explicit that the aim is timing and accuracy, not more tax. The benefit values, the CO2 percentages and the rates are set separately and are not changed by this measure.

3. What it looks like on the payslip

Your gross salary does not change and the benefit is not paid to you as cash. What appears is a notional amount added to taxable pay for the period, sometimes labelled "payrolled benefit" or similar, on which tax is calculated, and then removed again before the net figure so you are not paid it. Your net pay falls by the tax on the benefit, spread evenly across the year.

Two consequences. First, take-home pay is lower from the first month of a benefit and higher from the first month after it ends, which is the whole point. Second, your tax code should no longer contain a deduction for that benefit, because the tax is being collected directly. If the code still does, you are being charged twice, and that is the year one problem in section 5.

Employees do not pay National Insurance on benefits in kind, and that does not change. The Class 1A charge is the employer's, though HMRC's impact note acknowledges that employers' costs feed through to pay over time.

4. A worked example in Coventry

Dev in Coventry is a field engineer on £38,000 with a petrol company car. The list price was £30,000 and its CO2 band gives an appropriate percentage of 30%, so the taxable benefit is £9,000 a year. His employer also pays £720 a year for private medical cover. Total benefits: £9,720. He is a basic rate taxpayer, so the tax due is 20% of £9,720, which is £1,944 a year, £162 a month.

Today. His tax code carries a deduction of £9,720, reducing his personal allowance from £12,570 to £2,850, so about £162 a month extra tax comes out of his salary. The code is set on last year's P11D, so if the car changes, the deduction is wrong until HMRC updates it, and a P800 reconciliation letter can follow.

From April 2027. Each month £810 of car and medical benefit is added to his taxable pay, £162 of tax is deducted on it, and the £810 is removed again before net pay. Same £162. His tax code should return to the standard 1257L, personal allowance intact, because nothing is being collected through it any more.

The trap. If his April 2027 coding notice still shows the £9,720 deduction, he pays £162 through the code and £162 through payroll: £324 a month, until it is corrected. It is refundable, but it is a real hole in take-home pay while it lasts.

5. The tax code check that matters in April 2027

Three things, in order.

First, when the P60 and any last P11D arrive in mid-2027 for the 2026 to 2027 year, keep them. The year one transition is where old-method and new-method figures meet.

Second, when the 2027 to 2028 coding notice arrives, usually February or March 2027, look for a deduction labelled car benefit, fuel benefit or medical insurance. If your employer is payrolling those from April, that deduction should be gone. If it is still there, tell HMRC through the personal tax account or the app; it is a two minute correction.

Third, look at the first payslip after 6 April 2027 for a payrolled benefit line. If it is there and the code is clean, you are done. If it is not there, ask payroll whether the benefit is being reported through RTI as the rules now require, because the alternative is that the tax has not been collected at all and a bill arrives later.

Employers who already payroll benefits voluntarily, and their staff, see no change at all in April 2027.

6. The honest verdict

The tax on your company car or health cover is not rising in April 2027. It moves from a year-late tax code adjustment to a monthly payroll deduction of the same amount.

The only money at risk is a double deduction in year one, if your tax code keeps a benefit deduction while payroll starts taxing the same thing. Check the coding notice in spring 2027.

Loans and accommodation stay on the old route; most other benefits follow in April 2028. Nothing here needs any action before next spring.

Frequently asked

Will I pay more tax on my company car from April 2027? No. The taxable value and your rate are unchanged. The tax is collected monthly through payroll instead of through a tax code adjustment based on last year's P11D.

Which benefits are affected in 2027? Company cars, vans, car and van fuel, and employer-provided medical benefit, from 6 April 2027. Most other benefits follow from 6 April 2028. Loans and living accommodation are not yet mandated.

Do I pay National Insurance on the benefit? No. Employees do not pay NI on benefits in kind. The employer's Class 1A NI moves to real time along with the tax.

My employer already payrolls benefits. Does anything change? Not for you. Voluntary payrolling has existed since 2016 and works the same way. The 2027 change makes it compulsory for the listed benefits.
