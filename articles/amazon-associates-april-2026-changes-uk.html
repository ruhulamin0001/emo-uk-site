Amazon Quietly Rewrote The Associates Agreement On 14 April. Four Changes Are In The Document. The Rate Cuts Everyone Talks About Are Not.

Search for what changed at Amazon Associates in 2026 and you will find dozens of posts about commission cuts of up to 50%. Open Amazon UK's own changelog for the Operating Agreement effective 14 April 2026 and you will find no rate table at all. What is there is four rule changes, each of which quietly moves money away from certain kinds of affiliate. Here is what the document says, what it does not, and what each change does to a UK site's income.

The short version

  • Amazon UK's changelog lists four changes effective 14 April 2026. Source: Associates Programme Operating Agreement, What's Changed, affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk.
  • Change 1: a 180-day limit. A product must be shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer within 180 days to qualify for commission.
  • Change 2: paid-ad referrals are disqualified. Purchases by customers who reached Amazon through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon no longer qualify, whatever keywords were used. Limited exceptions apply.
  • Change 3: onsite commission applies only to Direct Qualifying Purchases of the same ASIN variant as the linked product page. The so-called halo on related purchases is gone for onsite content.
  • Change 4: original content must contain commentary, analysis or transformation that adds value. A definition, for the first time.
  • Not in the document: any commission rate change. The reported cuts came from publisher conversations reported by Adweek and others. They may be real for those publishers. They are not in Amazon's published agreement.

1. What the document actually says

Amazon publishes a comparison page in Associates Central headed "Associates Program Operating Agreement, What's Changed", listing every update to the Operating Agreement and Program Policies effective 14 April 2026. We read the UK version on 3 September 2026. In full, the changes are these.

SectionChange
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesAdded a 180-day time limit: products must be shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer to qualify for commission
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesExpanded disqualified purchases to include products bought by customers referred through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon, regardless of whether prohibited keywords are used. Limited exceptions apply
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesOnsite commission income now applies only to Direct Qualifying Purchases of the same ASIN variant as the linked product detail page
Participation RequirementsOriginal Content Definition added: original content must contain commentary, analysis or transformation for additional value
Amazon Influencer Program RegistrationCompleting registration gives access to a Storefront and a Unique Creator Link, not only to Influencer Program participants

That is the whole changelog. No rate table, no category percentages, no mention of bonuses. Hold that thought for section 3.

2. What each change does to your income

The 180-day limit mostly affects pre-orders and backordered items. A reader who pre-orders a console or a book through your link, with a release date more than six months out, generates nothing. Before April, a qualifying purchase was a qualifying purchase whenever it shipped. For most UK content sites this is small; for anyone covering pre-orders it removes a line of income.

Paid-ad disqualification is the big one for a certain kind of affiliate. If you run Google, Meta or TikTok adverts that send traffic to Amazon, directly or via a landing page, purchases from those visitors no longer qualify, whatever keywords you used. The old rule banned specific keywords; the new one is about the traffic source. Paid-to-Amazon arbitrage, as a business model, is over on Associates. Organic content sites are unaffected.

The onsite ASIN restriction concerns onsite commissions, meaning content that appears on Amazon itself through the Influencer and Creator programmes: shoppable videos and photos on product pages. Previously, onsite content earned on related purchases in the same category. Now it earns only when the customer buys the exact ASIN variant the content is attached to. If your video is on the blue kettle and they buy the red one, nothing. This is what the trade press calls the end of the halo bonus. It does not change how ordinary offsite affiliate links from your own website are credited.

The original content definition gives Amazon a written standard for rejecting or closing accounts whose content is scraped, spun or a bare list of links. Commentary, analysis or transformation is the test. A UK site publishing honest reviews with its own testing or arithmetic passes it. A page that copies Amazon's product description under a heading does not.

3. The rate cuts: reported, not published

This is where the coverage and the document part ways. From April 2026 onward, publishers told Adweek and other trade outlets that Amazon had cut their commission rates, in some categories by as much as half, with premium categories falling from around 10% to 4 or 5%, and that long-standing publishers had kept better terms than newer ones. Milestone bonuses were reported to have gone at the same time.

None of that appears in the 14 April changelog, because commission rates are not part of the Operating Agreement text. They sit in a separate Commission Income Statement and, for larger publishers, in individually negotiated terms. So two things can both be true: the published agreement contains no rate change, and specific publishers have had their rates cut.

What that means for a UK affiliate is simple. Do not take a rate from a blog post, ours included. Log in to Associates Central and read your own Commission Income Statement, because it is the only rate that applies to you. If it has changed since April, that is your cut. If it has not, the headlines were about someone else. 4. A worked example: Priyanka's kitchen site in Bolton

Priyanka runs a small kitchen equipment review site from Bolton. She earns through ordinary Associates links on her own pages and has a modest Influencer storefront with a handful of shoppable videos on Amazon.

Her website links: unchanged. Every one of the four changes leaves offsite, organic, original-content links exactly as they were. Her reviews contain her own testing, so the original content definition is a formality.

Her onsite videos: cut. Her most watched video sits on a particular 4.5 litre air fryer. In March, a viewer who watched it and bought the 6 litre version from the same range earned her a commission. From 14 April, only the exact variant on that product page counts. If a fifth of her onsite sales were related-variant purchases, a fifth of that income has gone, and she would see it as a fall in onsite earnings with no change in views.

The paid-ad experiment she was planning: cancelled. She had budgeted £200 to test Meta adverts pointing at her best review page, hoping the Amazon commission would cover the spend. Whether that would have counted is now doubtful; the rule disqualifies purchases by customers referred through any paid advertisement linking to Amazon, and a landing page that then links to Amazon is exactly the grey area the "limited exceptions" language does not clarify. The honest position is that she cannot rely on it, so the £200 stays in the bank.

5. What a UK affiliate should do this month

Three actions, all free. Read your own Commission Income Statement in Associates Central and note the rates by category; that is your real rate card. If you run any paid traffic that ends at Amazon, stop, or accept that it may earn nothing. And if you have onsite content, check which ASIN each piece is attached to, because that is now the only ASIN it can earn on.

The longer view: every one of the four changes rewards original content on your own site sending organic readers to a specific product. Amazon has just written that model into its agreement.

6. The honest verdict

If you write original reviews on your own site and link to Amazon, the 14 April changes did not touch you. Check your own rate card anyway; the reported cuts were real for some publishers and Amazon does not publish them.

If you buy traffic to send to Amazon, that model is finished on Associates. Purchases from paid referrals no longer qualify, whatever keywords you use.

If you make onsite videos, you now earn only on the exact variant you tag. Tag the variant people actually buy. There is nothing to purchase here; the agreement is free to read and we have linked it.

Frequently asked

What changed in the Amazon Associates agreement in April 2026? Amazon UK's changelog for 14 April 2026 lists a 180-day shipping and payment limit for qualifying purchases, disqualification of purchases referred through paid or boosted adverts, onsite commission restricted to the exact ASIN variant linked, a new original content definition, and a registration change for Storefront access.

Did Amazon cut commission rates? Not in the published agreement. Rate cuts of up to 50% in some categories were reported by Adweek and others based on what publishers said about their own accounts. Rates sit in the Commission Income Statement and in negotiated terms, so check yours in Associates Central.

Does the ASIN change affect my website links? No. It applies to onsite commission, meaning content that appears on Amazon itself through the Influencer and Creator programmes. Ordinary affiliate links from your own site are credited as before.

Can I still run adverts that link to Amazon? You can, but since 14 April 2026 purchases by customers referred through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon do not qualify for commission, regardless of keywords. Amazon says limited exceptions apply but does not list them on the changelog.
