How to Ask for a Pay Rise in the UK (and Actually Get One)

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

A pay rise is often the fastest way to more money, faster than any side hustle, yet most people never ask. They wait, hope, and feel resentful when nothing changes. Here is how to ask properly in a UK workplace, calmly and with a real chance of yes.

Why asking beats waiting

Pay rises rarely arrive on their own. Employers budget carefully and quiet, reliable staff are the easiest to leave on the same salary. Asking, with a clear case, moves you from forgotten to considered. The worst realistic outcome is a no, which leaves you exactly where you are now.

Time it well

Ask after a clear win, when you have just delivered something that mattered. Ask before budgets are set for the year, not after, when the money is already allocated. Avoid asking in a bad week for the business or your manager.

A performance review is a natural moment, but you do not have to wait for one. A short, booked meeting to discuss your role and pay is completely reasonable.

Build the case on value, not need

Your rent going up is not your employer's problem, and it will not move them. What moves them is evidence that you deliver more than your current pay reflects.

Write down what you have done since your last rise: results, extra responsibility, problems solved, money made or saved for the business. Turn it into three or four concrete points. That is your case.

Bring a real number

Vague requests get vague answers. Research what your role pays elsewhere in the UK, using salary guides and job adverts for similar positions. Come with a specific figure or range, justified by your value and the market. Anchoring to a number makes it a negotiation rather than a wish.

How to actually say it

  1. Book a short meeting rather than ambushing your manager in a corridor.
  2. State plainly that you would like to discuss your salary, and why you believe a rise is fair.
  3. Give your three or four points of evidence, then your number, then stop talking and let them respond.
  4. Stay calm and professional whatever they say. This is a normal business conversation, not a confrontation.

If they say no

A no is information, not the end. Ask what specifically would need to change for a yes, and by when. Get it in concrete terms, agree a date to revisit, and hold them to it.

If the answer is a firm no with no path forward, that tells you something too. Sometimes the fastest rise is a new job, and knowing your market value, as in our rate setting guide, puts you in a strong position either way.

The honest bottom line

Asking for a pay rise is a normal, professional thing to do, and the people who ask, with evidence and a number, are the people who get them. Prepare your case, time it well, name a figure, and stay calm. Pair this with a strong UK CV so you always have options.
