Most People Buy Zapier Before They Need It. Here Is How To Tell When You Actually Do

Zapier gives you 100 tasks a month for nothing, and a long list of steps that use no tasks at all. Used properly, that free tier covers far more of a one person business than the pricing page suggests. The question is not which plan to buy. It is which three jobs are worth handing over, and which one you should keep doing yourself forever.

1. What a task actually is, and what Zapier does not charge for

Nearly every guide to Zapier gets the pricing wrong in the same direction, by assuming that everything a Zap does costs you something. It does not, and the gap between what people assume and what Zapier actually charges is the difference between the free plan being useless and being enough.

Zapier's own definition: a task is used when a Zap successfully moves data or completes an action. Three things follow from that word, and Zapier states all three on its pricing page.

  • Triggers do not count. The event that starts the Zap is free.
  • Checking for new data does not count. Zapier polling your inbox every fifteen minutes, all month, costs nothing. In Zapier's own example, watching a Dropbox folder is free and only the Slack message that results is a task.
  • A failed action does not count. If the action does not complete, it does not come off your limit.

Then there is the list that matters most, and it is the one almost nobody quotes. Zapier says these steps do not count as a task: Zapier Tables, Zapier Forms, Filter by Zapier, Formatter by Zapier, Paths by Zapier, Delay, Looping, Sub-Zap, Digest, Zapier Manager and Storage.

Read that list again with a one person business in mind. Filtering, reformatting a date, splitting a name, branching down two routes, waiting three days, holding a value between runs: all free. What you pay for is the moment something lands in another app.

The practical consequence is a rule of thumb: put your filter as early in the Zap as you can. If a Zap fires on every email you receive but only 30 in 100 are invoices, a filter in position two means you pay for 30 runs rather than 100. Same Zap, same result, a third of the cost. On the free plan that is the difference between running out on the 9th of the month and not running out at all.

One thing does cost double, and it is worth knowing before you build around it. Zapier states that one Zapier MCP tool call uses two tasks. If you are wiring an AI assistant into your apps through MCP, budget accordingly.

2. The three jobs worth automating first

The test for whether a job belongs to a machine is not how annoying it is. It is whether the job has a fixed shape. If the same inputs always produce the same output, automate it. If you have ever paused over it, do not.

1. Getting paid, and filing the record at the same moment. When a payment lands, you want three things to happen: the invoice marked paid, a receipt sent, and a row written somewhere you can find in January. That is two or three tasks per payment. At twenty payments a month, that is 40 to 60 tasks, comfortably inside the free 100. The reason this one goes first is not the time saved, it is that the record gets written at the moment of the event rather than from memory four months later.

There is a deadline behind this one. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax reaches sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 in April 2027, and the qualifying figure is measured on an earlier tax year. Quarterly updates are much easier when the underlying records wrote themselves as they happened. Our guide to the April 2027 MTD threshold has the rules.

2. Getting every enquiry into one place. Most one person businesses lose work not through poor service but through an enquiry arriving on a channel nobody checked on Friday. A Zap that takes a form submission, writes it to one table and sends you one notification is two tasks per enquiry, and Zapier Forms and Zapier Tables both cost nothing to run. If you use Zapier's own form and its own table, a lead capture pipeline can genuinely cost zero tasks until the notification step.

3. Receipts into a folder your accountant can reach. Purchase receipt arrives by email, attachment goes to a dated folder, a row records supplier, amount and date. Two tasks. Put a filter first so it only fires on emails from your actual suppliers and you will pay for perhaps 15 runs a month rather than 400.

What these three share: no judgement, high frequency, and a real cost when they are skipped. That is the whole specification.

3. The one that is never worth automating

Anything a customer reads. Not the notification that tells you a customer wrote. The reply itself.

This is worth being blunt about, because it is sold hard and it is where the automation pitch stops being about admin. A one person business competes on being a person. The reason someone chooses a sole trader over a company with a support queue is that they get you. An automated first reply, however well written, is the one thing that removes the only advantage you have.

There is a narrower version of the same mistake: automating anything that needs you to make a decision on price, tone or timing. Chasing a late payer is the classic. The message that works on a good customer who has forgotten is not the message that works on a client who is stringing you along, and no rule you can write in a Zap builder knows which one you are dealing with. Send the reminder yourself. If you want the automation to help, have it tell you who is late and leave the writing to you.

There is also a quieter case where automation simply does not pay, and it has nothing to do with judgement. If you do the job less than about once a week, the build time will not come back. A Zap that takes an hour to build and test, saving you two minutes a month, needs thirty months to break even, and Zapier will have changed twice by then.

4. What it costs, and the bit the pricing page makes hard

Zapier's pricing page, checked in September 2026, lists four tiers: Free at 0 dollars with 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps, Professional starting from 19.99 dollars a month with multi-step Zaps, unlimited premium apps and webhooks, Team starting from 69 dollars a month for up to 25 users, and Enterprise on application.

Two things about those figures deserve saying plainly rather than being quietly rounded off, because both affect what you actually pay.

The page has a monthly and yearly toggle, and yearly is advertised as saving 33%. The headline figure changes with the toggle, so the number you see first is not necessarily the number you would pay monthly. Look at which side the toggle is on before you commit a budget.

Zapier prices in dollars by default but offers a currency selector including pounds. A pound price set by Zapier is not the same as today's exchange rate applied to the dollar price, and it will not move when the rate does. Switch the selector to GBP and read the pound figure rather than converting in your head.

Three more details from the same page that are easy to walk into. The Free plan polls every 15 minutes and Professional every 2, so a free Zap is not instant. There is a 14 day free trial of Professional on every new account with no card required, which is long enough to find out whether you needed it. And when you hit your task limit, Zapier switches you to pay-per-task billing at a higher rate than your subscription tasks unless you turn that off, in which case your Zaps pause until the next period. Neither is a trap, but one of them costs money silently and the other stops your business quietly, so choose deliberately.

Registered charities get 15% off paid plans under Zapier's non-profit programme.

5. Worked example: Farhana in Luton, 14 hours a year

Farhana runs a one person bookkeeping practice from home in Luton. She has 22 clients, sends about 24 invoices a month, and gets roughly 6 new enquiries a month through a form on her site.

What she automates. Payment received, so mark the invoice paid and write a row to a table: 2 tasks per payment. Enquiry form submitted, so write to the same table and send one notification: the form and table steps are free, so 1 task per enquiry. Supplier receipt arrives, filtered to her six actual suppliers, so file the attachment and log the amount: 2 tasks, and the filter itself is free.

The monthly task count. 24 payments at 2 tasks is 48. Six enquiries at 1 task is 6. She receives around 90 emails a month from suppliers and elsewhere, but the filter means only about 14 are genuine receipts, at 2 tasks each, which is 28. Total: 48 plus 6 plus 28 equals 82 tasks a month, inside the free 100 with room to spare.

Without the filter on the receipts Zap, those 90 emails would each cost 2 tasks, or 180 tasks, and the whole thing would be 234 a month. One free step is the difference between paying nothing and needing a paid tier.

The time. Marking invoices paid and writing the record took her about 90 seconds each, so 24 a month is 36 minutes. Filing receipts by hand was roughly 45 minutes a month. Chasing enquiries across three inboxes, perhaps 15 minutes. That is about 1 hour 36 minutes a month, or a bit over 19 hours a year. Allowing that the automations still need occasional attention, call the real saving 14 hours.

Fourteen hours a year, for nothing, on the free plan. That is the honest version of the Zapier pitch, and it is smaller than the marketing and larger than the sceptics. What it is not is a reason to buy a subscription. Farhana's usage says she should stay on Free until either her volume roughly doubles or she needs a Zap with more than two steps.

The honest verdict

For most one person businesses in the UK, the honest answer is that Zapier's free plan is enough, and will stay enough for longer than you expect. The 100 task limit sounds tiny until you learn that triggers, polling, filters, Formatter, Paths, Delay, Digest, Tables and Forms all cost nothing. Build the filter in first and the free tier stretches a long way.

The three jobs worth handing over are getting paid and filed, catching every enquiry in one place, and putting receipts where they can be found. All three have a fixed shape, all three cost you real money when they slip, and none of them needs you to think. With April 2027 and Making Tax Digital coming for sole traders over £30,000, the record keeping one has a deadline attached.

The one to keep is anything a customer reads. It is the only part of a one person business that a competitor with a bigger budget cannot copy, and handing it to a rule engine to save four minutes is the worst trade in this article.

If you are not sure, run it on Free for two months and look at your task count. If you are nowhere near 100, you have your answer and it costs you nothing.

Frequently asked

Is the free plan really usable, or is it a trial? It is a permanent plan, not a trial. The limits that bite are 100 tasks a month, two-step Zaps and 15 minute polling. The two-step limit is the one that usually forces an upgrade, not the task count, because a trigger plus one action is often not enough to be useful. Note that a separate 14 day Professional trial also runs on new accounts.

Do I need Zapier at all if I only use two apps? Often not. Many pairs of apps now connect directly, and a direct integration costs nothing and breaks less. Check whether your accounting software already talks to your bank and your invoicing tool before adding a third party in the middle.

What happens to my Zaps if I stop paying? Your account drops to Free. Anything relying on multi-step Zaps, premium apps or webhooks stops working. If you build your business on a paid tier, you are on that tier permanently, so price it as a fixed annual cost rather than a one-off.

Does an automated bookkeeping record satisfy HMRC? A digital record created automatically is still a digital record, and for Making Tax Digital that is the point. What matters is that it is complete, accurate and kept for the required period. Automation makes records more consistent, not automatically compliant, and the responsibility stays yours. Check the current requirements on GOV.UK.
