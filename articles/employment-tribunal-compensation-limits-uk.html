The Unfair Dismissal Cap Is £123,543. For Dismissals From January It Goes

Almost every article about tribunal compensation quotes one big number and stops. The number matters far less than three things nobody puts next to it: what the cap is actually the lower of, which claims it never applied to, and the two dates in the next four months that change the whole picture.

The short version

  • The unfair dismissal compensatory award is capped at £123,543, or 52 weeks' pay, whichever is LOWER. Set by the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026, SI 2026/310, made 16 March 2026 and in force 6 April 2026. For most people the 52 week figure bites first.

1. The two numbers, and which one actually bites

An unfair dismissal award has two parts, and only one is capped by the headline figure.

The basic award works like statutory redundancy pay, built from your age, length of service and a week's pay. A week's pay is capped at £751 from 6 April 2026, up from £719. Earn £1,400 a week and the calculation still uses £751.

The compensatory award covers your actual and projected financial loss, mainly lost earnings and lost pension. This is the one carrying the big number, and the wording matters: it is capped at the lower of £123,543 or 52 weeks' gross pay.

For anyone earning under about £123,500 a year, the cap that actually applies is 52 weeks of their own pay, not the headline figure.

That single word, lower, is what most coverage leaves out. On a £32,000 salary the ceiling is roughly £32,000. The larger number binds only those earning above it.

Both figures come from the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026, SI 2026/310, made 16 March 2026 and in force 6 April 2026. It revoked the 2025 order and raised the sums by 4.5%, the RPI increase between September 2024 and September 2025. This is not discretionary: section 34 of the Employment Relations Act 1999 requires indexation by that formula.

The same order lifted less famous limits too: the minimum basic award where a dismissal is automatically unfair rose from £8,763 to £9,157, the daily guarantee payment from £39 to £41, and the two tipping limits from £5,135 to £5,366.

2. What the cap does not apply to

This matters more than the figures. It is where people talk themselves out of a claim.

Discrimination claims are not subject to the unfair dismissal cap. A claim under the Equality Act 2010, for discrimination because of a protected characteristic such as age, disability, race, sex, religion, sexual orientation, pregnancy or maternity, is a different claim with a different remedy. It has no statutory ceiling, and it can include an award for injury to feelings, which unfair dismissal cannot.

Dismissal for whistleblowing is not capped. Where the reason for dismissal is that the employee made a protected disclosure, the compensatory award is uncapped. Dismissal for health and safety reasons is not capped in the equivalent circumstances.

Two cautions, stated as plainly as the rights. Uncapped does not mean unlimited: a tribunal awards what you evidence you have lost, so removing a ceiling does not raise the floor. And you can be dismissed unfairly and recover little, because finding a similar job quickly makes the loss small. A tribunal can also reduce an award for contributory conduct, or adjust it where the Acas Code was unreasonably ignored by either side.

3. Two dates in the next four months

The GOV.UK timetable for the Employment Rights Act 2025, updated 25 August 2026, puts two changes close enough to matter now.

1 October 2026: the time limit doubles. The limit for bringing a tribunal claim rises from 3 months to 6 months. For breach of contract claims in Scotland it changes on 9 November 2026. This is the most useful change for ordinary claimants, because the three month limit is strict and has always caught people who spent too long on an internal appeal.

January 2027: the cap is due to go. The timetable lists, for January 2027, the unfair dismissal qualifying period dropping to 6 months for dismissals from 1 January 2027, and uncapping compensatory awards. Fire and rehire protections sit at the same point.

Two honest qualifications. GOV.UK states that all future dates remain subject to parliamentary processes and may change, so this is a published intention, not a date to bank a decision on. And uncapping changes the ceiling, not the method: awards are still calculated on losses the claimant must evidence.

One more, often confused with the above. The Increase of Limits Order has a transitional rule: the new figures apply where the relevant event, for unfair dismissal the effective date of termination, falls on or after 6 April 2026. Dismissed before that, and the 2025 figures govern your claim.

4. Worked example: Dev in Coventry

Dev is 41, has 9 complete years with the same Coventry employer and earns £38,000, about £731 a week gross. He is dismissed in June 2026, so the 6 April 2026 figures apply.

Basic award. His £731 weekly pay is below the £751 cap, so the cap does not bite and £731 is used. The calculation then runs on his service and age bands rather than a flat multiplier.

Compensatory award ceiling. 52 weeks of gross pay is £38,012. That is far below £123,543, so £38,012 is his cap, not the headline number.

What he might recover. He finds a job 5 months later on £34,000. His loss is roughly 5 months of net pay, the £4,000 annual shortfall going forward, and lost pension. That lands in the low tens of thousands, well under his own ceiling.

Now change one fact. If Dev's evidence is that he was dismissed for raising a protected disclosure about safety reporting, the award is not capped at all and the £38,012 ceiling disappears. Why he was dismissed moves the ceiling far more than his salary does. And the date matters twice: dismissed in June 2026 he had 3 months to start early conciliation, while someone dismissed on 2 October 2026 will have 6.

The honest verdict

If you are weighing up a claim, stop looking at £123,543. It is almost certainly not your number. Work out 52 weeks of your own gross pay, then what you have actually lost. The smaller of the two is the realistic shape of a compensatory award.

Then ask the question that moves the figure most: is this only unfair dismissal, or is there a discrimination or whistleblowing element. Those are uncapped and carry remedies unfair dismissal does not.

Whatever you decide, the date cannot be fixed later. Contact Acas early conciliation before the limit runs, because that is what pauses the clock. Acas is free and it is a required step before most tribunal claims.

Frequently asked

Do these limits apply in Northern Ireland? No. SI 2026/310 extends to England, Wales and Scotland only. Northern Ireland has its own order and figures.

Does the cap include compensation for how I was treated? Not in an unfair dismissal claim. The compensatory award covers financial loss. An award for injury to feelings is available in discrimination claims, which is one of several reasons the two are worth telling apart early.
