Starting a Car Detailing Side Hustle in the UK

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Car cleaning is one of the lowest cost side hustles to start and one of the easiest to get wrong. The kit is cheap, the demand is steady, and the profit per hour can be good. But there are rules about water and waste that new starters forget, and forgetting them can cost you. Here is the real picture.

Detailing is not just a wash

A basic wash competes with the cheap hand car washes and pays little. Detailing, a deeper clean of paintwork, interior, wheels and trim to a high finish, is where the money is. Customers pay far more for a car that looks close to new than for a quick rinse. Aim for the detail, not the splash.

The starter kit

  • A pressure washer, a couple of buckets, and quality wash mitts.
  • Interior tools: a vacuum, brushes, and microfibre cloths.
  • Good products: shampoo, wheel cleaner, interior cleaner, a wax or sealant.
  • A safe way to carry water if you travel to customers.

You can start for a few hundred pounds, far less than most hustles, and add better kit from your first profits.

The rules people forget

Waste water is the big one. The dirty water from washing a car, full of chemicals and grime, is not allowed to run into street drains, which flow to rivers. You must control your run off, especially if you clean at customers' homes or on the road. Councils take this seriously.

If you work at people's homes you also need to think about their water and electricity use, and your own insurance in case you damage a vehicle. Public liability cover is cheap and essential the moment you touch someone else's car for money.

What a job realistically pays

A full detail takes a few hours and can pay a good hourly rate once you are quick and confident. The first few will be slow and feel underpaid while you learn. Speed and a tight routine are what turn it from a chore into real money per hour.

Regular customers are the goal. One car cleaned monthly is worth far more than chasing a new stranger every time. Treat the first job as the start of a repeat booking.

Finding your first customers

  1. Do a friend or family car brilliantly and photograph the before and after.
  2. Post those photos in local community groups, where this kind of work spreads fast.
  3. Offer a small first time discount to fill your first weekend and build reviews.
  4. Ask every happy customer for a monthly slot and a recommendation.

The tax side

This is trading income. Once total earnings pass £1,000 in a tax year, register for Self Assessment, as covered in our side hustle tax guide. Your products, fuel and kit are expenses that lower your tax. Keep receipts from day one.

The honest bottom line

Car detailing is a genuinely accessible UK side hustle with low startup cost and steady demand. The winners are quick, consistent, insured, and careful with waste water. Get those right and repeat customers can build it into a reliable second income.
