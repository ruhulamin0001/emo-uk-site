1. Valeting, detailing, and why the difference matters

These get used interchangeably and they are different businesses with different customers and different margins.

Valeting is cleaning. Wash, wheels, interior vacuum, glass, dress the trim. One to two hours. The customer wants a clean car.

Detailing is correction. Decontamination, clay bar, machine polishing to remove swirl marks and light scratches, then a sealant or ceramic coating. Six to ten hours. The customer wants the paint to look better than it did.

Most people start by calling themselves a detailer, charging valeting prices, and doing valeting work. Decide which you are. The valeting customer wants speed and price. The detailing customer wants evidence you know what you are doing and will pay five times as much for it.

The sensible path is to start as a valet, build a round of repeat customers, and add detailing once you have practised machine polishing on your own car and a few friends' cars. Machine polishing badly burns through the lacquer, and that is a respray, not a refund.

3. The kit, in the order you should buy it

Stage one, about £250, enough to take money.

  • Two buckets with grit guards, and a wash mitt. The two bucket method is the difference between cleaning a car and scratching it
  • pH neutral shampoo, wheel cleaner, iron fallout remover, glass cleaner, interior detailer, tyre dressing
  • A stack of microfibre cloths and two large drying towels. Buy more than you think, they get filthy
  • A decent wet and dry vacuum with a crevice tool
  • A pressure washer, entry level is fine to start
  • Kneeling mat, detailing brushes, an applicator set

Stage two, once you are booked out. A snow foam lance, a water tank and 12V pump so you are not dependent on the customer's tap, and a decent extractor for interiors.

Stage three, only after practice. A dual action polisher, a pad set and compounds. A DA is far more forgiving than a rotary and is what you should learn on.

You can put a starter kit together from Amazon UK, though for chemicals the specialist UK detailing suppliers are usually better value per litre and sell in 5L containers. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What not to buy in month one: a ceramic coating kit, a rotary polisher, a branded uniform, or a van wrap. All of those are things people buy instead of finding customers.

4. Water, power and the weather problem

This is the part that quietly kills mobile detailing businesses and nobody writes about it.

Water. Many customers will not want you using their outside tap, and plenty of flats and terraces do not have one. A 100 to 200 litre tank in the back of the van with a 12V pump removes that objection entirely and lets you say yes to jobs your competitors turn down. It is the single best equipment purchase after the basics.

Power. Same problem with extension leads through a window. A leisure battery and an inverter, or a petrol pressure washer, makes you independent.

Weather. This is the real one. In a British winter you will lose a meaningful number of working days to rain, and detailing in the cold is miserable and produces worse results because products behave differently below about 10 degrees. Plan for it:

  • Push interior work in winter. It is weather independent and it pays well.
  • Build a rain policy into your booking messages so cancellations are not awkward.
  • If you can access a garage, a unit or even a carport, your winter income roughly doubles.

6. Where the customers actually come from

  1. Facebook local groups and Marketplace. Overwhelmingly the number one source for mobile detailing in the UK. Free, and the audience is already local.
  2. Before and after photographs. This trade sells on images more than any other. Same angle, same light, side by side. Post every single job.
  3. Google Business Profile. Free, and "car valeting near me" is a high intent search. Get reviews on it from day one.
  4. Small used car dealers. Steady repeat work preparing stock. Lower price per car, but it is volume and it fills weekdays.
  5. Private sellers. Someone about to list a car will happily pay £130 to add several hundred to the asking price. Genuinely worth telling them to have the paperwork ready too, since a buyer will check the MOT history before they view, and a clean car with a messy history still sits unsold.
  6. Street presence. Working on a driveway in good weather generates neighbours asking. Have cards in your pocket.

7. Insurance, waste water and the law

  • Public liability insurance. £1m minimum, typically £150 to £300 a year for a mobile detailer. This is the one thing you must not skip. Damage claims on paintwork are expensive and common.
  • Motor trade or custody cover if you ever move a customer's car, even ten feet on a driveway. Standard public liability does not cover it.
  • Waste water. Detergent run off going into a surface water drain is an offence under environmental regulations. In practice, wash on a permeable surface or a drive that drains to foul, and never near a roadside gully. Some councils enforce this and most do not, but the liability is yours.
  • Self employment. Register with HMRC once income passes the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, by 5 October following the end of that tax year.
  • COSHH. If you take on help, you have duties around the chemicals. Wheel acids and fallout removers are not harmless.

This is general information rather than legal advice, and insurance wording varies, so read what you are actually buying.

Myths that cost people money

"I need a ceramic coating certification to charge properly." Manufacturer accreditations are largely a sales channel for the coating brands. Your portfolio of before and after photographs sells the work. Learn to polish properly first, and worry about branded coatings later.

"Buy the van and wrap it, then customers come." A wrapped van is a £1,500 advert for a business with no customers. Get the customers, then let the work pay for the van.

"Undercut the local competition." Detailing at £15 a car attracts customers who will complain about a £15 car and never rebook. Price in the middle, be reliable, and photograph everything.

The honest verdict

This works if you are physically fit, you are genuinely fussy about detail, and you have somewhere to work in poor weather. £700 to £1,000 a month net from weekends is realistic within a few months, and the ceiling is high if you go full time with a unit.

This does not work if you have no covered space, no transport, and you expect it to be passive. It is manual labour outdoors in a country with a lot of rain, and the winter is genuinely difficult.

Start with about £250 of kit and one very well photographed car. Do not spend more until people are paying you.

Frequently asked

Do I need a van? No. A car with a decent boot works for valeting. The van becomes worthwhile when you add a water tank and a proper extractor.

How do I learn machine polishing? Practise on a scrap panel from a breaker, then your own car, then friends. A paint depth gauge is worth £60 once you are charging for correction work, because it tells you how much lacquer you have left to work with.

What is a realistic first month? Three to eight jobs, mostly from Facebook and word of mouth, at £45 to £65 each. Anyone claiming a full diary in week one is selling a course.

Should I offer a subscription? Yes, once you have repeat customers. A monthly maintenance wash at £30, booked in advance for a round of twelve cars in one area, is the most efficient work in this trade.
