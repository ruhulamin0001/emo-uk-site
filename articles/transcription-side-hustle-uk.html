Transcription as a Side Hustle in the UK: What It Really Pays

21 July 2026 · 6 min read · Checked July 2026

Transcription means turning audio into text: interviews, meetings, podcasts, legal and medical recordings. It is a genuine home based side hustle in the UK for anyone who types well and listens carefully. Here is the honest picture of the work and the pay.

What the job actually is

You listen to a recording and type out what is said, accurately and in the format the client wants. Some jobs are clean and easy, one clear speaker in a quiet room. Others are hard, several people talking over each other with background noise. The pay usually reflects the difficulty.

What it really pays

Transcription is priced per audio minute or per audio hour, and here is the part beginners miss: one hour of audio takes far longer than one hour to transcribe. A beginner might take three or four hours to do one hour of audio, so the real hourly rate starts low and rises fast as you get quicker.

Specialist work, like legal or medical transcription, pays much more because it needs training and accuracy. General transcription pays less but is open to anyone willing to learn.

The kit you need

  • A reliable computer and a good pair of headphones to catch every word.
  • Fast, accurate typing. Speed is literally your pay rate here.
  • Free or cheap transcription software with a foot pedal or keyboard shortcuts to pause and rewind quickly.
  • A quiet space to concentrate.

How to get quicker, and paid more

Speed comes with practice and shortcuts. Learning to control playback without leaving the keyboard, and using text expansion for common phrases, cuts your time dramatically. As your real hourly rate climbs, the same work pays far better.

Accuracy matters more than raw speed. A transcript full of errors gets rejected, so careful work builds the reputation that brings repeat clients.

Finding your first work

  1. Start on transcription platforms to get tested, get work, and build a track record.
  2. Practise on free audio to raise your speed before chasing better paid jobs.
  3. Consider a specialism, like legal, once you are confident, since it pays far more.
  4. Track your real hourly rate so you know which jobs are worth taking.

The tax side

Transcription income is self employed income. Once your total earnings pass £1,000 in a tax year you register for Self Assessment, as covered in our side hustle tax guide. Keep records from the first paid job.

The honest bottom line

Transcription is a real, flexible UK home income that rewards fast, accurate typists. The pay feels low until your speed climbs, then it becomes worthwhile, and specialising lifts it further. It suits the same around a job approach as our virtual assistant guide.
