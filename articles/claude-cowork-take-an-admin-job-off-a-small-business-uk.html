Claude Cowork Works Inside Your Folders, Not a Chat Box. What It Can Actually Take Off a UK Small Business, What It Cannot, and What a Client Month Costs to Run

Most people who have used Claude have used the chat, where you paste something in and copy something out. Cowork is a different product: you point it at folders and tools, describe the outcome, and it does the work in place, including on a schedule and, since August 2026, in its own built-in browser. That makes it the first Claude product that a person could plausibly run for a small business as a service. This guide sets out what it does on the day of writing, what it cannot do, and what a client month costs before you charge a penny.

1. What Cowork is, in Anthropic's own words

Anthropic's product page, read on 5 September 2026, describes Cowork as the place where you hand Claude real work: it runs on desktop, with web and mobile in beta, works in the files and tools you choose, and completes multi-step tasks from start to finish. The FAQ draws the line with ordinary chat plainly. In chat Claude responds to messages but cannot access your files directly. In Cowork it has permission to read, edit and create files in folders you specify, so it can complete tasks rather than describe how to do them. The same FAQ separates it from Claude Code, which is built for software engineering; Cowork is the same approach pointed at research, analysis, documents and admin.

The capabilities that matter for admin work: it works directly in folders and tools you choose, shows each step so you can redirect it, runs scheduled tasks unattended, and splits big jobs into chunks. It reads Word, PDF, plain text, Markdown, CSV, Excel, PowerPoint, common image formats, JSON and code. Connectors add Microsoft 365, Google Drive, Slack and others, and plugins bundle skills, connectors and sub-agents for a role.

The newest piece is a built-in browser, announced on 26 August 2026: when a task needs a site opened, a page read or a form filled, Claude opens a browser in the Cowork side panel instead of yours, separate from your own logins and tabs, available in the desktop app on Pro, Max and Team. Computer use, where Claude controls your actual screen, is still described as a research preview and as the last resort after connectors and the browser. The Chrome extensions guide covers the browser extension side of that story, and the plans guide covers what each tier includes.

2. The jobs it suits, and the ones it does not

Anthropic's own examples are all the same shape: a recurring report from a named folder, a reconciliation of exports against a budget file, a first-pass contract review against a playbook, a memo from a quarter of call notes. Each has three properties: the inputs live somewhere you can point at, the output is a document a human reviews, and the job repeats, so a scheduled task earns its setup time.

3. Usage limits, permissions and the safety guide

Three things from Anthropic's pages should shape any plan to run this for someone else. First, usage. The FAQ says agentic tasks consume more capacity than regular chat because Claude coordinates multiple sub-agents and tool calls, and the pricing card for Pro adds that Cowork consumes limits faster than chat and suggests upgrading for heavy use. Limits sit on a rolling five-hour window with weekly caps. A scheduled weekly report is light; a daily job across a large folder may not be. Run the job for a fortnight on Pro and read the usage screen before promising anything.

Second, permissions. The page says you decide what Claude can access, that it cannot reach anything else, that deleting anything needs your approval, and that with permission settings on it shows its plan and waits for approval before anything significant. For a service business that is the contract: you grant the client's one folder and one connector, nothing more, and the deletion prompt is a feature you tell the client about.

Third, the safety guide. Anthropic publishes an article on using Cowork safely, and its Chrome listing warns that websites can hide instructions that try to redirect the agent. The practical rules are the ones you would give a new junior: work on files you own, use ask-before-acting on sites you do not know, read the prompts, and keep it away from banking and payment portals.

4. What a client month costs to run

The subscription is the easy line. Pro is USD 20 a month, or USD 17 on the annual plan, and Anthropic says the price excludes tax, so with 20% VAT at HMRC's September 2026 rate of USD 1.3554 to GBP 1 that is about GBP 17.71 monthly. One Pro plan can serve several clients if the usage fits, but the honest planning assumption for a first client is that they get the whole plan. Cowork also does not need a domain, hosting or a payment processor; the client pays you by bank transfer, and the getting paid guide covers the fees if they pay any other way.

The real cost is your time, in two kinds. Setup: mapping the job, agreeing the folder, writing the instruction, running it three times and fixing what came out wrong; allow six to ten hours for a first client. Review: reading every output before the client relies on it; allow two hours a month for a weekly report until you have three months of clean runs.

The table says something uncomfortable. If the job saves the client only four hours a month, a fee of about GBP 100 barely covers your review time and never recovers the setup. It pays at eight hours saved, or when the same setup is sold to a second and third client in the same trade. The honest version of this business is one job type, several near-identical clients, and a fee that includes a written review step.

5. A worked example in Bolton

Rashid in Bolton does the books for two family-run takeaways and wants a third income line. Both owners spend Sunday evening building a weekly summary from the till export and the delivery platforms' statements. He sets up Cowork on Pro, has each owner drop the week's exports into one shared folder, and writes a scheduled Monday task that produces a one-page summary: takings by day, platform commission, refunds, and three lines the owner should look at. Setup takes him nine hours across two weekends, including one week where a changed export format broke the totals and he had to add a check.

He charges each takeaway GBP 60 a month with a written promise that he reads every summary before it goes out and that the owner's own accounts remain the record. Month one costs him GBP 17.71 plus about 11 hours, so he loses money. From month two his time is about 90 minutes a month for both clients, so his cost is GBP 17.71 plus GBP 37.50 at his GBP 25 rate, against GBP 120 in fees, leaving GBP 64.79 a month. It is not a living. It is a repeatable product he can offer to the other four takeaways whose owners he knows, and at six clients the same plan and roughly three hours of review a month would net him about GBP 267.

He is already registered for Self Assessment for his bookkeeping, so the new line goes on the same return; a beginner would use the GBP 1,000 trading allowance until they passed it and then register by the 5 October after the tax year they started, as the side hustle tax rules guide explains.

6. Data, contracts and the UK admin

Running a tool inside another business's files is a data protection matter. Under UK GDPR the takeaway is the controller of its customer and staff data and Rashid is a processor, which means a short written agreement describing what he does with the data, where it goes and when it is deleted. The Information Commissioner's Office publishes guidance for small organisations and the fee tier for most small businesses is modest, but check your own position on its site rather than assuming. Keep the client's folder to the minimum the job needs, and do not copy it elsewhere.

The models and usage table on Anthropic's pricing page lists model training as opt-out for Free, Pro and Max, and as none by default for Team and Enterprise. A client who asks the question deserves a straight answer: on a Pro plan you should check and set the training preference yourself, and if the client wants contractual assurances that is what the Team plan's commercial terms are for. Say so before they ask.

The honest verdict

Cowork is the first Claude product that makes a small recurring admin job deliverable as a service rather than a one-off favour, and on Pro it costs about GBP 17.71 a month to run. The arithmetic only works when one setup serves several similar clients and the job saves each of them eight hours a month or more, and it stops working the moment you promise it runs unattended, because Anthropic's own pages say every output is for review. Sell one bounded job, write the review step into the fee, keep it off anything that sends money or files with HMRC, and expect the first client to cost you more than it pays.

Frequently asked

Is Cowork on the free Claude plan? No. On 5 September 2026 the pricing page lists Claude Cowork as included on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise and not on Free. Pro is USD 20 a month before tax, about GBP 17.71 with VAT.

Does Cowork use more of my usage than chat? Yes. Anthropic's FAQ says agentic tasks consume more capacity than regular chat because Claude coordinates sub-agents and tool calls, and the Pro card on the Cowork page says it consumes limits faster than chat.

Can Cowork delete a client's files? The product page says deleting anything needs your approval, that you choose the folders and tools and Claude cannot reach anything else. Treat that as a reason to grant one folder per job.

Do I need the desktop app? The FAQ says no: Cowork runs on web and mobile in beta. The desktop app adds access to folders and applications on your own computer, and the built-in browser is in the desktop app on Pro, Max and Team.
