Five Student Loan Plans, Five Thresholds: What Each One Takes From a £32,000 Salary

Two people on identical salaries, doing identical jobs, can have student loan deductions that differ by more than £50 a month, purely because of where and when they started their course. Here are the 2026 to 2027 thresholds, and the same salary run through every plan so you can see the spread.

Ruhul Amin Published 16 September 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked September 2026
Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Plan 1: £26,900. Plan 2: £29,385. Plan 4: £33,795. Plan 5: £25,000. Postgraduate: £21,000.
  • Deductions are 9% on Plans 1, 2, 4 and 5, and 6% on a postgraduate loan.
  • You pay on income above the threshold, not on all of it. This is the single most misunderstood part of the system.
  • On £32,000, Plan 4 takes nothing at all because the threshold is higher than the salary.
  • Two loans deduct at the same time. Plan 2 plus a postgraduate loan on £32,000 is about £74.61 a month.
  • Which plan you are on is set by where and when you studied, not by how much you borrowed or how much is left.

1. The 2026 to 2027 thresholds

These are the earnings thresholds your employer's payroll uses to work out deductions, published by HMRC for the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

Plan 1: £26,900 a year, £2,241.66 a month, £517.30 a week. Deduction 9%.

Plan 2: £29,385 a year, £2,448.75 a month, £565.09 a week. Deduction 9%.

Plan 4: £33,795 a year, £2,816.25 a month, £649.90 a week. Deduction 9%.

Plan 5: £25,000 a year, £2,083.33 a month, £480.76 a week. Deduction 9%.

Postgraduate loan: £21,000 a year, £1,750 a month, £403.84 a week. Deduction 6%.

Note the spread. The gap between the lowest threshold, Plan 5 at £25,000, and the highest, Plan 4 at £33,795, is £8,895. At 9%, that difference alone is worth £800 a year to someone earning above both.

2. Which plan you are actually on

You do not choose, and it has nothing to do with the size of your balance. In broad terms:

  • Plan 1 is the older England and Wales arrangement, and also covers Northern Ireland students.
  • Plan 2 covers England and Wales students who started after the 2012 fee change and before the Plan 5 cutover.
  • Plan 4 is the Scottish plan, which is why it has the highest threshold on the list.
  • Plan 5 is the newest English plan, for courses starting from August 2023, and it has the lowest threshold of the four undergraduate plans.
  • Postgraduate sits on top of any of them, at a lower threshold and a lower percentage.

If you are not certain which one you are on, do not guess from this list. Your online student loan account states it, and so does your payslip, which will show a plan type against the deduction. Getting this wrong in either direction is common, and an employer applying the wrong plan is one of the more frequent payroll errors people never notice.

3. The same salary through all five plans

One salary, £32,000 a year, run through each plan. The calculation is always the same shape: take the salary, subtract the threshold, apply the percentage.

Plan 1. £32,000 less £26,900 is £5,100. At 9%, £459 a year, about £38.25 a month.

Plan 2. £32,000 less £29,385 is £2,615. At 9%, £235.35 a year, about £19.61 a month.

Plan 4. £32,000 is below the £33,795 threshold. Nothing at all.

Plan 5. £32,000 less £25,000 is £7,000. At 9%, £630 a year, about £52.50 a month.

Postgraduate. £32,000 less £21,000 is £11,000. At 6%, £660 a year, £55 a month.

Two things jump out of that. The postgraduate loan, despite the lower 6% rate, takes more per month than any single undergraduate plan at this salary, because its threshold is so much lower. And the difference between the best and worst undergraduate outcome on identical pay is £630 a year, decided entirely by which side of a start date you happened to fall on.

4. Worked example: Chloe in Sunderland

Chloe, Sunderland. Earns £32,000. She has a Plan 2 undergraduate loan and a postgraduate loan from a master's she did afterwards. Both deduct at the same time.

Plan 2: £2,615 above threshold at 9% is £235.35 a year.

Postgraduate: £11,000 above threshold at 6% is £660 a year.

Combined: £895.35 a year, about £74.61 a month, roughly 2.8% of her gross salary.

If she takes a £3,000 pay rise to £35,000, the extra deduction is 9% of £3,000 plus 6% of £3,000, so £450 more a year. The rise is still worth having. But the effective marginal rate on that £3,000, once income tax at 20% and National Insurance at 8% are added, is 43%, and that is the number worth knowing before you conclude a pay rise did not show up in your bank account.

5. Three things people get wrong

  1. Thinking the deduction is on the whole salary. It is on the amount above the threshold. Someone on Plan 2 earning £30,000 is not paying 9% of £30,000, they are paying 9% of £615, which is about £4.61 a month.
  2. Overpaying to clear it early without doing the sums. This is a genuinely personal decision and it depends on your plan, your balance, your interest rate and how long you have left. For many borrowers on the newer plans the balance is never cleared and voluntary overpayments simply hand over money that would otherwise have been written off. For others, near the end of a small balance on an older plan, clearing it is sensible. Do not take a general rule from either camp.
  3. Assuming it stops when you go self employed. It does not. It moves into self assessment and is collected with your tax bill.

The honest verdict

The system is not complicated once you see the shape of it: salary, minus threshold, times percentage. What makes it feel arbitrary is that the threshold you get is decided by an administrative fact about your course, and the spread between them is wide enough to matter.

The one number worth taking away is the marginal rate. If you are on Plan 5 or have a postgraduate loan alongside an undergraduate one, the slice of every pay rise that reaches your account is smaller than the headline suggests. That is an argument for understanding the arithmetic, and for looking at salary sacrifice arrangements like pension contributions which reduce the earnings the deduction is calculated on. It is not an argument against the pay rise.

Frequently asked

I have two loans. Do they both come out at once? Yes. A postgraduate loan sits alongside an undergraduate plan and both are deducted in the same pay period, each against its own threshold and percentage. On £32,000 with Plan 2 plus postgraduate, that is about £74.61 a month in total.

My salary is below the threshold. Does interest still build up? Repayments and interest are separate things. Not earning above the threshold means no repayment is due, but it does not by itself freeze the balance. How interest is applied differs by plan, and the current rates and the write off rules for your plan are set out on the GOV.UK repaying pages linked below.

Can my employer deduct the wrong plan? Yes, and it happens. If a new employer does not receive the right plan type they can apply a default. Check the plan type shown on your payslip against the plan in your online student loan account. If it is wrong, tell your employer, and contact the Student Loans Company about any over deduction.

Does a bonus get hit too? Yes. Deductions are worked out on the earnings in that pay period, so a month containing a bonus can produce a much larger deduction than usual. It evens out over the year for most people, but it is a common reason a bonus lands smaller than expected.
