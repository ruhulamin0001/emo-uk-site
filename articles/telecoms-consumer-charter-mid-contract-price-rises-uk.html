April 2026 Was The Last Inflation-Linked Broadband Price Rise. The Charter Behind That Promise Is Voluntary, And Here Is Exactly What It Does And Does Not Stop.

Mid-contract price rises are the reason broadband and mobile bills drift upward without anyone agreeing to it. Two things have changed the rules: an Ofcom regulation from January 2025 that forces rises to be stated in pounds and pence before you sign, and a voluntary charter from February 2026 in which the big providers promised that the price you sign up to is the price you pay. This is what each one actually guarantees, and what neither does.

1. Two different things: the Ofcom rule and the charter

It helps to separate the regulation from the promise, because news coverage runs them together.

The regulation is Ofcom's. Since 17 January 2025, providers cannot write inflation-linked mid-contract price rises, the old "CPI plus 3.9%" formula, into new contracts, and any scheduled rise must be set out in pounds and pence, prominently, before the customer signs. Ofcom can enforce that with fines. Contracts signed before that date could keep their inflation-linked terms until they ended, which is why so many people still saw a CPI-plus rise in April 2025 and April 2026.

The promise is the government's. On 11 February 2026 the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology published the Telecoms Consumer Charter, a set of voluntary commitments signed by the largest providers after what the government described as an intervention by the Chancellor and the Technology Secretary. The charter's own text says it "builds on" Ofcom's rules, including the January 2025 pounds-and-pence approach, end-of-contract notifications and One Touch Switching. It adds commitments; it does not add powers.

2. What the charter commits signatories to

The charter has three headings. The ones that touch your bill are under transparency.

The price you sign up to is the price you pay. All signatories commit that, where a contract includes a mid-contract price increase, the core subscription price customers sign up to is the price they will pay, with any exception "limited strictly to unforeseeable and externally driven events that materially affect the cost of providing services". Read carefully, this is about the rise being the one disclosed at signing, not about there being no rise.

April 2026 was the last inflation-linked rise. For any customer still on legacy inflation-linked terms for consumer services, April 2026 was the final increase expressed in those terms for the core subscription. Those contracts move to the pounds-and-pence system, and some providers said they would tell customers the pound figure ahead of that date.

Clearer information. Signatories commit to presenting the full package in one place, headline price, contract length, key terms and add-ons, and to highlighting any agreed changes such as mid-contract rises throughout the life of the contract, and to plain-English end-of-contract and annual best tariff notifications.

Under the third heading, support for people struggling to pay, signatories commit to signposting social tariffs to eligible customers in end-of-contract notifications, on websites and in apps, and to offering practical help such as moving to a cheaper package without charge or a manageable payment plan.

The signatories as at 19 June 2026: BT Group, VMO2, VodafoneThree, Sky, TalkTalk, KCOM, Community Fibre, WightFibre, Hyperoptic and Olilo. The charter says providers can sign at any time.

3. What it does not do

It is not enforceable. The charter is voluntary. A signatory that breaks it faces the government's displeasure, not a fine. The enforceable protections remain Ofcom's rules.

It does not ban mid-contract rises. A new contract can still say "£3 a month more from April 2027 and April 2028". The charter says that figure must be the one you were shown; Ofcom's rule says it must be in pounds and pence. Neither says it must be zero.

It covers the core subscription only. Add-ons, out-of-bundle charges and equipment are outside the commitment.

It binds signatories only. Providers who have not signed are bound by Ofcom's rules and nothing more. Check the list before assuming a promise applies to your provider.

The exception is open-ended. "Unforeseeable and externally driven events" is not defined. Energy costs and regulatory charges have been cited by providers before. The charter gives no examples and no test.

MoneySavingExpert's Martin Lewis called the charter an improvement that "leaves two glaring gaps", and those two gaps are the ones above: fixed pound rises are still permitted in new contracts, and the whole thing is voluntary.

4. A worked example in Croydon

Femi in Croydon signs a 24 month full-fibre broadband contract on 1 September 2026 at £29.99 a month. The contract summary, as Ofcom requires, states a £3 a month rise on 1 April 2027 and a further £3 on 1 April 2028.

Our arithmetic: seven months at £29.99 (September to March) is £209.93; twelve months at £32.99 (April 2027 to March 2028) is £395.88; five months at £35.99 (April to August 2028) is £179.95. Total £785.76 over 24 months, an effective £32.74 a month. The headline £29.99 is what the comparison table shows; the £32.74 is what the contract costs.

What the charter gives him: certainty that £3 is £3, not £3 plus whatever inflation turns out to be, unless his provider invokes an unforeseeable external event. What Ofcom's rule gives him: the right to have seen that £3 in pounds and pence before he signed. What neither gives him: a contract with no rise. A rival's £31.49 with no scheduled rises would cost £755.76 over the same 24 months, £30 less, despite the higher headline.

Had Femi signed in 2024 on a "CPI plus 3.9%" contract, April 2026 would have been his last rise on that formula, and under the charter his provider should now have told him the pound figure for anything after that.

5. How to use it when you sign, and when you are already in contract

Signing a new deal: find the mid-contract rise line in the contract summary; it must be there in pounds and pence. Multiply out the whole term as above and compare totals, not headlines. If a provider is a signatory and the rise is not the one you were shown when your first bill after April arrives, quote the charter and the contract summary together.

Already in contract with a legacy inflation-linked deal: April 2026 should have been the last CPI-plus rise from a signatory. If a bill after that shows an inflation-linked increase from one of the ten, complain in writing, and if unresolved after eight weeks take it to the Communications Ombudsman or CISAS, whichever scheme your provider belongs to; both are free.

Struggling to pay: the charter commits signatories to point eligible customers to social tariffs and to allow a move to a cheaper package without penalty. Ask for both by name. Social tariffs for people on Universal Credit and other means-tested benefits are a real product and are cheaper than almost anything advertised.

Out of contract: none of this matters as much as the end-of-contract notification. Out-of-contract prices are where the real overpayment sits, and One Touch Switching makes moving a single request to the new provider.

6. The honest verdict

The enforceable protection is Ofcom's: rises in pounds and pence, shown before you sign, since January 2025. Use it every time you compare a deal, by pricing the whole term.

The charter is a promise from ten providers that the pound figure they showed you is the one you pay, and that April 2026 ended CPI-plus rises on old contracts. It is worth quoting. It is not worth relying on as if it were law.

Neither stops a provider writing a fixed rise into a new contract. The only defence against that is arithmetic before you sign, and a provider that does not do it.

Frequently asked

Are mid-contract price rises banned now? No. Ofcom banned inflation-linked rises in new contracts from 17 January 2025 and requires any rise to be stated in pounds and pence before signing. Fixed pound rises are still allowed. The charter does not change that.

Which providers signed the charter? As at 19 June 2026: BT Group, VMO2, VodafoneThree, Sky, TalkTalk, KCOM, Community Fibre, WightFibre, Hyperoptic and Olilo. Others can sign later.

My contract is from 2024 and rose with CPI in April 2026. Will it rise again? Under the charter, April 2026 was the last inflation-linked rise on legacy terms from a signatory. Any further rise should be a pound figure told to you in advance. If not, complain and escalate to the ombudsman after eight weeks.

Can Ofcom fine a provider for breaking the charter? No. The charter is voluntary. Ofcom enforces its own rules, including the pounds-and-pence requirement, not the charter.
