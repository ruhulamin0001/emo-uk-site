Written Off, but Only This Debt: Who Ofgem's Debt Relief Scheme Was Built For, and Why Its Own Page Still Says Awaiting Decision

In October 2025 Ofgem announced a scheme to write off up to £500 million of energy debt for around 195,000 households, with the first phase expected in early 2026. It is the largest write-off of household energy debt the regulator has proposed, and the rules are narrow: means-tested benefits, more than £100 of debt, built up in one two-year window, no application. Here is who it was built for, the things it was never going to cover, what Ofgem's own website says about its status on 5 September 2026, and the help that exists for energy debt whether or not the scheme arrives.

The short version

  • Phase 1 was designed for households on means-tested benefits with more than £100 of energy debt built up between April 2022 and March 2024. Source: Ofgem press release, 30 October 2025, and the Debt Relief Scheme statutory consultation, published 6 November 2025.
  • No application. Eligible customers were to be identified automatically, using DWP benefit data shared with suppliers, and contacted by their supplier. That data sharing needed legislation, which is why the launch was "subject to the passage of legislation in Parliament".
  • Up to £500 million for around 195,000 customers, against £4.4 billion of debt in the energy system. Ofgem says the cost of recovering or writing off that debt "is spread across everyone's bills".
  • Status on 5 September 2026: Ofgem's statutory consultation page shows "Closed (awaiting decision)". It said a decision was expected in January 2026. Ofgem's publications search returns no decision document. We cannot confirm from Ofgem's site that any debt has been written off.
  • What it does NOT do: nothing for debt built up after March 2024, nothing for households not on a means-tested benefit in phase 1, nothing for anyone who refuses to engage with a repayment plan or debt advice, and nothing you can apply for.
  • Our verdict: if you are on a means-tested benefit with crisis-era debt, watch for a letter from your supplier and do not pay a third party to "apply" for you. Whatever happens to the scheme, an affordable repayment plan, Breathing Space and the Warm Home Discount exist today.

1. What Ofgem designed: the phase 1 rules

Ofgem's press release of 30 October 2025 set out the shape: "A scheme to tackle historical debt built up during the energy crisis could help around 195,000 customers by writing off up to £500m." The first phase "will focus on people in receipt of means-tested benefits with more than £100 of debt built up between during the energy crisis (April 2022 to March 2024)". Eligible households "will be expected to be making some contribution towards their debt or ongoing energy use, or if they are unable to do so at this time, be willing to work with a debt advice charity", and "they would also be identified automatically and contacted by suppliers".

The statutory consultation followed on 6 November 2025, proposing licence changes to make suppliers deliver the scheme and a delivery guidance document for them. Its description gives the timing and the condition in one sentence: "We propose that this should be implemented in early 2026, subject to the passage of legislation in Parliament to allow sharing of data on customer eligibility for Means Tested Benefits between energy suppliers and the Department for Work and Pensions." The consultation closed in December 2025, and Ofgem said it expected "to publish our decision on whether to proceed with the proposed licence changes in January 2026". A second phase, for customers not on means-tested benefits, was to be assessed on ability to pay; Ofgem has published no rules for it.

2. Where it stands on 5 September 2026

This is the part we cannot dress up. On 5 September 2026 Ofgem's statutory consultation page carries the status line "Closed (awaiting decision)", with a closing date of 19 December 2025. A search of Ofgem's publications for "debt relief scheme" returns two documents, the November 2025 consultation and the October 2025 press release, and no decision. The "early 2026" launch depended on Parliament passing data-sharing legislation, and nothing on Ofgem's site records that it did. It is possible that suppliers have begun contacting customers under arrangements Ofgem has not published; it is also possible the scheme has slipped. What we can say from the primary source is that no decision document has been published, and that any letter, call or advert telling you to "apply now" for the Debt Relief Scheme is not describing a scheme Ofgem has launched. There is no application, and there never was going to be one.

3. What the scheme does not do

It does nothing for debt built up after March 2024, however it arose. It does nothing in phase 1 for a household not on a means-tested benefit, whatever its income; phase 2 was meant to reach those people and has no published rules. It does not write off debt for a customer who refuses both a repayment contribution and debt advice. It does not touch debt on a prepayment meter that was never billed as arrears, or debt owed to a supplier that has since failed, unless the delivery guidance says otherwise. It does not reduce the standing charge or unit rate; Ofgem's own line is that the cost is spread across everyone's bills. And it is not an application scheme, so nobody can get you onto it, and anyone charging a fee to try is selling nothing.

4. What to do about energy debt today

The help that exists now does not depend on the scheme. Every supplier must offer a repayment plan you can afford, based on your ability to pay, under its Ofgem licence; ask for one in writing and say what you can pay. Breathing Space, the government scheme run through debt advisers, freezes interest, fees and enforcement on qualifying debts including energy for 60 days while you get advice. The Warm Home Discount puts £150 on the electricity account of every means-tested benefit household this winter with no application. If you are on Pension Credit or a disability benefit, ask your supplier to add you to the Priority Services Register. And if a supplier is threatening a prepayment meter, Ofgem's rules bar forced installation for many vulnerable households, and Citizens Advice will tell you whether you are one of them. The January 2027 cap forecast is the number to plan repayments around, because it is going up.

5. A worked example in Lincoln

Donna in Lincoln gets Universal Credit and owes her supplier £1,340. Her account history shows £900 of that built up between spring 2022 and early 2024, when her direct debit could not keep pace with the crisis rates, and £440 since. She pays £40 a month towards it on top of current use.

Under the phase 1 rules as proposed, she is exactly who the scheme was for: means-tested benefit, over £100 of crisis-era debt, already making a contribution. If the scheme runs as designed, up to the £900 could be written off without her doing anything, leaving £440, which at £40 a month clears in eleven months rather than nearly three years. What she should not do is stop paying while she waits, because engagement was a condition. What she should do now is ask her supplier to confirm the repayment plan in writing, check the Warm Home Discount lands on the account in the winter, and, if she is contacted, keep the letter and check the sender is her supplier before answering. If nothing has arrived by the time Ofgem publishes a decision, or by the end of 2026, the £40 a month plan is still the right one.

6. The honest verdict

Ofgem's Debt Relief Scheme was built for a narrow group: households on means-tested benefits with more than £100 of energy debt from April 2022 to March 2024, identified automatically and written to by their supplier. Up to £500 million, around 195,000 households, no application.

On 5 September 2026 Ofgem's own page still says the decision is awaited and no decision document is published, so nobody should count on it, and nobody should pay anyone to apply for it. Ask for an affordable repayment plan, use Breathing Space if you need it, make sure the Warm Home Discount arrives, and treat a supplier letter about the scheme as a bonus if it comes.

Frequently asked

Who qualifies for the Ofgem Debt Relief Scheme? As proposed for phase 1: households on a means-tested benefit with more than £100 of energy debt built up between April 2022 and March 2024, who are making some contribution or willing to take debt advice. Source: Ofgem press release, 30 October 2025.

How do I apply? You cannot. The scheme was designed with no application; suppliers identify eligible customers using DWP data and contact them. Anyone offering to apply for you is not offering anything real.

Has the scheme started? Ofgem's statutory consultation page showed "Closed (awaiting decision)" on 5 September 2026 and its publications search returns no decision document. We cannot confirm from Ofgem's site that any debt has been written off.

Does it cover debt from this year? No. Phase 1 covers debt built up between April 2022 and March 2024 only.
